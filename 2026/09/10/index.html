★★★ 1. Extended gamma-ray emission in the vicinity of Westerlund 1 and Kes 41 seen by Fermi-LAT

L. Tibaldo, R. Bernet, L. Härer, M. Lemoine-Goumard, L. Mohrmann, G. Peron, B. Reville, T. Vieu
arXiv:2609.10176

Using more than 16 years of Fermi-LAT data above 0.8 GeV, the authors identify three new extended, soft-spectrum gamma-ray components in the Westerlund 1/Kes 41 region. One is associated with the natal cloud of Kes 41 and is statistically preferred over the previously used point-source description. Two others overlap neutral gas within roughly 100 pc of the edge of the Westerlund 1 superbubble.

There is an important caveat: they cannot yet cleanly distinguish real locally injected CR populations from residual mismodelling of the Galactic interstellar emission. If the components are physical, the Kes 41 emission requires ≲5% of the SNR energy budget in accelerated particles, while the two Wd1 components correspond to a gamma-ray luminosity of roughly 10−4 of the cluster-wind mechanical power.

Significance: this is precisely the sort of intermediate-scale CR-transport phenomenology that can alter how one interprets TeV–PeV emission and neutrino expectations from stellar-cluster environments.

Classification: new observational result; interpretation not yet unique.

★★★ 2. Nonthermal Solar Stalling of an Inelastic Scalar Signal in Xenon

XinXin Qi, Hao Sun
arXiv:2609.10636

This light-mediator scalar model tests how nonthermal solar capture affects the interpretation of the LZ recoil candidate.

The authors construct a concrete anomaly-free

U(1)B−3Lτ

model containing an 840 GeV complex scalar DM particle undergoing an endothermic transition with splitting

δ≃320 keV,

mediated by a 160 MeV vector. Their benchmark gives about 0.96 expected accepted events in their reconstruction of the high-energy LZ search, i.e. roughly the scale required to interpret the single 248 keV LZ recoil candidate.

The calculation follows the evolution of DM after solar capture.

Instead of assuming rapid thermalization into a compact central DM distribution, they evolve the captured particles in energy, angular momentum and internal state, including thermal nuclear motion, finite scattering rates and excited-state decay. Because the scattering is endothermic, the particles eventually reach orbital configurations for which further up-scattering is kinematically forbidden.

They find a nonthermal population that effectively stalls around

reff≃0.194−0.198R⊙,

rather than thermalizing in the solar core. The resulting annihilation rate is only

Γann~1.3×1015 s−1,

which in their benchmark is more than three orders of magnitude below their IceCube-based neutrino proxy.

Solar-neutrino constraints on electroweak inelastic DM cannot automatically be carried over to every inelastic-DM realization.

This paper provides an explicit light-mediator scalar counterexample.

Significance: the lesson is broader than the LZ anomaly. Whenever one maps DM capture to a neutrino signal, the usual capture–thermalization–annihilation equilibrium assumption can fail badly if the scattering has nontrivial kinematic thresholds.

Classification: new model-specific phenomenology; particularly valuable as a counterpoint to broad solar-neutrino exclusion arguments.
