ArXiv highlights

★★★ 1. IceCube neutrino point-source searches in the direction of the KM3NeT ultra-high-energy event

IceCube Collaboration — R. Abbasi et al.
arXiv:2609.10931

IceCube uses 15 years of data to search the direction of KM3-230213A, the extraordinary KM3NeT event with reconstructed energy around 200 PeV, under three temporal hypotheses: a steady source, a flare coincident with the KM3NeT event, and a transient occurring at an arbitrary time. They find no evidence for neutrino emission in any search and derive point-source flux limits. The important conclusion is that if KM3-230213A came from an astrophysical point source, the simplest interpretation in which that source produces an E−2-like flux at the normalization inferred from the single KM3NeT event is now strongly constrained, both for steady and transient scenarios.

Significance: this significantly sharpens the astrophysical interpretation problem surrounding the KM3NeT event. The combination of one extraordinarily energetic KM3NeT neutrino and a long IceCube null exposure is becoming an interesting consistency test rather than merely an “extreme-energy neutrino” story.

Classification: genuinely new experimental constraint.

★★★ 2. Solar Neutrino Constraints on Inelastic Dark Matter Scattering in Light of Recent LUX-ZEPLIN Observations

Thong T. Q. Nguyen, Tim Linden, Dan Hooper
arXiv:2609.11833

The recent LZ high-recoil candidate has encouraged DM models in which the recoil spectrum is strongly momentum-dependent or the scattering is inelastic, allowing a large high-energy recoil without producing the many low-energy events expected from conventional elastic WIMPs. Nguyen, Linden and Hooper point out an independent astroparticle constraint: such particles can be captured in the Sun, annihilate there, and generate high-energy solar neutrinos. IceCube sees no corresponding excess.

Their conclusion is that IceCube solar-neutrino observations strongly constrain a substantial part of the inelastic-DM parameter space relevant to the LZ event. This tests the recoil interpretation through an independent messenger.

For perspective, LZ itself reports only 2.6σ local significance, approximately a 0.5% background probability, and explicitly does not claim a DM discovery.

Significance: a multimessenger connection between direct detection, solar capture and neutrino-telescope observations.

Classification: new phenomenological constraint on interpretations of the LZ candidate.

★★★ 3. The Dark Dimension and Majorana Neutrinos

Arturo de Giorgi, Dhruv Pasari
arXiv:2609.10668

The setup is the “Dark Dimension” scenario motivated by swampland arguments: an extra spatial dimension with characteristic size

R~0.1−10 μm,

with Majorana fermions propagating in the bulk and masses in roughly the 1−10 keV regime. The authors explicitly diagonalize the resulting neutrino/Kaluza–Klein system and then confront it with three rather different observables:

Daya Bay oscillations,KATRIN β-decay,KamLAND-Zen 0νββ.

The technically interesting point is the neutrinoless-double-beta-decay calculation. They derive a closed expression for the effective Majorana mass including the KK tower and find that the tower can screen the 0νββ amplitude once the characteristic nuclear momentum exceeds the compactification and Majorana scales. Thus adding a tower of Majorana states does not simply add positive contributions to mββ; destructive structure emerges from the complete spectrum.

Within their preferred Dark-Dimension parameter window, KATRIN becomes the strongest terrestrial constraint, pushing the relevant Yukawa coupling to approximately

y≲O(10−3),

while oscillation experiments and 0νββ probe complementary regions of the Majorana-mass parameter space.

Significance: it is an unusually clean example where oscillations, direct kinematic neutrino-mass measurements and 0νββ constrain the same explicit BSM neutrino construction. The KK screening of 0νββ is a distinctive feature of the calculation.

Classification: new neutrino/BSM phenomenology; high-interest theory paper.

★★☆ 4. Low-Mass Magnetic Monopoles in the Galaxy: Simulations and Comparison with Ultra-High-Energy Cosmic-Ray Data

Ala’a AL-Zetoun, Igor Ostrovskiy, Peiwen Mi, Daniele Perri
arXiv:2609.10784

The authors implement Galactic propagation of low-mass magnetic monopoles in a custom CRPropa-based simulation with a realistic Galactic magnetic field. Because magnetic monopoles are accelerated along magnetic-field structures rather than deflected in the ordinary qv×B sense, their trajectories develop what the authors call Galactic magnetic funnels, producing strongly anisotropic arrival directions. They then compare the predicted directional/energy distributions with Pierre Auger data.

The data generally favor zero monopole contribution, and the profile-likelihood analysis yields a 90% C.L. integral flux limit below roughly

2.1×10−23 cm−2s−1sr−1.

Significance: beyond monopole phenomenology, the study develops methods for modelling a highly anisotropic exotic charged population propagating through the GMF.

Classification: new simulation + Auger constraint; niche but technically interesting.

★★☆ 5. EFT Approaches to Sommerfeld Enhancement and Bound States in Singular Potentials

Arindam Bhattacharya, Rashmish K. Mishra, Tracy R. Slatyer
arXiv:2609.10690

The issue is that singular long-range potentials make the usual Schrödinger boundary-condition treatment of Sommerfeld enhancement and bound-state formation ambiguous. They attack the problem both with velocity power counting in nonrelativistic EFT and by explicitly regularizing the potential at short distance.

For a pseudoscalar mediator coupled to massive Dirac DM, an important result is that a weakly coupled UV-complete theory need not generate Sommerfeld enhancement at all. Conversely, derivative/higher-dimensional interactions can produce enhancement and bound states at weak coupling when there is a sufficiently large hierarchy between the EFT cutoff and mχ. In that case the enhancement retains genuine UV sensitivity.

Significance: it is a useful warning against blindly inserting a singular potential into the Schrödinger equation and interpreting the resulting large enhancement as robust DM phenomenology.

Classification: conceptual/methodological advance rather than a new observational result.

★★☆ 6. Re-examining the sensitivity of JWST to decaying axion dark matter

Caleb Gemmell, Christopher Dessert, Andrea Caputo, Joshua W. Foster
arXiv:2609.10674

Different JWST searches for the infrared line from a→γγ had been claiming coupling sensitivities differing by roughly an order of magnitude. The authors redo the data reduction and use flexible non-parametric inference, concluding that some of the strongest previously claimed sensitivities are simply not achievable with the datasets used.

Their robust analysis nevertheless excludes QCD-axion DM over approximately

ma≃0.5−2.5 eV,

and gives complementary ALP constraints at 0.1−0.5 eV. Importantly, they argue that the present analyses are approaching a systematics floor, so simply accumulating more exposure will not give the naive statistical improvement unless JWST instrumental modelling and reduction pipelines improve.

Significance: not “new physics discovered,” but exactly the kind of systematic reassessment that determines whether a spectacular exclusion plot is actually trustworthy.

Classification: important re-analysis/systematics paper.

★★☆ 7. Strong Black Hole Natal Kicks in Magnetized Accretion-Powered Explosions

Sean E. Li, Ore Gottlieb, Brian D. Metzger
arXiv:2609.10694

Using 3D general-relativistic MHD simulations extending to ~30 s after collapse, they show that rapidly rotating, strongly magnetized collapsars can give newly formed black holes natal kicks of

vkick~102−103 kms−1,

substantially larger than the weak BH kicks often assumed.

Three effects contribute:

asymmetric electromagnetic outflow,
magnetic-flux eruptions,

and

gravitational pull from asymmetric ejecta.

Faster-spinning BHs tend to receive larger and more spin-aligned kicks. Particularly strong asymmetries occur when the system undergoes a delayed transition into a magnetically arrested disk (MAD) state.

The astrophysical connection is that the strong coherent magnetic flux required to generate these kicks is also precisely the ingredient invoked for relativistic GRB jets and jet-driven hypernovae. Thus strong BH kicks might be an intrinsic consequence of some collapsar engines rather than an independent phenomenon.

Consequences include BH retention in clusters, binary disruption, spin-orbit misalignment and formation channels of high-spin BHs in or near the pair-instability mass gap.

Significance: this connects GRB-engine MHD directly to the compact-object populations measured by gravitational waves.

Classification: genuinely new numerical result; strong high-energy-astrophysics paper.

★★☆ 8. Teglon: A Pixel-Level Pipeline for Galaxy-Informed Gravitational-Wave Follow-up Planning and Efficiency Analysis

D. A. Coulter, P. Darc, C. D. Kilpatrick, R. J. Foley, P. McGill, S. D. Wyatt, R. Hausen
arXiv:2609.10736

Teglon takes a three-dimensional GW localization posterior and combines it with a 3D galaxy-completeness model, producing a revised probability map informed by the known galaxy distribution. It can then ingest an arbitrary telescope footprint and continuously interpolate between pure galaxy targeting and ordinary sky tiling.

The clever extension is integration with Redback, allowing pixel-by-pixel detection efficiencies to be computed for transient models. The pipeline evaluates more than

P(source is here),

but effectively

P(detect∣source here,instrument,t,transient model).

This supports an assessment of the detection efficiency of a follow-up campaign.

They report particularly substantial gains for telescopes with fields of view ≲1 deg2, and Teglon has already been used through O3/O4 and is being prepared for O5.

Significance: the tool quantitatively connects GW posterior volumes to gamma-ray, X-ray and optical counterpart searches.

Classification: methodological/tool paper, not a new astrophysical result.

★★☆ 9. Hierarchical Population Inference with Normalizing Flows for Binary Black Holes

Leonardo Iampieri, Simone Mastrogiovanni
arXiv:2609.11885

Instead of assuming a low-dimensional parametric BBH mass model, they represent the source population

p(z,m1,m2)

with a normalizing flow embedded directly inside the hierarchical GW likelihood. Event posterior samples propagate individual-event uncertainty, while injection campaigns encode detector selection effects.

Restricting the inference to the region sufficiently sampled by injections can itself create an apparent correlation between mass and redshift. They therefore introduce a mutual-information diagnostic comparing the inferred distribution with a redshift-independent reference subject to the same support restriction.

Applied to GWTC-5.0, the reconstruction independently recovers population features near

m1~10M⊙,35M⊙,

but finds no significant evidence that the intrinsic primary-mass distribution evolves with redshift.

Significance: mainly methodology—the warning about spurious correlations induced by selection/support boundaries is applicable well beyond gravitational-wave inference.

Classification: strong methodological work; result itself is broadly consistent with existing LVK population conclusions.

★☆☆ 10. Ultralight Axial Dark Matter

Anson Hook, Junwu Huang, Kevin Zhou
arXiv:2609.10671

They explore ultralight DM as an axial vector field rather than the usual scalar/axion or vector dark photon. Fermionic couplings generate spin torques that can be parametrically enhanced relative to axion DM. They also construct an analogue of the axion-photon coupling, though its consistent realization requires a photon mass, and discuss signatures involving polarimetry, superconducting cavities, electric fields and the CMB. The massive axial vector description is dual to a massive two-form/Kalb–Ramond field.

Significance: an expansion of the ultralight-DM model space with unusual experimental signatures.

Classification: new theoretical framework/model-building.

★☆☆ 11. Cosmological Evolution of Primordial Black Holes: UV/IR Decoupling and the KM3NeT 220 PeV Neutrino Anomaly

Debdatta Dey, Tausif Parvez, S. Shankaranarayanan
arXiv:2609.11511

The proposal is that KM3-230213A came from the terminal Hawking evaporation of a primordial black hole. The authors embed the PBH in a McVittie cosmological spacetime: early-time cosmological accretion and expansion modify the PBH lifetime and initial mass required to evaporate at the present epoch, while the final z≃0 phase becomes essentially Schwarzschild. They argue that this can produce a large local ~220 PeV neutrino signal while avoiding excessive diffuse early-time emission.

This theoretical interpretation is highly model-dependent, and the IceCube point-source search further constrains source interpretations of KM3-230213A.

Significance: the paper explores an exotic origin for the KM3NeT event, with viability dependent on the underlying PBH and source assumptions.

Classification: speculative interpretation.

★★★ A population of “hypersoft X-ray sources”

Muhibullah, Irwin and Di Stefano report in Nature Astronomy a previously largely missed population of luminous, point-like, non-nuclear objects emitting predominantly or exclusively below 0.3 keV. The brightest have narrow-band X-ray luminosities approaching 1038 erg/s, while spectral modelling implies even larger bolometric luminosities dominated by the difficult-to-observe extreme-UV band.

NASA reports 84 such sources in six galaxies, found retrospectively in Chandra archival data. Candidate physical systems include accreting white dwarfs/post-novae and accreting black-hole binaries. Because their EUV luminosity can be enormous, they might also provide a previously underestimated contribution to ionizing galactic gas.

★★☆ Magnetars may constitute roughly half of isolated neutron-star births/population

A new Nature Astronomy population-synthesis study by Celsa Pardo-Araujo, Nanda Rea, Michele Ronchi and Vanessa Graber finds a surprisingly large magnetar fraction. Among observed Galactic neutron stars younger than 2 kyr, only 9 of 23 are ordinary rotation-powered pulsars; the remainder are magnetars or central compact objects. Their combined dynamical, spin-down and magneto-thermal population synthesis favors a bimodal birth-field distribution in which magnetars peak around

B0~(1−2.5)×1014 G

and constitute on average roughly 50% of the isolated neutron-star population. The model also prefers a Galactic core-collapse rate above two per century.

That is astrophysically nontrivial because magnetar birth rates enter models for GRBs, superluminous SNe, FRBs and other engine-powered transients.

★★☆ IceCube: no significant neutrino signal from a sample of 89 tidal-disruption events

IceCube has released a dedicated TDE search using 89 optically/UV/X-ray selected TDEs. They perform both source-by-source time-dependent searches and stacking analyses of jetted and non-jetted populations. No statistically significant neutrino excess is found, and the result instead tightens limits on the contribution of these TDE populations to the diffuse astrophysical neutrino intensity.

This is useful negative information: TDEs remain viable in some corners of parameter space, but ordinary optically/X-ray selected TDE populations are increasingly constrained as the dominant origin of IceCube's diffuse flux.

SVOM trigger GRB 260913A — retracted on 14 September

Correction, 14 September 2026: the trigger was identified as non-astrophysical coding noise. SVOM retraction. The following measurements describe the initial 13 September alert.

At 07:38:21 UTC on 13 September, SVOM/ECLAIRs recorded a trigger initially designated GRB 260913A. It was detected in the 8−50 keV band with image S/N

SNR=8.29

on a 20.4-s trigger window. The initial position is

RA=105.2715∘,Dec=+58.8936∘,

with a 90% localization radius of 9.5′.

The initial circular classified the trigger as faint and low priority. Its significance was below SVOM's autonomous slew threshold, and solar-angle constraints prevented SVOM from performing its own follow-up.

The first circular reported no redshift, secure afterglow, unusual spectral property or neutrino/GW association.

★★★ ALICE sees a saturation-like suppression of J/ψ production at subnucleonic scales

Science coverage on 12 September revisited an ALICE measurement originally posted in 2025 and published in Physical Review Letters in 2026.

ALICE studied incoherent photonuclear J/ψ production in ultraperipheral Pb-Pb collisions at

sNN=5.02 TeV.

By binning simultaneously in photon-nucleus energy and momentum transfer t, the measurement probes progressively smaller transverse structures within the nucleus. At the highest measured momentum transfer,

0.81<|t|<1.44 GeV2,

ALICE becomes sensitive to subnucleonic gluon structure.

The striking observation is that the J/ψ cross section grows with energy significantly more slowly at high |t| than at low |t|. The reported suppression is at roughly the 3σ level. Conventional nuclear-shadowing descriptions have difficulty reproducing the pattern, while models containing gluon saturation naturally generate similar behaviour.

Conceptually, the relevant competition is

g→gg

versus high-density nonlinear recombination such as

gg→g,

eventually preventing the small-x gluon density from increasing indefinitely.

The effective transverse resolution reaches roughly

0.2 fm,

about one-quarter of a proton radius.

This is not a discovery of the Color Glass Condensate or definitive observation of saturation. It is better described as evidence for a suppression pattern consistent with saturation and difficult for simple shadowing models to explain.

ALICE arXiv paper
Published PRL DOI

Significance: saturation/small-x QCD ultimately feeds into the extrapolation of hadronic interactions far above accelerator energies, so improved experimental access to nonlinear nuclear gluon dynamics is relevant conceptually to UHECR shower modelling as well.

★★☆ Transient watch: SN 2026aaiv in NGC 7331

A nearby Type Ia supernova, SN 2026aaiv, discovered by ATLAS on 1 September, continues to attract attention as it evolves in NGC 7331. Its host has

zhost≃0.00272,

corresponding to a distance of order 40-45 million light-years. Spectroscopic observations classify the event as Type Ia.

NGC 7331 also hosted the bright Type Ia SN 2025rbs in 2025. Two Type Ia explosions in the same nearby galaxy on such a short human timescale make this a particularly attractive monitoring target even though there is currently no indication of exotic physics or a high-energy counterpart.

Status: watch-list event.
