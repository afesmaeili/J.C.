ArXiv highlights

★★★ 1. On the importance of cosmic-ray background in the Atomki anomaly

Hicham Benmansour et al.
arXiv:2609.18383

The authors construct detailed Geant4 models of the five- and six-arm e+e− spectrometers used in the Atomki searches for the proposed 17 MeV “X17” boson.

Cosmic-ray muons producing two-arm coincidences generate peaks whose locations are controlled by the detectors’ discrete azimuthal geometry:

θe+e−~140∘

in the 8Be energy window and approximately 120∘ in the 4He window. These structures resemble the reported Atomki anomalies not only in angle, but also in their energy-window and pair-asymmetry dependence.

For the five-arm apparatus, normalization to representative running conditions gives a cosmic-to-internal-pair-conversion ratio above unity in the signal region. The calculation does not prove that cosmic rays explain the anomaly, because the conclusion ultimately depends on the actual beam-off data and event-selection procedure. It does, however, establish cosmic backgrounds as a potentially leading contribution that must be demonstrated experimentally to be under control.

Assessment: important challenge to the X17 interpretation; background study rather than a definitive refutation.

★★★ 2. Detectability of 100 TeV Gamma Rays from PeV Cosmic Rays Accelerated in the Early Phase of a Supernova Shock Wave

Tomotaka Nishikawa, Tsuyoshi Inoue, Alexandre Marcowith
arXiv:2609.18226

Ordinary supernova remnants hundreds of years old often appear unable to accelerate particles to the cosmic-ray knee. This work instead examines the first tens of days after a Type II-P explosion, when the shock crosses a dense red-supergiant wind.

Using cosmic-ray distributions from kinetic-MHD simulations that reach proton energies near 3 PeV, the authors calculate the time-dependent hadronic gamma-ray signal, including γγ attenuation on the supernova photospheric radiation field and diffuse cosmic backgrounds.

The photospheric field suppresses the intrinsic 1−100 TeV flux by roughly one order of magnitude, but does not erase it. For

M˙RSG~10−3M⊙yr−1,RCSM~3×1015 cm,

they estimate that a 50-hour CTA observation could detect emission around 100 TeV from distances up to approximately 4 Mpc. Reducing the circumstellar extent to 2×1015 cm lowers the horizon to roughly 3 Mpc.

Assessment: strong, testable prediction for identifying the short-lived PeVatron phase of nearby supernovae.

★★★ 3. Probing Feebly Interacting Particles with 511 keV Line from Circumstellar Medium of Supernovae

Yu Cheng, Chui-Fan Kong, Yen-Hsun Lin, Meng-Ru Wu, Seokhoon Yun
arXiv:2609.18247

This paper proposes a new electromagnetic precursor of core-collapse supernovae. Feebly interacting particles produced in the core can escape the star and decay as

X→e+e−.

If the decay occurs in the circumstellar medium, the positrons slow down and annihilate before the outgoing shock arrives, generating a transient 511 keV line.

Using MeV-scale dark photons as a benchmark and circumstellar profiles motivated by SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi, the authors find that a Galactic explosion at 10 kpc could allow COSI—and a future AMEGO-like mission—to probe parameter space beyond existing supernova limits.

The same deposited energy can heat the circumstellar gas and destroy dust, producing a rapid infrared-to-optical/UV transition. A coincident 511 keV line and dust-sublimation signature would therefore be substantially harder to mimic than either signal alone.

Assessment: genuinely new multimessenger search channel; forecast contingent on a nearby core-collapse supernova.

★★★ 4. Assessment of Super- and Hyper-Kamiokande Sensitivity to the DSNB

Pablo Blanco-Mas, André de Gouvêa, Iván Martínez-Soler
arXiv:2609.17705

The authors reconstruct the current Super-Kamiokande limits on the diffuse supernova neutrino background and forecast ten-year sensitivities for Super-Kamiokande and Hyper-Kamiokande.

The technically important component is their treatment of atmospheric-neutrino backgrounds. Oscillations and propagation modify the flavor and energy distributions entering the DSNB window; approximating these backgrounds too crudely can bias both the discovery significance and the reconstructed DSNB flux.

Their conclusion is that additional Super-K exposure produces only modest sensitivity gains, whereas Hyper-K has the potential for a statistically significant DSNB detection after approximately ten years.

Assessment: useful quantitative sensitivity study; no new detection, but careful background treatment affects realistic forecasts.

★★★ 5. The unidentified PeV source LHAASO J2108+5157: A microquasar remnant?

Josep Martí et al.
arXiv:2609.18353

LHAASO J2108+5157 emits up to hundreds of TeV but has no obvious conventional counterpart. Its compact GeV emission and hard TeV–PeV component are separated by stringent intermediate-energy limits from VERITAS and LST-1.

The proposed explanation is a past microquasar outburst injecting one population of relativistic protons. Energy-dependent diffusion then separates that population:

lower-energy protons&→remain near dense gas→GeV emission,higher-energy protons&→reach more distant gas→TeV--PeV emission.

The same transport model accounts for the compact–extended morphological difference and the absence of a bright present-day radio or X-ray source.

Assessment: coherent new Galactic-PeVatron interpretation, but presently a viable source model rather than a unique identification.

★★★ 6. Unifying spatial correlation with hierarchical population inference to constrain the AGN channel for binary black hole mergers

Lucas Pouw, Lorenzo Speri, Niccolò Veronesi, Elena Maria Rossi
arXiv:2609.17665

Previous tests of binary-black-hole formation in AGN disks generally used either correlations between GW localizations and AGN catalogues or the inferred masses and spins of the binaries. This paper combines both sources of information in one hierarchical Bayesian likelihood.

Applied to GWTC-5.0 and the Quaia AGN catalogue, the analysis does not exclude an AGN contribution. For unobscured AGN above three luminosity thresholds,

Lbol≥{1044.5,1045.5,1046.5} ergs−1,

the corresponding 95% credible upper limits on the AGN-hosted merger fraction are approximately

fAGN<{0.52,0.41,0.61}.

The current bounds are therefore moderate, but the joint framework should become considerably more informative as GW localization, population statistics and AGN catalogues improve.

Assessment: significant methodological advance in GW–AGN population inference; current data do not yet isolate the AGN channel.

★★☆ 7. 1D PIC Simulations of Resonant Scattering-Driven Pair Cascades in Magnetar Magnetospheres

Jens F. Mahlmann et al.
arXiv:2609.17661

These particle-in-cell simulations model resonant inverse-Compton scattering, pair creation, particle acceleration and radiative drag self-consistently along twisted magnetar field lines.

Despite strong drag opposing both electron and positron flows, the circuit reorganizes into a single accelerating gap near the stellar surface in one hemisphere. The resulting outflow becomes heavily pair-loaded, while ions extracted from the surface help transfer momentum across the equatorial region through streaming instabilities.

A notable prediction is that confinement of the gap to one hemisphere should produce asymmetric hard-X-ray emission that may be visible in phase-resolved spectra.

Assessment: valuable kinetic result connecting pair-cascade microphysics to an observable magnetar asymmetry; limited for now to one-dimensional field-line dynamics.

★★☆ 8. WIMPs with Enhanced Annihilation from a Feebly Interacting Unstable Partner

Chance Hoskinson, Pearl Sandick, Barmak Shams Es Haghi
arXiv:2609.17672

The authors extend thermal WIMP cosmology by adding a heavier, feebly interacting state populated through inverse decays while WIMPs remain in equilibrium.

If the partner decays after WIMP freeze-out, it injects a nonthermal WIMP population. Obtaining the observed relic density then requires re-annihilation and an annihilation cross section potentially up to

⟨σv⟩~103⟨σv⟩canonical.

This creates a relic-density mechanism that remains WIMP-like but predicts much stronger present-day indirect-detection signals. A light-higgsino realization is exhibited in the NMSSM.

Assessment: interesting modification of standard freeze-out with clear indirect-detection consequences; primarily a theoretical framework rather than a new constraint.

No genuinely new experimental UHECR composition/anisotropy measurement or precision neutrino-oscillation result appears in today’s batch. The paper on lensing-induced neutrino decoherence, arXiv:2609.18202, presents a novel idea, but its observational utility depends critically on whether the relevant astrophysical neutrino wave packets retain sufficient coherence before encountering the proposed stochastic lensing structure.

Verified science news

★★★ IceCube-260916A: new high-energy track alert

IceCube detected a track-like event at 17:33:51.90 UTC on 16 September 2026. It entered the Bronze stream, meaning it has a moderate probability of astrophysical origin, with an estimated atmospheric-background false-alarm rate of

3.8028 yr−1.

The refined direction is

RA=156.18∘,Dec=+4.22∘,

with an approximately 0.5∘ 90%-containment uncertainty in each coordinate. No source from the Fermi-LAT 16-year catalogue lies inside the 90% region. No convincing electromagnetic counterpart has yet been reported. This remains a preliminary single-event alert, not evidence for a source association. Official IceCube GCN Circular 45598

★★☆ Elias 2-24 b confirmed as the youngest known planet

Astronomers have confirmed a roughly Jupiter-mass planet embedded in the disk of Elias 2-24, a young system approximately 450 light-years away. The system is estimated to be less than one million years old, compared with ages above five million years for the previous youngest confirmed planets.

Archival Keck coronagraph observations from 2018 and 2020 show that the object shares the star’s motion and occupies the disk gap previously observed by ALMA. It lies roughly 55 times farther from its star than Earth does from the Sun and is still accreting disk material.

The result is challenging because conventional core-accretion calculations generally require several million years to assemble a Jupiter-mass planet at much smaller orbital distances. NASA announcement and primary ApJL paper

  • GRB 260917B is a faint 20-second SVOM/ECLAIRs burst detected at 03:52:21 UTC. COLIBRÍ found an uncatalogued optical candidate around r=21.3, subsequently confirmed by LCO. No redshift or unusual high-energy association has yet been reported. SVOM detection, optical candidate
