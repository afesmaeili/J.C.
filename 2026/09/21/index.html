ArXiv highlights

★★★ 1. Observation of the doubly charmed baryon Ωcc+

LHCb Collaboration
arXiv:2609.21921

LHCb reports the first observation of the doubly charmed strange baryon Ωcc+, reconstructed through

Ωcc+→Ωc0π+,Ωc0→pK−K−π+.

Using 6.3 fb−1 of 13.6-TeV proton-proton data collected with the upgraded detector in 2024, the analysis finds a peaking structure with a global significance of 8.7σ. Its measured mass is

mΩcc+=3725.9±1.0stat±0.2syst±0.4lifetime±0.6external MeV/c2.

Its weak decay establishes that the state is a ground-state doubly charmed baryon rather than a short-lived excited resonance.

★★★ 2. First measurement of differential cross sections in kinematic-imbalance variables with electron-neutrino interactions using MicroBooNE

MicroBooNE Collaboration
arXiv:2609.20949

MicroBooNE measures flux-integrated νe-argon cross sections in transverse kinematic-imbalance variables using its complete Booster Neutrino Beam exposure of approximately

1.3×1021 protons on target.

These observables separate simple quasielastic scattering from Fermi motion, multinucleon interactions and final-state rescattering more effectively than inclusive lepton kinematics. Comparisons with commonly used neutrino-event generators reveal discrepancies in parts of phase space expected to be relatively insensitive to final-state interactions, pointing to deficiencies in the primary nuclear-interaction model as well as in intranuclear transport.

★★★ 3. Systematic uncertainties and their impact on gamma-ray searches for dark matter in dwarf galaxies

Toni Bertólez-Martínez, Dan Hooper, Arifa Khatee Zathul
arXiv:2609.20944

The paper examines whether quoted dwarf-spheroidal J-factor uncertainties adequately describe uncertainties from stellar membership, unresolved binaries, equilibrium and spherical-symmetry assumptions, and the adopted stellar and dark-matter density profiles.

The spread among published determinations is substantially larger than many nominal statistical error bars. The authors argue that an additional uncertainty of at least

Δlog10J~0.5

is plausible. In simulated stacked Fermi-LAT analyses of 27 dwarfs, adding systematic uncertainties of 0.5 and 1.0 dex weakens median annihilation limits by factors of approximately 1.4 and 3.2, respectively. Treating spatially extended halos as point sources can further overstate the sensitivity.

★★★ 4. Energy partition in relativistic electron–positron–ion reconnection

Nilay Mancini, Luca Comisso, Lorenzo Sironi
arXiv:2609.21118

The authors combine analytic particle-orbit arguments with particle-in-cell simulations to determine how magnetic-reconnection energy is divided between leptons and ions in mixed pair-ion plasmas.

They find that the mean lepton energy scales approximately with the effective electron magnetization, while the ion energy in the nonrelativistic-escape regime behaves as

⟨γi−1⟩∝μ1/3(σe*)2/3,

where μ=mi/me. Ion and lepton energies become comparable only when ion escape is itself ultrarelativistic. In pair-dominated systems, the ion energy fraction can consequently lie orders of magnitude below simple equipartition assumptions.

★★★ 5. Test of lepton-flavor universality with B¯→D(*)τ−ν¯τ decays at Belle II

Belle II Collaboration
arXiv:2609.21947

Belle II performs a simultaneous hadronic-tag measurement using 387 million Υ(4S) decays and obtains

R(D*)=0.242±0.019stat±0.016syst,
R(D)=0.439±0.055stat±0.046syst.

The two measurements are negatively correlated. Their combination agrees with the Standard Model at 1.5σ, while the updated global average differs by approximately 1.3σ.

★★★ 6. AutoTherm: Automated thermal field-theory rates for cosmology

Killian Bouzoud, Jacopo Ghiglieri, Greg Jackson
arXiv:2609.20936

AutoTherm is a Wolfram/Python pipeline that converts particle-physics models into ultrarelativistic thermal production rates. It automates 2↔2 matrix elements, thermal phase-space integration and hard-thermal-loop resummation for massless t-channel mediators.

Three formally leading-order-equivalent prescriptions are provided to expose sensitivity to the hard-soft separation. Validation cases include right-handed neutrinos, gravitons, gravitinos, axions and dark photons; the comparisons reproduce most known results while identifying corrections or unexpectedly large scheme dependence in several cases.

★★☆ 7. LUX-ZEPLIN’s Stairway to Heaνen: limits on elastic dark-matter scattering from solar capture

Debajit Bose, Akash Kumar Saha, Nirmal Raj, Tarak Nath Maity, Ranjan Laha
arXiv:2609.21823

The authors reconstruct nonrelativistic effective-operator interpretations of LZ’s isolated 248-keV recoil candidate and calculate the corresponding capture and annihilation of dark matter in the Sun.

For annihilation into W+W−, bb¯, τ+τ− or neutrino pairs, Super-Kamiokande and IceCube exclude substantial mass ranges in which elastic scattering could reproduce the LZ event. Direct annihilation to neutrinos generally provides the strongest constraint.

★★☆ 8. Searching for axions from atmospheric kaon decays

Tousif Raza
arXiv:2609.21991

Cosmic-ray air showers produce charged kaons that could undergo

K+→π+a.

A sufficiently long-lived axion-like particle can reach an underground detector before decaying to two photons. The paper uses MCEq with atmospheric-density and hadronic-interaction models to predict the angular and energy distributions, then derives constraints from published Super-Kamiokande and IceCube data for masses extending to approximately 350 MeV.

★★☆ 9. The high-velocity dark-matter halo and the LZ high-recoil event

Ciaran A. J. O’Hare
arXiv:2609.21444

This study uses 98 Milky-Way analogues from TNG50 and three high-resolution FIRE/Latte halos to determine whether realistic halo dynamics materially alter the high-speed dark-matter population required for the LZ recoil candidate.

For the event date, the inferred maximum laboratory-frame speed is

vmax=792.7−95.6+82.9 kms−1

at 95% containment, close to the conventional Standard Halo Model estimate. Simulated halos generally co-rotate with their stellar disks, but their negatively skewed velocity distributions retain substantial counter-rotating high-speed tails. At thresholds above roughly 700 kms−1, annual modulation can approach unity in some halos.

Also notable

  • Gamma–proton separation using reconstructed photon-emission positions in imaging atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes, Jianling Liu and Hu Liu, arXiv:2609.21635. Simulations indicate considerably better background rejection than mean-reduced-scaled-width cuts. The comparison is not yet against current full multivariate or deep-learning analyses, and no real-data calibration or point-source sensitivity is demonstrated. For context, H.E.S.S. has used boosted-decision-tree separation since at least arXiv:0904.1136.

  • The Auger Radio Infill SKALA Extension: First Air-Shower Measurements, Carmen Merx for the Pierre Auger Collaboration, arXiv:2609.21105. The 18-station prototype has obtained its first coincident showers with directions consistent with the surface array. This is an instrument-commissioning milestone, not yet a new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy result.

Verified science news

IceCube-260919A follow-up finds no additional neutrino excess

IceCube searched for further track-like neutrinos around the Gold alert reported on 19 September. Excluding the alert itself, it found zero additional candidates in the ±500-second interval and no significant excess in a two-day window; both searches give p=1.00.

For an assumed E−2.5 spectrum, the reported sensitivity at 1 TeV is approximately

E2dNdE≃(1.3−1.5)×10−1 GeVcm−2.

The event therefore remains an isolated high-energy-neutrino candidate without evidence for a neutrino flare or identified electromagnetic counterpart. Official IceCube follow-up

EP260920b: fast X-ray transient with a fading optical counterpart

Einstein Probe detected EP260920b beginning at 14:06:32 UTC on 20 September. The event showed two broad X-ray pulses over approximately 500 seconds, with an average unabsorbed 0.5−4 keV flux of about

2.1×10−10 ergcm−2s−1.

An FXT observation six hours later found the source fading to approximately 1.3×10−12 ergcm−2s−1. Einstein Probe analysis

LCO identified an uncatalogued optical source at r=18.97±0.04 approximately 0.68 hours after the trigger. (LCO report) COLIBRÍ subsequently measured it near r=20.9, consistent with a power-law decline with index around 0.6. A nearby spiral galaxy has z=0.0519, but associating it with the transient would require a projected offset of roughly 55 kpc; the host identification is therefore uncertain. (COLIBRÍ follow-up)

Status: a fast X-ray transient with a fading optical counterpart, but its class, distance and host remain unconfirmed. No neutrino or gravitational-wave association has been reported.
