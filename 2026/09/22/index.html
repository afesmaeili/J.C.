★★★ 1. New bounds on light scalars from the tip of the red giant branch

Natnael Debru, Audrey Fung, Saniya Heeba, Hugo Schérer, Katelin Schutz, Aaron C. Vincent
arXiv:2609.22638

Light scalars coupled to electrons can be resonantly produced through longitudinal-plasmon conversion in degenerate red-giant cores. The resulting energy loss delays helium ignition and brightens the tip of the red giant branch.

Rather than evaluating the exotic emissivity at fixed benchmark core conditions, the authors incorporate it into MESA and evolve the stellar structure self-consistently. Comparing with the bolometric TRGB magnitudes of 27 Milky Way globular clusters gives

αϕee≡ge24π<1.6×10−34

for sub-keV scalar masses. For a Higgs-mixed scalar this becomes

sinθ<2.2×10−11,

the strongest reported bound over approximately 1eV−10keV.

History

Hardy and Lasenby established that plasma mixing makes resonant longitudinal-plasmon conversion dominate low-mass scalar emission, but their red-giant estimate used representative core conditions rather than a full evolutionary calculation. Subsequent work concentrated largely on revised bremsstrahlung rates, including Balaji et al. and Yamamoto and Yoshioka. The present calculation combines the resonant rate with stellar backreaction and a multi-cluster likelihood, strengthening the previous TRGB scalar bound by nearly two orders of magnitude. The remaining caveat is the dependence on stellar-evolution inputs and globular-cluster calibration, although the paper explicitly varies helium abundance, metallicity, mass loss and mixing length.

★★★ 2. Importance of the Resonant Cosmic-Ray Streaming Instability Upstream of Collisionless Shocks

Bricker Ostler, Benedikt Schroer, Damiano Caprioli
arXiv:2609.22480

Escaping maximum-energy cosmic rays form a relativistic, forward-beamed population upstream of collisionless shocks. Solving the kinetic dispersion relation for cold and finite-width beams, the authors find that the resonant streaming mode can grow faster than the usually assumed non-resonant Bell mode.

At sufficiently slow shocks or large pitch-angle spread, the non-resonant mode can even become stable while the resonant mode survives. Relativistic hybrid-PIC simulations reproduce the linear result and yield nonlinear saturation around

δB/B0~1.

Because the growing wavelength is resonant with the escaping particles themselves, it directly supplies fluctuations capable of confining the highest-energy cosmic rays.

History

Since Bell’s 2004 analysis, upstream magnetic amplification has generally been organized around the fast non-resonant current-driven mode. Later kinetic and hybrid studies showed simultaneous resonant and non-resonant activity, for example Haggerty, Caprioli and Zweibel, but did not establish the resonant mode as the dominant instability specifically for the narrow, maximum-energy escaping population. This paper changes that ordering and shows that neglecting resonance can underestimate amplification at precisely the gyroradius required for confinement. Its nonlinear simulations treat an idealized homogeneous beam rather than a self-consistent shock precursor, so the impact on maximum SNR energy still needs to be demonstrated in shock simulations.

★★★ 3. Resolving the Galactic Center Region’s Gamma-Ray Emission and Searching for its Neutrino Counterpart with HAWC and IceCube

S. Yun-Cárcamo, R. Babu, for the HAWC and IceCube collaborations
arXiv:2609.22577

Using approximately 2860 days of HAWC data and improved event reconstruction, the analysis resolves the Galactic-center emission into a point-like component, HAWC J1745−2857, and Galactic diffuse emission. The point-source spectrum remains an unbroken power law beyond 100 TeV.

The diffuse contribution is approximately 35% of the point-source flux at the Galactic center. The resulting gamma-ray morphology is converted into a hadronic neutrino template and searched for using 12 years of IceCube muon-track data. No excess is found:

p=0.49,

and the 90% upper limit is approximately twelve times the neutrino flux predicted from the nominal hadronic gamma-ray model.

History

HAWC’s earlier analysis detected >100-TeV Galactic-center emission but modeled it essentially as one point source. (Previous HAWC result) The new analysis retains that hard, cutoff-free component while explicitly separating diffuse ridge emission. IceCube had already established diffuse Galactic-plane neutrinos at population level (Science 380, adc9818), but this is the first IceCube limit tied to HAWC’s two-component Galactic-center morphology. Numerically, however, the limit remains a factor of twelve above the transparent pp prediction: it neither confirms nor excludes the hadronic interpretation. The principal advance is therefore the spatially consistent multimessenger template, not a constraining neutrino result.

★★★ 4. Hunting Thermal Relics in the DESI DR1 Lyα Forest

Emanuelly Silva, Artur Ladeira, Rafael C. Nunes, Eleonora Di Valentino
arXiv:2609.22581

The authors combine Planck 2018, DESI DR2 BAO and DESI DR1 one-dimensional Lyman-α forest power-spectrum information to constrain extra radiation and thermal sterile neutrinos whose temperature may differ from that of active neutrinos.

They validate DESI’s two-parameter P1D compression for this nonstandard model, finding residual cosmological dependence below 0.15%. No extra radiation or sterile component is preferred. The combined limits are

Neff<3.41,mseff<0.061 eV

at 95% credibility. Lyman-α information improves the mseff limit by roughly a factor of seven relative to CMB alone.

History

The DESI DR1 Lyman-α cosmology analysis introduced the compressed small-scale-power likelihood but tested mostly standard cosmological extensions (Chaves-Montero et al.). Different-temperature sterile relics had recently been constrained with CMB and large-scale structure without this Lyman-α information (Ladeira et al.). The new contribution is both the validation of the compression for a colder sterile distribution and the resulting 0.061-eV effective-mass bound. mseff is an abundance-weighted cosmological parameter, not the physical sterile mass; a colder, dilute population can still contain substantially heavier particles. The limit also inherits the astrophysical and hydrodynamical assumptions used to compress the forest likelihood.

★★★ 5. Gamma-Ray Echo as a Probe of Supernova Neutrino Emission Anisotropy and Long-Baseline Effects

Garv Chauhan, Cecilia Lunardini, Yago Porto
arXiv:2609.22806

Electron antineutrinos from a core-collapse supernova undergo inverse beta decay in the progenitor’s outer hydrogen envelope. The resulting positrons thermalize and produce a delayed 511-keV annihilation signal—the gamma-ray echo.

The authors compare the ν¯e fluence inferred from this echo with the fluence measured directly at Earth. Because the envelope and Earth view the source along different effective directions and baselines, discrepancies can probe intrinsic neutrino-emission anisotropy or propagation effects such as invisible neutrino decay. For a nearby supernova, tens-of-percent effects could be tested with a 511-keV effective area of order

Aeff~104 cm2.

History

Lunardini et al. introduced the modern quantitative gamma-ray-echo calculation for an approximately isotropic neutrino burst. The new paper generalizes it to dipolar and quadrupolar emission and, more originally, formulates the stellar envelope and terrestrial detector as an astrophysical near–far experiment. There is no previous quantitative sensitivity study of this particular echo/direct-neutrino ratio. The limitation is instrumental: the required narrow-band MeV effective area exceeds that of current instruments, and intrinsic anisotropy is partly degenerate with exotic propagation. The latter would require time, energy or directional structure to disentangle.

★★★ 6. Search for a top-philic heavy resonance in association with top quarks

ATLAS Collaboration
arXiv:2609.24950

ATLAS searches for a color-singlet vector Z′ that couples preferentially to top quarks, using the complete 140 fb−1 Run-2 sample plus 56 fb−1 from Run 3.

Events with two same-sign leptons or at least three leptons are classified using a mass-parameterized transformer network. No significant excess is found. For a purely left-handed benchmark with ct=1,

mZ′<1.15 TeV

is excluded at 95% confidence, compared with an expected reach of 1.25 TeV. For ct=3, the observed exclusion reaches 2.0 TeV.

History

ATLAS’s earlier full-Run-2 single-lepton search constrained cross sections but could not exclude any mass for the ct=1 benchmark. (Previous ATLAS search) The new multilepton analysis improves the cross-section sensitivity by approximately a factor of fifteen; the Run-3 addition alone supplies about 30% more sensitivity at 1.5 TeV. This is therefore a substantial experimental improvement, not merely a luminosity update. The observed reach is modestly weaker than expected, and the quoted mass exclusions apply to a simplified resonance with specified coupling and chirality rather than generic top-compositeness models.

★★☆ 7. Constraints on Axion-like Dark Matter from Cosmic Birefringence with a Polarization Array of Repeating Fast Radio Bursts

Xiaohui Liu, Zi-Yan Yuwen, Yun-Long Zhang, Zerui Liu, Shiqian Zhao, Shuai Feng, Wei-Yang Wang, Xuelei Chen
arXiv:2609.24085

A coherently oscillating axion field produces periodic rotations of linear polarization. The authors analyze three highly active repeating FRBs and jointly treat their independent source terms and correlated Earth term in a hierarchical Bayesian model that includes intrinsic burst-to-burst polarization-angle scatter.

No oscillation is found. Across

10−23≲ma/eV≲10−18,

the polarization-array limits range from roughly 10−12.6 to 10−7.9 GeV−1.

History

Pulsar-polarization arrays already exploit the same correlated Earth term (Liu, Lou and Ren; PPTA limits), while a single-FRB analysis was performed by Wang et al.. This is the first multi-source repeating-FRB polarization array. Combining the three sources improves the low-mass limit by factors of about 2−10 relative to the best individual FRB, primarily by combining different observing baselines. At present, however, auto-correlations dominate; the shared Earth term adds little, and the bounds remain weaker than several pulsar and other astrophysical constraints. With only three sources and a maximum baseline of about 472 days, this is a proof of concept.

★★☆ 8. Particle Acceleration in Stratified 2D MRI Turbulence

Astor Sandoval, Mario Riquelme, Jorge Cuadra
arXiv:2609.24735

Particle-in-cell simulations identify three stages of acceleration in collisionless magnetorotational turbulence:

  1. reconnection electric fields inject particles during plasmoid splitting;
  2. converging separating plasmoids produce rapid first-order-Fermi acceleration;
  3. repeated plasmoid encounters produce slower second-order-Fermi acceleration, with a subdominant shear contribution.

This explains why the maximum energy scales as

Emax∝ωc,0Ω0,

while the late stochastic phase increases energies by only a factor of a few.

History

The authors’ earlier stratified simulations discovered the Emax∝ωc/Ω scaling without isolating its physical origin (Sandoval et al. 2024). Other kinetic MRI simulations established persistent nonthermal acceleration in unstratified boxes (Bacchini et al.). The new work explains the scaling through rapid plasmoid-driven first-order acceleration rather than ordinary slow stochastic energization. The main caveat is substantial: the simulations remain two-dimensional and use a pair plasma. Three-dimensional topology, electron–ion energy partition and radiative losses may change both injection and saturation.

★★☆ 9. A New Probe of Strongly-Interacting Dark Sectors using Neutrino Telescopes

Jose A. Macias Cruz, Christopher V. Cappiello, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Suchita Kulkarni
arXiv:2609.24887

High-energy cosmic neutrinos can resonantly annihilate on the cosmic neutrino background through dark vector mesons. The resulting conversion into dark pions produces an absorption feature whose position reflects the dark-hadron spectrum.

Unlike the Standard-Model ρ resonance, the dark vector can possess a much larger branching ratio into neutrinos, allowing an observable dip without requiring a large local relic-neutrino overdensity. Forecasts indicate sensitivity with future detectors, particularly IceCube-Gen2 Radio.

History

Resonant CνB absorption is an old idea, originally involving the Z resonance, and has been extensively adapted to light secret-neutrino mediators (Ioka and Murase; Esteban et al.). Standard QCD-vector-meson absorption was also considered, but its tiny neutrino branching ratio makes detection impractical without a large relic-neutrino overdensity. The new element is to use the resonance tower of a confining dark sector as a spectroscopic observable. This remains a forecast: sensitivity depends on the uncertain ultrahigh-energy neutrino flux, the absolute neutrino mass, detector energy resolution and the assumed dark-neutrino portal.

The 22 September selection includes no new UHECR composition or anisotropy measurement and no new precision laboratory-neutrino oscillation result.

★★☆ EP260920b identified as the high-redshift long GRB 260920B

The fast X-ray transient EP260920b has been securely classified as a long gamma-ray burst.

Einstein Probe observed two broad soft-X-ray pulses lasting approximately 500 seconds, followed by rapid fading. The mean unabsorbed 0.5−4 keV flux during the outburst was

FX≃2.1×10−10 ergcm−2s−1.

A VLT/X-shooter spectrum of the optical afterglow detected the Lyα forest and several metal absorption lines at

z=3.582.

An independent GTC spectrum confirms z=3.580. (VLT spectroscopy, GTC confirmation)

GECAM-B subsequently identified the gamma-ray counterpart, designated GRB 260920B, with two main episodes and

T90=162−10+118 s

in the 70 keV−6 MeV band. (GECAM-B detection)

This converts the event from an unclassified fast X-ray transient into a well-localized, spectroscopically confirmed high-redshift long GRB. No exceptional neutrino or gravitational-wave association has been reported.
