★★★ 1. Search for the 16O(ppp)→13Cπ+π+e+ decay mode in Super-Kamiokande using machine learning techniques

Super-Kamiokande Collaboration
arXiv:2609.25681

Super-Kamiokande searches for a baryon-number-violating ΔB=3 decay of three bound protons. An attention-based convolutional neural network classifies the complex multi-ring topology—the first CNN-based nucleon-decay analysis by Super-K.

In 0.401 megaton-years of SK-I–V data, one event survives, consistent with an expected background of 1.33 events. The resulting 90%-confidence lower limit is

τ/Br>4.2×1032 yr.

History

Previous direct searches by GERDA and the Majorana Demonstrator obtained limits of approximately 4.7×1025 and 1.6×1026 years, respectively. The new result therefore improves the direct bound by more than six orders of magnitude and, for the first time, reaches the ~1033-year range motivated by some anomaly-free Z6 baryon-parity models. This is a new sensitivity regime rather than an incremental exposure update. Its interpretation remains specific to this nuclear final state; efficiency and background estimates depend on intranuclear pion transport, nuclear de-excitation, and atmospheric-neutrino simulations.

★★★ 2. Einstein Probe discovery of the magnetar EP J223759.5+531421

Nanda Rea et al.
arXiv:2609.25990

Einstein Probe discovered a new Galactic X-ray transient on 28 June 2026. Follow-up by XMM-Newton, NuSTAR, SVOM and IXPE revealed short magnetar-like bursts and coherent pulsations with

P≃6 s,P˙≃2.8×10−12 ss−1.

Under vacuum dipole braking, these imply Bdip≃2.6×1014 G and a characteristic age near 34 kyr. Its 0.5−30 keV luminosity declined from roughly 1.2×1035 to 5.7×1034 ergs−1 during the first month, assuming a distance of 3.3 kpc. No radio or near-infrared counterpart was found.

History

The period, period derivative, bursts, hard X-ray tail and thermal spectrum together elevate EP J223759.5+531421 from a candidate to a securely identified magnetar. It adds a new object to the population summarized in the McGill magnetar catalogue, with unusually comprehensive observations immediately after discovery. Its position about 0.26 kpc below the Galactic plane is difficult to reconcile with the nominal characteristic age if it was born close to the midplane. That tension is suggestive but not yet anomalous physics: the adopted distance is uncertain, and magnetar torque variability makes τc=P/(2P˙) an unreliable chronological age.

★★★ 3. IXPE observation of the new magnetar source EP J223759.5+531421

Roberto Taverna et al.
arXiv:2609.25958

IXPE began observing the newly discovered magnetar approximately eight days after its outburst. The phase-integrated polarization degree is about 7%, detected above 4σ, but varies strongly with rotational phase and energy. It reaches approximately 63% in the 4−7.5 keV band near the secondary pulse.

The polarization angle swings through almost 180∘. A rotating-vector fit gives approximate angles of 28∘ between the line of sight and spin axis and 109∘ between the magnetic and spin axes. The authors interpret the cooler component as emission from condensed surface regions and the hotter component as radiation processed through a magnetized atmosphere.

History

IXPE previously found much stronger energy-integrated polarization in 1RXS J170849.0−400910, rising from roughly 20% at low energies to about 80% above 6 keV (previous IXPE result). The new observation extends this program to a magnetar caught immediately after discovery and provides unusually rapid phase-resolved constraints on its viewing geometry. The proposed mixture of condensed-surface and atmospheric emission confirms and refines interpretations already developed for 1RXS J170849 and 4U 0142+61, rather than introducing an entirely new emission mechanism. The 63% value occurs in a restricted phase-energy bin with limited counts, and the geometric inference depends on an idealized dipolar rotating-vector model.

★★★ 4. Big Bang nucleosynthesis confronts domain walls and first-order phase transitions as pulsar-timing-array explanations

Fatemeh Elahi, Diego Fortini, Yann Gouttenoire, Nicholas Leister, Pedro Schwaller
arXiv:2609.25950

Domain-wall annihilation or a first-order phase transition can reheat the visible plasma inhomogeneously, producing baryon-to-photon fluctuations that survive until nucleosynthesis. Because deuterium production responds nonlinearly to these fluctuations, the spatially averaged abundance differs from homogeneous-BBN predictions.

Using PRIMAT nuclear rates, fluctuations with RMS amplitude

⟨SB2⟩≃0.12−0.29

can reduce the existing mild deuterium discrepancy, whereas larger amplitudes overproduce deuterium. PArthENoPE already agrees with the observed abundance without inhomogeneities and instead gives an approximate upper limit of 0.19. For fully visible reheating, the authors infer αDW≲0.25 for domain walls and exclude particularly strong, slow transitions in parts of pulsar-timing-array parameter space.

History

The NANOGrav 15-year analysis established a common-spectrum signal compatible with a stochastic gravitational-wave background, prompting many phase-transition and domain-wall interpretations. Earlier inhomogeneous-BBN studies established that deuterium is sensitive to baryon fluctuations, but this paper explicitly maps fluctuations from those two gravitational-wave sources into BBN and overlays the result on PTA posteriors. The constraint may be the strongest current BBN test of these particular early-Universe PTA explanations. Its main limitation is unusually consequential: switching between PRIMAT and PArthENoPE changes a nominally preferred fluctuation interval into only an upper bound. Visible branching fraction, diffusion, source morphology and thermalization assumptions also enter directly.

★★★ 5. Limitations of quantum tomography in Higgs-boson decays to four leptons

Morgan Del Gratta, Fabio Maltoni, Davide Pagani, Giovanni Pelliccioli
arXiv:2609.25217

The paper examines whether the angular distribution in

H→ZZ*→4ℓ

can consistently be interpreted as quantum-state tomography of two spin-1 particles beyond leading order.

At NLO, the conventionally reconstructed two-qutrit density matrix can acquire negative eigenvalues even after radiatively corrected decay analyzers are used. The failure originates from coherent Z* and γ* contributions to the off-shell dilepton system, which do not share a universal production-times-decay factorization. A neutrino control channel restores positivity, while an SMEFT HZγ contribution can generate the same problem already at tree level. A reduced rank-two angular sector remains well behaved and can still supply a lower bound on concurrence.

History

This is an important qualification to the recent ATLAS and CMS evidence for ZZ* entanglement (CERN summary, ATLAS result). It does not show that the measured angular correlations or the entanglement conclusion are false. Instead, it demonstrates that interpreting the complete four-lepton distribution as a physical two-Z density matrix becomes theoretically ambiguous once off-shell photon interference and radiative corrections matter. The robust rank-two construction is therefore the paper’s practical advance. Detector acceptance and unfolding may introduce additional complications beyond the parton-level issue studied here.

★★★ 6. A multi-mission detection framework for high-energy transients: application to a 13-year Fermi/GBM and Swift/BAT sample

Ariel Perera, Barak Zackay, Tejaswi Venumadhav
arXiv:2609.25672

The authors construct a joint probability statistic from Fermi-GBM and Swift-BAT trigger information and apply it to their overlapping 2013–2025 data. Instead of using one instrument merely to validate candidates from the other, the foreground and background distributions are modeled jointly.

Depending on the assumed transient class and favorable spacecraft geometry, the accessible volume can increase by as much as 60% for short-GRB-like events. Candidates with high joint probability have an estimated contamination fraction near 4%.

History

Previous GBM targeted searches and BAT/GBM coincidence studies recovered subthreshold events by conditioning on an external trigger—famously important for weak counterparts such as GRB 170817A. The present work advances this into a statistically calibrated multi-instrument ranking rather than a post-hoc confirmation test. The quoted 60% volume gain is an optimistic upper envelope: the current implementation is still seeded by a GBM trigger threshold, and it does not fully marginalize over realistic field-of-view, orientation, energy-response and duty-cycle effects. A fully symmetric low-level search would be the decisive next step.

★★☆ 7. Self-consistent modeling of energy-dependent synchrotron polarization in blazars with application to Mrk 421

Frederike Apel, Motseothata Tisang, Victor Barbosa Martins, Xavier Rodrigues, Markus Böttcher, Anna Franckowiak
arXiv:2609.26574

The AM3 transport framework is extended to follow synchrotron polarization, pitch-angle-dependent cooling and multiple magnetic-field structures self-consistently. A single-zone helical-field model reproduces the broad polarization behavior of Mrk 421 during May 2022: approximately 15% X-ray polarization versus about 3% in the optical and infrared.

In the shock-in-jet calculation, the X-ray polarization angle rotates by roughly 70∘ over five hours while the optical angle stays nearly fixed. The result demonstrates that chromatic polarization need not imply spatially separated emission zones.

History

The contemporaneous MAGIC/IXPE campaign interpreted the X-ray polarization exceeding the optical and radio values by factors of two to five as evidence for an energy-stratified region near a shock. This paper supplies an explicit counterexample: pitch-angle-dependent cooling in one co-spatial helical region can produce similar stratification. Time-dependent helical-field models existed previously, but the self-consistent polarized transport is a real methodological improvement. The fit is not unique, modest polarization residuals remain, and the magnetic geometry is prescribed rather than generated dynamically; the data therefore do not yet distinguish this scenario from multi-zone models.

★★☆ 8. Cosmic birefringence and axiogenesis

Raymond T. Co, Lawrence J. Hall, Keisuke Harigaya, Taegyu Lee
arXiv:2609.25202

A rotating axion-like field is proposed as a common origin for the baryon asymmetry through axiogenesis and the tentative isotropic CMB-polarization rotation near 0.3∘.

The minimal construction predicts a positive birefringence angle and an early matter-dominated era followed by kination, ending at temperatures of approximately 3−50 MeV. Producing the required conserved axion charge is difficult in a single-field realization, so the authors construct a multi-field clockwork model that exponentially enhances the angular momentum.

History

Planck analyses previously reported a possible isotropic rotation of β=0.35±0.14∘ (Minami and Komatsu), while axiogenesis was developed independently as a baryogenesis mechanism driven by axion rotation. This paper’s new contribution is a single dynamical origin for both effects, with a sign prediction and a constrained pre-BBN expansion history. It should not yet be read as evidence for an axion: the birefringence measurement remains sensitive to polarization calibration and Galactic foregrounds, while the necessary charge requires an elaborate multi-field sector. The late kination phase also lies close enough to BBN to require careful thermal-history tests.

★☆☆ 9. Dark-matter relay for ultra-high-energy cosmic rays

Mikhail Sekretov
arXiv:2609.26611

The paper proposes that an ultrahigh-energy nucleon near an active galaxy transfers energy to a light neutral dark-matter particle. The boosted particle then crosses cosmological distances without GZK attenuation and scatters back into a baryon near the Milky Way.

For a benchmark mχ~25 MeV dark-photon model, the required high-energy χN cross section is of order 10−25 cm2. The probability of reconversion is estimated at roughly 5×10−5 through the local Galactic disk and potentially 10−2 along dark-matter-rich Galactic-center directions.

History

Cosmic-ray-accelerated and boosted dark matter have been studied previously, as have dark-matter interactions that cool AGN cosmic rays (related recent study). The two-step “relay” mechanism appears to be new. It remains a feasibility argument rather than a predictive UHECR model: there is no end-to-end spectrum, composition or event-rate calculation, and the motivating BL Lac correlations are themselves statistically controversial. The large deep-inelastic cross section must also survive accelerator, cosmological and boosted-dark-matter constraints, while secondary gamma rays and neutrinos may provide stronger limits.

Search for boosted vector-boson-scattering W±W±H production with ATLAS

ATLAS Collaboration
arXiv:2609.25153

No excess is found in 140 fb−1, and κWW values outside approximately (−2.1,4.1) are excluded.

History

This is the first ATLAS search isolating the same-sign WWH topology, but its quartic-coupling reach is considerably weaker than combined VBF di-Higgs constraints. Its value is channel specificity and boosted-Higgs methodology, not a competitive universal κ2V bound.

The Super-Composite Higgs

Kaustubh Agashe, Gian F. Giudice, Riccardo Rattazzi and Raman Sundrum
arXiv:2609.26413

This extensive construction uses supersymmetry to stabilize the Higgs scale and partial compositeness to generate flavor hierarchies.

History

Earlier partially supersymmetric composite-Higgs models already combined the two structures, but with different assignments of which sector primarily solves naturalness and flavor. The new work supplies unusually complete four-dimensional and warped-space realizations plus correlated EDM, gravitino and future-collider targets. Those predictions remain strongly dependent on mediation and cosmological assumptions; this is model-building synthesis, not evidence for a new state.

The 23 September selection includes no new precision laboratory-neutrino oscillation result and no new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy measurement.

Verified science news

GRB 260922A: rapidly fading Swift burst with an optical candidate

Swift-BAT triggered on GRB 260922A at 10:00:39 UTC on 22 September. The prompt emission was multi-peaked and lasted approximately 50 seconds. XRT began observing 109 seconds later and found a bright fading source with an initial 0.2−10 keV flux near

2.3×10−9 ergcm−2s−1.

The X-ray light curve initially decayed steeply, approximately as t−2.8, then flattened after about 463 seconds to t−0.77. (Swift discovery, refined XRT analysis)

Optical imaging subsequently identified an uncatalogued candidate at approximately r=22.44 and z=22.35 AB magnitude within the refined XRT region. Its fading has not yet been firmly established, and no spectroscopic redshift is available. (Optical candidate)

Status: a confirmed long-duration gamma-ray burst with a plausible optical counterpart, but no secure distance, host, neutrino association or gravitational-wave counterpart has yet been reported.
