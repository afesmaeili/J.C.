Ratings distinguish especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and original but preliminary or model-dependent proposals (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. Plasma Effects Suppress Mixing-Induced Collisional Freeze-In

Shao-Ping Li, Josef Pradler
arXiv:2609.29911

The paper examines whether electroweak gauge-boson decays can produce keV–MeV sterile-neutrino dark matter through its small vacuum mixing with active neutrinos. A flavor-covariant Kadanoff–Baym calculation shows that the collision rate is controlled by the in-medium, rather than vacuum, mixing angle:

sin22θeff=mN4sin22θ(mN2cos2θ−Πν)2+mN4sin22θ.

For a 10-keV sterile neutrino, the predicted abundance is approximately 1020 times smaller than in the semiclassical calculation; across the studied range the suppression is 1012−1024. Production from the Standard Model plasma therefore cannot supply all the dark matter.

History

Datta, Roshan and Sil previously used vacuum mixing in a Boltzmann collision term and found viable gauge-boson freeze-in. Conversely, Lello, Boyanovsky and Pisarski found strong medium suppression from finite-temperature Dirac equations. The new paper resolves that disagreement: the plasma rotates the propagation eigenstates, a multiplicative effect that adding thermal masses only to phase space misses. This is a substantial closure of a claimed production window, not a small rate correction. The conclusion applies to minimal Standard-Model-plasma production; resonant production from a lepton asymmetry, decays of additional heavy particles and other nonthermal channels remain viable. Narrow-width and quasiparticle approximations also warrant further checking near possible resonances.

★★★ 2. Composition-dependent acceleration of UHE Cosmic Rays in AGN electric fields

E. Koutsoumpou, I. Contopoulos, A. Nathanail, A. Loules, D. Kazanas
arXiv:2609.28668

The authors connect particle acceleration in near-horizon current sheets of magnetically arrested AGN disks directly to Auger composition observables. Nuclei sample a log-normal distribution of effective voltage fractions η, acquire energy proportional to ZηVmax, and are propagated through photodisintegration, photopion, Bethe–Heitler and adiabatic losses.

Bayesian fits to the 2025 Auger deep-learning Xmax moments favor a proton-poor injection at source distances of 10–30 Mpc. At 30 Mpc,

σlnη=0.59−0.10+0.13,

corresponding to a factor ~1.8 dispersion in sampled voltage. A template containing about 53% hydrogen is disfavored by Δχ2=18−26; both tested templates fit poorly at 100 Mpc.

History

GRMHD simulations had already identified reconnection layers and large electric potentials in magnetically arrested disks (Ripperda et al.; Nathanail et al.), while Auger source fits had established that hard, relatively heavy injection can reproduce the spectrum and composition (Auger combined fit). The new contribution is the explicit mapping from stochastic voltage sampling to both the mean and width of the latest Auger Xmax distributions. The preferred composition is not independently derived—the successful injection template is itself Auger-inspired—and the log-normal voltage distribution is phenomenological. A joint fit of spectrum, Xmax, anisotropy and source population would be needed before interpreting the distance preference as evidence for nearby AGN.

★★★ 3. Multi-messenger and multi-band signal from first-order phase transitions in proto-neutron stars

Christian Ecker, Mauro Giliberti, Luciano Rezzolla
arXiv:2609.29761

A first-order hadron–quark transition in a proto-neutron star should produce three correlated signals: a kHz gravitational-wave burst from global stellar contraction and oscillation, MHz acoustic gravitational waves from colliding quark bubbles, and a delayed second neutrino burst.

The authors evolve accreting stars through approximately 5×104 model-agnostic equations of state. A 5% measurement of the kHz mode would reduce the logarithmic uncertainty in the predicted MHz frequency interval by about 8.7%. Their weighted population gives a median delay of roughly 2.1 seconds between the ordinary collapse neutrino burst and the transition-driven second burst.

History

Phase-transition-induced kHz emission and second neutrino bursts were studied separately in collapse simulations (Abdikamalov et al.; Sagert et al.), while bubble-collision MHz emission has recently been proposed for compact-star transitions (Blas et al.). This is the first systematic attempt to correlate all three signals across a large equation-of-state ensemble, and apparently the first population estimate of the neutrino delay. The forecast is not yet a realistic detectability calculation: the stellar evolution uses cold equations of state, the kHz amplitude assumes maximally efficient mode excitation, and the MHz calculation adapts a simplified sound-shell treatment developed for cosmological transitions. MHz detection itself remains highly speculative.

★★☆ 4. Probing the Metallicity Dependence of Fast Radio Burst Progenitors with CHIME/FRB Outrigger Dwarf Host Galaxies

Y. Dong et al.
arXiv:2609.28629

Among 78 accurately localized CHIME/FRB Outrigger events, the authors identify eight dwarf-galaxy candidates and spectroscopically confirm five new dwarf hosts. Combining these with the literature suggests a metallicity-dependent split:

  • nonrepeaters preferentially occupy relatively metal-rich dwarfs, generally 12+log(O/H)≳8.35;
  • repeaters preferentially occupy lower-metallicity hosts;
  • nearly every repeater with a persistent radio source lies below approximately 8.15.

No correlation exceeding 3σ is found between metallicity and host dispersion, rotation measure or scattering.

History

Earlier demographic studies established that FRB hosts span a wide galaxy population (Gordon et al.), while the DSA sample suggested an overall preference for massive, metal-rich star-forming galaxies (Sharma et al.). Individual nonrepeaters in dwarfs were already known (Bhandari et al.). The advance is a systematic, uniformly selected dwarf-host search with direct gas-phase metallicities, revealing that the global high-metallicity preference may conceal different repeater and nonrepeater channels. The evidence remains sample-limited—particularly for repeaters—and “nonrepeating” is an exposure-dependent classification. Strong-line metallicity calibrations, heterogeneous literature follow-up and optical host-selection effects can shift the proposed thresholds.

★★☆ 5. Dynamically Stable Magnetic Fields in Relativistic Neutron Stars: A Diverse Landscape of GRMHD Equilibria

Mai Li, Vasileios Paschalidis, Antonios Tsokaros, Maria Mutz, Koji Uryu
arXiv:2609.28651

Fully relativistic MHD simulations evolve slowly rotating neutron stars with several self-consistent mixed poloidal–toroidal initial fields for tens of Alfvén times. The relaxed fields are neither unique nor universally dipolar: they retain memory of the initial current geometry and contain substantial higher multipoles.

A more limited regularity survives—the angle-averaged toroidal-to-poloidal field-amplitude ratio is typically 20–40% through much of the stellar interior.

History

Purely poloidal and toroidal configurations were long known to be unstable, and previous calculations often suggested relaxation toward a broadly “twisted-torus” geometry (Ciolfi and Rezzolla; Sur et al.). By starting from multiple self-consistent current structures and decomposing the relaxed fields into vector spherical harmonics, the new simulations demonstrate that the twisted-torus picture is not a unique dynamical attractor. This materially changes the interpretation of magnetar internal-field models. Stability is established only in ideal GRMHD over dynamical times: resistive evolution, Hall drift, crust formation, rapid rotation and realistic stratification could erase or reorganize the retained multipoles.

★★☆ 6. Binary-pulsar Timing: A 3D Solar-System Accelerometer and Unknown-Source Probe

Zheng-Long Wang, Zi-Qing Xia, Bo Zhang, Yi-Zhong Fan
arXiv:2609.29162

Orbital-period derivatives from 26 detached binary pulsars are combined to reconstruct the full three-dimensional acceleration of the Solar-System barycenter. No anomalous acceleration is detected. Directional 95%-confidence limits are 0.198, 0.262 and 0.693 μasyr−1 over 50%, 75% and the full sampled sky.

Including both the observer- and pulsar-side response excludes a 10M⊙ unseen object within 0.39, 0.34 and 0.21 pc over the corresponding sky fractions.

History

Zakamska and Tremaine introduced astronomical-clock limits on peculiar Solar-System acceleration, and later timing plus VLBI of PSR J0437−4715 improved the single-direction constraint. The new network reconstruction is the first three-dimensional binary-pulsar measurement and improves historical pulsar limits by factors of 12–28; it is also tighter than a matched Gaia EDR3 comparison over most directions. The mass bounds improve on a rescaled INPOP19a benchmark beyond roughly 0.2 pc, but that comparison is not confidence-matched. Full timing covariances are unavailable for much of the heterogeneous sample, only six or seven pulsars dominate the precision, and the method tests quasi-static perturbers rather than rapid flybys.

★★☆ 7. Constraining the Quadratic-mode Amplitude Coupling in GW250114

Yuxin Yang, Changfu Shi, Yi-Ming Hu
arXiv:2609.28953

The GW250114 ringdown is fitted with the linear 220 mode, its first overtone 221, and the quadratic 220×220 mode. The analysis directly infers the nonlinear amplitude-coupling coefficient, which second-order perturbation theory predicts should depend on the remnant spin.

The measurement agrees with both numerical-relativity fits and second-order black-hole perturbation calculations; none differs from the posterior by more than 1.3σ. Present data cannot discriminate between the competing spin-dependent predictions.

History

Earlier analyses reported evidence that a quadratic mode improves the GW250114 ringdown fit (Yang, Shi and Hu) and, using more restrictive merger-informed priors, claimed approximately 3σ evidence for several nonlinear modes (Wang et al.). The new paper advances from asking whether a quadratic component is present to measuring its mode-specific coupling coefficient. Agreement with theory is encouraging but not yet a precision nonlinear-gravity test: the result remains sensitive to ringdown start time, the assumed mode set and correlations with the 221 overtone, and it cannot distinguish numerical-relativity from perturbative predictions.

Wave-Optics Imprints of Warm Dark Matter Subhalos with Prompt Cusps on Strongly Lensed Gravitational Waves

Bhashin A. Thakore and Shin’ichiro Ando
arXiv:2609.28626

LISA-band diffraction by 104−107M⊙ subhalos could distinguish approximately 20–40-keV warm dark matter from more strongly suppressed models. Prompt r−3/2 cusps change the modulation only mildly, so the signal probes subhalo abundance rather than the central density profile.

History

This extends existing wave-optics forecasts to a self-consistent WDM population, but requires a rare, strongly lensed massive-black-hole merger and percent-level waveform control.

Plasma instability splits axion-photon conversion lines

Elizabeth A. Tolman
arXiv:2609.28704

A parametric plasma instability can turn the usual narrow axion-conversion line into a polarized, proportionally spaced line set.

History

This is a useful warning that single-line radio searches may use incomplete templates. The suggested qualitative resemblance to a Crab-pulsar feature is explicitly not evidence for axions and lacks a source-specific statistical analysis.

A Narrow Neutrino Window for the LZ Event

Vedran Brdar and Dibya S. Chattopadhyay
arXiv:2609.30255

A two-stage atmospheric-neutrino process, ν→N1→N2, is engineered so that a roughly 250-MeV N2 can produce LZ’s isolated 248-keV recoil while remaining kinematically forbidden on lighter neutrino-detector targets.

History

The construction is original but highly tuned, requiring a dark-matter-induced parametric resonance and a comparatively strong gauged-U(1)B interaction. With only one unusual LZ event, this is a testable possibility rather than evidence for new physics.

The 25 September selection includes no new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy measurement, no new precision laboratory-neutrino oscillation result, and no major new gamma-ray-cascade calculation.

Verified science news

GRB 260924A: exceptionally bright, high-redshift long burst

Fermi triggered on GRB 260924A at 01:13:23 UTC on 24 September. GBM measures a preliminary

T90≃338 s,Epeak=645±4 keV,

with a 10−1000 keV fluence of approximately 2.84×10−4 ergcm−2. Swift independently obtains T90=344.8±1.8 s in its narrower band. (Fermi-GBM report, Swift-BAT analysis)

Fermi-LAT detected a 43.3-GeV photon 77.5 seconds after the trigger and measured an initial >100-MeV photon flux of (8.3±0.5)×10−5 cm−2s−1. (Fermi-LAT report)

Calar Alto spectroscopy finds Lyman-α and Lyman-limit breaks consistent with

z=3.51±0.10.

The inferred rest-frame ultraviolet magnitude near 0.7 hours is approximately −31, placing the afterglow among the most optically luminous known. The redshift currently rests primarily on continuum breaks rather than high-significance metal absorption lines, so additional spectroscopy would be valuable. (Calar Alto spectroscopy)

No secure neutrino or gravitational-wave association has been reported.
