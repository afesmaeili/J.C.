ArXiv highlights

Ratings distinguish especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and original but strongly model-dependent proposals (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. 4FHL: The Fourth Catalog of Hard Fermi-LAT Sources

Fermi-LAT Collaboration
arXiv:2609.31457

Using 16 years of LAT data, the collaboration constructs the deepest all-sky catalog yet in the 50 GeV−2 TeV band. The catalog contains 673 sources:

  • 84.7% have extragalactic associations, predominantly BL Lac objects;
  • 36 associated sources lie at z>1;
  • approximately 10% are Galactic, chiefly pulsar-wind nebulae and supernova remnants;
  • about 5% remain unassociated or uncertain;
  • 20 have no counterparts in previous LAT catalogs.

Sensitivity and localization are each improved by approximately a factor of two relative to the previous catalog covering the same energy range.

History

The 2015 2FHL catalog used 80 months of Pass-8 data and contained 360 sources above 50 GeV. 4FHL nearly doubles that sample while preserving the same energy threshold, making this a major catalog-level advance rather than a routine duration update. The intervening 3FHL catalog contained more objects but extended down to 10 GeV, so it is not the appropriate direct sensitivity comparison.

The larger sample should materially improve blazar luminosity-function, extragalactic-background and CTA-target studies. Its principal selection limitation is the 16-year time average: steady hard-spectrum sources are favored over brief flares. At high redshift, EBL absorption also makes the 36 z>1 objects a spectrally selected subset rather than an unbiased blazar population.

★★★ 2. Hybrid Partial Dressing: Correct Effective Potentials at All Temperatures

Raphaël Berthiaume, David Curtin, Michael Luke, Andrija Rasovic, Jyotirmoy Roy
arXiv:2609.30366

The authors introduce Hybrid Partial Dressing, a resummation prescription for finite-temperature scalar effective potentials. It is designed to remain valid outside the high-temperature expansion while being exactly correct through two loops and avoiding the overlapping-momentum problem of conventional partial dressing.

In multi-scalar Z2-breaking examples, HPD reproduces two-loop dimensional reduction in the high-temperature regime and continues smoothly into temperatures where dimensional reduction becomes unreliable. Renormalization-group improvement is incorporated, and the resulting potentials are used to calculate first-order-transition gravitational-wave spectra.

History

Conventional Parwani and Arnold–Espinosa daisy prescriptions are easy to use but can miscount diagrams or fail when thermal and zero-temperature masses are comparable. The authors’ earlier Partial Dressing framework removed the high-temperature restriction, while Optimized Partial Dressing improved numerical convergence and substantially altered some gravitational-wave forecasts. HPD’s advance is to combine all-temperature applicability with explicit two-loop exactness and agreement with dimensional reduction where the latter is controlled.

This potentially removes an important theoretical systematic from BSM phase-transition scans. The present demonstrations are nevertheless scalar theories. Gauge dependence, gauge-boson resummation and realistic multi-field tunneling calculations remain to be established before HPD can be treated as a universal replacement for existing electroweak-transition pipelines.

★★☆ 3. Mass and spin properties of black-hole mergers reveal formation in triples

Jakob Stegmann, Aleksandra Olejak
arXiv:2609.30378

Population-synthesis and secular-dynamics calculations follow massive hierarchical triples through stellar evolution and tertiary-induced black-hole mergers. The resulting population simultaneously reproduces several structures inferred from current gravitational-wave data:

m1≃10M⊙

as the dominant primary-mass peak, a preference for nearly equal masses, and broad, asymmetric distributions of the effective and precessing-spin parameters χeff and χp. The model predicts a sharp decline near m1≃30M⊙, above which another formation channel may dominate.

History

Field-triple calculations previously established that Lidov–Kozai excitation can produce mergers with misaligned spins and χeff concentrated near zero (Antonini et al.; Fragione and Kocsis). Separate work found that triples can produce distinctive mass-ratio distributions, but did not claim agreement with the complete observed population. The authors’ own recent hierarchical inference argued statistically that a large triple contribution is favored (Stegmann et al.).

The new paper supplies the missing forward-model demonstration: one channel can reproduce the low-mass peak, mass ratios and two spin observables together. That is substantive, but “reveal formation in triples” remains stronger than the evidence warrants. Triple initial conditions, natal kicks, common-envelope evolution and stellar-spin transport are uncertain, while selection effects and mixtures of isolated-binary, cluster and triple channels can imitate parts of the same distributions.

★★☆ 4. A Multidimensional General-Relativistic Boltzmann Solver for Neutrino Transport

Arthur Offermans, Harry Ho-Yin Ng, Patrick Chi-Kit Cheong, Anthony Mezzacappa, Tjonnie Guang Feng Li
arXiv:2609.31611

The full six-dimensional Boltzmann equation for neutrinos is incorporated into the general-relativistic MHD code Gmunu, using finite-volume discretization in spatial, energy and angular coordinates.

A factorization based on the Legendre expansion of interaction kernels reduces the cost of the stiff implicit collision solve. In a one-dimensional core-collapse snapshot with 14 angular bins, it is approximately 300 times faster than full-matrix LU decomposition. Tests show quadratic numerical convergence and roughly 1% energy conservation with 20 energy bins. Compared with M1 transport, free-streaming luminosities can differ by about 10%, although mean energies and fluid profiles at bounce agree reasonably well.

History

Fully general-relativistic multidimensional Boltzmann solvers already exist, notably the discrete-ordinate implementation of Akaho et al., while three-dimensional supernova calculations with six-dimensional transport have been demonstrated in Newtonian or approximate-GR settings (Iwakami et al.). The novelty is therefore not the first multidimensional Boltzmann calculation, but the flexible integration with a GRMHD framework and the large reduction in the species- and energy-coupled collision cost.

The validation remains mostly one-dimensional and near core bounce. The decisive test will be whether the optimization makes long-duration multidimensional GRMHD simulations practical while retaining sufficient angular and energy resolution; the quoted 300-fold gain is relative to an especially expensive dense-matrix baseline.

★★☆ 5. Tracing the Host-Galaxy Environments of FRBs with a New Sample of Localized Sources Detected with the DSA-110

Frank M. Verdi, Vikram Ravi, Myles B. Sherman, Jakob T. Faber, Stella Koch Ocker, Kritti Sharma, Casey J. Law, Gregg Hallinan, Kaitlyn Shin, Liam Connor
arXiv:2609.30616

The paper presents 24 localized DSA-110 FRBs with spectroscopic host redshifts, including four near or beyond z=1, and combines them with earlier detections to form a homogeneous 43-burst sample.

After subtracting Milky-Way and intergalactic contributions, host dispersion and rotation measures are significantly correlated. Scattered bursts also approximately follow the scattering–DM relation measured for Galactic pulsars. The joint evidence indicates that much of the excess DM, RM and temporal broadening originates in dense, magnetized host-galaxy plasma, plausibly H II regions associated with young magnetars.

History

The first uniform DSA-110 sample contained 11 localized FRBs, while an earlier polarimetric analysis of ten DSA events already indicated appreciable host RM contributions (Law et al.; Sherman et al.). The new 43-object sample turns those hints into a joint DM–RM–scattering environmental result and extends the redshift baseline beyond unity.

The conclusion favors young, core-collapse magnetars but does not uniquely identify them: compact binaries or older neutron stars passing through dense gas could produce similar propagation measures. Host inclination, uncertain halo/intergalactic DM subtraction and DSA’s fluence and scattering selection functions must be propagated before inferring the intrinsic progenitor fraction.

★★☆ 6. Saturation Mechanism of Cosmic Ray Streaming Instabilities with a Shell Distribution

Mohamad Shalaby, Rouven Lemmerz, Christoph Pfrommer
arXiv:2609.30376

Fully kinetic PIC and fluid-PIC simulations show that a gyroresonant cosmic-ray streaming instability saturates through gyro-phase bunching around the excited Alfvén waves. Simulations with and without Landau closures reach the same saturation amplitude and fully isotropize the cosmic rays in the wave frame.

At the simulated parameters,

vA=0.01c,nCR/ni=0.01,

nonlinear Landau damping is therefore not responsible for either saturation or isotropization. Increasing the domain size by a factor of ten leaves the saturation amplitude unchanged, supporting a local wave–particle mechanism.

History

Earlier PIC and MHD-PIC studies established quasilinear growth, nonlinear pitch-angle scattering and eventual isotropization, but generally characterized saturation through wave growth, damping and redistribution of the CR anisotropy (Holcomb and Spitkovsky; Bai et al.). The new result identifies a more specific microscopic mechanism and explicitly falsifies nonlinear Landau damping as the controlling process in this regime.

The extrapolation caveat is unusually important: the adopted CR density is many orders of magnitude above typical diffuse-ISM values, and vA is also comparatively large. The result challenges a common closure assumption in galaxy simulations but does not yet demonstrate that gyro-phase bunching controls saturation under realistic Galactic conditions.

★★☆ 7. Natural Saturation of Sterile-Neutrino Dark-Matter Resonant Production by a High Lepton Flavor Asymmetry

Dmitry Gorbunov, Dmitry Kalashnikov
arXiv:2609.31340

At very large primordial flavor asymmetry, the active–sterile resonance occurs later and at lower temperature. The authors show that this can overlap with the onset of active-neutrino flavor oscillations, which redistribute or erase the asymmetry driving resonant sterile-neutrino production.

Consequently, the final abundance saturates: increasing the initial asymmetry no longer proportionally increases sterile-dark-matter production. The surviving resonant-production region spans approximately two orders of magnitude in the mixing parameter sin22θ.

History

The Shi–Fuller mechanism has long relied on a lepton asymmetry to resonantly produce colder sterile dark matter. Recent work reopened much larger asymmetries consistent with BBN and used them to enlarge the viable production region (Vogel and Abazajian). The present paper adds a countervailing effect that was not included in that simple monotonic picture: sufficiently delayed production encounters active-flavor equilibration and ceases to benefit from a larger initial asymmetry.

The conclusion is sensitive to flavor-dependent initial conditions and the treatment of quantum kinetics through the QCD epoch. Its discussion of pion condensation and an associated gravitational-wave transition is more speculative and should be separated from the better-motivated abundance-saturation result.

Also notable

Metastable Neutron Stars as Transient-Rate Detectors of Heavy Dark Matter

Ziwen Yin and Hong-Yi Zhang
arXiv:2609.30335

Dark-matter-triggered conversion to quark-containing stars is constrained using the present transient rate rather than merely the continued existence of old neutron stars. Population depletion makes the excluded interaction-rate interval nonmonotonic.

History

Earlier work already proposed WIMP-induced strange-matter nucleation (Pérez-García, Silk and Stone); the new element is the population-rate treatment. The constraint is highly conditional on metastable hadronic stars, uncertain bubble-nucleation microphysics, and the assumption that an appreciable fraction of the beaming-corrected short-GRB rate can represent such conversions.

Convolutional non-parametric Gamma-Ray Signal and Background Separation

Scarlet Betterman, Emmanuel Moulin and Martin White
arXiv:2609.30917

An ensemble of variational autoencoders separates gamma-ray signal from residual cosmic-ray background without a predefined source template or background-control region, recovering simulated Galactic-center dark-matter emission and public H.E.S.S. observations.

History

The methodological novelty is strong, but “complete reconstruction” is demonstrated on limited controlled examples; robustness against instrumental systematics and mismodeled diffuse emission remains untested.
