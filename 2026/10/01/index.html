Ratings distinguish especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and interesting but preliminary proposals (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. First double-differential cross-section measurements in proton multiplicity and kinematics for mesonless νμ charged-current interactions on argon

MicroBooNE Collaboration
arXiv:2609.40237

Using MicroBooNE’s complete 1.30×1021-proton-on-target Booster Neutrino Beam exposure, the collaboration measures mesonless charged-current cross sections jointly as functions of proton multiplicity and kinematics. Observables include the momenta and muon–proton opening angles of the first through fourth protons and the total available hadronic kinetic energy.

No tested event generator adequately describes the complete correlated dataset. The largest discrepancies appear in proton multiplicities, backward muon–proton configurations and subleading-proton kinematics—precisely the regions most sensitive to intranuclear rescattering and nucleon ejection.

History

MicroBooNE’s earlier CC0π analyses established argon cross sections with final-state protons, including a 359-bin double-differential dataset, but primarily constrained inclusive or leading-particle kinematics (arXiv:2403.19574). The new result resolves the hadronic cascade by proton rank through the fourth proton and exposes generator failures that leading-proton observables can conceal.

This is therefore a genuinely new constraint on final-state-interaction modeling, important for neutrino-energy reconstruction in liquid-argon oscillation experiments. It does not isolate a unique microscopic defect: nuclear ground-state modeling, multinucleon interactions and intranuclear transport contribute correlated uncertainties, and generator tuning to these data will need validation against independent targets and beam energies.

★★★ 2. New Constraints on r-process Nucleosynthesis in Neutron Star Mergers from GW170817 Late-Phase Spectra

Salma Rahmouni, Masaomi Tanaka, Daiji Kato, Gediminas Gaigalas, Kenta Hotokezaka
arXiv:2609.38682

An analytic nebular model follows allowed and forbidden emission from collisionally excited ions in AT2017gfo. It identifies La III and Ce III as the principal contributors near 1.4 and 1.6μm, respectively, and confirms Te III at 2.1μm.

The inferred mass fractions are

X(La)≃0.025−0.05,X(Ce)≃0.05−0.10,X(Te)≃0.04−0.08,

with X(Kr)≲0.03 and X(Sb)≲0.003. The implied inner ejecta have a suppressed first r-process peak, enhanced heavy elements relative to the solar pattern and a lanthanide fraction XLN≃0.03−0.06.

History

The 2.1μm feature had already been attributed to [Te III] using a solar heavy-r-process abundance pattern (Hotokezaka et al.); JWST subsequently detected a similar feature in the GRB 230307A kilonova. (JWST kilonova spectroscopy) The new advance is the simultaneous identification and abundance inference for several heavy species, including constraints from absent lines, rather than another qualitative Te identification.

The La and Ce numbers remain tentative because they depend strongly on the poorly known radiation field, ionization balance and atomic collision data. The analytic treatment also cannot reproduce the full geometric and time-dependent radiative-transfer problem. The heavy-element enhancement is consequently evidence about the inner ejecta, not yet a model-independent total yield for GW170817.

★★★ 3. Chandra X-ray Spectral Evidence of the Dual AGN Nature of MG B2016+112 at z=3.273 with 175-pc separation

Daniel A. Schwartz, Julia M. Sisk-Reynes, Anna Barnacka
arXiv:2609.38590

A new 175-ks Chandra observation uses extreme gravitational magnification to obtain separate spectra for two compact X-ray emitters in MG B2016+112. The highly magnified component inside the lens caustic shows intrinsic absorption

NH>4×1022 cm−2

at 3σ, identifying it as an obscured AGN alongside the previously known type-II nucleus. The projected source-plane separation is approximately 175 pc, making this the tightest confirmed high-redshift AGN pair reported so far.

History

Earlier VLBI proper-motion measurements and archival Chandra imaging had resolved two emitters and favored a dual-AGN interpretation, but could not exclude one AGN plus a highly magnified parsec-scale jet. (VLBI study; archival Chandra analysis) The distinct absorbed X-ray spectrum is the decisive new discriminator; this is more than a deeper-image confirmation.

The system is nevertheless not a dynamically verified binary: the 175-pc distance is projected and lens-model-dependent, and this X-ray analysis does not measure an orbit or dynamically determine the two black-hole masses. Its immediate significance is as a rare compact merger phase and potential precursor population for massive-black-hole gravitational-wave sources.

★★★ 4. NEXUS: Discovery of a Luminous Young Transient at z=5

Ming-Yang Zhuang, Yue Shen, Lei Hu, Justin D. R. Pierel, Zhiwei Pan, Zachary Stone, Adam J. Burgasser, Xiaohui Fan, Jenny E. Greene, Junyao Li, Feige Wang
arXiv:2609.38373

JWST/NEXUS discovered AT 2026abck in a galaxy at z=5.017±0.001. It brightened by more than 1.4 magnitudes in at most 12 rest-frame days and was probably caught only 4–10 days after explosion.

A blackbody fit gives T≃1.7×104 K, R≃7×1014 cm and Lbol≃3×1043 ergs−1. Its off-nuclear position and blue continuum favor a core-collapse supernova, although it is hotter and more luminous than ordinary early Type-II events. It is the first robust z≳5 transient found in an unlensed field.

History

SN Eos at z=5.133 remains the stronger object-level classification because it was multiply imaged, strongly lensed and spectroscopically confirmed as a metal-poor Type-II supernova (arXiv:2601.04156). AT 2026abck changes the survey context: no lens magnification was required, and the high cadence captured the UV-bright rise in a host roughly 103.3 times more massive than that of Eos.

The event itself lacks a spectroscopic subtype and a mature light curve. A superluminous supernova, interaction-powered event or other unusual core-collapse transient is not yet excluded, so “core-collapse supernova” should be read as the favored photometric interpretation.

★★☆ 5. Constraints on Complex Electroweak WIMPs from Solar Capture

Juan P. Luengas, Maxim Pospelov, Harikrishnan Ramani
arXiv:2609.38316

The authors calculate capture, energy loss and annihilation in the Sun for scalar and fermionic electroweak dark-matter multiplets with sub-MeV inelastic splittings. For higher representations, IceCube’s non-observation of solar annihilation neutrinos requires approximately

δ≳350 keV

over masses extending to 100 TeV. Under thermal freeze-out and the Standard Halo Model, this excludes most such multiplets as explanations of LZ’s single 248-keV recoil candidate. An LMC-induced high-velocity tail can, however, move the LZ-preferred splitting above the solar limit for several representations.

History

A study published in September 2026 applied the same solar-acceleration mechanism specifically to the 1.08-TeV Higgsino and obtained the stronger doublet-specific bound δ>566 keV (arXiv:2609.02775). The present paper’s substantive advance is the generalization to scalar and fermionic higher multiplets, revealing an approximately representation-independent 350-keV floor.

The conclusion about the LZ event is conditional. LZ reported one candidate, not a dark-matter discovery, and the mapping to a preferred splitting depends on thermal abundance and the poorly known high-speed halo tail. Nonstandard production, a subdominant WIMP abundance or modified local phase space can weaken the stated exclusion.

★★☆ 6. XENONnT pp-Dominated Solar Neutrinos Constrain the ν1 Pseudo-Dirac Splitting

Gio Leone
arXiv:2609.38233

The published XENONnT low-energy electron-recoil spectrum is reanalyzed for active–sterile oscillations caused by a pseudo-Dirac splitting of the first neutrino mass eigenstate. The calculation includes the pp and 7Be recoil spectra and finds no preference for a nonzero splitting.

The resulting two-sigma limit is

δm12≲5.9×10−13 eV2.

This reaches the regime previously associated mainly with future low-energy solar-neutrino detectors.

History

Solar data had generally limited pseudo-Dirac splittings at roughly the 10−11 eV2 scale. Dedicated forecasts found that a 300-ton-year DARWIN exposure could reach 10−13 eV2, while a pp-inclusive JUNO analysis might reach 2.9×10−13 eV2. (DARWIN forecast; JUNO forecast) The new paper is the first actual xenon-pp constraint in this range, although it does not yet equal those full-exposure projections.

This is an independent phenomenological likelihood built from published spectra, not an official XENONnT oscillation analysis. Correlated background, detector-response and solar-flux nuisance parameters may therefore be treated less completely than in a collaboration likelihood.

★★☆ 7. Close Pulsar Pairs See Dark Matter Substructure through the Gravitational-Wave Background

Abhiram Cherukupalli, Vincent S. H. Lee, Kim V. Berghaus, Kathryn M. Zurek
arXiv:2609.38305

The proposal uses sub-parsec pulsar pairs as a differential detector. A nanohertz gravitational-wave background produces nearly common timing residuals and cancels when the pair is differenced, whereas the gravitational perturbation from a nearby dark subhalo remains differential.

For separations near 0.1 pc, the projected sensitivity to approximately 10−6M⊙ subhalos improves by more than an order of magnitude relative to an otherwise identical array of widely separated pulsars. Globular clusters naturally supply candidate pairs.

History

The same authors recently showed that the measured stochastic gravitational-wave background degrades ordinary PTA sensitivity to substructure by one to three orders of magnitude (arXiv:2607.03533). The new paper supplies a geometrical cancellation strategy for that specific limitation rather than merely updating a forecast.

Its practicality remains unproven. Globular clusters may have lost much of their primordial dark-matter content, and their stellar accelerations, encounters, intracluster plasma and timing-model correlations can all generate differential signals. The forecast also assumes suitable, precisely timed pulsar pairs that do not yet constitute a demonstrated observing array.

★★☆ 8. How collective modes terminate in the dispersion relation of neutrino plasmas

Damiano F. G. Fiorillo
arXiv:2609.38314

The paper studies how nonzero neutrino masses and the associated velocity dispersion modify singular endpoints of collective flavor-wave branches. Masses remove the frequently seen symmetry-axis degeneracy of fast-limit modes, although degeneracy lines may remain in the full three-dimensional wavevector space. Abrupt termination at a branch cut of the flavor dielectric function is instead generic.

A useful analytic result is that a Lorentzian neutrino-frequency distribution is exactly equivalent to a monochromatic distribution with an effective collision rate, allowing more general spectra to be approximated by sums of simpler components.

History

Recent work unified fast, slow and collisionally driven instabilities within a common neutrino-plasma dispersion framework but generally treated mass or collision effects perturbatively around simpler distributions (arXiv:2505.20389). The present result clarifies which apparent mode endpoints are symmetry artifacts and which survive realistic velocity dispersion.

This is a rigorous linear-dispersion result, not a calculation of nonlinear flavor conversion in a supernova or merger. Inhomogeneity, time-dependent transport, angular structure and backreaction may determine whether any of these modes significantly alter an observable neutrino signal.

Also notable

VLBI Astrometric Procedure and Performance with CHIME/FRB Outriggers

Shion Andrew et al.
arXiv:2609.38344

More than one thousand calibrator and pulsar tests demonstrate routine ~50-mas localization at 400–800 MHz.

History

Traditional VLBI localizes selected repeaters after discovery; the advance is validated wide-field astrometry for one-off CHIME bursts. This is an instrumental milestone, not yet a new FRB population result.

Measurement of the 39Ar specific activity in atmospheric argon with DArT

P. Agnes et al.
arXiv:2609.39400

DArT measures 0.955±0.008 Bqkg−1, the most precise atmospheric-argon determination.

History

The value confirms earlier measurements rather than shifting the central activity; the advance is precision and the background-subtraction method using underground argon, validating the assay chain for DarkSide-20k and LEGEND-1000.

Science Radar

Notable developments beyond astroparticle physics, with sources, scientific context, and clearly stated limitations. This section begins with the 1 October 2026 report.

Asgard archaea have been observed crawling with actin-filled protrusions

A peer-reviewed, open-access Nature paper reports live anaerobic microscopy of cultivated Lokiarchaea and Hodarchaea. Their protrusions grow and retract at roughly 1.5−4.8μmmin−1, and attached cells crawl across surfaces at speeds comparable to some eukaryotic cells. Actin inhibitors arrest the dynamics, implicating the internal Lokiactin cytoskeleton.

This is striking because Asgard archaea are the closest known prokaryotic relatives of eukaryotes. The result supplies an observed example of actin-dependent crawling in a prokaryote, informing models in which dynamic protrusions facilitated interactions with the bacterial ancestor of mitochondria. (Nature research paper)

Status and caveat: Peer reviewed, published 30 September 2026; an earlier version appeared on bioRxiv in December 2025. Actin-inhibitor sensitivity is not equivalent to genetic proof of the complete motility mechanism, and movement on laboratory glass may not reproduce behavior in marine sediment. (Original bioRxiv preprint)

A single four-hour topical treatment eliminated severe scabies in pigs

A peer-reviewed Science Translational Medicine study combines abametapir, an ovicidal metalloprotease inhibitor, with flavesone, a rapidly miticidal β-triketone. In a porcine model that closely resembles human scabies, one four-hour topical application eradicated both live mites and eggs and reduced itching, outperforming a standard two-dose oral ivermectin regimen. (Science Translational Medicine study)

The combination addresses a central limitation of existing drugs: most do not reliably kill eggs, making repeat treatment and strict adherence necessary. Earlier work had demonstrated flavesone’s activity against mites and eggs and tested the combination only in vitro; the new study extends that work to efficacy in a large-animal model. (Earlier combination study)

Status and caveat: Peer reviewed, published 30 September 2026, but still preclinical. Human safety, dosing, formulation, comparative efficacy and the potential evolution of resistance all require clinical trials before this can be considered a treatment for patients.
