ArXiv highlights

The selection covers the 7 October astro-ph.HE, hep-ph, hep-ex, and astro-ph.CO listings and relevant cross-lists. The 7 October listings contain 17, 33, 4, and 14 new submissions, respectively.

Ratings denote especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and original but exploratory proposals (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. The neutrino magnetic moment and supernovae with strong magnetic fields

Clément Ehrhardt, Maria Cristina Volpe, Miguel Ángel Aloy, Martin Obergaulinger
Submitted: 6 October 2026
arXiv:2610.08139

The authors propagate three-flavor Majorana neutrinos with transition magnetic moments through two detailed three-dimensional core-collapse simulations: a neutrino-driven 17M⊙ explosion leaving a neutron star and a magnetorotational 26M⊙ event ending in black-hole formation. They calculate fluxes and event rates for JUNO and Hyper-Kamiokande. Resonant spin-flavor precession materially alters the neutronization burst and accretion signal for transition moments around 10−14μB in the ordinary model, and can act at 10−15μB during early magnetorotational evolution.

History

Early studies established magnetic-moment-driven spin-flavor conversion in supernova fields using mostly prescribed density and field profiles (de Gouvêa and Shalgar 2012; 2013). The authors’ recent precursor treated one 13M⊙ magnetorotational model; this paper extends the calculation to two qualitatively different 3D explosions, including black-hole formation, and maps the flavor evolution into detector counts. The result is therefore a realistic signal-level sensitivity study, not a new bound on μν. Its reach is highly model-dependent: equal transition moments are assumed, neutrino-neutrino refraction is neglected, and only two progenitor and magnetic-field histories are sampled.

★★★ 2. Predicting High-Energy neutrino emission from X-ray absorption in CSM-interacting SNe

Silvia Gagliardini, Simone Dall’Osso, Dafne Guetta
Submitted: 6 October 2026
arXiv:2610.08462

This paper replaces an assumed circumstellar density law with one reconstructed from observations. The time-dependent X-ray absorbing column NH(t) constrains the material ahead of the shock, while the thermal X-ray temperature supplies the shock speed. Applied to SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi, the method reconstructs the shock-breakout epoch and predicts IceCube and KM3NeT/ARCA rates. Hadronic neutrino production is concentrated near breakout, producing a source-specific short search window with better signal-to-background than a generic weeks-long integration.

History

Previous SN 2023ixf calculations inferred the target density from optical or bolometric light curves, or adopted wind profiles. Representative estimates found a fluence below IceCube sensitivity (Sarmah 2023) and about 0.04 IceCube muon events over 50 days in a later model (Cosentino et al. 2025). The advance is not a larger signal but a data-driven inversion of the evolving X-ray column and temperature, applied consistently to two supernovae, to define both the flux and optimal window. It remains a forecast: spherical symmetry, early X-ray obscuration, acceleration efficiency, magnetic amplification, and the non-unique conversion from fitted NH to a CSM profile are important systematics.

★★★ 3. The Potential Contribution of Radiative Supernova Remnants to the Local Cosmic Ray Spectrum

P. Cristofari, V. H. M. Phan, S. Recchia, S. Gabici
Submitted: 6 October 2026; accepted in Astronomy & Astrophysics
arXiv:2610.08474

The authors follow diffusive shock acceleration and reacceleration of pre-existing Galactic cosmic rays through both the adiabatic and early radiative stages of representative remnants. If acceleration continues for even part of the radiative phase, the integrated particle energy can exceed the budget required to sustain the Galactic cosmic-ray population. They infer that the instantaneous fraction of ram pressure placed in cosmic rays should generally remain below roughly 1%, rather than the frequently assumed 5−10%. The radiative phase can also reshape the released spectrum up to about 10 TeV.

History

The canonical SNR paradigm requires an order-ten-percent integrated conversion of supernova kinetic energy into cosmic rays, while earlier population models showed that source dispersion and nonlinear propagation can help reproduce spectral hardening (Recchia and Gabici 2017). This paper changes the bookkeeping by retaining acceleration and reacceleration after the Sedov stage: the longer radiative lifetime makes modest instantaneous efficiency sufficient and can otherwise overproduce the spectrum. That is a meaningful correction to standard efficiency estimates, but not evidence that radiative SNRs dominate. The calculation is spherical, confines particles until final release, uses an averaged magnetic field, and does not derive the radiative-shock spectrum self-consistently. Ion-neutral damping could cap acceleration below a TeV.

★★☆ 4. Evolution of neutrino distributions under repeated electron scattering

Jens Chluba, Bryce Cyr, Michiru Uwabo-Niibo, Masahide Yamaguchi
Submitted: 6 October 2026
arXiv:2610.08201

Starting from the Boltzmann collision term, the authors evolve neutrino spectra scattered repeatedly by an isotropic thermal electron bath. Their one-dimensional redistribution operator retains recoil, relativistic kinematics, and Pauli blocking. Two independent derivations give a Fokker–Planck limit—a fermionic analogue of the Kompaneets equation—and conservative numerical schemes identify where diffusion fails for narrow features. Because the weak cross section grows with energy, the high-energy tail relaxes much faster than the low-energy spectrum.

History

A neutrino Kompaneets equation was previously developed for inelastic neutrino–nucleon scattering in supernova matter (Suwa et al. 2019), and numerical decoupling calculations already include neutrino–electron collision integrals. The authors’ companion paper reduced the exact isotropic neutrino–electron kernel from five dimensions to two (arXiv:2607.25436). The new work dynamically implements that kernel, compares it with the Fokker–Planck limit, and derives analytic distortion modes. The gain is methodological rather than a changed cosmological prediction. Homogeneity and isotropy are assumed, while flavor oscillations, neutrino self-interactions, nucleon scattering, and bulk motion are omitted.

★★☆ 5. First observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz decay ψ(3686)→μ+μ−η′

BESIII Collaboration
Submitted: 5 October 2026
arXiv:2610.07054

Using 2.7124×109 ψ(3686) decays, BESIII observes the muonic Dalitz channel at 6.1σ and measures

B[ψ(3686)→μ+μ−η′]=(4.1±1.0stat±0.4syst)×10−7.

Combining it with BESIII’s electron-channel measurement gives Bμμ/Bee=0.22±0.07, consistent with vector-meson dominance and lepton-flavor universality.

History

BESIII previously observed ψ(3686)→e+e−η′ with a branching fraction near 1.9×10−6 (arXiv:1803.09714). The new result supplies the first direct electron–muon comparison in this transition and tests the timelike ψ(3686)η′ form factor above the dimuon threshold. It confirms the vector-meson-dominance expectation rather than exposing a discrepancy. The roughly 25% statistical uncertainty makes the universality test coarse, and substantially more data would be needed to resolve nontrivial q2 dependence.

★★☆ 6. Spectator Dark Matter and the Higgs Portal

Stephen E. Henrich, Yann Mambrini, Keith A. Olive
Submitted: 5 October 2026
arXiv:2610.07331

Light scalar spectators acquire inflationary fluctuations that can survive as an excessive dark-matter condensate. An extremely weak Higgs portal, 10−28≲λHS≲10−9, generates a radiative self-interaction and thermal mass that make the condensate oscillate and redshift early without thermalizing it. The minimal construction favors 0.1eV≲mS≲10GeV and low reheating temperatures. If inflation did not reach stochastic equilibrium, isocurvature requires approximately N≳1010 e-folds.

History

Self-interacting spectator dark matter and its isocurvature constraints were treated previously (Markkanen et al. 2019), while coupling a spectator to the inflaton was known to suppress its condensate (arXiv:2303.07359). The distinct result is that a portal far below the freeze-in threshold can erase the condensate through radiatively induced self-coupling and the Standard-Model thermal bath without equilibrating the dark sector. It supplies a consistent initial condition for later freeze-in, rather than a new detectable signature. The N≳1010 nonequilibrium branch is extreme, and the preferred region depends strongly on inflation, reheating, curvature coupling, and thermalization assumptions.

★☆☆ 7. Testing Dark Matter Coscattering with the High-Energy LZ Recoil

Sreemanti Chakraborti, Soumyajit Datta
Submitted: 6 October 2026
arXiv:2610.08648

The authors interpret LZ’s isolated 248-keV nuclear-recoil candidate using singlet–doublet fermion dark matter, a light dark photon, and an approximately 100-keV inelastic splitting. Slow conversion from the mostly singlet state into the doublet controls freeze-out, while the light mediator enhances the same endothermic transition in xenon. For mA′=250 MeV, the LZ-sensitive band covers nearly all of the relic-compatible conversion-driven region in their scan, selecting masses around 0.75−1.6 TeV.

History

Coscattering was introduced as a fourth thermal freeze-out mechanism when conversion, rather than annihilation, is the bottleneck (D’Agnolo, Pappadopulo and Ruderman 2017); conversion-driven freeze-out in coannihilating sectors was developed independently at the same time (Garny et al. 2017). The novelty is to make the conversion fixing the abundance observable as an inelastic recoil, instead of fitting the LZ event with an unrelated interaction. The interpretation remains speculative because it rests on one candidate near the edge of an extended window. It is also sensitive to backgrounds, the high-velocity halo tail, mediator constraints, nuclear response, and momentum dependence in the conversion Boltzmann equation.

★☆☆ 8. Lunar radio emission from dark photon dark matter

Ariel Arza
Submitted: 5 October 2026
arXiv:2610.07066

Dark-photon dark matter crossing a dielectric discontinuity can convert coherently into ordinary radio emission. This forecast treats the lunar regolith–vacuum boundary as an enormous natural conversion surface and calculates signals observable by ALMA, SKA, FAST, and the Green Bank Telescope.

History

Laboratory dish antennas have long exploited hidden-photon conversion at conducting boundaries (Horns et al. 2012), and broadband experiments have demonstrated the method experimentally (BREAD 2024). No close prior study was found in the literature checked for using the Moon’s dielectric surface itself as the conversion interface; that remote, passive “planet-sized dish” is the specific novelty. This is a sensitivity forecast, not an observation. Lunar dielectric heterogeneity, roughness, loss, coherence across the visible surface, radio backgrounds, and calibration determine whether the idealized area advantage survives.

Verified science news

2026 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Francis Halzen for IceCube and cosmic neutrinos

On 6 October 2026, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin.” The scientific account emphasizes Halzen’s role in realizing a cubic-kilometre detector in Antarctic ice and IceCube’s transformation of neutrinos into an astronomical messenger. (Official Nobel press release; APS overview)

The award recognizes the instrument and the 2013 discovery of an astrophysical high-energy neutrino flux, which founded modern neutrino astronomy. The underlying measurements are collective achievements of the IceCube Collaboration; the prize’s legal form names one individual, not the collaboration. This is an award announcement, not a new experimental result.

Science Radar

AI-generated mathematics: 722 manuscripts and open verification questions

On 6 October 2026, OpenAI released a public repository containing 722 mathematical manuscripts grouped into 372 result families, generated mainly by an unreleased internal model after it was posed approximately 4,000 research problems. The repository includes reasoning summaries for ten families and Lean formalizations for many, but not all, manuscripts. OpenAI explicitly states that the collection is at different stages of verification and that unformalized results may contain errors. (OpenAI announcement; repository)

This is neither a peer-reviewed publication set nor independent confirmation that hundreds of open problems have been solved. Its immediate significance is scale: it is an unusually large test of AI-generated research mathematics, with machine-checkable artifacts for part of the corpus. Scientific value will depend on expert checking, correction, attribution to prior literature, and whether the arguments yield human-understandable methods rather than only valid endpoint proofs.

The release also sharpens a research-governance dispute. The independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence recommends prompt disclosure, community-controlled repositories, complete model/prompt/compute metadata, formalization where possible, and funding for human understanding. It opposes using proprietary models to test frontier problems and warns against marketing-led releases. OpenAI adopted some recommendations—public artifacts, revision history, partial formalization, and compute summaries—but used a company-controlled GitHub repository and disclosed only ten reasoning summaries. Several mathematicians welcome the capability while criticizing the release mechanism, attribution practices, and concentration of access. (AGMAI recommendations; independent reporting)

Status: unrefereed, heterogeneous, and only partly formalized. Individual results should be treated as claims or candidate proofs until specialists or proof assistants verify the relevant manuscripts; the repository and its stated contents are verified.
