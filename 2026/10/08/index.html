The 8 October listings contain 19 new astro-ph.HE, 23 new hep-ph, 2 new hep-ex, and 10 new astro-ph.CO papers, before cross-lists and replacements (astro-ph.HE, hep-ph, hep-ex, astro-ph.CO). The selection below also includes two substantive 6 October submissions that appear in this listing window.

Ratings denote especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and useful but forecast- or model-dependent work (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. Polarised X-ray emission from the accreting millisecond pulsar SAX J1808.4–3658 during an X-ray outburst and a type-I X-ray burst

Alessandro Papitto et al.
arXiv:2610.09477 — submitted 7 October 2026 — new measurement

IXPE observed the 2025 outburst of the 2.5-ms accreting millisecond pulsar SAX J1808.4–3658. During the decay, the phase-averaged 2–8 keV polarization degree was 2.9±0.5%, at position angle 46∘±5∘; no significant pulse-phase dependence of the Stokes parameters was found. Simple two-spot modeling places the emitting regions close to the spin axis. During the peak of a thermonuclear burst, IXPE obtained a tentative 2−6 keV polarization degree of 15.5±6.1%, with the angle approximately orthogonal to the persistent emission.

History

IXPE’s earlier observation of SRGA J144459.2−604207—the only other accreting millisecond pulsar targeted so far—found 2.3±0.4% polarization and similarly weak phase dependence (Papitto et al. 2025). SAX J1808 therefore confirms, in a second source, that mildly relativistic Comptonization above broad, nearly spin-aligned hot spots can yield only a few percent polarization. The genuinely new element is the possible burst polarization and its roughly 90∘ angle change, which thin-disc reflection alone does not naturally produce. That burst result is only about 2.5σ, is based on a 15-s interval, and requires confirmation; the spot geometry is also preliminary because the adopted model uses point spots and simplified energy-independent polarization.

★★★ 2. Gamma-rays from black hole coronae: disentangling the leptonic and hadronic contributions

Sébastien Le Bihan, Daniel Grošelj, Martin Lemoine
arXiv:2610.09676 — submitted 7 October 2026 — multimessenger modeling advance

The authors combine particle distributions motivated by kinetic simulations of magnetized coronal turbulence with the AM3 radiative-transport code, including hadronic electromagnetic cascades. For NGC 4151, a nonthermal electron component dominates the MeV emission and remains COSI-detectable if it carries at least about 5% of the electron energy; the hadronic cascade generally takes over only toward the sub-GeV band and is below COSI’s sensitivity. The same electron prescription can also reproduce the 0.1−1 MeV excess of the cosmic X-ray background and the hard-state MeV tail of Cygnus X-1.

History

IceCube previously found only a 2.9σ post-trial excess toward NGC 4151, not a secure source detection (IceCube hard-X-ray AGN search). Fermi analyses and coronal models had already shown that sub-GeV photons can accompany neutrino-producing Seyfert coronae (Murase et al. 2024), while jet and ultra-fast-outflow alternatives remain viable (Peretti et al. 2023). This paper’s advance is to calculate primary-electron and hadronic-cascade components in one kinetic framework and identify the MeV band as a cleaner probe of electron energization than of hadrons. The result does not establish a coronal origin for the putative neutrinos: the proton acceleration efficiency is tuned against gamma-ray bounds, the geometry is effectively one-zone, and the predicted crossover depends on the uncertain nonthermal electron fraction.

★★★ 3. Remnant-wide mapping of heterogeneous presupernova burning histories in the O-rich ejecta of Cassiopeia A

Yui Kuboike, Toshiki Sato, Kai Matsunaga
arXiv:2610.09612 — submitted 7 October 2026 — new observational mapping

Using deep Chandra data, the authors apply principal-component analysis to O, Ne, Mg, Si, and Fe narrow-band images, then fit spectra from 20 representative regions. They find a continuous compositional sequence from Ne-rich through Ne–Si-rich to O/Si-rich ejecta, with systematic Ne and Mg depletion toward the latter. The O/Si-rich abundances from O through Ca agree simultaneously with the O layers of 17−20M⊙ one-dimensional progenitor models that underwent strong shell-merger burning. Ne-rich and O/Si-rich material are segregated mainly to the north and southeast, respectively.

History

Earlier Chandra work established Cas A’s large-scale ejecta asymmetries, and selected regions had already shown Ne/Mg-poor O-rich material compatible with pre-collapse shell merging; recent XRISM spectroscopy added odd-Z nucleosynthetic evidence. The present work changes the evidentiary scale: it maps a remnant-wide continuum of burning histories rather than inferring shell merging from a few knots, and argues that heterogeneous progenitor structure survived the explosion. This is stronger than a generic explosion-asymmetry interpretation, but not unique proof of a shell merger. The comparison uses one-dimensional stellar models, while the inferred spatial history is intrinsically three-dimensional; projection, nonequilibrium-ionization modeling, and post-shock mixing can also reshape abundance maps.

★★★ 4. Scalar Perturbations and Induced Gravitational Waves from First-Order Phase Transitions in Lattice Simulations

Yuan-Jie Li, Jing Liu, Zhuan Ning, Xiang-Xi Zeng, Zong-Kuan Guo
arXiv:2610.09325 — submitted 7 October 2026 — methodological advance

This is a three-dimensional, multi-bubble lattice calculation of a radiation-era first-order phase transition that evolves the scalar field, scalar metric potentials, and radiation density and velocity perturbations together from the bubble-wall scale through the horizon scale. It yields the curvature spectrum, a non-Gaussian horizon-scale density PDF, and source-resolved gravitational-wave spectra. Radiation and metric-sourced contributions can exceed one third of the direct scalar-field contribution on superhorizon scales and alter the infrared spectral slope. For the simulated strengths and rates, ordinary horizon-scale fluctuations remain well below primordial-black-hole collapse thresholds.

History

Recent analyses treated transition-generated curvature perturbations primarily with semi-analytic or separate-universe methods (Franciolini, Gouttenoire & Jinno 2026; Wang et al. 2026), while earlier lattice studies concentrated on direct scalar/fluid gravitational waves or on density perturbations without this coupled metric treatment. The new calculation closes the wall-to-horizon gap and shows quantitatively that omitting radiation and scalar-metric sources biases the infrared gravitational-wave spectrum. Its domain is deliberately restricted: metric perturbations remain linear, scalar–radiation coupling is only gravitational, and acoustic gravitational waves from direct fluid interactions are absent. Rare, exceptionally delayed regions that might form primordial black holes are not resolved.

★★☆ 5. Toward Simulations of Cold Baryogenesis via Supercooled Phase Transitions

Naoya Kitajima, Shota Nakagawa, Yuichiro Nakai, Yaoduo Wang, Junxuan Xu
arXiv:2610.09257 — submitted 7 October 2026 — simulation advance with model-dependent baryon yield

Three-dimensional real-time lattice simulations follow a transition-driving scalar, the Higgs field, and electroweak gauge fields from bounce-derived bubbles through collision and nonlinear relaxation. Higgs winding and Chern–Simons number are still produced efficiently for strongly non-degenerate vacua, ϵ~10−3−10−4, where the released vacuum energy can also source a strong gravitational-wave signal. A perturbative higher-dimensional CP-violating operator then gives benchmark baryon yields compatible with the observed asymmetry.

History

Classical-lattice cold electroweak baryogenesis was established in tachyonic or externally quenched transitions two decades ago (Tranberg, Smit & Hindmarsh 2006). This work extends that program to nucleated, strongly supercooled bubbles initialized from bounce solutions and shows that large vacuum non-degeneracy need not suppress topological production. The baryon abundance is not yet a prediction of a complete model: β/H~O(10) is imposed rather than thermally derived, the simulated microscopic-to-bubble scale hierarchy is much smaller than the phenomenological one, and the CP-violating operator is inserted perturbatively rather than evolved dynamically. The gravitational-wave/baryogenesis coexistence is therefore demonstrated in principle, not mapped into a robust experimental target.

★★☆ 6. Dynamical pairing in gravitational wave populations

Fabio Antonini, Aleksandra Olejak, Isobel Romero-Shaw, Matthew Mould
arXiv:2610.09834 — submitted 7 October 2026 — population-inference method; tentative data feature

The paper derives an “ordered-draw” null model for dynamically assembled black-hole binaries: component masses are independent draws from an effective one-body mass reservoir and are then labeled m1≥m2. Once the primary-mass spectrum is inferred, the conditional p(m2|m1) is fixed. Cluster simulations reproduce the resulting fixed-m2 ridges, unlike the approximately fixed-q=m2/m1 structures expected more naturally from isolated binaries. A nonparametric reconstruction shows a tentative ridge near m2≃30M⊙ for m1≳40M⊙, consistent with—but not evidence for—the dynamical pattern.

History

Previous population analyses parameterized pairing with a mass-ratio power law or tested whether primary and secondary distributions share features (Fishbach & Holz 2020; Farah, Fishbach & Holz 2024). The new contribution is a parameter-free conditional prediction tied to dynamical assembly and a geometric discriminator—fixed secondary-mass ridges—rather than another flexible mixture fit. The 30M⊙ feature is explicitly tentative and sensitive to smoothing, selection effects, and uncertainty in the one-body spectrum. Ordered draws can also fail when hierarchical remnants, multiple metallicities, or generation-dependent pairing correlations are important, so agreement would not uniquely identify a cluster channel.

★★☆ 7. Observation of the electromagnetic Dalitz transition J/ψ→e+e−ηc

BESIII Collaboration
arXiv:2610.09626 — submitted 7 October 2026 — first observation

With (10.087±0.044)×109 J/ψ decays, BESIII observes the internal-conversion transition J/ψ→e+e−ηc for the first time. Relative to J/ψ→γηc, the rate is

R=(0.65±0.02stat±0.07syst)%,

corresponding to an absolute branching fraction (0.92±0.03±0.10±0.09)×10−4. The extracted timelike transition form factor shows no significant deviation from either vector-meson dominance (0.606%) or the lattice-QCD prediction (0.608%).

History

The radiative M1 transition J/ψ→γηc is well measured, and both vector-meson-dominance and lattice-QCD calculations predicted the Dalitz/radiative ratio before this analysis (HPQCD 2023). The first observation validates those calculations in the low-q2, timelike region and opens a direct form-factor measurement; it does not reveal anomalous charmonium dynamics. The total error is already dominated by systematics, and the small available dilepton-mass range gives limited power to discriminate smooth form-factor models.

★★☆ 8. Predictions for Short Gamma-Ray Burst Detections by COSI

Eliza Neights et al.
arXiv:2610.08940 — submitted 6 October 2026 — instrument forecast

The authors inject 2,720 Fermi-GBM-derived short-GRB realizations into simulated COSI backgrounds and run an emulation of the actual onboard anticoincidence-shield trigger. Folding the measured trigger efficiency through an intrinsic population gives 47−89 short-GRB detections per year (90% range), of which about 7−10 should be localized within 2.5∘. If COSI operates into the LVK O5 era, the predicted binary-neutron-star GW/GRB yield is 0−3 joint events per year.

History

Earlier COSI/BTO studies forecast broad transient returns and roughly 150 GRBs per year for the lower-energy BTO subsystem (Gulick et al. 2024). The present work is more operationally specific: it propagates a short-GRB population through the flight trigger logic and separates trigger, localization, and joint-GW yields. It remains a forecast, not an on-orbit sensitivity measurement. Rates depend on the adopted luminosity function, simulated backgrounds, duty cycle, mission extension into O5, and uncertain future GW-network sensitivity; the 0−3 interval is therefore more informative than its central value.

★☆☆ 9. Sensitivity targets for dark photon searches in charged kaon decays

M. Fabbrichesi, E. Gabrielli, K. Müürsepp
arXiv:2610.08911 — submitted 6 October 2026 — phenomenology forecast

The study calculates K+→π+π0γD and the newly proposed radiative channel K+→π+γγD, with an invisible massless or massive dark photon. A flavor-changing dimension-five magnetic-dipole operator dominates; ordinary kinetic mixing is negligible for these modes. For a massless dark photon, existing K0−K¯0 constraints permit branching fractions up to roughly 7×10−7 and 6×10−10, respectively. For mγD≳5 MeV, K+→π+X and K+→π+νν¯ bounds force the two-pion target below 10−9; the radiative mode falls more slowly with mass and covers a wider kinematic range.

History

The same framework previously identified K+→π+π0γD as a promising massless-dark-photon mode (Fabbrichesi, Gabrielli & Mele 2017). The new work adds the one-pion radiative channel and treats massive dark photons consistently with current missing-energy constraints. This is useful target-setting, not a new exclusion: there is no detector-level background or acceptance study, and the rates inherit chiral-quark-model matrix elements and an assumed effective flavor-changing operator whose ultraviolet origin is unspecified.

Verified science news

2026 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Francis Halzen for IceCube and astrophysical neutrinos

Catch-up item; announced 6 October 2026. The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin” (official Nobel page; NASA recognition, posted 7 October). The citation recognizes the creation of cubic-kilometre neutrino astronomy and the 2013 establishment of an astrophysical high-energy neutrino population, rather than a new measurement announced on 8 October. It is unusually consequential for astroparticle physics because it places detector conception and the opening of a new astronomical messenger at the center of the award.

GRB 261007A / EP261007a follow-up

GRB 261007A/EP261007a is a long gamma-ray burst detected by SVOM, Einstein Probe and Fermi, with an optical afterglow at z=2.101 (GCN event page). No neutrino or gravitational-wave association was reported by the 8 October cutoff.

Science Radar

2026 Chemistry Nobel: Kagan and Soai recognized for amplifying molecular handedness

Award announced 7 October 2026; recognition of established peer-reviewed work, not a new experiment. Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry “for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis” (official press release; independent context from Nature). Kagan showed that nonlinear catalyst behavior can amplify enantiomeric excess, while the Soai reaction demonstrated asymmetric autocatalysis capable of turning a minute initial imbalance into a strongly one-handed product. The work changed practical asymmetric synthesis and supplied a concrete chemical mechanism relevant to—but not by itself a complete explanation of—the origin of biological homochirality.

OpenAI releases 719 AI-generated mathematics manuscripts; validation is only beginning

Repository release dated 6 October 2026; unreviewed research output. OpenAI published 719 manuscripts grouped into 372 result families, generated mostly by an unreleased internal model after it was posed about 4,000 problems. The repository reports that about 42% of top-line results currently have Lean formalizations, releases ten abridged reasoning summaries, and states an average compute equivalent to roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking per result (OpenAI release; public repository). The repository itself warns that the collection spans different verification stages and that unformalized results may contain errors.

This is potentially a major change in mathematical research practice, but “372 breakthroughs” would be premature. Lean checking establishes correctness only for the formalized statements relative to their encoded assumptions and libraries; it does not establish novelty, significance, attribution, or the correctness of unformalized companion claims. The independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence called the release an important event while explicitly withholding endorsement and stressing that public release is the beginning—not the completion—of human assessment and incorporation into mathematics (AGMAI statement). Its broader recommendations also criticize testing frontier open problems on proprietary models and call for citable community repositories, fuller provenance, broad tool access, and funded human understanding. As of 8 October, the verified result is the scale and partial formalization of the release; the mathematical value of individual families remains to be established by expert review.
