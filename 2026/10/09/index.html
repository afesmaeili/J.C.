Ratings distinguish especially consequential results (★★★), substantive advances (★★☆), and preliminary or model-dependent work (★☆☆).

★★★ 1. Neutrino flavor conversion alters the lepton-emission self-sustained asymmetry in core-collapse supernovae

Noah Roux, Irene Tamborra, Maryna Mesiura
arXiv:2610.10685 — v1 submitted 7 October 2026; announced 9 October

Can quantum flavor evolution feed back on LESA, the dipolar electron-lepton-number emission asymmetry seen in multidimensional core-collapse-supernova simulations? The authors solve two-flavor quantum kinetic equations with advection and collisions in a two-dimensional half-annulus spanning 20–45 km, with energy and polar-angle resolution and two effective azimuthal momentum bins. Their imposed LESA-like background produces collisional instability near decoupling, fast conversion mainly in the electron-lepton-number-depleted hemisphere, and slow instabilities elsewhere. Nonlinear conversion is direction dependent and raises the normalized LESA dipole by about 5–10% beyond 30 km, with little dependence on mass ordering.

History

LESA was discovered in three-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations by Tamborra et al. (2014), while earlier work on LESA and collective oscillations within a bulb-like treatment found matter suppression (Chakraborty et al. 2015). More recent quantum-kinetic studies established that advection, collisions, and fast and slow modes can coexist near decoupling (Shalgar & Tamborra 2023), but did not calculate their effect on a global LESA geometry. The substantive novelty is therefore the first multidimensional kinetic calculation of flavor conversion in a LESA-like configuration and the sign and scale of the resulting dipole change.

This is not yet a self-consistent supernova result. The dipole is imposed on a spherically symmetric 18.6-solar-mass snapshot rather than generated by hydrodynamics; matter feedback is absent; only two flavors and two effective azimuthal bins are retained; and the neutrino self-interaction Hamiltonian is attenuated by a factor 0.025 for tractability. The authors explicitly note that this rescaling can affect slow and collisional modes. The 5–10% enhancement is thus a controlled result for the adopted setup, not a calibrated correction ready for global explosion simulations.

★★★ 2. The Origin of the Fermi Halo-like Emission: A New Model of the Fermi Bubbles

Ilias Cholis, Leo Qiyuan Hu, Yi-Ming Zhong
arXiv:2610.10695 — v1 submitted 7 October 2026; announced 9 October

Is the broad high-latitude, roughly 20-GeV residual reported in Fermi-LAT data evidence for dark-matter annihilation in the Galactic halo? Using 2008–2025 LAT data from 0.3 to 900 GeV, the authors repeat the template analysis across multiple sky regions, source masks, fit parameterizations, and 100 Galactic diffuse-emission models. They confirm that an unmodeled bright component exists, but find that above |b|≥20∘ its normalization changes across regions in a way inconsistent with a single annihilation template. Updated Fermi-Bubble boundaries plus separate flat-bubble, cocoon, and newly introduced inner-bubble templates absorb the claimed halo-like component. A dark-matter signal as bright as the earlier claim is no longer required.

History

Totani (2025) found the residual with six diffuse models and interpreted its morphology and spectrum as compatible with an approximately 0.5-TeV annihilating particle, requiring a cross section of order 6×10−25cm3s−1. The new work does not dispute the photons; it changes their attribution. The broader foreground library and region-by-region normalization test expose a degeneracy with inverse-Compton emission from incompletely modeled Fermi-Bubble substructure. This is a direct, technically substantial challenge to that specific dark-matter interpretation, while leaving the inner-Galaxy excess essentially unchanged.

The limitation is that the replacement components are still phenomenological spatial templates, not a predictive cosmic-ray transport solution. Bubble, Loop I, inverse-Compton, and diffuse-Galactic components remain correlated, and some fitted morphology changes across diffuse models. The result therefore removes the need for the reported bright halo dark-matter component; it does not prove that every weaker high-latitude dark-matter contribution is absent.

★★★ 3. Observation of single top-quark production in the s-channel in proton–proton collisions at s=13 TeV with the ATLAS detector

ATLAS Collaboration
arXiv:2610.11465 — submitted 8 October 2026; announced 9 October

ATLAS searches for the highly suppressed electroweak process qq¯′→W*→tb¯ in the full 140fb−1 Run-2 sample. Events contain one electron or muon, missing transverse momentum, and exactly two b-tagged jets. A graph neural network separates signal from tt¯, W+jets, and t-channel single-top backgrounds. The fitted significance is 5.1 standard deviations observed (5.2 expected), and

σtb¯+t¯b=10.8±1.5(stat.)±1.6(syst.) pb,

consistent with the Standard Model.

History

The Tevatron first observed the s channel only by combining CDF and D0, reaching 6.3 sigma (CDF+D0 2014). At the LHC, ATLAS obtained 3.2 sigma at 8 TeV (ATLAS 2016) and 3.3 sigma with the same 139–140 fb−1 Run-2 exposure at 13 TeV, measuring 8.2−2.9+3.5 pb (ATLAS 2023). The new result crosses the discovery threshold without additional luminosity; the advance is the graph-network discrimination and revised analysis strategy, not an exposure update. It is the first single-experiment observation of this channel.

The measurement uncertainty remains about 20%, with statistical and systematic terms comparable, so it is a confirmation of the Standard Model rather than a precision constraint on anomalous Wtb couplings. Comparisons with the older analysis also require care because the event modeling and multivariate discriminant changed together.

★★★ 4. Discovery of a nearby ultra-fast magnetic white dwarf propeller: common pathways to magnetic cataclysmic variables

Antonio C. Rodriguez, Kareem El-Badry, R. Michael Rich, Kevin N. Ortiz Ceballos, Ingrid Pelisoli, Casey J. Law, Kaya Mori, Boris Gänsicke, Edo Berger, Liam Connor, Ilkham Galiullin, Ilaria Caiazzo, Matthew Graham, Samuel Whitebook
arXiv:2610.10689 — v1 submitted 7 October 2026; announced 9 October

A cross-match of eROSITA and Gaia using the “X-ray Main Sequence” identifies 1eRASS J042633.5−250226 (Valeria), 265 pc away. TESS and ZTF show AE Aqr-like flaring, a 6.62-hour orbit, and month-scale state changes; phase-resolved spectroscopy gives K2=202±7kms−1 and no accretion disk or stream. Zeeman splitting suggests BWD=26±3 MG, with an alternative solution near 33 MG. High-speed optical data show a candidate 26.8-second pulsation, though the robust statement is only Pspin<60 s. For any plausible transfer rate, the inferred magnetospheric radius greatly exceeds corotation, making the propeller classification secure. The authors infer a local birth-rate density near 8×10−16pc−3yr−1 and argue that many magnetic cataclysmic variables pass through this phase.

History

AE Aquarii was long the sole white-dwarf propeller; LAMOST J0240+1952 became the second (Garnavich et al. 2021), with its 24.939-second spin later confirmed in the far ultraviolet (Pelisoli et al. 2025). Valeria is the third and the nearest. Unlike the second case at discovery, it combines a direct magnetic-field estimate, strong dynamical constraints, and multi-survey variability. The common hot, undermassive donors in all three systems give the first population-level support—still based on only three objects—for a recent thermal-timescale-transfer origin and a pathway toward ordinary magnetic CVs.

The 26.8-second period is not yet a secure spin: it is intermittent, absent from eROSITA X-rays, and requires independent confirmation. The Zeeman fit has aliases, the source is radio-undetected, and the space-density/birth-rate estimate is dominated by small-number and selection uncertainties. The object itself is robust; the claim that a substantial fraction of magnetic CVs traverse this phase remains provisional.

★★☆ 5. Evidence for orbital eccentricity supports a hierarchical origin for GW231123

Baoxiang Wang, Tao Yang, Zhoujian Cao, Bin Hu, Xikai Shan, Rong-Gen Cai
arXiv:2610.12205 — v1 submitted 8 October 2026; announced 9 October

Could the exceptional masses and near-extremal inferred spins of GW231123 partly reflect a quasi-circular waveform assumption? The authors analyze Hanford data from 11–448 Hz and Livingston from 20–448 Hz with the eccentric, precessing, higher-mode TEOBResumS–Dalí model, including calibration uncertainty. They obtain

e5.5Hz=0.34−0.23+0.12(90% credible),

with a Bayes factor of 10.2 over the quasi-circular version of the same waveform family. Allowing eccentricity lowers the secondary-spin median from about 0.90 to 0.74, close to the expected spin of a comparable-mass merger remnant, while leaving the primary rapidly spinning. Zero-noise injection studies show that discarding the 11–20 Hz Hanford information can hide this eccentricity.

History

The LVK discovery analysis inferred source-frame masses near 137 and 101M⊙ and high spins under quasi-circular models (LVK 2025). A subsequent numerical-relativity study argued that eccentricity can masquerade as high spin but found quasi-circular templates still preferred and concluded that better eccentric merger models were needed (Chandra et al. 2026). The new analysis changes that conclusion by using a precessing eccentric effective-one-body model and lower-frequency Hanford data; at a 20-Hz cutoff it recovers a weak constraint consistent with prior work. If correct, the secondary becomes more naturally second-generation and less suitable as a direct marker of the pair-instability gap's upper edge.

This is meaningful but not decisive evidence: a Bayes factor of 10 is moderate, the posterior retains support near zero eccentricity, the decisive extra information comes from one detector's glitch-subtracted 11–20 Hz band, and the result is waveform- and prior-dependent for a signal with very few cycles. Independent eccentric waveform families and collaboration-level reanalysis are required before treating the hierarchical interpretation as established.

★★☆ 6. Short-Baseline Near Detector (SBND): Design and Initial Performance

SBND Collaboration
arXiv:2610.11782 — v1 submitted 8 October 2026; announced 9 October

The paper documents the as-built 112-tonne active-mass liquid-argon TPC 110 m from Fermilab's Booster Neutrino Beam and reports its first year of beam performance. Electron lifetimes exceeded 30 ms—ten times the design requirement—for more than 95% of Run 1; 99.3% of TPC channels were live; collection-wire noise averaged 388 electrons; photon yield exceeded 10 photoelectrons/MeV with few-nanosecond timing; and the system achieved 98.6% data-collection efficiency. These conditions have already produced the world's largest neutrino–argon interaction sample.

History

Fermilab announced SBND's first neutrino interactions in September 2024 (official announcement), and previous papers described expected photon-system performance (Machado et al. 2024) and the broader physics program (SBND 2025). The new article is the first consolidated as-built detector and measured-performance reference. The factor-of-ten purity margin, near-theoretical wire noise, high live fraction, and resolved 19-ns beam bunch structure turn design forecasts into demonstrated operating capability.

This is an instrumentation milestone, not yet a sterile-neutrino or cross-section measurement. Detector stability does not by itself establish the final flux, reconstruction, and correlated near/far systematic uncertainties that will control SBN oscillation tests. Physics reach still depends on paired ICARUS analyses and validated interaction models.

★★☆ 7. Effective field theory for strong phase transitions

Oliver Gould, Farbod-Sayyed Rassouli, Paul M. Saffin
arXiv:2610.10692 — v1 submitted 7 October 2026; announced 9 October

Standard high-temperature dimensional reduction becomes unreliable precisely for the strong first-order transitions that would yield the largest gravitational-wave signals. The authors identify a new power counting in which the order parameter remains soft while fields coupled to its large background become hard. Integrating out those modes gives a three-dimensional EFT with non-polynomial field dependence but a controlled derivative expansion. In a two-scalar model they match through NLO, add the first infrared correction at NNLO, demonstrate renormalization-scale cancellation, and find decreasing corrections to the latent heat. They extend the construction to Yukawa and gauge theories and give the useful strong-transition criterion λ~g4/π2.

History

Conventional dimensional-reduction EFTs are systematically controlled for weak or intermediate transitions, and earlier uncertainty studies showed that their largest errors often occur at the strongest, most gravitational-wave-visible points (Croon et al. 2021; Gould & Tenkanen 2021). The new paper does not merely calculate another loop order; it defines a different EFT hierarchy and resums the large background-field dependence that invalidates the polynomial expansion. That is a genuine methodological advance toward credible gravitational-wave forecasts.

The numerical demonstration is still a benchmark two-scalar model. Supercooling can change the hierarchy between critical and nucleation temperatures, gauge and fermion examples are not carried to the same quantitative order, and automation comparable to DRalgo remains future work. The framework improves thermodynamic control; it does not yet supply complete nucleation and gravitational-wave predictions for realistic models.

★★☆ 8. From GeV to PeV: Orbital Modulation and Wind-Regulated Ultrahigh-energy Emission in Cygnus X-3

Su-Yu Wan, Ruo-Yu Liu, Xing-Fu Zhang, Dmitriy Khangulyan
arXiv:2610.11881 — v1 submitted 8 October 2026; announced 9 October

The authors build a common binary–jet model for Cygnus X-3's contemporaneous GeV and PeV spectra and orbital light curves. Anisotropic inverse-Compton emission from jet electrons fixes the geometry using high-statistics GeV modulation. Multi-PeV protons then escape ballistically and make TeV–PeV photons through pγ and pp interactions. In the fitted present state, a fast accretion-disk wind displaces the much denser Wolf–Rayet wind from observer-directed proton trajectories, suppressing pp emission. Modest changes in wind orientation or collimation can instead expose the protons to the dense stellar wind and raise the PeV luminosity by orders of magnitude.

History

LHAASO established variable emission from 0.06 to 3.7 PeV and reported 3.2-sigma evidence for 4.8-hour orbital modulation (LHAASO 2026). Immediate models explained the UHE component alone, including a counterjet-dominated geometry, but did not simultaneously reproduce the GeV light curve. The present work's advance is to let the GeV data constrain geometry before interpreting the PeV component; in the authors' test, the earlier UHE geometry fails that joint constraint. The wind-switching mechanism also offers a concrete—though unverified—connection to the much brighter 1980s PeV claims.

The model assumes ballistic multi-PeV propagation, an effective proton escape height, and idealized thin-shell wind interaction; shocked-layer mixing, clumping, and detailed magnetic transport are neglected. The historical PeV detections remain unconfirmed, so the predicted orders-of-magnitude state dependence is a scenario, not an observed cycle. Contemporaneous X-ray/radio wind diagnostics and more orbital PeV exposure are the decisive tests.

Verified science news

GRB 261007A and EP261007b follow-up

GCN circulars through 9 October report follow-up of the long burst GRB 261007A / EP261007a, with a secure VLT/X-shooter redshift z=2.101 and preliminary Fermi-GBM fluence 9.01×10−5ergcm−2; no neutrino or gravitational-wave association is reported. A new SVOM detection of the optical transient near EP261007b does not establish an association because the reported variable lies outside the Einstein Probe FXT localization (GCN 45864).
