ArXiv highlights

The 12 October listings contain 9 new astro-ph.HE, 20 new hep-ph, 5 new hep-ex, and 7 new astro-ph.CO submissions, excluding cross-lists and replacements. The eight selected papers were submitted on 8–9 October and announced in the 12 October listings.

★★★ 1. TeV Gamma-Ray Emission in the DA 495 Region from HAWC Observations of J1952+2924, J1954+2837, and J1958+2848

HAWC Collaboration
arXiv:2610.13771

New measurement

Using 3,071 days of fifth-pass HAWC data, the collaboration resolves three extended sources with curved spectra in a confused Galactic-plane field: HAWC J1952+2924, associated with the pulsar-wind nebula DA 495, and J1954+2837 and J1958+2848, both plausible TeV-halo candidates. A fourth component is only a hint and is sensitive to the adopted Galactic diffuse-emission model.

For DA 495, the new spectrum extrapolates to

dNdE|1TeV=(3.89±1.27stat)×10−13TeV−1cm−2s−1,

consistent within 1σ with VERITAS. A one-zone leptonic interpretation uses B~10μG and a roughly 50-TeV electron cutoff. The two other sources require diffusion coefficients around 3.2×1026 and 1.4×1027cm2s−1 at their fitted cutoff energies, far below the canonical interstellar value.

History

The earlier VERITAS/Fermi follow-up confirmed TeV emission from DA 495 but measured a substantially smaller flux than HAWC’s 2HWC catalog; the later HAWC–VERITAS–NuSTAR study could not resolve whether the mismatch arose from morphology or source confusion. The dedicated three-source likelihood fit now removes that discrepancy without invoking variability and is therefore a substantive observational advance.

It also strengthens, but does not establish, the TeV-halo interpretation of J1954+2837 and J1958+2848. Their pulsar associations are positional and model dependent, the tentative fourth source changes with the diffuse template, and the DA 495 distance and age remain uncertain. At the often-used 1-kpc distance the inferred transport can fall below the Bohm limit; this indicates that the geometry or distance assumptions need revision, not exotic diffusion physics.

★★★ 2. Perpendicular transport in isotropic magnetic turbulence: The role of stochastic drifts

F. Pucci, P. Blasi, O. Pezzi, C. Evoli, F. Malara
arXiv:2610.13865

Methodological/theory advance

The authors propagate test particles through wavelet-generated, magnetostatic isotropic turbulence spanning six to seven decades in wavenumber—about two orders of magnitude more dynamic range than conventional grid realizations. Parallel diffusion follows D∥∝E1/3 at low rigidity and D∥∝E2 in the quasi-ballistic regime. The ratio D⟂/D∥ is nearly constant only for rL≲10−2Lc, then rises with rigidity.

Extending nonlinear guiding-center theory with stochastic curvature/gradient drifts and a telegraph closure reproduces this transition better than standard NLGC/UNLT formulas.

History

Test-particle simulations had already found unequal parallel and perpendicular energy scalings, notably in Dundović et al. 2020. Kuhlen, Mertsch and Phan instead emphasized magnetic-field-line subdiffusion as the origin of the growing ratio. The new work both extends the resolved inertial range and supplies a quantitatively successful alternative mechanism—random guiding-center drifts caused by turbulent field components parallel to the guide field.

It does not decisively exclude the field-line explanation; the paper explicitly allows both effects to contribute. The calculation uses prescribed, isotropic, magnetostatic turbulence, a fitted closure parameter, and no self-consistent cosmic-ray feedback, so its scaling should not be transplanted directly to anisotropic Galactic turbulence.

★★★ 3. Seeing in the Dark: Gravitational Waves from Resonant Production of Dark Photon Dark Matter

Yanou Cui, Pankaj Saha, Evangelos I. Sfakianakis
arXiv:2610.12564

Nonlinear calculation and forecast

An oscillating Abelian-Higgs condensate can parametrically amplify the longitudinal mode of a dark photon. Dedicated three-dimensional lattice simulations now follow the linear resonance through backreaction, fragmentation, vacuum trapping, scalar–vector rescattering, and gravitational-wave production.

The final relic is generically a mixture of dark photons and dark-Higgs excitations; the partition is fixed by nonlinear evolution rather than by the linear Floquet exponent. If this sector supplies all dark matter, the gravitational-wave peak and amplitude correlate with the resonance scale. Frequencies from nanohertz to about 100 Hz map, in the studied benchmarks, to dark-photon masses from roughly 10−18 eV to the sub-µeV range.

History

Dror, Harigaya and Narayan established the analytic parametric-resonance mechanism but could not determine the post-backreaction abundance, phase-space distribution, or gravitational-wave spectrum. The new lattice calculation changes that status materially: the scalar remnant can be comparable to the vector abundance, and the observable is a correlated multicomponent-dark-matter/GW signal rather than a pure-vector relic.

This is still a forecast, not a detection claim. Finite lattice dynamic range, approximate post-trapping number-conserving scatterings, the minimal quartic potential, and the assumption that the sector makes all dark matter control the reach; astrophysical constraints on the mixed scalar–vector population are not yet propagated globally.

★★☆ 4. The road to H0, Ωm, and w with a global neutrino-telescope network

Pavel Zhelnin, Gonzalo Herrera, Nicholas Kamp, Carlos A. Argüelles
arXiv:2610.12577

Current reanalysis plus forecast

This study treats the empirical Seyfert neutrino–X-ray luminosity relation, Lν∝LXβ, as a distance indicator. Applying it to 14 years of public IceCube data for 26 northern and southern Seyferts, anchored by geometric distances to NGC 1068 and NGC 4945, gives

H0=66−37+44kms−1Mpc−1,β=0.72−0.16+0.14.

A 2030 IceCube+KM3NeT-ARCA+Baikal-GVD forecast with five anchors gives H0=66−13+15 and β=0.72±0.04, a nearly 65% reduction in the quoted H0 uncertainty and a projected ≳5σ rejection of the calorimetric slope β=1. A high-redshift “little-red-dot” extension to Ωm and w is explicitly conditional on those objects becoming neutrino sources obeying the same relation.

History

The authors’ initial neutrino H0 analysis used a smaller northern-sky Seyfert sample and one distance anchor. The present real-data fit adds the southern sky and a second anchor but remains a proof of principle: its roughly 60% fractional uncertainty is not cosmologically competitive.

The principal advance is the all-sky network design and demonstration that better angular reconstruction and multiple anchors can separately constrain H0 and β. The forecast assumes that a single luminosity law—with controlled X-ray absorption, variability, source selection, and intrinsic scatter—applies to every Seyfert and future detector. Those systematics may dominate before the advertised statistical precision is reached. The high-redshift Ωm,w exercise is more speculative still.

★★★ 5. nCTEQ26 nuclear parton distribution functions

nCTEQ Collaboration: K. Greve, T. Ježo, A. Kusina, J. Wissmann, M. Klasen, K. Kovařík, C. Léger, J. G. Morfín, F. I. Olness, P. Risse, I. Schienbein, J. Y. Yu
arXiv:2610.13633

Global analysis and public resource

nCTEQ26 fits 3,598 measurements—neutral- and charged-current DIS, neutrino dimuons, fixed-target Drell–Yan, electroweak bosons, inclusive hadrons, open heavy flavor and quarkonia from RHIC and LHC Runs 1–2—with 36 free parameters at NLO.

A flexible x dependence is combined with quadratic logarithmic nuclear-A dependence, target-mass and deuteron corrections, and the CJ15 proton baseline. The data reach x~10−5, while relaxed cuts admit high-x Jefferson Lab information. Hessian grids are released through LHAPDF and HEPForge.

History

nCTEQ15 used 740 points, chiefly fixed-target DIS/Drell–Yan plus RHIC pion data. EPPS21 and nNNPDF3.0 had already shown the leverage of LHC dijets, electroweak bosons and heavy flavor on small-x gluons.

nCTEQ26’s advance is therefore not a first observation of shadowing but the consolidation of the nCTEQ program into a much larger, process-diverse fit with explicit high-x corrections and public error sets. Direct precision comparisons across groups remain sensitive to parametrization and tolerance choices: nCTEQ26 uses a Hessian tolerance T=50, works at NLO, fixes the proton baseline rather than propagating its full uncertainty, and does not include a general missing-higher-order error. Data-driven quarkonium/open-heavy-flavor constraints may also absorb production-model effects into the fitted nPDFs.

★★★ 6. Complete four-loop pressure of cold QED

Aapeli Kärkkäinen, Pablo Navarrete, Mika Nurmela, Kaapo Seppänen, Samuel Abreu, Maximilian Delto, Aleksi Kurkela, Risto Paatelainen, Aleksi Vuorinen
arXiv:2610.13873

First complete calculation

The authors complete the next-to-next-to-next-to-leading-order pressure of a zero-temperature ultrarelativistic electron gas. The calculation combines hard, soft and mixed momentum regions; the remaining finite-density four-loop integrals are evaluated with dense loop–tree duality.

It is the first thermodynamic quantity calculated completely through four loops in a physical gauge theory. The series shows good apparent convergence and weak renormalization-scale dependence even at relatively large couplings, although a Bayesian missing-order analysis gives uncertainties larger than scale variation alone.

History

Freedman and McLerran’s 1977 calculation fixed the cold-QED pressure through N2LO. The 2023 result Degenerate fermionic matter at N3LO: Quantum electrodynamics determined all logarithmic and factorized pieces but left the finite hard four-loop constant unevaluated. This paper supplies that last contribution and is therefore a genuine completion rather than an incremental coefficient update.

Its largest impact is methodological: it validates tools aimed at the still-incomplete N3LO cold-QCD pressure relevant to neutron-star equations of state. QED itself is weakly coupled, so the direct phenomenological correction to ordinary electron matter is small; furthermore, scale variation alone demonstrably understates truncation uncertainty. The analogous non-Abelian calculation remains substantially harder.

★★☆ 7. ALP interactions with mesons and baryons at O(1/fa): a general field-redefinition-invariant approach

Jorge Alda, Stefano Rigolin
arXiv:2610.13671

Formalism and phenomenology

Starting from general CP-conserving quark and gluon couplings, the paper identifies field-redefinition-invariant Wilson combinations and matches them into U(3) chiral perturbation theory. LSZ reduction is used to choose a no-mixing basis, so physical ALP–meson amplitudes depend only on invariant direct couplings.

The authors derive general vector and axial couplings to the baryon octet, including a second topological-singlet ALP–baryon structure, constrain flavor-changing terms with rare hyperon decays, and show in a UV example how running generates flavor change from diagonal high-scale couplings. In a gluophilic benchmark the summed baryon–antibaryon width can exceed any individual exclusive mesonic width for 2≲ma/GeV≲3.

History

Earlier weak-chiral analyses, notably Bauer et al., enforced consistent mesonic matching, while Balkin et al. developed a covariant description of ALP–hadron interactions. The new work extends field-basis invariance beyond the anomalous Wess–Zumino–Witten sector and systematically includes both flavor-changing quark couplings and the baryon octet.

No close prior study was found in the literature checked for the second topological-singlet term used as an independent dynamical ALP–baryon coupling; that is the paper’s sharpest formal novelty. The 2–3 GeV width estimates sit near the edge of controlled chiral reasoning and use phenomenological form factors/duality assumptions. Rare-hyperon bounds and collider comparisons also depend on the ALP decay branching fractions and UV flavor structure.

★★★ 8. Nailing down the Hubble constant value through time-delay cosmography: A 3% measurement from SN Refsdal

S. Schuldt, C. Grillo, S. H. Suyu, A. Acebron, L. Bazzanini, P. Bergamini, G. Di Rosa, A. Halkola, A. Mercurio, P. Rosati
arXiv:2610.12595

New cosmographic measurement

The analysis adds an extended-source reconstruction of 77,000 HST pixels from the SN Refsdal host to the MACS J1149.5+2223 cluster lens and its measured supernova time delays. In flat ΛCDM it obtains

H0=65.0−1.8+2.0kms−1Mpc−1,Ωm=0.30−0.09+0.08.

With Ωm free, the surface-brightness information halves the H0 uncertainty relative to the same point-image model, reaching 3.0%. Four source/weighting realizations give mutually consistent results.

History

The blinded multi-model Kelly et al. analysis found H0=64.8−4.3+4.4, while the position-and-delay cluster model of Grillo et al. obtained 65.1−3.4+3.5 in a broader cosmological fit. The central value is therefore confirmation, not a shift; the new result is the factor-of-two precision gain obtained by using resolved host-galaxy structure and the first joint H0,Ωm inference from such an extended lensed-SN reconstruction.

The 3% figure is conditional on a fixed family of cluster mass parametrizations. The authors vary source reconstruction and weighting but deliberately do not explore the full mass-model space, external convergence, or every line-of-sight systematic. It should therefore not yet be read as a 3% total-error resolution of the Hubble tension.

Verified science news

GRB 261011A: a long, very soft event with a short hard spike — preliminary alert sequence

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered at 13:49:04 UTC on 11 October and reported a bright long burst with a preliminary T90=66.77 s; Swift/BAT had independently triggered at 13:46:41 UTC.

Swift saw a broad, mostly sub-50-keV pulse from trigger to about +130 s, a shoulder extending to roughly +260 s, and a separate approximately five-second structure near +95 s visible through the 100–350 keV band. The prompt emission combines an extended soft component with a separate short, harder structure. (SVOM circular, Swift circular)

Swift/UVOT found a White≃17.78 candidate 321 seconds after the trigger. Multiple facilities confirmed an optical counterpart; by 18:56 UTC, 5.17 hours after the burst, LCO measured g=21.91±0.17 and r=21.09±0.14 AB, demonstrating rapid fading. (LCO follow-up)

Status: confirmed gamma-ray burst with a fading optical afterglow, but still preliminary. No spectroscopic redshift, secure progenitor classification, neutrino association or gravitational-wave counterpart was reported in the circulars checked by the report cutoff. The two-timescale prompt morphology is observationally interesting but does not by itself establish a new burst class.
