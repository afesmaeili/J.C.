ArXiv Highlights

1. Cosmic-ray self-confinement: damping depends on the waves actually present

Rouven Lemmerz, Mohamad Shalaby, Ellen Zweibel, Christoph Pfrommer and Timon Thomas — “The impact of nonlinear Landau damping on cosmic ray streaming and the role of external turbulence.” arXiv:2610.20695v1. Submitted 14 October, 18:00:00 UTC; 16 October listing. Preprint; no journal reference listed.

Can nonlinear Landau damping (NLLD) saturate the streaming instability by itself? One-dimensional fluid–particle-in-cell simulations toggle Landau closures while keeping other inputs fixed. A monoenergetic cosmic-ray shell begins at vd=5vA, with vA=0.01c, βi=βe=1 and nCR/nbg=5×10−4.

Without external waves, damping-on/off runs agree and relax toward vd≃vA. Seeded, counter-propagating large-scale waves make NLLD effective and can instead drive the drift toward the background frame. Reducing particle sampling from 4,080 to 204 cosmic-ray macroparticles per ion skin depth produces spurious backward waves and an inverse cascade.

This is a controlled numerical result, not a measured Galactic diffusion coefficient. The shell distribution, reduced mass ratio, non-cascading external modes and absence of thermal-particle trapping in the closure limit generalization. Full text, Sections III–V.

History

Self-confinement couples cosmic-ray transport to the waves the particles excite; damping then controls scattering, heating and momentum transfer. Bai et al. (2019) established detailed MHD-PIC tests of streaming growth and pitch-angle scattering, but ideal-MHD backgrounds do not capture NLLD.

Schroer, Caprioli and Blasi (2024 preprint; PRL 2025) found NLLD, heating, an inverse cascade and a steady drift around 3.7vA in a continuously replenished hybrid-PIC setup. The new paper challenges the interpretation of inverse-cascade signatures, but it does not reproduce that experiment identically: boundaries, driving, plasma treatment and sampling differ. Its numerical-artifact explanation is a testable hypothesis about the disagreement, not proof that the earlier calculation is invalid. Realistic broad cosmic-ray spectra and cross-code comparisons remain necessary.

2. Vertical air showers could extend UHE-neutrino follow-up

Jaime Alvarez-Muñiz, Ruben Conceição, Pedro J. Costa, David Dias and Mário Pimenta — “Identification of ultra-high-energy neutrinos in vertical air showers with surface detectors.” arXiv:2610.21495v1. Submitted 15 October, 08:32:23 UTC; 16 October listing. Preprint; no acceptance listed.

CORSIKA/HERWIG showers and Geant4 detector simulations test whether combined scintillator and water-Cherenkov signals distinguish electromagnetic-rich neutrino showers from protons. The quantitative benchmark is Eν=1018 eV, θ=30∘, with 1,500 m station spacing.

At proton leakage εbkg=2×10−5, motivated by a one-hour, 3° transient search, the detector-level electron-neutrino effective area is approximately 1.6×10−2 km²; ideal electromagnetic/muon separation yields 4.0×10−2 km². These are simulated responses, not observed flux limits.

Important simplifications include fixed shower cores, dense virtual sampling at 1,000 m instead of reconstructed lateral profiles, and a lowered 1.2 VEM station threshold. Efficiency across energy and zenith angle remains unvalidated. Photon showers resemble electron-neutrino showers, so this selection identifies neutral-particle candidates rather than uniquely identifying neutrinos. Full text, Sections II–VI.

History

Established Auger searches exploit old, muon-rich inclined cosmic-ray showers versus young, electromagnetic-rich neutrino showers, including Earth-skimming tau decays. The 2019 Auger point-source analysis searched 1 January 2004–31 August 2018, finding no candidates and reporting declination-dependent limits over its UHE response range.

The new method targets a complementary field of view, conventionally called vertical here for θ≤60∘. Its strongest case is short, directionally constrained follow-up, where backgrounds are reduced externally. An instantaneous single-energy effective area cannot establish an improvement over a published exposure-integrated, flavor-dependent bound. The paper's wider-angle sensitivity extrapolation assumes constant identification efficiency; it is not a demonstrated full detector response.

3. NGC 1068: compact-source inference with an important statistical qualification

Zhi-Peng Ma and Kai Wang — “Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of supermassive black hole in NGC 1068.” arXiv:2610.21110v1. Submitted 15 October, 02:13:36 UTC; 16 October listing. Preprint; no journal reference listed.

A steady proton model includes pp and pγ production, losses and electromagnetic cascades. It samples 10,000 five-parameter configurations and five magnetic-energy ratios, adjusting proton luminosity against neutrino and Fermi-LAT spectra. The radius scan spans 0–15Rs, where Rs=2GM/c2; escape is fixed initially to 10R/c.

The authors quote exclusions of R≳6Rs at “3σ” and R≳10Rs at “5σ.” However, their statistic treats 15 points sampled from a power-law confidence band as independent, without the fitted-spectrum covariance. These nominal significances should not be adopted as calibrated confidence limits.

Also, fcov>0.8 means over 80% of sampled energy points lie inside the band—not 80% of the neutrino flux. The selected models favor R≲3Rs and stochastic acceleration, conditional on this spectral criterion and geometry. Sample percentiles are not marginalized likelihood intervals. Methods, Tables I–II and appendices.

History

The underlying puzzle is established: a bright neutrino source with much weaker escaping gamma rays requires opacity and energy reprocessing. The IceCube analysis used here fits an unbroken spectrum; points drawn along its band are not additional independent measurements.

Das, Murase and Zhang (2026) already favored R~3–10Rs for photohadronic coronae, while allowing larger regions in some hadronuclear settings. Those are conditional physical constraints, not directly comparable Gaussian radius exclusions. The new scan changes spectral acceptance and parameter sampling; it does not supply a model-independent improvement factor.

Composition is another unvaried assumption. Esmaeili, Esmaili and Serpico (2026) explicitly propagate nuclei and nuclear/electromagnetic cascades in NGC 1068. Their framework is not identical—their illustrated electromagnetic treatment neglects synchrotron losses—but it shows why a proton-only scan does not cover all source physics. A covariance-aware likelihood and explicit near-horizon geometry are needed before interpreting the new radius thresholds as robust exclusions.

4. Solar neutrinos test explanations of the gallium anomaly

Steven R. Elliott and R. G. Hamish Robertson — “Gallium Anomaly and Solar Neutrinos.” arXiv:2610.22219v1. Submitted 15 October, 17:02:46 UTC; 16 October listing. Preprint.

Can an incorrect gallium capture cross section or extraction efficiency explain the artificial-source deficit? The authors combine Borexino elastic-scattering measurements, SNO neutral-current data, the solar luminosity constraint, updated capture cross sections and neutrino mixing inputs.

They predict 67.5−1.1+1.4 SNU, versus the radiochemical solar result 66.1±3.1 SNU; the measured/predicted ratio is 0.980−0.049+0.050. One SNU is 10−36 captures per target atom per second. These are rate estimates with propagated uncertainties, not sterile-neutrino exclusion contours.

The agreement challenges a universal approximately 20% response suppression. It does not resolve the artificial-source anomaly. The inference retains solar-density dependence through MSW conversion, a luminosity-constrained pp flux, oscillation inputs and correlated nuclear uncertainties. Extending solar-run efficiency validation to radioactive-source runs is an inference from similar procedures, not a direct remeasurement. Calculation, Tables II–III and Figure 3.

History

BEST measured deficient capture rates in both concentric gallium zones, without establishing the distance dependence needed to identify an oscillation pattern. The anomaly concerns neutrinos from intense chromium/argon sources; it is distinct from the historical solar-neutrino problem.

Cadeddu et al., now published in PRL, proposed that treating leptonic wave functions beyond factorization could lower the capture cross section while preserving the germanium electron-capture lifetime. The new solar comparison supplies an independent empirical consistency test of such explanations. Whether an energy-dependent correction could reconcile both datasets requires a joint calculation with the same nuclear response and covariance. Neither paper alone establishes sterile neutrinos or definitively eliminates all conventional explanations.

5. Reconnection injection need not pass through electric-dominated regions

Omar French, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Qile Zhang, Yi-Hsin Liu, William Daughton and Hui Li — “Are E>B regions essential for particle injection in relativistic magnetic reconnection?” arXiv:2610.22118v1. Submitted 15 October, 15:58:42 UTC; 16 October listing. Submitted to MNRAS; not reported accepted.

Two-dimensional pair-plasma PIC simulations partition each tracer's electric work before γinj=σ/4 into work inside E>B regions, post-exit work, and remaining B>E work. Here σ=B02/(4πn0mec2) uses the total pair density; σ=12–800.

Table 2 gives the magnetized channel the largest contribution for 93%, 70%, 65% and 63% of injected tracers at σ=12,50,200,800, respectively. These exact table entries are preferable to rounded abstract percentages. Many particles reach injection without earlier E>B contact.

This is a trajectory-budget result with negligible guide field and no radiation losses. “Magnetized” here means a field-magnitude classification, not demonstrated adiabatic motion. Three-dimensional, radiative and electron–ion systems need separate tests. Full analysis, Sections II–V.

History

The injection question asks how thermal particles first enter the nonthermal population, before subsequent high-energy acceleration. Sironi (2022) argued that encounters with nonideal fields were necessary, using energetic-particle histories and altered test-particle trajectories.

Gupta, Sridhar and Sironi (2025) found large pre-injection electric-dominated work fractions for particles selected to end at especially high energies. The new paper instead emphasizes the injection shares of the whole injected population and separately tracks post-injection selection. These statistics answer different questions: a channel can inject a minority yet be overrepresented near the eventual cutoff. Boundary conditions and altered-trajectory experiments also differ. The result directly challenges universal necessity of E>B contact in the tested regime; it does not show that every definition of nonideal electric work is unimportant.

Science News

1. EP261015a: a possible optical counterpart, with subtraction uncertainty

Circular: 16 October, 05:15 UTC; observations: 16 October, approximately 03:00 UTC. NOT observed the 15 October Einstein Probe X-ray transient, starting 13.72 hours after its trigger. A difference-image source lies at RA 02:57:19.83, Dec −13:21:17.3 (J2000). NOT measures r=22.74±0.21 AB, and an independent LCO telescope measures r=22.83±0.16 AB at nearly the same epoch, without Galactic-extinction correction.

Both images support the candidate, but an underlying galaxy could produce a subtraction artefact. The near-simultaneous observations do not establish fading. No redshift, neutrino association or progenitor classification is established. The reviewed circular does not provide a calibrated association probability. GCN 45955.

2. A separate Einstein Probe trigger is likely a stellar flare

Event: 16 October, 03:43 UTC; classification circular: 04:40 UTC. The EP team identifies WXT trigger 01709321333 as likely associated with the low-mass star 1RXS J043137.9−304237. Its estimated 0.5–4.0 keV flux is 1.4×10−10 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, with reported luminosity approximately 1.1×1032 erg s⁻¹.

This is a likely physical stellar transient, not confirmation of a cosmological explosion or a statement that the trigger was instrumental noise. The circular gives no formal classification probability. GCN 45954.

3. GRB 261010A: faint infrared candidate and a radio upper limit

Updates: 15 October, 14:40 UTC and 16 October, 02:10 UTC; burst: 10 October. LBT reports a tentative H=24.0±0.3 AB excess near the afterglow position, approximately 4.3 days after the burst. Its calendar-date string is malformed; the elapsed time and stated 01:56 UTC midtime are retained without silently repairing that date.

The excess could be afterglow, underlying host light or a host region. It does not establish a kilonova, and the proposed z=0.07 and z=0.785 associations remain alternatives. Separately, ASKAP observations on 14 October, 04:40–10:19 UTC find no counterpart at 943 MHz, giving a 3σ limit of 135 μJy. This constrains radio emission at that epoch, not the existence of an astrophysical counterpart. GCN 45952; GCN 45953.

4. MG2 J130304+2434 brightens in Fermi-LAT gamma rays

Announcement: 15 October, 17:38 UTC; enhanced emission: 14 October. Fermi-LAT reports activity positionally consistent with the BL Lac MG2 J130304+2434 / 4FGL J1303.0+2434, at z=0.993. The daily E>100 MeV photon flux is (0.4±0.1)×10−6 cm⁻² s⁻¹, approximately 20 times its catalog average, with photon index 1.9±0.2. Quoted uncertainties are statistical; the analysis is preliminary.

This motivates multiwavelength follow-up. No associated neutrino detection is reported in this telegram. ATel 18105.

5. LVK outlines an intermediate observing run

The 15 October official observing-plan update states that LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA are undergoing upgrades and commissioning. A six-month IR1 run targeting mid-January 2027 is planned, with LIGO and Virgo participating and KAGRA joining as available; the start may involve two detectors. This is a plan, not a confirmed observing start.

Short low-noise windows remain possible during upgrades, and an exceptional gravitational-wave event could still generate a public alert. The commissioning interval therefore must not be interpreted as a guarantee of no alerts. O5 planning remains under discussion. Official IGWN observing plan.

Science Radar

1. Designed enzymes tackle peptide-bond cleavage

Hojae Choi and colleagues — “De novo design of cysteine proteases”; peer-reviewed Nature publication: 14 October 2026; reported here as a catch-up. Computationally designed proteins use an activated cysteine to cleave peptide backbones in a sequence-dependent manner. The study reports 13 active designs out of 69 tested (19%), with catalytic rate enhancement over the uncatalysed reaction up to 3×107. Six crystal structures agree with design models within 1.2 Å Cα RMSD.

The key advance is experimentally validated catalysis of a difficult chemical reaction, beyond predicting a stable protein fold. The rate enhancement is relative to uncatalysed chemistry, not a claim of outperforming natural proteases. Active-site organization and substrate positioning still limit performance. Cleavage in mammalian cells supports a synthetic-biology application, not demonstrated therapeutic safety or clinical efficacy. Original publication and accessible experimental captions; 15 October crystallography-facility announcement.

2. A Jurassic bird adds a terrestrial branch to early avian diversity

L. Xu, M. Wang, G. Lin and colleagues — “Jurassic flightless bird reveals early ecological diversity of avialans”; peer-reviewed Nature publication: 14 October; publisher announcement: 15 October 2026. The approximately 148–150-million-year-old Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis from southeastern China combines avialan and dinosaur-like anatomy, including a hyperextensible second toe.

Its forelimbs are roughly one-third shorter than its hind limbs. Phylogenetically informed comparisons, including 377 living bird species, support a terrestrial, flightless interpretation. This expands the ecological diversity inferred for early avialans rather than implying that every early bird followed a single trajectory toward improved flight.

The inference rests on one incomplete specimen and comparative anatomy; behavior is reconstructed, not observed. Its precise evolutionary placement and the history of flight loss require more fossils. Original paper; publisher's dated account.
