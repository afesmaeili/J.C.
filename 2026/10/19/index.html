ArXiv Highlights

1. Quiet black holes as UHECR accelerators: an energy ceiling, not a source detection

Elena Manao, Elisa Resconi, Frank M. Rieger, Paolo Padovani and Gia Dvali — “Exploring Cosmic Rays from Gravity-Limited Acceleration at Supermassive Black Holes.” arXiv:2610.23393v1. Submitted 16 October, 12:51:28 UTC; 19 October listing. Accepted for PRL, according to arXiv.

A gravity-based magnetic-energy estimate gives Bmax∝M−1, hence a formal horizon-scale voltage ceiling

Egeom=aZeBRS~1027Za eV,

where a is dimensionless spin and Ze the particle charge. This is not an achievable-energy prediction: realistic fields are much smaller, and curvature losses impose another ceiling. The quoted Ecurv∝M1/4 assumes the gravity-limited field scaling and horizon-scale curvature.

An angular scan correlates 2,635 Auger events above 32 EeV with 90 galaxy nuclei, separated into active and quiescent classes. The strongest local excess, reported as 2.79σ, falls to 1.25σ after trials. The authors find no significant class preference. Full text and supplement.

Gap survival, magnetic-flux supply and nuclear escape remain model-dependent; the formal ceiling does not establish an accelerator population or explain its flux.

History

The physical issue is whether a source can supply enough voltage while remaining transparent, rather than merely confine a high-rigidity particle. Boldt and Ghosh, “Cosmic rays from remnants of quasars?” (1999), already proposed horizon-scale acceleration in dormant nuclei, with induced voltage approximately 4.4×1020B4M9 V, where B4=B/(104G) and M9=M/(109M⊙). Their low-luminosity condition was intended to avoid photon losses and shorting of the accelerator.

The present work adds a gravitational ceiling and an explicit modern arrival-direction test to that established proposal. Its much larger formal voltage is not a measured improvement over earlier attainable energies: the magnetic-field assumptions differ. Radiatively inefficient accretion must still provide currents and magnetic flux without screening the gap. A nonsignificant correlation also cannot decide whether these nuclei contribute alongside other source classes. The source-power budget, composition-dependent propagation and self-consistent gap dynamics remain unresolved.

2. JUNO-TAO reports its first reactor antineutrino sample

JUNO Collaboration — “Reactor Antineutrino Detection with JUNO-TAO: Commissioning Performance and First Results.” arXiv:2610.23331v1. Submitted 16 October, 11:32:51 UTC; 19 October listing. Preprint; no journal acceptance listed.

TAO operates a 2.8-ton scintillator target, 43.2 m from a reactor core, at approximately −50°C. Physics data taking began 10 February 2026. Prompt positron–delayed neutron coincidences give candidate rates of 2900±10 per day reactor-on and 419±4 per day reactor-off, using 31.3 and 29.4 days, respectively. The total selection efficiency is (46.6±5.0)%.

Calibration yields approximately 4,000 photoelectrons/MeV after dark-noise/crosstalk corrections and 2.8% energy resolution for central 68Ge events. These are source- and position-specific calibration results, not a universal positron resolution law. Full text, Sections 4–8.

Reactor-off subtraction and a forward-folded response validate the detection chain. The spectrum is still primarily a commissioning result: statistics, fiducial-volume efficiency, nonlinear response and energy leakage limit precision. It is neither a mass-ordering measurement nor a new sterile-neutrino exclusion.

History

TAO's 2020 conceptual design report proposed a high-resolution near-reactor reference spectrum to reduce JUNO's dependence on reactor calculations. Its roughly 4,500 photoelectrons/MeV and sub-percent resolution over much of the energy range were design projections, including energy-dependent effects; they cannot be compared directly with one central gamma calibration point as a simple success/failure ratio. The realized baseline and selections also differ from the original design.

The need for a measured reference is well established. Daya Bay's full-sample result (PRL, 2025), using 4.7 million IBD candidates, measured the total IBD yield as (5.84±0.07)×10−43 cm²/fission and found spectral disagreement with the tested reactor models, including the familiar excess around 5 MeV. This yield includes the IBD interaction cross section; it is not simply neutrinos emitted per fission.

TAO's advance is demonstrated operation of the proposed precision technology and a reactor-correlated sample. A fine-structure reference competitive in systematic control requires more exposure and calibrated response covariance; small calibration widths alone do not provide it.

3. Mapping MeV nuclear lines to test low-energy Galactic cosmic rays

Tomohiko Oka, Thomas Siegert, Tristan Bouchet, Laura Eisenberger, Mika A. Gelowicz, Saurabh Mittal, Rudi Reinhardt, Manuel R. H. W. Skalka, Dimitris Tsatsis, Hiroki Yoneda and Manja Zimmerer — “Three-dimensional spatial modelling of nuclear de-excitation lines.” arXiv:2610.23380v1. Submitted 16 October, 12:36:28 UTC; 19 October listing. Accepted for A&A, according to arXiv.

GALPROP transport plus TALYS nuclear reactions produce sky maps of cosmic-ray-induced lines, especially 4.439 MeV carbon and 6.129 MeV oxygen. Additional low-energy ions can enhance the central signal: concentrated models reach approximately 10−6 photons cm⁻² s⁻¹ within 2° of the Galactic centre. Instrument-response/background simulations are still needed to establish detectability. Full text.

The bright models demand 7×1042–1043 erg/s of injected cosmic-ray power, versus approximately 1041 erg/s in the conventional Galactic budget. This is a major physical difficulty. The imposed 20% allowance relative to the local baseline proton flux is a modeling selection, not a confidence limit; metallicity, gas and cross sections add uncertainty.

History

Low-energy ions efficiently ionize gas but are difficult to reconstruct from local particle data because of transport and heliospheric effects. Their collisions excite nuclei, making gamma lines a complementary probe of the ion population.

Benhabiles-Mezhoud et al. (2013; 2012 preprint) already connected cloud ionization to these lines. For their extended inner-Galaxy region they predicted (0.1–2.0)×10−5 photons cm⁻² s⁻¹ in the 4.4-MeV line, with substantial cosmic-ray and metallicity uncertainty; they found existing SPI sensitivity inadequate. That was a prediction, not a detection or bound on the ion population.

The new contribution is a three-dimensional spatial treatment and explicit comparison with the spatially varying continuum. Its small central aperture and added cosmic-ray distribution differ from the older integrated calculation, so the fluxes are not an improvement-factor comparison. Moreover, modern ionization-rate reassessments alter the normalization problem. Spatially localized acceleration and transport may be more realistic than a smooth extra population, but must be tested against both line brightness and available power.

4. The 20 GeV halo residual survives some foreground tests, but its dark-matter interpretation weakens

Kevork N. Abazajian, Youyou Li and Oscar Macias — “Foreground dependence of the 20 GeV Galactic halo gamma-ray excess and its proposed connection to the LUX-ZEPLIN event.” arXiv:2610.23713v1. Submitted 16 October, 16:34:04 UTC; 19 October listing. Preprint; no acceptance listed.

The authors reanalyze 15 years of Fermi-LAT data with 281 diffuse configurations, alternative bubble/Loop-I templates and finer, response-folded bins. Outside the original Fermi-bubble mask, nominal halo-template significances of 4–16σ become approximately 1–7σ after enlarged boundaries are excluded. These are conditional template significances, not a global dark-matter significance.

Independent sky regions do not support one common NFW normalization. Simulated foreground mismatches can create substantial halo-correlated emission, although they do not reproduce every observed spectral feature. A 420 GeV W+W− benchmark fits some foreground choices and fails others; the full LZ model/likelihood is not fitted. Full text, Sections III–VI and validation appendices.

This weakens attribution of the entire residual to a smooth annihilation halo without excluding a smaller dark-matter contribution.

History

For annihilation, the intensity scales as ⟨σv⟩J/mχ2, with J=∫ρ2ds. A template-shaped residual therefore does not determine particle physics without a density model and reliable foreground separation.

Totani's original analysis (JCAP, 2025) favored approximately 0.5–0.8 TeV, bb¯ annihilation with ⟨σv⟩~(5–8)×10−25 cm³/s. These were conditional best fits, not exclusion limits. Cholis, Hu and Zhong (7 October 2026 preprint) instead modeled bubble substructure as the origin. The new paper supports a major foreground role while finding additional residuals in some fits; differing templates, fit freedoms and data selections matter.

Galactic-centre constraints and dwarf-galaxy analyses independently test annihilation. Comparing them requires the same channel, velocity dependence, density/J-factor treatment and dark-matter abundance; this reanalysis supplies no new universal exclusion curve or improvement factor.

Separately, LZ's extended-recoil search reported one 248 ± 23(stat) ± 23(sys) keV recoil candidate, observed 16 June 2023, with 2.6σ global background-only tension. Its 2026 reporting date is not its event date. A shared mass scale or an acceptable gamma spectral fit does not establish a common origin or combine their significances.

Science News

1. GRB 261017A: hard prompt emission and a possible merger origin, still unconfirmed

Event: 17 October, 16:05:05 UTC; updates through 18 October, 23:24 UTC. Swift and Fermi detected the burst. GBM reports T90≃9.2 s in 50–300 keV, Epeak=1024±22 keV, and preliminary 10–1000 keV fluence (1.22±0.03)×10−5 erg/cm². GCN 45974, 45976.

Refined BAT analysis instead finds T90=89.36±4.78 s in 15–350 keV, including weak late pulses; its quoted uncertainties are 90%. These durations use different bands and sensitivities and should not be silently interchanged. A reported minimum pulse width of 31−8+11 ms motivates a merger interpretation, but does not classify the progenitor by itself. GCN 45985, 45984.

The enhanced XRT position is RA 139.90347°, Dec +12.63779°, radius 2.5 arcsec at 90%. No confirmed redshift, kilonova or GW association is established in the checked circulars; no calibrated progenitor probability is supplied. GCN 45977.

2. GRB 261010A: the tentative Keck infrared source is retracted

Correction published 17 October, 18:13 UTC; observations approximately 5.4 days after the 10 October burst. Re-reduction removes the previously reported >3σ MOSFIRE source. The revised result is J>23.5 AB at 3σ, replacing the tentative detection. It provides no positive infrared evidence for a kilonova. Retraction, GCN 45975; original report, 45957.

VLA observations at 10 GHz, mid-time 15 October, 23:40 UTC, give <20 μJy at 3σ, reported 16 October. The radio luminosity interpretation depends strongly on which proposed host/redshift is adopted; this flux limit alone does not settle the association or progenitor. GCN 45961.

3. EP261015a: tentative optical fading, and the separate FXT source is probably a quasar

X-ray event: 15 October, 13:15:36 UTC; optical update: 17 October, 16:03 UTC. WXT found a roughly 130-s event with 3-arcmin, 90% localization. NOT photometry gives r=23.18±0.10 AB at 17 October, 02:20:22 UTC, versus revised earlier r=22.64±0.12. The authors explicitly cannot assign a reliable fading significance because image-subtraction systematics are omitted. GCN 45956, 45972.

FXT finds no source at the optical candidate, with 0.5–10 keV flux <1.33×10−13 erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ at 90%. Its separate X-ray source is positionally consistent with a spectroscopically confirmed quasar and is likely unrelated. The optical transient remains a candidate counterpart; an unrelated transient is not excluded. GCN 45969, 45972.

4. EP261015b: a stellar flare is favored, with competing X-ray sources in the field

Discovery observation: 15 October, 13:56:38 UTC; follow-up reports: 17–18 October. FXT detects two sources, each with approximately 10-arcsec, 90% error radius. One lies 2.04 arcsec from the high-proper-motion star UCAC4 605-000101; its thermal fit gives kT=1.44−0.25+0.77 keV, with 90% errors. The other field contains quasars. The EP team favors a stellar flare. GCN 45958, 45971.

NOT images with mid-time 17 October, 00:00:59 UTC find the star at its catalogued brightness and no additional transient to r>24.5 AB at 3σ. A late optical non-detection is compatible with a faded stellar flare but does not confirm it. The quasar-containing X-ray localization should not be treated as an established extragalactic counterpart. GCN 45983.

5. EP261015c: soft spectra and fading favor a stellar flare

Event: 15 October, 17:37:52 UTC; refined interpretation: 18 October, 08:34 UTC. FXT follow-up beginning 17 October, 17:08:30 UTC detects a source at RA 21.9613°, Dec +66.6144°, radius 10 arcsec at 90%. Three Gaia stars lie in the error circle. A thermal-plasma fit gives kT=1.5−0.2+0.3 keV; soft spectra and an approximately two-order-of-magnitude flux decline motivate the stellar-flare interpretation. No unique star is securely identified. GCN 45959, 45981.

Source correction: GCN 45981's opening sentence says “EP261015b,” but its title, event field, cited discovery circular and onset time identify EP261015c. The interpretation above follows those matching identifiers. The quoted hydrogen-column value also lacks an explicit unit scale, so it is not propagated here.

Science Radar

1. AI agents learn from wet-lab failures—but the aptamer result is a feasibility study

Technical report dated 12 October; publicly highlighted by Google Research on 16 October 2026. Zachary Cutts and colleagues' Aptamers as a sandbox for lab-in-the-loop agentic molecular discovery tests three design–experiment cycles against two proteins, using pooled DNA candidates, bead-binding assays and sequencing feedback. Reported bead-corrected hit rates rise 0 → 26.0 → 42.1% for α-thrombin and 10.2 → 3.7 → 33.5% for myoglobin. The temporary decline exposes nonspecific “sticky” binding rather than useful affinity. Primary technical report; 16 October announcement.

Single-clone tests find preliminary 24.6–133 nM apparent dissociation constants for modified literature-derived motifs; strictly de novo scaffolds show KD>1 μM. The nanomolar values lack a fully saturated titration plateau. They should not be conflated with the separate protein-binder results in the announcement.

The agent system changes between rounds, and matched-budget no-feedback/non-reasoning controls are absent. This demonstrates a workable empirical feedback loop, not isolated causal proof that agent reasoning outperforms other search methods. Human researchers executed the wet-lab work; peer review is not established by this technical report.

2. Chemical experiments expose blind spots in AI protein–ligand predictions

Peer-reviewed, open-access Nature Chemical Biology article, published 16 October 2026. Rachel E. Hayward, Raymond F. Berkeley and colleagues vary chemical groups distant from a probe's reactive site and measure protein engagement in human cells. They identify 95 stereoselectively liganded cysteines across a survey of more than 24,000 cysteines. The result shows how modest chemical changes can open a different landscape of protein targets. Original research, DOI 10.1038/s41589-026-02348-1.

One compound engages GRPEL1 C124, disrupts its association with the mitochondrial chaperone HSPA9, and impairs protein import and respiration. Inactive mirror-image compounds and a C124 mutant provide mechanistic controls.

Boltz-2 cofolding often places probes in familiar binding pockets instead of the experimentally engaged sites and does not reliably reproduce stereochemical preferences. Correctly preserving input stereochemistry in a later version does not fix the site-placement problem. These tests concern this chemical series and prediction setup, not every task for structure models. The compounds are research tools demonstrated in cells and protein assays; the study does not establish a therapeutic treatment.
