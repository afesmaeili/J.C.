ArXiv Highlights

1. LHAASO searches for ultra-relativistic magnetic monopoles

Zhen Cao et al. — arXiv:2610.30968v1. Submitted 22 October, 09:13 UTC. Experimental null result.

Using 1,438.81 live days of full-array KM2A data, the authors seek the compact, muon-poor air showers produced by monopoles. A neural classifier combines shower morphology; a likelihood fit uses data control regions for the background and profiles normalization and shape uncertainties.

For one Dirac magnetic charge and reference mass 105 GeV, the observed 90% confidence CLs flux limits are:

Lorentz factor γ Flux limit, cm−2s−1sr−1
105 2.6×10−16
106 2.24×10−18
107 8.8×10−19

There is no significant excess. Transferring these limits to other masses requires negligible atmospheric deceleration and the assumed energy-loss regime. Shower simulation and the portability of background control templates remain important dependencies. Methods, flux table and supplement.

History

IceCube's relativistic-monopole search covered speeds 0.75<β<0.995, with 90% limits below 2×10−19 over much of that interval. Auger's ultra-relativistic search reached approximately 10−19 at γ=109, improving to 2.5×10−21 at 1012, also at 90% confidence. Those numbers concern different speeds and detector responses; they cannot be ranked against KM2A without aligning the physical regime. The advance is coverage between existing search regimes, not a universal strongest monopole bound. Likewise, the Parker argument concerns survival of Galactic magnetic fields and is an astrophysical model constraint, not an experimental confidence interval.

2. A possible pulsar-wind nebula near HESS J1702–420A

Hui Yang, Yichao Lin, Jeremy Hare, Seth Gagnon and Oleg Kargaltsev — arXiv:2610.30378v1. Submitted 21 October, 19:40 UTC; submitted to A&A. Observational candidate identification.

50.42 ks of Chandra observations resolve the previously extended XMM source into a faint point source, CXO J170144.5−421415, and asymmetric diffuse lobes. The combined diffuse emission has photon index 1.8±0.5 and observed 0.5–7 keV flux (1.5±0.1)×10−13 ergcm−2s−1; quoted spectral uncertainties are 68% intervals.

The morphology and spectrum support a pulsar/pulsar-wind-nebula interpretation. However, the point source contains only 17 photons, no pulsations establish its identity, and its roughly seven-arcminute displacement from the TeV centroid complicates association. Distance and motion assumptions enter a relic-nebula scenario. This is a useful counterpart candidate, not proof that HESS J1702–420A is leptonic or hadronic. Imaging, spectroscopy and interpretation.

History

The H.E.S.S. component analysis isolated a hard component extending toward 100 TeV. In a hadronic interpretation, its proton cutoff exceeded 0.5 PeV at 95% confidence; this was conditional on the radiation model, not direct identification of accelerated protons. Giunti et al.'s 2022 XMM study found no clear counterpart, constrained diffuse synchrotron emission and identified an extended X-ray source with uncertain association. Chandra now provides the spatial detail needed to examine that source. The historical ambiguity persists: high-energy gamma rays establish an extreme accelerator, while morphology, magnetic field and particle-population assumptions determine whether electrons or hadrons produce the emission.

3. Einstein Probe directions in archival IceCube data: the timing matters

Sri Devaki Meduri and Shantanu Desai — arXiv:2610.30722v1. Submitted 22 October, 04:43 UTC. Archival position-based null search.

An unbinned spatial-and-energy likelihood searches the directions of 27 spectroscopically confirmed fast X-ray transients. The largest test statistic is 4.77, at EP240801a, with local p=0.04; this is not significant across 27 targets. The strongest reported 90% flux bound is E2Φ=2.1×10−10 GeVcm−2s−1 for an E−2 spectrum toward EP241021a.

The decisive limitation is temporal: IceTracks-DR2 covers 6 April 2008–23 May 2022, whereas the Einstein Probe events occurred in 2024–2026. The data therefore cannot test neutrinos emitted during those observed bursts. The result concerns earlier, time-integrated emission at those sky positions, potentially relevant to persistent hosts. It must not be presented as a prompt-burst fluence limit or a direct exclusion of choked jets in the observed transients. Dataset and analysis.

History

The IceCube DR2 release supplies a long, well-characterized track sample for source searches, but its publication in 2026 does not extend its observing period to 2026. Genuine transient tests, exemplified by IceCube's GRB searches, align neutrino observations with prompt, precursor or delayed emission windows and calibrate the associated trials. Archival positional and time-coincident searches answer different questions. This distinction also blocks a numerical comparison between an average flux in GeVcm−2s−1 and a burst fluence in GeVcm−2 without specifying duration and emission history. A contemporaneous EP analysis remains necessary.

4. Natural chlorine could extend deep-sea supernova-neutrino reach

Ruike Cao, Iwan Morton-Blake, Donglian Xu et al. — arXiv:2610.30754v1. Submitted 22 October, 05:22 UTC. Detector simulation with laboratory validation.

After inverse beta decay, ν¯e+p→e++n, natural chlorine offers a brighter delayed neutron-capture signature: an approximately 8.6 MeV gamma cascade, compared with hydrogen's 2.2 MeV photon. Prompt–delayed coincidences help distinguish signal from optical backgrounds.

For a 10 km³ TRIDENT configuration, a one-second, nonoscillated 27-solar-mass supernova benchmark gives a simulated 5σ horizon increasing from roughly 50 to beyond 100 kpc. This is model-dependent sensitivity, not demonstrated operating-detector reach.

A 0.5-ton underground tank with an AmBe source tests the capture tag: the NaCl configuration gives 3.4σ evidence and capture time 0.14±0.04 ms. AmBe supplies neutrons and gammas, not a detected antineutrino burst. Time-dependent ocean bioluminescence is not modeled, and source spectra, oscillations and detector performance affect the forecast. Simulation, background treatment and tank experiment.

History

Salt-assisted neutron detection has a strong precedent: SNO's salt phase dissolved NaCl in heavy water to improve neutral-current neutron capture and separate the total active solar-neutrino flux. That measured solar flux is a different observable from a supernova detection horizon. Meanwhile, IceCube's supernova method detects a collective rise in optical-module rates despite its sparse geometry. Here the interesting combination is seawater's existing chlorine, a multi-photomultiplier optical module and a delayed coincidence tag. Comparing horizon numbers across SNO, IceCube and TRIDENT would require matched supernova spectra, integration windows, significance definitions and backgrounds. The next decisive validation is sustained operation under realistic ocean noise.

5. Imperfect axions give nuclear clocks a linear signal

Salvatore Bottaro, Andrea Caputo, Gilad Perez, Yotam Soreq and Alfredo Stanzione — arXiv:2610.30238v1. Submitted 21 October, 18:00 UTC. Phenomenology and sensitivity projections.

Explicit Peccei–Quinn breaking or additional CP violation can generate a residual angle θeff. The proposed thorium-229 clock response is

δνν=K[2x*θeffδafa+xπδ(a2)fa2].

Here a is the axion field, fa its decay constant, K~104–105 the nuclear enhancement and xπ≃0.007 a hadronic response coefficient. The linear term oscillates at the axion Compton frequency; the quadratic term at twice that frequency.

Quark chromo-electric dipoles also alter the response directly, so x* is not universally xπ. Hadronic uncertainties and electric-dipole-moment constraints matter; enlarged residual angles can require cancellations. Dark-matter projections assume a local axion abundance, while the Earth-sourced static-field proposal does not. Projected 10−19 clock precision is not achieved sensitivity. Matching, benchmarks and appendices.

History

Kim and Perez developed the quadratic axion-induced variation of hadronic and atomic energies, including nuclear-clock prospects. Plakkot et al. already examined CP-violating axions, oscillating constants, fifth forces and EDM constraints; linear sensitivity is therefore not an entirely new principle. Delaunay et al. developed nuclear clocks as probes of new forces. The present contribution connects these strands through the enhanced thorium hadronic response and explicit CP-violating matching. Its strongest forecasts should be compared at fixed axion mass, density, CP-violating operator and EDM assumptions. Otherwise a nominal sensitivity improvement can simply reflect changing the underlying model.

6. Supernova thermal gravitons at an extraordinary frequency

Xun-Jie Xu, Yong Xu and Qiqin Yin — arXiv:2610.30984v1. Submitted 22 October, 09:32 UTC. Theoretical emission benchmark.

The authors calculate thermal graviton production in a hot, degenerate electron–proton plasma, integrating collision terms with Pauli blocking over a benchmark supernova profile. Electron/proton photoproduction, electron bremsstrahlung and electron–positron annihilation contribute.

The modeled luminosity is 1.27×1026 W, with the spectrum peaking near 0.15 GeV, or 4×1022 Hz. Photoproduction supplies about 87% of the power, whereas bremsstrahlung supplies about 64% of the graviton number. Relativistic kinematics and electron degeneracy make this more than a temperature rescaling of a solar calculation.

This is a plasma/profile benchmark, not a measured supernova signal or a demonstrated detector opportunity. The authors state that no proposed high-frequency detector reaches the required sensitivity. Their optimistic magnetic-conversion discussion counts photons incident on the whole Earth, not on an instrument. Collision terms, spectra and detectability.

History

García-Cely and Ringwald's solar calculation, first posted in 2024 and revised in 2025, assembled the thermal solar spectrum including plasma effects and macroscopic hydrodynamic fluctuations. It already placed the signal far below proposed helioscope sensitivities. The supernova study extends this microscopic framework into relativistic, degenerate matter, where the dominant channel near the peak changes. These thermal gravitons occupy a radically different frequency band from coherent stellar-collapse signals sought by interferometers. A larger emitted luminosity alone does not imply greater detectability: distance, spectral distribution, conversion probability and detector response must all enter the comparison.

Science News

IceCube-261020A: no significant additional-neutrino excess

Issued 22 October, 22:24 UTC, GCN 46011 reports follow-up of the 20 October, 03:58:53 UTC alert. Excluding the alerting event, the ±500-second search finds no additional tracks in its 90% containment region, with p=1.00. A two-day window gives p=0.21.

For an E−2.5 spectrum, the circular quotes sensitivities of 0.14 and 0.17 GeV cm⁻², respectively, expressed as E2dN/dE at 1 TeV. Despite the circular's title, these numbers are explicitly labeled sensitivities in its body and should not be relabeled observed upper limits. This updates yesterday's alert coverage without establishing a source association.

NuSTAR reports an unclassified 830-second hard-X-ray transient

Announced 22 October, 16:56 UTC, GCN 46010 describes an event observed 20 October, 13:07:42.5 UTC. Its position is RA 334.36690°, Dec −44.941794°, with a quoted 7-arcsecond uncertainty. The reported 3–79 keV flux is 1.9−1.2+2.7×10−10 ergcm−2s−1; the circular does not specify the confidence level of these errors.

The source remains unclassified and follow-up is requested. Its duration and hard-X-ray detection alone do not establish a GRB, stellar flare, compact-object origin or connection with an unrelated alert.

EP261015a: new multiband measurements of the optical candidate

GCN 46009 v2, issued 22 October, 15:15 UTC and revised 16:29 UTC, reports simultaneous MuSCAT3 observations taken 20 October, 14:06–14:29 UTC, approximately five days after the X-ray event.

After archival-template subtraction, the preliminary AB magnitudes are g=23.51±0.22 and r=22.26±0.06, with i>22.34 and z>22.14 at 5σ. The derived color is g−r=1.25±0.23, assuming independent reported errors. This adds spectral information to yesterday's optical coverage; interpreting the red color requires extinction and subtraction checks. The measurements alone do not settle the transient's progenitor.

Science Radar

A current-driven molecular rotor changes its own conductance

Anil Kumar Singh et al., Nature, published 22 October 2026 — DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-11312-x. Peer-reviewed, accepted early version.

A helicene molecule suspended between electrode tips displays cyclic conductance behavior whose direction reverses with voltage polarity. The authors' calculations attribute this to current-induced torque and predict a transition from stepwise to continuous rotation. Above a voltage threshold, rotation accesses more conductive configurations; stretching or compressing the junction changes the onset.

The interest is the feedback between electrical transport and molecular motion. The reported perfect directional preference applies to the studied regime, not arbitrary junctions or operating conditions. Access limitation: this assessment uses the accessible accepted-article abstract/preview; the full experimental methods were not independently assessed here. It is an intriguing molecular-machine result, not evidence of a deployable motor technology.

Protein design from natural-language instructions

Fengyuan Dai, Shiyang You et al., Nature, published 21 October 2026 — DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-11205-z. Peer-reviewed; catch-up item, not a 23 October publication.

Pinal is a 16-billion-parameter model trained on 1.7 billion synthetically annotated protein–text pairs. The paper reports experimental function in four design tasks: a fluorescent protein, PET hydrolase, alcohol dehydrogenase and metabolic H-protein. A designed H-protein increased product titre 1.7-fold relative to the corresponding native E. coli protein in a multienzyme CO₂-fixation pathway, without iterative experimental optimization.

Synthetic annotations are not 1.7 billion experimentally validated functions. Likewise, the pathway result is not a 1.7-fold improvement in whole-system carbon-capture efficiency. Access limitation: the published abstract/preview supports these demonstrations, but does not suffice to independently judge all candidate-selection steps, failure rates or generalization. The result supports language-guided candidate generation; it does not yet establish reliable design of arbitrary requested functions.
