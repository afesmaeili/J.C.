01HAWC, Fermi-LAT and Chandra Detections of High Energy Particles in the S106 Star Forming RegionRishi Babu, Stephen DiKerby, Tulun Ergin, Shuo Zhang, Mehr Un Nisa and the HAWC Collaboration

1. S106: gamma rays do not uniquely identify a proton accelerator

“HAWC, Fermi-LAT and Chandra Detections of High Energy Particles in the S106 Star Forming Region” — Rishi Babu, Stephen DiKerby, Tulun Ergin, Shuo Zhang, Mehr Un Nisa and the HAWC Collaboration (arXiv author listing; the PDF gives the full collaboration roster). arXiv:2610.41163v1, PDF. Submitted 30 October 2026, 02:38:48 UTC; listed 2 November; primary category astro-ph.HE. Observational analysis plus competing emission models; preprint.

Can a star-forming region's very-high-energy gamma rays be produced by electrons, or do they require accelerated protons and accompanying neutrinos? The authors combine approximately 2,900 days of HAWC data, Fermi-LAT observations from 4 August 2008 to 20 July 2024, and a 45 ks archival Chandra exposure of Sharpless 106, at an adopted distance of 1.4 kpc. They fit neighboring sources and Galactic diffuse emission, then model the broadband spectrum with synchrotron radiation, bremsstrahlung, inverse-Compton scattering and, alternatively, pion decay.

The HAWC fit using a Planck dust spatial template gives TS = 31 and photon index 2.61 ± 0.11 statistical, with systematic uncertainty +0.21/−0.19. The fitted differential flux at 7 TeV is 4.8 (+1.1/−0.9 statistical; +2.3/−1.4 systematic) × 10⁻¹⁵ TeV⁻¹ cm⁻² s⁻¹. These are the reported fit errors, not a separately calibrated confidence interval for source association. The Fermi analysis favors an extended source, with Gaussian radius 0.39° ± 0.07° and a 200 MeV–1 TeV photon index 2.18 ± 0.06.

The important modeling ingredient is S106's own infrared radiation:

uIR(r)=Ldust4πr2c≃60(rpc)−2eVcm−3.

With gas density fixed at 250 cm⁻³, one electron population can reproduce the broadband spectrum. Its representative fit has B = 2.38 μG, electron cutoff 1,753 TeV, electron energy above 1 GeV 4.11 × 10⁴⁵ erg, and characteristic inverse-Compton distance 3.55 pc. These correlated model values are not independent measurements or confidence limits. Figure 7 demonstrates spectral compatibility, not a unique identification of the particles.

A hybrid model also works: its magnetic field is about 18.2 μG, with a proton component carrying about 3.1 × 10⁴⁶ erg above 1 GeV. The proton cutoff reaches the imposed 1,000 TeV upper boundary. A boundary-hitting fit is not a measured cutoff, and does not by itself establish a proton PeVatron.

The diffuse-background treatment matters: using the unmasked diffuse template gives a reported 4.8σ post-trial result, whereas the adopted analysis masks the dense S106 cloud out of that background and fits its contribution separately. The electron-only fit also uses a field below the 10–500 μG range cited for the core; different emission zones may explain this, but that spatial interpretation is not independently established. Unresolved stellar X-rays can contaminate the nominal diffuse component, and the Chandra snapshot is not contemporaneous with the long gamma-ray integrations. No neutrino detection from S106 is reported.

History

Giardino, Favata and Micela (2004), astro-ph/0406344, already analyzed the 3 November 2001 Chandra observation, identifying 87 X-ray sources within 4.4 arcmin and examining the central young massive object. The new paper reuses this exposure but masks resolved sources to investigate diffuse emission. Its contribution is the broadband connection to GeV–TeV emission, rather than discovery of S106's X-ray activity. The earlier source census makes unresolved-source contamination a concrete concern.

Aharonian, Yang and de Oña Wilhelmi (2019), arXiv:1804.02331v2, argued for hadronic emission around massive clusters using spatially resolved gamma rays and gas maps. Their approximately 1/r cosmic-ray density profiles supported sustained injection; cooling and the absence of a central inverse-Compton brightening disfavored electrons for those extended systems. Their argument was morphological and source-specific, not a theorem that every star-forming region must be hadronic.

S106 therefore does not overturn the cluster evidence. It illustrates why a compact region with a strong local infrared field needs its own transport and radiation model. The decisive unresolved issue is whether a physically consistent spatial model can reproduce the X-ray, gas and gamma-ray morphologies together. The current spectral degeneracy leaves both electron-dominated and hybrid interpretations open.

02Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star MergersRyan J. Foley

2. “Missing mergers”: a long-delay claim driven by one old host

“Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star Mergers” — Ryan J. Foley. arXiv:2610.40782v1, PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:10 UTC; listed 2 November; primary category astro-ph.HE. Population inference under restrictive assumptions; preprint.

The delay-time distribution, or DTD, describes the interval between star formation and a binary-neutron-star merger. For gravitational-wave inspiral, the strong dependence of merger time on orbital separation motivates a broad distribution close to t⁻¹. GW170817's host, NGC 4993, instead presents a particularly old stellar population.

Foley fits ultraviolet-to-infrared photometry and an integrated VLT/MUSE spectrum with a 20-bin, nonparametric star-formation history. The nominal model uses a Chabrier initial mass function, MIST stellar tracks, MILES spectra and a continuity prior between adjacent star-formation bins, while fitting metallicity, dust and nuisance parameters. It yields a mass-weighted stellar age of 12.4 (+0.5/−0.7) Gyr, at 90% posterior credibility. This is a host-population inference, not a direct clock reading for the merging binary.

The population model is

DTD(t)=Atα,tmin≤t≤13.68Gyr.

It combines the host history with a cosmic star-formation history and two GW-detected BNS events, GW170817 and GW190425. Only the first supplies host-age information. The nominal priors are uniform in −10 < α < 0 and 0.01 < t_min < 13.45 Gyr; the maximum age is fixed. Metallicity-dependent binary formation is ignored.

Under the paper's formation-efficiency normalization treatment, marginalizing the normalization and slope gives t_min > 7.5 Gyr at 90% credibility and >5.6 Gyr at 95%, with approximately 0.2 Gyr run-to-run scatter in each threshold. That scatter is not an additional observational confidence interval. A fixed model with (t_min, α) = (12.6 Gyr, −1) has a normalization-marginalized likelihood 40.6 ± 9.4 times that of (10 Myr, −1) in the efficiency treatment, or 138 ± 32 in the rate treatment. These are comparisons of specified DTD shapes, not Bayes factors integrated over their shape parameters or probabilities that a formation channel is correct.

A crucial limitation appears in the likelihood itself. In the rate-normalized treatment, the Poisson count, surveyed volume-time and rate prior factor out of the DTD-shape inference. Its remaining dependence is proportional to

∫tmintunivSFH4993(t)tαdt∫tmintunivψcosmic(t)tαdt.

The posterior then accumulates at its upper prior boundary. Changing to the efficiency treatment changes the implied shape prior and produces the quoted finite bounds. Thus the result is driven chiefly by one host relative to cosmic star formation, rather than by several independently dated mergers. The stellar fit also leaves abundance-ratio systematics unmodeled, and the paper acknowledges that a mixture of formation channels is more plausible than its single truncated power law. “Missing mergers” is a model-dependent tension, not evidence that LVK overlooked a measured population.

History

Zevin et al. (2022), arXiv:2206.02814v2, inferred a DTD from 68 short-GRB hosts, obtaining α = −1.83 (+0.35/−0.39) and t_min = 184 (+67/−79) Myr, both 90% credible intervals. They marginalized host-history and DTD uncertainties, fixed the binary formation efficiency, and assumed detection selection did not depend on DTD parameters. Their minimum-delay prior covered 5–500 Myr. Faint hosts, large offsets and afterglow selection were explicit limitations. Those results concern a selected GRB population and cannot be translated directly into a universal BNS bound.

An independent, more recent comparison is Pracchia and Salafia, arXiv:2601.03861v2, revised 5 August 2026. Their hierarchical population analysis uses 210 flux-selected Fermi short bursts and a joint Fermi/Swift sample of 18 bursts, 16 with redshifts, explicitly modeling detection and redshift-selection cuts. Both empirical luminosity-function and structured-jet treatments favor short delays. Their power-law models give mean-delay medians of 77 Myr and 105 Myr, respectively, with broad 90% intervals extending to hundreds of Myr. These are population means, not minimum-delay bounds, and their slope convention has the opposite sign to Foley's.

The new paper identifies a potentially important conflict between an old nearby GW host and selected cosmological GRBs. It does not yet resolve that conflict: host-age modeling, selection, neutron-star–black-hole contamination of the GRB sample, and multiple BNS channels remain alternatives. The new conditional lower bound is numerically much larger than earlier estimates, but calling that an “improvement” would be misleading because the populations, priors and likelihoods differ.

03Hints of self-interacting dark matter from strong gravitational lensingDaniel Gilman, Kalliopi Petraki, Thomas Pignard, James Gurian, Alex Kusenko, Anna Nierenberg

3. Quasar lenses favor collapsed SIDM halos—but not a unique particle model

“Hints of self-interacting dark matter from strong gravitational lensing” — Daniel Gilman, Kalliopi Petraki, Thomas Pignard, James Gurian, Alex Kusenko, Anna Nierenberg. arXiv:2610.40766v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:06 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category astro-ph.CO. Lensing inference and particle-model interpretation. Preprint submitted to Physical Review Letters.

The paper interprets 29 quadruply imaged quasars using gravothermal collapse in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). Both long- and short-mean-free-path heat conduction are included: very strong scattering can slow collapse rather than accelerate it indefinitely. The example particle model contains symmetric Dirac dark matter and a light scalar mediator.

The relevant statistic is the viscosity cross section, σV=(3/2)∫sin2θ(dσ/dΩ)dΩ, averaged over the relevant particle populations, and σ10=σV(10kms−1)/mχ. Two halo prescriptions peak near 1,000 and 200 cm² g⁻¹; they differ in subhalo abundance, inner slope and tidal enhancement. With αχ fixed to 10⁻³, likelihood features favor mχ ≈ 20–80 GeV and mφ below 20 MeV. These are conditional preferred regions, not standalone confidence bounds.

The 8:1–24:1 Bayes factors compare specified collapsed-halo scenarios with their CDM reference. They do not independently exclude CDM, and the underlying lensing data are reused from a companion analysis. Cluster-scale scattering penalties and thermal-relic/kinetic-decoupling requirements are imposed inputs. Halo evolution and abundance assumptions remain decisive; the figures demonstrate this dependence rather than a unique particle solution.

History

Gravothermal SIDM can first form cores and later dense collapsed centers. The direct observational predecessor is the companion lensing analysis, 2609.35974v2, not an independent second data set.

Independent constraints deserve comparison. Andrade et al., 2012.06611, inferred σ/m < 0.13 cm² g⁻¹ at 95% confidence from eight cluster lenses at characteristic relative speed about 1,458 km s⁻¹, conditional on their cluster-evolution model. Ando et al., 2503.13650v1, used eight classical and 23 ultrafaint dwarfs, Jeans modeling and SIDM subhalo evolution. With a log-uniform cross-section normalization prior, their 95th-percentile limits on σ₀/m are 0.8 cm² g⁻¹ at fixed turnover speed w = 10 km s⁻¹, or 0.04 cm² g⁻¹ for velocity-independent scattering; a high-cross-section mode can reappear for smaller w. Those normalization definitions, velocity dependences and galaxy-formation assumptions differ from σ₁₀ here. Potential dwarf-scale tension remains important, but a universal improvement factor or model-independent exclusion would be unjustified.

04When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisationDaniela Breitman

4. When a good 21-cm fit is a bad astrophysical model

“When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation” — Daniela Breitman. arXiv:2610.40770v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:08 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category astro-ph.CO. Statistical diagnostic tested on forecasts and galaxy data. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

Breitman tests whether disagreement between subsets of data can reveal missing astrophysics despite an acceptable overall fit. The mock signal includes atomic- and molecular-cooling galaxies; an intentionally incomplete 21cmEMU inference model includes only the former. Bayesian suspiciousness removes an information-volume term from the evidence ratio: logS=logR−(DA+DB−DAB), with D the posterior-to-prior Kullback–Leibler divergence.

For a 1,080-hour SKA mock, the incomplete model fits the power spectrum while shifting the logarithmic X-ray luminosity per star-formation rate from injected 40.5 to approximately 41.5, and the escape-fraction slope from 0 to −0.5. A representative log S = −7.45 is more extreme than all 150 calibration mocks, giving a calibrated lower significance of 2.72σ; stronger extrapolations depend on distributional assumptions. In another test, suspiciousness flags 48/50 misspecified fits at a 5% threshold, compared with 15/50 for the chosen χ² diagnostic.

Splitting JWST luminosity functions below and above z = 10 yields roughly 5σ tension within the restricted galaxy model and Gaussian calibration, falling to 2.1σ after doubling high-redshift errors. This is not a ΛCDM falsification. Cosmic variance, interlopers, Eddington bias, emulator error and partition selection matter. The relevant setup fixes σ₈ = 0.8118 and samples astrophysical parameters with stated uniform or informative priors. HERA upper limits offer much weaker consistency discrimination.

History

Handley and Lemos, 1902.04029, introduced suspiciousness to reduce arbitrary prior-volume dependence in Bayesian consistency tests. It is not a posterior probability that a model is true. Its invariance to prior-width changes assumes that those changes leave the posteriors essentially unchanged. The advance here is controlled calibration for 21-cm model misspecification and a galaxy-data application, not a new statistic. The false-alarm interpretation remains conditional on the simulated model and tested partition; searching many partitions creates an additional selection issue.

05Leaking Sound Waves: Gravitational Waves From Dark Sectors Weakly Coupled to the Standard ModelMarco Finetti, Helena Kolešová, Daniil Krichevskiy

5. Hidden-sector sound waves can transfer their energy to visible matter

“Leaking Sound Waves: Gravitational Waves From Dark Sectors Weakly Coupled to the Standard Model” — Marco Finetti, Helena Kolešová, Daniil Krichevskiy. arXiv:2610.40781v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:10 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category gr-qc. Phenomenological gravitational-wave prediction. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

The usual acoustic gravitational-wave calculation treats the fluid excited by a first-order transition as effectively isolated. This work couples two ultrarelativistic fluids through an energy-momentum exchange current. For common temperature and sound speed squared 1/3, the relative velocity decays while the total-fluid mode survives. The assumed interaction time is longer than bubble growth but can be shorter than the acoustic-source lifetime.

The unequal-time gravitational-wave source acquires factors F(τ)=[1+(wv/wd)e−2ΓΔτ]/[1+wv/wd], with w the enthalpy density and Γ the interaction rate. For benchmark dark transition strength 0.1, wall speed 0.9c, visible/dark enthalpy ratio 10, and Γ/H = 100, the peak power can fall to about 4% of the isolated-fluid result for acoustic lifetime 0.1 H⁻¹; a lifetime of 0.01 H⁻¹ gives a ratio around 0.3. These are model examples, not measured suppression or confidence limits.

The infrared k³ behavior survives, but the spectral modification is not generally one constant rescaling. Γ remains phenomenological. No full nonlinear two-fluid transition simulation or model-independent portal-coupling bound is supplied; a particle model must also explain why its interactions did not already change bubble growth.

History

The sound-shell model of Hindmarsh and Hijazi, 1909.10040, derives acoustic spectra from velocity correlations and bubble-scale flow profiles, controlled by transition strength, wall speed and bubble separation. Its limitations from nonlinear evolution and particular flow regimes were already recognized. The new ingredient is post-transition energy exchange between fluids. This modifies the isolated-source assumption, rather than invalidating acoustic production or establishing the same suppression for every hidden-sector transition.

06Model-independent and reconstruction-free evidence for cosmic acceleration from DESI baryon acoustic oscillationsLaura Herold, Graeme E. Addison, Adam G. Riess

6. DESI BAO tests acceleration without an absolute ruler calibration

“Model-independent and reconstruction-free evidence for cosmic acceleration from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations” — Laura Herold, Graeme E. Addison, Adam G. Riess. arXiv:2610.40785v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:13 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category astro-ph.CO. Kinematic observational tests. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

The authors construct three inequalities that a never-accelerating universe must obey, using DESI DR2 BAO and FLRW geometry without calibrating the absolute sound horizon or imposing a dark-energy equation of state. One involves SF=(1+z)ln(1+z)−FAP, where positive values violate the corresponding flat-universe non-acceleration inequality. Others compare distances across redshifts, with the BGS measurement as a low-redshift reference.

For anisotropic bins around z = 0.510, 0.706 and 0.934, the three constructions give maximum-bin significances 2.39σ, 3.04σ and 4.06σ after the stated three-bin correction; average-based versions give 2.19σ, 2.85σ and 3.22σ. These share data and must not be combined as independent discoveries. Scanning |Ωk| ≤ 1 leaves two tests near 2.4–3.5σ over the explored cases; that scan is not Bayesian curvature marginalization.

This is kinematic evidence for acceleration under geometric assumptions, not a measurement of its physical cause. FLRW distance relations and a common BAO ruler still enter. Removing an absolute r_d or H₀ calibration does not remove all early-universe, geometry or observational-systematics assumptions.

History

Sutherland's cosmic speed-trap, 1105.3838v2, already used BAO distance ratios and one-sided inequalities to test acceleration with limited dependence on gravity and the absolute ruler scale. The present advance is the DESI DR2 implementation, complementary statistics and explicit redshift-bin and curvature checks. Its correlated tests strengthen the assessment of one physical question; they should not be advertised as three independent confirmations or the first model-light BAO acceleration test.

07Layered Wire-Grid Haloscope for Millielectronvolt Axions and Dark PhotonsAndrea Caputo

7. MADGRID: a plasma haloscope aimed at meV dark matter

“Layered Wire-Grid Haloscope for Millielectronvolt Axions and Dark Photons” — Andrea Caputo. arXiv:2610.40793v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:25 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category hep-ph. Instrument concept and sensitivity forecast. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

MADGRID proposes stacked copper-wire grids that convert axions or dark photons into electromagnetic power, combining a dispersive wire-medium response with discrete Maxwell solutions and extraction through the stack's end face. Five cartridges with approximately 8–15 grids span 0.25–8 meV. The projections assume 0.1 m² aperture, 10 T for axions, 0.1 K, local dark-matter density 0.4 GeV cm⁻³, and the candidate comprising all local dark matter.

For one live year per octave and local S/N = 5, an illustrative 1 meV threshold is g_aγ ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻¹³ GeV⁻¹, or dark-photon mixing χ ≈ 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ with random-polarization factor 1/3. These are forecasts, not 95% confidence exclusions. The kinetic-inductance option assumes NEP = 6 × 10⁻¹⁹ W Hz⁻¹/². A more ambitious photon-counter option assumes 50% efficiency, 1 mHz dark counts per channel, and 100 or 400 channels; it forecasts KSVZ coverage over roughly 0.8–3.9 meV, and DFSZ over 0.8–1.2 meV.

The combined magnet, detector, terahertz material response and cryogenic performance have not been demonstrated here. Coupling losses and adopted aperture efficiency around 0.65 directly affect reach. This is a technically specified proposal, not an operating dark-matter search.

History

Lawson et al., 1904.11872v2, established tunable wire-plasma haloscopes, with illustrative coverage at 35–400 μeV. MADGRID develops a layered finite geometry and a higher-mass reach. Independently, CAST, 1705.02290v2, measured g_aγ < 6.6 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻¹ at 95% confidence for m_a ≲ 0.02 eV using solar axions. CAST does not require axions to make up Galactic dark matter, whereas this forecast does. Their numerical difference is therefore neither an achieved improvement nor an assumption-matched comparison. QCD-axion lines are theoretical benchmarks, not observed confidence regions.

08Phenomenological Chiral Effective Field Theory for Neutrino Event GeneratorsZara Graham-Jones, Mareen Hoppe, Joshua Isaacson, Shirley Weishi Li, Miša Toman

8. Automating coherent pion production for neutrino generators

“Phenomenological Chiral Effective Field Theory for Neutrino Event Generators” — Zara Graham-Jones, Mareen Hoppe, Joshua Isaacson, Shirley Weishi Li, Miša Toman. arXiv:2610.40866v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:41:31 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category hep-ph. Interaction-model and software development. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

This work exports chiral-theory amplitudes through FeynRules/UFO to Sherpa/Comix and connects them to Achilles, combining coherent Δ and nonresonant Born contributions with spectral-function initial states and nuclear final-state interactions. It addresses interaction modeling needed by oscillation experiments, but is a tree-level phenomenological implementation rather than a complete EFT truncation-error calculation.

Resonance conventions matter strongly. With a constant Δ pole width, free-nucleon cross sections for W < 1.4 GeV overshoot reference data by about 80%, 50% and 15% in the three displayed charge channels. A running width proportional to (k/k0)3mΔ/W, together with a revised Breit–Wigner parametrization, improves agreement. The illustrated nuclear excesses change from about 50% to 5–10% for T2K, and 60–70% to 10–20% for MicroBooNE.

Those improved running-width results use reweighting: they are not yet a complete implemented generator feature. The axial normalization changes from C₅ᴬ(0) = 1.27 to 1.18 in the revised treatment. Unitarization, higher resonances, two-body and medium effects remain unresolved. Better agreement in these samples does not establish full phase-space validity or a measured reduction in oscillation-parameter bias.

History

Hernández, Nieves and Valverde, hep-ph/0701149v2, already included coherent Δ-pole and chiral-background amplitudes and showed their impact on extracting the axial transition form factor from ANL data. Interference is established physics, not the novelty. The advance is the automated amplitude-to-nuclear-generator workflow and demonstrations of sensitivity to width conventions. C₅ᴬ values must be compared within their fitted amplitude and resonance prescriptions, rather than as independently measured constants.

09Stars Can Quench Superradiance Around Supermassive Black HolesYu Cheng, Andrea Caputo, Giovanni Maria Tomaselli, Samuel J. Witte

9. How orbiting stars erode superradiant boson clouds

“Stars Can Quench Superradiance Around Supermassive Black Holes” — Yu Cheng, Andrea Caputo, Giovanni Maria Tomaselli, Samuel J. Witte. arXiv:2610.40895v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 19:17:01 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category hep-ph. Theoretical environment calculation. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

The authors calculate stellar perturbations that transfer bosons from gravitationally bound cloud states into continuum states. They combine hydrogenic wavefunctions, relativistic growth rates and orbital averages, with evolution M˙c/Mc=τsr−1−τloss−1. A Sgr A-like benchmark fixes M = 4 × 10⁶ M⊙ and spin 0.9*; the latter is not an observed spin determination.

Gravitational encounters generally do not quench cloud growth in the studied stellar populations. For α = GMμ = 0.45 in natural units, an illustrative 433 state has loss/growth-time ratio about 2, halving net growth; a 322 case has a ratio near 8, slowing growth by roughly 13%. Encounters may still dominate over gravitational-wave depletion after saturation.

Quadratic matter couplings make stars more effective scatterers. The convention is −dNmNϕ2N¯N/(2MPl2), with positive d_N for the repulsive case studied. Threshold statements such as negligible effects below d_N ≈ 900 for one cusp or 50 for a mass-segregated cusp are conditional benchmarks, not observational bounds. Stellar distributions, orbital geometry, omitted bound-to-bound mixing and cloud self-interactions limit extrapolation. The analysis does not deliver one environment-independent corrected superradiance exclusion.

History

Baumann, Chia and Porto, 1804.03208, established tidal mixing and resonant cloud depletion in binaries. Environmental disruption is thus a known issue. The new calculation isolates bound-to-continuum ionization by a stellar population and adds quadratic matter interactions. A loss process that beats late-time gravitational-wave emission need not outrun initial superradiant growth; those comparisons have different implications for spin constraints.

10The Importance of Curium-250 to the γ-Ray Signature of r-Process RemnantsAxel Gross, Samuel Cupp, G. Wendell Misch, Matthew R. Mumpower

10. Curium-250 as a gamma-ray beacon of old merger remnants

“The Importance of Curium-250 to the $\gamma$-Ray Signature of $r$-Process Remnants” — Axel Gross, Samuel Cupp, G. Wendell Misch, Matthew R. Mumpower. arXiv:2610.40907v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 19:27:38 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category astro-ph.HE. Nuclear-network and emission forecast. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

The paper follows long-lived r-process ejecta with 15,625 PRISM trajectories, varying density, expansion time and electron fraction with specified mass, fission and de-excitation models. It includes prompt fission photons and radioactive daughters, asking which isotopes remain bright thousands of years after a merger.

²⁵⁰Cm has adopted spontaneous-fission partial half-life 11,300 ± 500 years and inferred total half-life about 8,300 years, including competing branches; some branch inputs are estimates rather than measurements. Its mass fraction is about 0.35% in a standard example and reaches 2% within the grid. In an illustrative composition its fission products supply over half the 0.2–5 MeV flux for approximately 2,200–22,000 years, peaking near 67%. The predicted spectrum includes potentially useful daughter features such as 1,079 keV ²⁴⁶ᵐAm.

Detectability examples use 2 × 10⁻⁴ M⊙ of ²⁵⁰Cm at 1 kpc. That yield and a corresponding nearby remnant are not observed. Fission yields, neutron-rich nuclei, isomers and the merger ejecta distribution remain major uncertainties. Instrument comparisons are forecasts, not a detection or a measured Galactic merger rate.

History

Korobkin et al., 1905.05089, already calculated gamma-ray signatures of fresh ejecta and old Galactic merger remnants, emphasizing nuclear-yield uncertainty. The strategy therefore predates this paper. The addition is a potentially important long-lived fission contribution and its daughter emission, which can change the useful isotope and age windows. It does not remove actinide-production uncertainties or guarantee an observable remnant within a kiloparsec.

11Search for long-lived heavy neutral leptons in signatures with one prompt muon and a secondary vertex formed by two non-prompt muons in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 and 13.6 TeVCMS Collaboration

11. CMS searches farther from the beam line for long-lived heavy neutrinos

“Search for long-lived heavy neutral leptons in signatures with one prompt muon and a secondary vertex formed by two non-prompt muons in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 and 13.6 TeV” — CMS Collaboration. arXiv:2610.40911v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 19:30:10 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category hep-ex. Collider exclusion analysis. Preprint submitted to JHEP.

CMS targets W→μN, followed by a displaced N→μμν decay, using 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV (2016–2018) and 34.6 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV (2022). Two displaced standalone muons without tracker requirements extend the useful transverse decay radius to approximately 50–400 cm. The interpretation assumes a Majorana heavy neutral lepton with mass 1–5 GeV, mixing only with muon neutrinos; electron and tau mixings are fixed to zero.

An ABCD construction estimates instrumental backgrounds from data, with transfer factors and uncertainties propagated through the signal-region bins. The observed sample shows no significant excess. Profile-likelihood CLs limits at 95% confidence exclude regions in the mass–|VμN|2 plane, rather than supplying one mass-independent mixing bound. The expected sensitivity improves over earlier CMS searches over roughly 2.5–4.2 GeV; this does not establish a global observed record throughout that interval.

The result is particularly sensitive to lifetime and detector acceptance. Changing flavor admixtures, whether N is Majorana or Dirac, or the decay model changes both production and branching/lifetime relationships. The figures' exclusion contours should therefore be used with their specified assumptions, not quoted as generic bounds on every sterile-neutrino model.

History

CMS, 2402.18658, previously searched for HNL decays through showers in the muon system. Its pure-muon Majorana interpretation reached |VμN|² = 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ at mN = 2.8 GeV, at 95% confidence. The often nearby quoted 4.6 × 10⁻⁶ at 3.3 GeV belongs to its Dirac interpretation and must not be substituted. Beam-dump constraints are particularly relevant at lower masses, while collider strategies sample different lifetimes and final states. The new displaced-dimuon reconstruction fills a complementary acceptance region. A single improvement factor would hide those differences and the mass dependence of the observed contours.

The proposed observable uses the relativistic imaginary cross-spectrum of bright and faint galaxies to compare their motion and gravitational redshift.

An SKA2 forecast over z = 0.2–0.8 uses six redshift bins, a freely sampled matter spectrum in 12 bins over k = 0.01–0.12 h Mpc⁻¹, and fitted bias/damping terms. It assumes a 1% Gaussian distance prior, fixed evolution bias and accurately known magnification effects. At fiducial βdm = 0.2, β = 0.1, allowing both couplings gives marginalized 1σ uncertainties σ(βdm) ≈ 0.26 and σ(β) ≈ 0.43. In the simpler dark-coupling-only model with the universal coupling fixed to zero, σ(βdm) ≈ 0.044, or 22% relative precision.

The large degradation is the scientific point: a precise projected observable need not yield a precise unique coupling. These are Fisher forecasts around specified models, not new experimental bounds. The chosen coupling benchmark can conflict with constraints on a coupling assumed constant throughout cosmic history; the paper's late-time setting and growth/quasistatic approximations must be kept explicit.

History

Castello et al., 2412.08627, already introduced a multitracer, model-light equivalence-principle statistic and forecast redshift-dependent SKA2 precision around 7–25%. The new work projects such information onto a doubly coupled dark-energy model, exposing parameter degeneracy. It does not invent the relativistic-dipole test or supersede laboratory and cosmological equivalence-principle limits that constrain different species, scales and time dependences.

25. AGN-disk mergers connected to the evolving cosmic AGN population

“Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks” — Maria Paola Vaccaro, Alessandro Trinca, Michela Mapelli, Lumen Boco, Raffaella Schneider, Rosa Valiante, Dominika Wylezalek. arXiv:2610.40787v1 · full text · PDF. Submitted 29 October 2026, 18:00:14 UTC; UTC listing 2 November 2026; primary category astro-ph.HE. Population synthesis and detection-rate forecast. Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record.

ARISE connects FastClusterAGN calculations of capture, migration, gas hardening, encounters and hierarchical mergers to evolving AGN abundances and supermassive-black-hole populations. Disk examples span central masses 10⁵–10⁹ M⊙, Eddington ratios 10⁻³–10, and viscosity parameters α = 0.01 or 0.1, with alternative disk/cluster and high-redshift population prescriptions.

At z = 0.2, representative merger-rate ranges are 12–37 or 0.5–6 Gpc⁻³ yr⁻¹ across different model families. These are between-model spreads, not statistical confidence intervals. Rate peaks shift from around z = 1.8 in a conservative abundance model to 3.3 or 3.9 in alternative growth histories; one model remains high to z ≈ 10.

For a 10 km triangular Einstein Telescope, 85% duty cycle and S/N > 12, forecasts reach approximately 0.9–1.6 × 10⁵ detections yr⁻¹ for α = 0.01 and 1.1–2.3 × 10⁴ yr⁻¹ for α = 0.1. These large rates depend on disk lifetimes, replenishment, migration/hardening prescriptions and high-redshift AGN demographics. Low-viscosity models can overproduce the allowed overall rate. The paper's redshift-integrated AGN fractions are not detector-weighted observed branching fractions; luminosity-selected AGN-association bounds cannot be applied indiscriminately to every modeled AGN.

History

Bartos et al., 1602.03831, already showed how dense AGN disks can accelerate mergers of pre-existing stellar-mass black-hole binaries and discussed uncertain electromagnetic counterparts. The contribution here is the explicit connection between local disk dynamics and cosmic AGN/black-hole evolution, rather than the original proposal of an AGN merger channel. The very broad forecast range demonstrates how much the cosmological extrapolation depends on disk physics and population history. Neither present GW data nor these simulations establish a unique AGN contribution.

4. GRB 261029A follow-up: a candidate soft thermal spectral component

A substantive update to Friday's burst coverage came from SVOM/ECLAIRs on 30 October at 21:20 UTC. For its trigger at 29 October 01:27:07 UTC, ECLAIRs measures T₉₀ = 47.5 (+1.0/−0.9) s in 4–120 keV. A power law plus blackbody fits the time-averaged 5–120 keV spectrum with χ²/dof = 40/26, compared with 83.8/28 for a simple power law. Chemical imaging of living cells reaches 19.2 three-dimensional volumes per second

Masato Fukushima, Keiichiro Toda, Yusei Sugawara, Shotaro Kawano and Takuro Ideguchi, “Video-rate volumetric chemical imaging via mid-infrared photothermal optical diffraction tomography,” published 30 October 2026 in Nature Communications, 17, 11075. Research article and full text; dated university announcement.

Mid-infrared absorption heats molecular bonds slightly; visible-light measurements detect the resulting refractive-index change. Rapid scanning over 11 illumination angles and differential tomographic reconstruction produce label-free chemical volumes at 19.2 volumes s⁻¹, about 52 ms per volume, without temporal averaging. A separate multi-wavenumber mode covers 300 cm⁻¹ in 1 s—the fastest volumetric rate should not be confused with a complete spectrum at every time point.

The biological demonstration tracks 50 lipid-droplet trajectories and distinguishes motion that two-dimensional projection obscures. This is a peer-reviewed imaging-method result, not a disease-treatment result. The live-cell experiment used three cells from one dish; larger replication applies to other imaging tests. Demonstrated specimens are only several to about ten micrometers thick. Water absorption, scattering and the weak-scattering reconstruction assumption limit extension to deep tissue. The advance is fast three-dimensional chemical dynamics at the single-cell scale; broad biological generalization remains to be established.
