ArXiv Highlights

UHECR origins: a stronger case for an extra light component, not a unique identification of neutron-star mergers

Paper: Lightest and heaviest nuclei as keys to the origin of ultrahigh energy cosmic rays — Anna M. Suliga and Glennys R. Farrar. arXiv:2611.00205v1, PDF. Submitted 30 October 2026, 18:00:03 UTC; 3 November listing. Preprint, described as under review at Science. Classification: source-model inference.

The authors extend source processing and propagation to nuclei heavier than iron, fitting Auger's spectrum and composition with EPOS-LHC-R shower interpretation. A separate high-energy proton component improves the binary-neutron-star-evolution benchmark from χ²/dof = 260.5/58 to 118.8/55. The reported 7.6σ comes from a rescaled χ² difference with three extra parameters; it is not a significance for identifying the astrophysical source class.

The preferred maximum rigidity is approximately 7.2 EV, with a 563 K source photon field. The best-fit A = 80 injection number fraction is about 7% at fixed energy. Figure 2 also permits a solution without nuclei heavier than iron within the authors' 1σ region: ultraheavy nuclei are allowed, not detected. The proton component supplies approximately 5% of the injected energy above 10 EeV in the benchmark; this is a different fraction.

The fit assumes a common source population and prescribed evolution. Energy-scale and shower-depth shifts are discrete refits, not a marginalization over all systematics. The remaining reduced χ² of 2.16, source geometry and air-shower modeling limit the interpretation.

History

The central physical problem is how one source population could produce light particles below the ankle and progressively heavier composition above it. Unger, Farrar and Anchordoqui (2015) already showed how photon interactions and rigidity-dependent escape create a high-pass filter: lower-energy nuclei fragment before escape, while higher-energy nuclei survive. That mechanism did not require a unique source class.

Crucially, Muzio, Unger and Farrar (2019), §IV and Fig. 10 already found that an extra proton component could substantially improve Auger fits. The new paper updates the data and shower treatment, extends the nuclear network and develops the merger interpretation; the extra-component idea itself is established. Farrar's merger proposal, published in 2025, supplied the physical motivation involving magnetized outflows and r-process material.

An independent comparison is Zhang, Murase, Ekanger, Bhattacharya and Horiuchi, published in PRL on 7 May 2026. It propagated ultraheavy nuclei and considered both mergers and collapsars. Its injection fractions and allowed energy-generation ranges use different source assumptions and fit criteria, so they cannot be converted into a clean improvement factor here. Assessment: the new fit is an interesting test of the merger hypothesis, but neither its extra protons nor its permitted ultraheavy component establishes mergers uniquely. Its statistical calibration and competing source models remain important open tests.

X-ray-selected AGN: a practical neutrino target catalog, with conditional discovery forecasts

Paper: Hidden Neutrino Active Galaxy Candidates: Prospects for Catalog and Stacking Searches with IceCube, IceCube-Gen2, and KM3NeT — Mainak Mukhopadhyay, Jose Alonso Carpio, Heer Patel, Ali Kheirandish and Kohta Murase. arXiv:2611.00198v1, PDF. Submitted 30 October, 18:00:01 UTC; 3 November listing; submitted to ApJ. Classification: catalog, emission modeling and forecast.

XHiNAC selects 28 AGN using intrinsic 2–195 keV fluxes, hard-X-ray surveys and NuSTAR-based absorption corrections. Its 10⁻⁷ GeV cm⁻² s⁻¹ flux threshold is a catalog cut, not a confidence limit. AMES calculations model cosmic-ray acceleration and interactions in compact coronae; turbulence, coronal radius and magnetic field define alternative benchmarks.

For favorable benchmarks, stacking sources other than NGC 1068 reaches nominal 5σ in roughly 5–10 years with future detectors. The selected nearby objects supply only about 1–3% of the modeled diffuse intensity at 10 TeV; that is not a limit on all AGN.

These are counting forecasts using design/scaled effective areas, atmospheric backgrounds, an assumed 0.4° angular resolution and one broad energy bin. They omit a full reconstructed-energy/direction likelihood and detector-systematic treatment. X-ray–neutrino scaling, absorption corrections and uncertain black-hole masses also affect rankings. The quoted times are conditional exposures, not scheduled discovery dates.

History

The motivation is that an opaque AGN corona can convert proton energy into neutrinos while reprocessing its gamma rays. The observational anchor is IceCube's 2022 NGC 1068 analysis, which reported 4.2σ evidence in its predefined source search. That supports studying obscured AGN; it does not uniquely identify coronal acceleration.

Population evidence already exists. The 13.1-year northern-sky analysis, published in 2026, obtained a 3.3σ binomial excess involving 11 X-ray-bright sources, excluding NGC 1068. The southern-sky starting-track analysis found 6.7⁺⁴·⁰₋₃·₂ fitted signal events, a 3.0σ stacked excess under its disk-corona spectral model. Its strongest individual source, Circinus, had only 1.8σ after trials. These are distinct analyses and statistics, not significance values to add.

The advance is therefore an absorption-corrected target selection and explicit detector forecasts, rather than the first evidence for an AGN neutrino population. The forecast significance should be recalculated with collaboration-level response models before interpreting it as a discovery probability.

Cygnus: can collective stellar winds account for the gamma-ray bubble?

Paper: A Multi-messenger Study of the Cygnus bubble — Abhijit Roy, Giovanni Morlino, C Rishi, Jagdish C. Joshi, Soebur Razzaque and Neelam Panwar. arXiv:2611.03248v1, PDF. Submitted 2 November, 14:57:56 UTC; 3 November listing; preprint prepared for JCAP. Classification: transport-model fit and neutrino forecast.

The model accelerates protons at a spherical collective-wind termination shock in Cygnus OB2, then propagates them through a three-dimensional gas model. Spectra from Fermi, HAWC, ARGO and LHAASO are combined with LHAASO radial profiles; Bohm, Kraichnan and Kolmogorov diffusion are compared.

The preferred Bohm benchmark has wind power 2.62 × 10³⁸ erg s⁻¹, cosmic-ray efficiency 2.0%, shock radius 26.0 pc, bubble radius 185 pc and acceleration scale Emax = 3.28 PeV. However, its combined reduced χ² = 6.16: being best among three choices does not make it an adequate overall fit. Aperture conversions, cloud positions and unresolved sources matter.

For ten years, the model predicts approximately 95 signal/530 background events in IceCube and 58/338 in KM3NeT/ARCA within 6°, over 1–1000 TeV. The quoted 4.11σ/3.18σ are S/√B estimates, not likelihood-calibrated discoveries. The fitted acceleration scale is not a measured proton cutoff.

History

LHAASO's Cygnus-bubble study, published in Science Bulletin in 2024, established the extended ultrahigh-energy gamma-ray target. Today's paper does not discover the bubble or its PeV photons. Morlino, Blasi, Peretti and Cristofari (2021) developed the wind-termination-shock transport framework used here. The new contribution combines updated gas information, spectral data, spatial profiles and neutrino expectations.

A significant counterpoint is Vieu and colleagues' 2024 hydrodynamic simulation of Cygnus OB2. Resolving individual stellar winds, they did not obtain the coherent spherical termination shock assumed by the new fit; the association's extended stellar distribution instead generated individual shocks and structured flows. That does not exclude collective acceleration generally, but it directly challenges this particular geometry.

Assessment: the multiwavelength comparison is useful, yet the poor absolute fit and disputed accelerator geometry prevent a source identification. A fixed electron-injection benchmark in the appendix cannot exclude the full range of leptonic or mixed scenarios. A measured neutrino morphology and spectrum would provide a stronger test than a forecast event count.

LHCb: evidence for B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻, with improved Bₛ observables

Paper: Evidence for the decay B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ and improved measurements of the decay B⁰ₛ → μ⁺μ⁻ — LHCb Collaboration, R. Aaij et al. arXiv:2611.00230v1, PDF. Submitted 30 October, 18:11:34 UTC; 3 November listing; submitted to Physical Review X. Classification: measurement and evidence.

A simultaneous mass-fit analysis uses 18 fb⁻¹ of 2024–2025 data at 13.6 TeV, then combines it with earlier LHCb data. The B⁰ significance is 3.0σ in the new sample and 3.2σ combined: evidence, below discovery convention.

Combined results, with statistical then systematic uncertainties, are:

  • B(Bₛ⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻) = (3.25⁺⁰·²⁶₋₀·₂₅ ⁺⁰·¹³₋₀·₁₂) × 10⁻⁹;
  • B(B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻) = (1.14⁺⁰·⁴²₋₀·₃₉ ⁺⁰·⁰⁶₋₀·₀₅) × 10⁻¹⁰;
  • effective Bₛ lifetime = 1.60 ± 0.16 ± 0.01 ps.

Backgrounds, normalization channels, efficiencies and constrained nuisance parameters enter the fits. The branching-fraction extraction assumes AΔΓ = +1 for Bₛ decay-time acceptance; changing it to 0 or −1 shifts the Run-3 result upward by 4.0% or 9.2%. Thus these are not assumption-free bounds on arbitrary new scalar interactions. Results remain compatible with the Standard Model.

History

These decays are flavor-changing neutral currents, loop- and helicity-suppressed in the Standard Model. Their small branching fractions make extra scalar or pseudoscalar amplitudes potentially visible, but extracting a coupling bound requires a specified effective Hamiltonian and correlated inputs.

The CMS–LHCb combination published in Nature in 2015 established Bₛ → μμ and already reported roughly 3σ evidence for B⁰ → μμ. That earlier combined hint must not be erased when describing the new single-experiment evidence. LHCb's Run-1/2 analysis subsequently set B(B⁰ → μμ) < 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 95% CL rather than establishing the decay. A former upper limit and today's asymmetric measurement are different statistical products; their ratio is not a sensitivity-improvement factor.

The new data make the B⁰ mode accessible to a direct measurement and strengthen the joint branching-fraction/lifetime test. They do not yet establish a departure from the Standard Model or resolve the CP structure of Bₛ decay.

LHCb searches for photon-coupled GeV-scale axions

Paper: Search for GeV-scale axion-like particles in PbPb collisions — LHCb Collaboration, R. Aaij et al. arXiv:2611.00135v1, PDF. Submitted 29 October, 18:32:11 UTC; 3 November listing; submitted to JHEP. Classification: exclusion search.

Coherent photon fields in ultraperipheral lead collisions can produce γγ → a → γγ. LHCb analyzes 0.228 ± 0.010 nb⁻¹ of 2018 PbPb data at √sNN = 5.02 TeV, selecting exclusive forward diphotons. It observes zero candidates, with approximately 0.2 background events expected.

For fixed masses 3–10 GeV, the 95% CLs upper limits on photon coupling gₐγ, in the conventional −gₐγaFμνF̃μν/4 normalization, span 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 9.5 × 10⁻⁴ GeV⁻¹. The interpretation assumes a photon-only ALP with prompt diphoton decay. Systematics are profiled; a 31% acceptance uncertainty from differing production generators dominates the approximately 32% total normalization uncertainty.

The result extends exclusive photon-fusion coverage to lower masses in the forward region. It is neither an ALP signal nor a bound valid for arbitrary additional decay channels or long lifetimes.

History

ATLAS's 2020 ultraperipheral analysis and CMS's full Run-2 PbPb analysis, published in JHEP in 2025, established this production channel at higher accessible masses. LHCb's claim of leading exclusive photon-fusion limits in 3–5 GeV is narrower than a claim of the world's strongest photon-coupling bound.

An essential independent update is Belle II's July 2026 search: 408 fb⁻¹, e⁺e⁻ → γa → 3γ, masses 0.17–9.80 GeV, with excluded resonance windows. Its 95% frequentist profile-likelihood limits reach order 10⁻⁴ GeV⁻¹ and are stronger across much of LHCb's low-mass interval under the same prompt, photon-dominated assumption. LHCb's comparison plot uses the older Belle II result, so that plot alone is insufficient for the current global comparison.

Beam-dump and supernova limits probe different mass/lifetime/production regimes; stellar cooling limits at much lower masses are not comparable GeV exclusions. No universal improvement factor follows. Additional ALP couplings can change both production and branching fraction, requiring reinterpretation of every curve.

CMS measures |Vcb| using real W bosons in top-quark events

Paper: Measurement of the |Vcb| element of the CKM matrix using tt̄ events in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV — CMS Collaboration. arXiv:2611.01274v1, PDF. Submitted 31 October, 21:17:30 UTC; 3 November listing; submitted to JHEP. Classification: complementary CKM measurement.

Using 138 fb⁻¹ of Run-2 data, CMS reconstructs semileptonic top pairs and separates the rare W → cb channel with jet-flavor information, boosted decision trees and a neural-network discriminant. A dilepton control region constrains extra-heavy-flavor backgrounds in the simultaneous fit.

The result is |Vcb| = [4.59⁺⁰·⁴⁴₋₀·₄₈(stat) ⁺⁰·⁶⁴₋₀·₇₆(syst)] × 10⁻², or (4.59⁺⁰·⁷⁸₋₀·₉₀) × 10⁻² in total at approximately 68% CL. Heavy-flavor production modeling dominates the uncertainty; flavor tagging also matters.

This probes an on-shell weak vertex without B-decay form factors or heavy-quark-expansion inputs. It still assumes B(t → Wb) = 1, Standard-Model couplings and CKM row unitarity, neglecting |Vub|². The PDG reference value normalizes the fitted signal-strength parameter; it does not constrain the result as a prior. Agreement with both inclusive and exclusive B-decay determinations leaves their discrepancy unresolved.

History

Traditional inclusive B → Xcℓν and exclusive B → D(*)ℓν determinations use different nonperturbative inputs. A useful independent measurement therefore changes the physical process and systematic uncertainties, rather than merely collecting more of the same B decays.

Harrison and Vladimirov (2019) proposed extracting |Vcb| from hadronic W decays in top events. ATLAS's first measurement, published 23 July 2026, used 140 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV and obtained (5.0⁺¹·¹₋₁·₄) × 10⁻². CMS therefore provides a more precise independent realization of an already demonstrated method, not its first measurement.

The two experiments agree within their broad uncertainties. Their precision is still insufficient to distinguish the much closer inclusive and exclusive B-decay values. The main opportunity is to improve heavy-flavor control and tagging while preserving independence from low-energy hadronic inputs; the present result is not evidence for CKM nonunitarity, which its extraction assumes.

DES Y3: how much of the S₈ constraint comes from parameter boundaries?

Paper: Baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignments in profile: a frequentist view of DES Y3 cosmic shear — Anoma Ganguly, Elisabeth Krause, Tanvi Karwal and Vivian Miranda. arXiv:2611.03537v1, PDF. Submitted 2 November, 18:01:34 UTC; verified 3 November listing; preprint, no journal publication listed. Classification: inference-method reanalysis.

The authors profile nuisance parameters instead of marginalizing them in KiDS-overlap-excised DES Y3 cosmic shear, using the Hybrid modeling framework. For S₈ = σ₈√(Ωm/0.3), their baseline gives 0.809⁺⁰·⁰⁴³₋₀·₀₂₂ from Δχ² ≤ 1, versus posterior 0.813 ± 0.020. It fixes Σmν = 0.06 eV, uses NLA intrinsic alignments with a₁ ≥ 0, and bounds log₁₀(TAGN/K) to 7.3–8.0.

Extending the feedback boundary to 8.3 stretches the high-S₈ profile. Compressed Planck information on ωb, ns and h breaks degeneracies, giving profile S₈ = 0.808 ± 0.019. It does not supply an independent shear-only resolution of the S₈ tension.

Calibration likelihoods and parameter boundaries remain part of the analysis. Only ten noisy realizations test the intervals; calibrated frequentist coverage is explicitly left for future work. “Profile likelihood” therefore does not mean assumption-free inference or guaranteed 68% coverage here.

History

Weak lensing mixes the clustering amplitude with baryonic suppression of small-scale power, galaxy intrinsic alignments and redshift calibration. Those degeneracies were already central to the DES Y3 modeling analysis and the joint DES Y3–KiDS-1000 Hybrid analysis. The latter explicitly distinguished its joint-survey marginal mean S₈ = 0.790⁺⁰·⁰¹⁸₋₀·₀₁₄ from its maximum-posterior value 0.801, discussing skewness and neutrino-mass projection effects. Those joint-survey numbers are not directly comparable to the new paper's DES-only, fixed-mass baseline.

The new work exposes the degeneracy through the best likelihood available at each S₈ rather than the nuisance-volume-weighted average. Figure 4 makes the boundary dependence particularly clear. Adding shear ratios also reduces the freedom introduced by richer intrinsic-alignment models.

Assessment: this is a useful robustness test, not new lensing data and not a demonstration that all low-S₈ measurements were prior artifacts. Its message is to state the feedback range, alignment model, neutrino treatment and external information alongside any reported tension.

A neutrino cross-section audit with a numerical caveat in v1

Paper: World average charged-current inclusive neutrino cross section from 6 to 200 GeV — Richard Gran. arXiv:2611.01435v1, PDF. Submitted 1 November, 02:19:08 UTC; 3 November listing; preprint, no journal publication listed. Classification: historical data synthesis and generator assessment.

Gran reconstructs the inclusive muon-neutrino/antineutrino charged-current σ/Eν per isoscalar nucleon, treating shared beam-flux uncertainties in nine high-energy measurements. Central values for 30–200 GeV are 0.677 and 0.335, respectively, in 10⁻³⁸ cm² GeV⁻¹. For 6–30 GeV, they become 0.698 and 0.316. These are averages of existing data, not new events.

Numerical caution: the abstract gives high-energy uncertainties ±0.013/±0.007, while §II gives ±0.012/±0.008. The low-energy neutrino uncertainty is ±0.018 in the abstract but ±0.016 in §VI A. These discrepancies should be resolved before adopting a precision normalization; they are not different confidence levels stated by the author.

The lower-energy average inherits the high-energy normalization through low-ν flux determinations. Its covariance treatment is approximate, including incomplete interexperiment correlations. The modest energy trends do not establish a significant departure from a constant σ/Eν, and extrapolation below 6 GeV is outside the analysis.

History

Here ν in “low-ν” means energy transfer, not neutrino energy. At sufficiently small transfer, the selected cross section varies weakly with incident energy, permitting a flux-shape measurement; an external cross section still supplies the absolute scale.

The original MINOS measurement, Adamson et al. (2010), Eq. 7 and normalization discussion, explicitly anchored its 30–50 GeV neutrino cross section to (0.675 ± 0.009) × 10⁻³⁸ cm² GeV⁻¹, propagating that uncertainty to all energies. This independently confirms why a low-energy point cannot be counted as a wholly independent absolute normalization. Gran traces the established high-energy benchmark to Seligman's 1997 synthesis and revisits earlier revisions to CERN/Fermilab flux calibrations.

The value of the new paper is the provenance audit, correlation accounting and comparison with GENIE implementations. It does not overturn the standard high-energy central values. Nuclear corrections, generator definitions of DIS versus non-DIS, and the unresolved printed uncertainties limit how literally its precision can be transferred into oscillation analyses.

Can prolonged X-rays make off-axis short bursts visible at TeV energies?

Paper: Enhanced Detectability of Very-High-Energy Gamma Rays from Off-Axis Short Gamma-Ray Bursts: Impacts of Structured Jets and External Inverse-Compton Scattering — Yuri Sato, Bing Theodore Zhang and Kohta Murase. arXiv:2610.41476v1, full text. Submitted 30 October 2026, 09:28:06 UTC; verified listing 2 November; v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. Classification: theoretical emission model and detection forecast; catch-up.

Question and method. Short gamma-ray bursts can show extended or plateau X-ray emission after their prompt flash. Those photons can be scattered to very high energies by electrons in the external forward shock: external inverse-Compton emission, or EIC. This differs from synchrotron self-Compton (SSC), whose seed photons are generated by the same shock electrons. The authors use the AMES radiation calculation, including Klein–Nishina effects and integration over equal-arrival-time surfaces, to compare Gaussian, power-law and uniform top-hat jets viewed away from their axes.

The fiducial Gaussian jet has core angle 3°, core Lorentz factor 100 and core isotropic-equivalent kinetic energy 6 × 10⁵² erg. The uniform external density is 6.3 × 10⁻³ cm⁻³; electron and magnetic energy fractions are εₑ = 0.04 and εᴮ = 2.1 × 10⁻⁵, with electron index p = 2.17 and accelerated-electron fraction 1. These are fixed model choices, not measured confidence bounds. Extended and plateau seed components have characteristic durations 300 s and 10⁴ s, respectively, and luminosity normalizations 10⁴⁹ and 10⁴⁷ erg s⁻¹ at their spectral breaks.

Quantitative result. At z = 0.01, approximately 43 Mpc, the modeled 1 TeV EIC peak exceeds the adopted CTAO medium-sized-telescope sensitivity for a 6° viewing angle in both seed-emission cases, but not for 15°. Gaussian and power-law jets give brighter off-axis EIC emission than the selected top-hat benchmark. This is a comparison of the adopted jet models, not a universal ordering at fixed total jet energy. The EIC peak follows the end of the seed-photon supply, providing a possible temporal discriminator from SSC; SSC can dominate later.

Using a sky-averaged binary-neutron-star gravitational-wave range of 300 Mpc for the assumed LIGO O5 configuration, and merger-rate density 10–1700 Gpc⁻³ yr⁻¹, the predicted joint GW–TeV rates are 0.003–0.5 yr⁻¹ for extended-emission EIC and 0.004–0.7 yr⁻¹ for plateau EIC, compared with 0.001–0.2 yr⁻¹ for SSC. The ranges propagate the adopted merger-rate normalization; they are not confidence intervals or measured yields. In the Gaussian benchmark, the TeV horizon drops rapidly beyond viewing angles of roughly 8°, so gravitational-wave detectability alone does not imply TeV detectability.

Limitations. The result depends on prolonged central-engine emission actually occurring, its luminosity and duration, the angular jet structure, ambient density and shock microphysics. The quoted forecasts do not marginalize over these population uncertainties. Peak-flux sensitivity comparisons also do not establish the yield of a real campaign with pointing delays, sky visibility and localization coverage. A nondetection would therefore not, by itself, exclude a structured jet or a long-lived engine.

History

The established physical question is whether short-burst TeV emission is supplied mainly by shock-generated synchrotron photons or by an additional central-engine radiation field. EIC itself is not new here. In Zhang, Murase, Yuan, Kimura and Mészáros, ApJ Letters 908, L36 (2021), arXiv:2012.09143v2, extended and plateau photons were already shown to enhance the high-energy afterglow, with Klein–Nishina effects and the end of seed injection shaping the light curve. Their application to GRB 160821B illustrated how EIC could exceed SSC under suitable parameters. The present advance is the explicit dependence on off-axis angular structure, followed through to joint GW–CTAO horizons and conditional rates; it is not the first proposal of this radiation mechanism.

The observational baseline also needs care. Acciari et al., MAGIC Collaboration, ApJ 908, 90 (2021), arXiv:2012.07193 reported a low-significance VHE excess for GRB 160821B. At the known burst position, the quoted 3.1σ pre-trial significance becomes 2.9σ after accounting for the two analysis cuts. That is not a secure short-burst TeV detection and does not validate EIC. The new calculation supplies testable light-curve and viewing-angle expectations, while both the radiation mechanism and the population rate remain uncertain. Its forecast rates cannot be interpreted as an improvement in an existing experimental bound.

Science News

EP261015a: a likely supernova counterpart, with a tentative redshift

The original Einstein Probe soft-X-ray event began on 15 October 2026 at 13:15:36 UTC, lasted approximately 130 s, and had a 3-arcmin radius, 90% confidence WXT localization. It was identified on the ground and a notice was sent manually, rather than triggered onboard (GCN 45956).

The new development is the Stargate team's 2 November, 21:40:20 UTC report of SN 2026ahoa, the likely optical counterpart. VLT/FORS2 spectroscopy began on 30 October at 06:30:55 UTC, 14.72 days after the X-ray event, with seven 600-s exposures spanning 4450–8650 Å. Weak host emission lines suggest z = 0.31; a separate 23 October spectrum offers low-significance supporting Hα evidence.

The spectrum shows features consistent with a pre-maximum broad-lined Type Ic supernova. Fixing z = 0.31 gives a satisfactory SN 1998bw template match 9.2 days before maximum, and an inferred Mᵣ ≈ −19.3. Both the template phase and absolute luminosity depend on that redshift. This strengthens the stellar-explosion interpretation of the X-ray transient, but the redshift and counterpart association remain tentative; no formal association false-alarm probability is supplied. The new result is spectroscopy of an October event, not a new November explosion. GCN 46131.

GRB 261029A: Konus-Wind adds an exceptionally large isotropic energy estimate

Event: 29 October 2026, 01:27:08.129 UTC at Konus-Wind. New report: GCN 46136, 3 November, 10:53:09 UTC. This updates the burst covered previously; it is not a new November event.

The multipeaked prompt episode lasts approximately 112 s, with emission detected to approximately 60 MeV. In 20 keV–10 MeV, Konus reports fluence (1.97 ± 0.11) × 10⁻⁴ erg cm⁻² and a 64-ms peak flux of (2.03 ± 0.19) × 10⁻⁵ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹. The time-integrated Band spectrum peaks at 590⁺⁴⁷₋₄₄ keV.

Assuming the reported z = 3.270 and the circular's cosmology (H₀ = 67.3 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, Ωm = 0.315), the isotropic-equivalent energy is (4.56 ± 0.25) × 10⁵⁴ erg. All these errors are 68% and the analysis is preliminary. Isotropic-equivalent energy is not the beaming-corrected explosion energy.

GCN 46133, issued 3 November, 06:25:43 UTC, reports an optical counterpart at Rc = 22.85 ± 0.17 Vega mag, observed 1 November, 3.82 days after the burst; it is uncorrected for Galactic extinction. The prompt spectrum and late optical detection are complementary observations, not a claimed neutrino or gravitational-wave association.

EP-WXT 01709322206: optical fading supports the stellar-flare classification

The Einstein Probe circular GCN 46132, issued 3 November, 00:25:26 UTC, gives an event time of 2 November, 23:26:00 UTC and identifies HG 7-33 as the likely source. Its estimated 0.5–4 keV flux is 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, corresponding to 2.8 × 10³¹ erg s⁻¹; uncertainties are not supplied.

The new BOOTES-5 circular 46134, issued 3 November, 07:56:25 UTC, reports 0.16 mag fading over 40 minutes in clear-filter observations of the star within the EP-FXT error circle. The team calls this confirmation of a stellar flare. Timing caveat: it gives observations beginning 3 November, 02:37 UTC and calls that “11 min after” detection, which is inconsistent with GCN 46132's 23:26 UTC trigger. The classification has additional support, but the inconsistent latency should not be silently propagated.

MASTER's 3 November follow-up reports clear-band transient-search limits as deep as 20.3 mag, beginning 07:07:18 UTC. Those limits do not negate the earlier flare or imply the absence of an astrophysical source.

GRB 261103A: a soft burst recovered in SVOM's ground search

GCN 46137, published 3 November, 11:24:02 UTC, reports an ECLAIRs ground-search detection with reference time 06:51:11 UTC that day. Its strongest image detection has S/N = 7.1 in 4–20 keV over 40.96 s. The quoted best window starts T₀ − 40.96 s, so T₀ should not be interpreted as a precisely measured physical onset.

The location is RA = 14.157°, Dec = +18.701° (J2000) with 11.04 arcmin radius at 90% confidence, including systematics. No bright catalogued X-ray source is identified in that region. The circular provides no trial-corrected false-alarm rate, secure counterpart or redshift; image S/N is not a global discovery significance. A cancer vulnerability created by losing one of two related enzymes

Jill Zimmerman and colleagues, including senior author Lukas E. Dow, published A selective TNKS2 inhibitor for tumor-restricted WNT suppression in Nature Chemical Biology on 2 November 2026. This is a peer-reviewed preclinical study combining cancer genomic data, genetic perturbations, structural analysis and cell/organoid experiments.

WNT signaling supports both cancer growth and normal tissue renewal, making broad inhibition toxic. The study exploits a different vulnerability: chromosome 8p deletions can remove or reduce TNKS1, leaving affected tumor cells more dependent on the related enzyme TNKS2. Structure-guided chemistry produced TDI-012804, which selectively suppressed WNT signaling in TNKS1-deficient models; its cellular WNT-reporter EC₅₀ was below 50 nM in the reported assay. This concentration measures activity in that experimental system, not a human dose or treatment response.

The decisive caveat is stronger than the accompanying publicity's suggestion of minimal side effects. The paper states that initial attempts to find a suitable formulation for in-vivo delivery and testing of TDI-012804 were unsuccessful. Consequently, the compound's safety could not be established. Genetic mouse experiments support the biological rationale, but they do not substitute for pharmacological safety testing. The advance is a selective chemical probe and a genotype-dependent therapeutic hypothesis; clinical efficacy and tolerability remain untested. Original article and full text; institutional announcement, 2 November.

Dinosaur heme: chemical evidence is stronger than the claim of identifiable blood cells

Kliti Grice and colleagues, Heme signatures in mineralized red blood cells within vascular canals of a 115-million-year-old dinosaur bone, PNAS, published online 2 November 2026; the issue date is 17 November. This is peer-reviewed research, not a newly excavated living tissue sample.

The study concerns Aratasaurus museunacionali limb bones from Brazil. HPLC–mass spectrometry finds heme B and altered heme-derived compounds in bone extracts, contrasting with chlorophyll-related signatures in the surrounding matrix. Raman spectroscopy and electron microscopy associate porphyrin signals and relevant elements with ellipsoidal structures inside vascular canals. The authors propose manganese mineralization, including hausmannite, Mn₃O₄, as part of the preservation mechanism. The spatial and chemical comparisons strengthen an endogenous-material interpretation; molecular survival does not by itself prove every cell-like structure is a red blood cell.

Independent scrutiny is substantive. In ABC's 3 November reporting, Mary Schweitzer accepts the plausibility of heme in the bone but questions the cell identification and morphology; Mauricio Barbi regards the evidence as persuasive while emphasizing that these are interpreted mineralized remnants, not intact blood cells or preserved functional hemoglobin. Earlier heme evidence already appears in Schweitzer et al., PNAS (1997), so “the first chemical evidence of dinosaur blood” would be misleading.

Access limitation: the new paper's primary abstract, significance statement, publication record and references were available, together with the Curtin announcement. Its full article and supplement could not be retrieved for this edition. This is therefore a verified, qualified news item, not a claimed full-text methodological audit. No intact dinosaur DNA or viable cells are established.
