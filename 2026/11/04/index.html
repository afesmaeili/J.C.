01Cosmic-Ray Signals from Dark Matter Annihilation into Scalars with Baryon NumberMattia Di Mauro, Caleb Gemmell, Dan Hooper and Fabrizio Vassallo

Can baryon-carrying dark-sector particles make cosmic antinuclei observable?

Paper: Cosmic-Ray Signals from Dark Matter Annihilation into Scalars with Baryon Number — Mattia Di Mauro, Caleb Gemmell, Dan Hooper and Fabrizio Vassallo. arXiv:2611.03785v1, full text. Submitted 2 November 2026 at 19:00:01 UTC, one second after the relevant cutoff; first listed 4 November. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. Classification: phenomenological dark-matter model, antiproton constraint and conditional antinucleus forecast; no antinucleus detection.

Question and method. Standard hadronic dark-matter annihilation has difficulty making heavy antinuclei without overproducing antiprotons. The authors study self-conjugate dark matter χ annihilating into color-singlet scalars ϕ with baryon and lepton numbers B=L=3. Each scalar then decays schematically to three copies of uddνe, distributing energy among many quarks and placing more antinucleons close enough in phase space to coalesce.

Pythia 8.309 generates the shower and hadronization. Antideuterons and antihelium-3 form when all constituent pairs satisfy calibrated momentum and spatial cuts: pcoal=0.219 GeV and dcoal=3 fm for antideuterons, and 0.206 GeV and 3 fm for antihelium-3. The spectra are propagated through a stationary two-zone Galactic model with a 4.2-kpc halo, NFW density profile normalized to ρ⊙=0.4 GeVcm−3, convection at 13 km s⁻¹, and force-field solar potential 0.736 GV. Continuous energy losses, reacceleration and charge-sign-dependent heliospheric transport are omitted.

At each dark-matter mass mχ and mediator mass mϕ, the authors derive a one-sided 95% confidence-level upper limit on the velocity-averaged annihilation cross section ⟨σv⟩ from the seven-year AMS-02 antiproton spectrum. They profile the secondary-antiproton normalization, a spectral-index shift limited to ±0.1, and the Fisk potential. The bound spans approximately 1.5 × 10⁻²⁹ to 6.5 × 10⁻²⁵ cm³ s⁻¹ over the scanned plane; the familiar 3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³ s⁻¹ thermal reference is excluded for roughly mϕ≲20 GeV in this setup. This is a model- and propagation-specific cross-section bound, not a generic limit on dark matter.

Quantitative forecast. Saturating that antiproton upper limit, broad parts of the plane yield at least one expected antideuteron event at AMS-02, and a region around mχ,mϕ~50–100 GeV also reaches GAPS. A benchmark with (mχ,mϕ)=(66,53) GeV and ⟨σv⟩=6.3×10−26 mcm3s−1 predicts 9.9 AMS-02 antideuterons and 1.2 GAPS antideuterons. By contrast, antihelium-3 remains difficult: the favorable (43,5)-GeV benchmark, using 9.8×10−28 mcm3s−1, gives 3.2 antideuterons and 0.5 antihelium-3 events at AMS-02, a ratio 0.16. Those event numbers are forecasts under assumed future acceptances, not measured excesses or confidence limits.

The same cascade fits the shape of the Galactic-centre gamma-ray excess near (mχ,mϕ)=(35,27) GeV, but requires ⟨σv⟩≃1.14×10−25 mcm3s−1, above the paper's antiproton limit. Even a larger allowed inner-Galaxy J-factor only reduces it to about 5.6×10−26 mcm3s−1. The authors therefore disfavor a simultaneous explanation under their reference halo and transport assumptions.

Limitations. The mediator decay is sampled with isotropic massless phase space rather than a specified ultraviolet completion. Coalescence universality, antiproton production, Galactic transport and solar modulation dominate the systematics; the plotted ±20% coalescence band does not encompass all of them. Most importantly, AMS-02 has not published a conclusive antideuteron or antihelium flux, and the analysis uses a privately communicated antihelium acceptance estimate. The result identifies testable parameter regions; it does not explain established antinucleus events.

History

The production mechanism was introduced by Gemmell, Hooper, Koren and Vassallo in Dark Matter, Baryon Number, and Cosmic-Ray Antinuclei, PRL 137, 171802 (2026), arXiv:2603.12314v2. That paper used event-level coalescence to show that a B=L=3 mediator could enhance antideuteron and antihelium multiplicities by factors of roughly 10–100 relative to direct bb¯ annihilation. The new work's substantive step is to propagate those particles, constrain the accompanying antiprotons with AMS-02, fold detector acceptances and test gamma rays. It consequently narrows the earlier qualitative claim: antideuterons can be observable in viable regions, while antihelium is only marginal under the more conservative sensitivity prescription.

The experimental baseline is still a null result. BESS-Polar II's published search gives Φd¯<6.7×10−5 (m2ssrGeV/n)−1 over 0.163–1.100 GeV/n; AMS-02 candidate reports have not become a published flux measurement. Conventional WIMP studies, such as Cholis, Linden and Hooper, PRD 102, 103019 (2020), arXiv:2001.08749, already found that antideuterons may approach experimental reach while antihelium generally does not. The baryon-carrying cascade changes that hierarchy, especially the antihelium-to-antideuteron ratio, but uncertainties in formation and acceptance remain large enough that confirmation requires published events, backgrounds and efficiencies.

02Presupernovae Strongly Constrain Electrophilic MeV BosonsYu Cheng, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo, Chui-Fan Kong and Edoardo Vitagliano

The years before core collapse may beat the supernova as a MeV-particle laboratory

Paper: Presupernovae Strongly Constrain Electrophilic MeV Bosons — Yu Cheng, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo, Chui-Fan Kong and Edoardo Vitagliano. arXiv:2611.03813v1, full text. Submitted 2 November 2026 at 19:00:16 UTC and first listed 4 November. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. Classification: model-dependent astrophysical exclusion from an existing gamma-ray dataset.

Parameter and physical question. A dark photon A′ kinetically mixed with the photon has dimensionless coupling ϵ and, above 2me, can decay to e+e−. Supernova explosions are standard factories for MeV particles, but their dense plasma has a frequency of order 10 MeV, suppressing in-medium mixing for lower-mass dark photons. Massive stars during carbon, oxygen and silicon burning are cooler but remain hot for years, with plasma frequencies only 30–70 keV. The paper asks whether their much longer exposure produces more escaping particles than the explosion.

Method. MESA r26.4.1 evolves solar-metallicity stars of 9–60 solar masses to the onset of core infall. For a representative 20-solar-mass star, the core advances from approximately 80 keV during carbon burning, through 190 keV in oxygen burning, to 320 keV in the final silicon-burning days. The authors calculate in-medium dark-photon production, decay and escape throughout these profiles, then average the positron yield over a Salpeter initial-mass function. A Galactic core-collapse rate of 2.3 per century and a spiral-arm source distribution are convolved with an intentionally broad 1-kpc positron-smearing scale.

The predicted disk-like 511-keV intensity is compared with INTEGRAL/SPI longitude bins at |l|<20∘. The one-sided statistic counts only model flux above the data and imposes χ2≤4, a 2σ construction without fitting or subtracting the bulge. It limits the number of locally annihilating positrons to 2.0 × 10⁵² per progenitor for the 1-kpc benchmark.

Result. For dark-photon masses approximately 1–3 MeV, the excluded lower-coupling boundary reaches ϵ~10−14, up to one to two orders of magnitude stronger than the paper's supernova comparisons. This improvement is genuinely comparable because it constrains the same kinetic-mixing parameter and mass interval, using presupernova rather than explosion emission. The authors also calculate an analogous electron-coupled axion-like-particle limit near gae~10−14. At larger couplings, decay inside the star, trapping and energy loss comparable to the neutrino luminosity delimit the calculation; above the LA′=Lν curve the unperturbed stellar tracks cannot support a reliable exclusion.

Limitations. The bound assumes that massive-star evolution, the Galactic core-collapse distribution, positron escape and propagation, and the SPI disk profile are described well enough by the adopted models. Varying the source distribution has little effect in the authors' tests, while changing the smearing length from 1 kpc to 100 pc produces a visible but modest shift. Dense circumstellar material weakens the 3-MeV bound by about 3% for the stated benchmark and less below 2.5 MeV. The upper boundary is deliberately conservative because sufficiently strong dark-photon cooling would modify the progenitor itself. The calculation is therefore an exclusion within a specified stellar and Galactic model, not a laboratory bound.

History

The observable builds on Calore et al., PRD 105, 063026 (2022), arXiv:2112.08382, which used the same Galactic 511-keV line to constrain positrons from core-collapse supernova production of sterile neutrinos and dark photons. That work demonstrated that the line could exceed the reach of the SN 1987A energy-loss argument for particles that escape and decay. The new paper moves the production epoch before collapse and gains exposure time plus weaker plasma screening; this is why its improvement applies specifically around 1–3 MeV rather than uniformly across mass and coupling.

Presupernova dark-photon emission was also calculated by Sieverding, Rrapaj, Guo and Qian, ApJ 912, 13 (2021), arXiv:2101.08672. They evolved massive stars with extra cooling and proposed changes to the presupernova neutrino signal; for a 15-solar-mass star at 500 pc, the illustrative sensitivity was around ϵ≲10−12 and depended on a nearby, identified progenitor. The present diffuse method integrates the entire Galactic population and counts decay positrons, reaching smaller coupling without waiting for a nearby star. Remaining uncertainty lies in the nonperturbative region: both studies indicate that new cooling can alter advanced burning, so standard stellar profiles cannot be extrapolated through the upper-coupling edge without self-consistent evolution.

03Fermi-LAT Detection of Extended GeV Emission toward the Middle-aged Supernova Remnant G156.2+05.7Wei-Liang Xu, Qing-Wen Tang and Long Li

Extended GeV emission appears around the evolved remnant G156.2+05.7

Paper: Fermi-LAT Detection of Extended GeV Emission toward the Middle-aged Supernova Remnant G156.2+05.7 — Wei-Liang Xu, Qing-Wen Tang and Long Li. arXiv:2611.04227v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 03:06:07 UTC and first listed 4 November. Accepted by Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society on 2 November; arXiv v1. Classification: observational gamma-ray detection plus conditional one-zone emission modelling.

Data and detection. The analysis uses 17 years of Fermi-LAT Pass 8 Source-class data from 4 August 2008 to 3 August 2025 over 100 MeV–1 TeV, with standard Galactic and isotropic diffuse models and 4FGL-DR4 sources. Replacing two catalogue point sources with an extended template gives extension test statistics TSext=74.9 for a uniform disk and 75.7 for a two-dimensional Gaussian. The adopted Gaussian has R68=0.84∘±0.09∘; at the reference distance 2.7 kpc, this is a physical radius near 40 pc. The Gaussian and disk are effectively tied under the Akaike criterion, while a foreground CO template fits worse.

The integrated 100 MeV–1 TeV photon flux is 1.41 × 10⁻⁸ ph cm⁻² s⁻¹. A log parabola is preferred to a power law by TS=11.429, nominally 3.4σ for one extra parameter, with spectral parameters Γ=2.101±0.089 and β=0.187±0.081. This is evidence for curvature, not a separate component detection. A 17-bin annual light curve has TSvar=16.1, below the paper's 99%-confidence variability threshold 34.0.

Emission modelling. Radio points and the LAT spectrum are fitted jointly with NAIMA and MCMC. The baseline fixes B=14 μG, d=2.7 kpc and proton cutoff Ep,cut=1 TeV; that cutoff is not constrained by the LAT data. The mixed leptonic–hadronic model has BIC 38.62, versus 68.84 for a single-electron leptonic model and 45.40 for a proton-dominated gamma-ray model with separate synchrotron electrons. Thus a hadronic contribution is strongly preferred to the deliberately minimal one-population leptonic fit, but the ΔBIC = 6.8 separation from the proton-dominated alternative is not decisive. The baseline finds electron cutoff about 1.18 TeV and steep proton index about 3.23.

The inferred proton energy above 1.22 GeV is

Wp≃1.4×1050(n1 cm−3)−1(d2.7 kpc)2 erg.
At the Suzaku-scaled preshock density n=0.036 cm−3, the one-zone requirement is roughly 3.7–4.0 times a canonical 1051-erg supernova energy across the tested magnetic fields. Denser clumps, a multiphase medium or re-acceleration of existing cosmic rays are therefore needed if the hadronic interpretation is correct. This is an energy-budget argument, not a measured gas density.

Limitations. The result depends on the diffuse foreground, spatial template and assumed distance. The northeast and southwest quadrants are brighter, but their spectral difference is only about 1.6σ; association with Hα filaments is suggestive. The model fixes the proton cutoff, magnetic field and one-zone geometry and uses broad priors, while target density and proton normalization are degenerate. The LAT detection of extended GeV emission is robust within the performed template tests; its division between inverse Compton and pion decay is not.

History

G156.2+05.7 was discovered as a large X-ray shell with unusually faint radio emission. Uchida et al., PASJ 64, 61 (2012), arXiv:1205.4188 used six Suzaku pointings to separate shocked interstellar gas and metal-rich ejecta. Once a background galaxy cluster was removed, the hard emission was consistent with the cosmic X-ray background: there was no robust nonthermal X-ray component. The present electron cutoff is therefore merely compatible with that null result, not measured by the X-rays.

The distance and age baseline changed with Katsuda et al., ApJ 826, 108 (2016), arXiv:1605.07271. Comparing Hα filaments over about 12 years gave a 1σ proper-motion upper limit of 0.06 arcsec yr⁻¹. Combined with an approximately 500-km-s⁻¹ shock, that implied distance >1.7 kpc and an age of at least a few 104 years, disfavoring an association with nearby 0.2–0.3-kpc clouds. The new paper adopts 2.7 kpc rather than measuring distance anew. Its actual advance is the first detailed extended GeV characterization and conditional particle budget. Whether it reveals freshly accelerated hadrons, re-accelerated Galactic cosmic rays or mixed electron/proton emission remains open.

04SS 433 Eastern Jet Turbulence Revealed by Spatially-Resolved IXPE Data AnalysisNingyue Fan, Jack T. Dinsmore, Roger W. Romani

SS 433 Eastern Jet Turbulence Revealed by Spatially-Resolved IXPE Data Analysis

Authors: Ningyue Fan, Jack T. Dinsmore and Roger W. Romani. Paper: arXiv:2611.03846v1, submitted 2 November 2026; first announced in the 4 November UTC listing. Status: observational reanalysis; arXiv reports acceptance by ApJ. This study concerns v1, not the 9 November replacement.

Question and method. How ordered is the magnetic field where SS 433's eastern jet accelerates X-ray-emitting electrons? The authors reanalyse the 2023 IXPE exposure using Chandra-derived spatial templates and an event likelihood that accounts for polarization leakage between neighbouring regions. They separate the bright head, spine and surrounding sheath, and compare moment-based and neural-network event reconstruction.

Results. For the neural-network reconstruction, the fitted polarization degrees are 25 ± 12% in the head, 26 ± 8% in the spine and 14 ± 12% in the sheath, with quoted one-standard-deviation errors. A multizone model improves the fit over the single-zone description at approximately 3.5σ, rising to about 3.8σ for overlapping templates. The electric-vector position angles remain compatible with a magnetic field along the jet. This supports spatial modelling of the emission, but the ordering of the three polarization fractions is much less significant: the inferred excess of sheath turbulence is only about 1–1.8σ.

For isotropic Gaussian field fluctuations, the conversion is

τ≡δBB0=[32Π0−PDPD]1/2sini,

using inclination i=79∘ and the synchrotron maximum polarization set by the X-ray photon index. It suggests τ≲1.5 in the head/spine and τ≳1.8 in the sheath. These are model-dependent characterizations, not confidence bounds. Unresolved field geometry, template choice and low polarized counts can also reduce the measured polarization. Kelvin–Helmholtz shear is a possible interpretation, not a detected instability.

History

Kaaret et al. (2024) analysed the same IXPE observation and obtained a background-corrected, single-region spectropolarimetric fraction of 52 ± 14%, with EVPA −3.7 ± 7.8°. The new analysis reproduces a compatible result when using a comparable single-zone extraction; its lower multizone values therefore do not establish temporal depolarization. The advance is treatment of spatial mixing and leakage in an existing data set.

H.E.S.S. (2024) independently used energy-dependent TeV morphology to locate acceleration and constrain downstream transport. Its preferred decelerating-flow model inferred an initial outer-jet speed of (0.083±0.026)c, with statistical error; alternative flow geometries also required advection. Polarimetry supplies complementary field information, but neither study uniquely fixes the turbulence spectrum or separates shear ordering from shock-related ordering.

05XMM-Newton and NuSTAR quasi-simultaneous follow-up of the very high energy flare of PKS 1725+123 in August 2025L. Marcotulli, A. Gokus, L. Heckmann, E. Visentin, I. Jaroschewski, J. Otero-Santos, M. Böttcher, M. Zacharias, R. Takeishi, S. Nozaki, Y. Suda, I. Sadeh, P. Boorman, E. de Oña Wilhelmi, D. Berge

XMM-Newton and NuSTAR quasi-simultaneous follow-up of the very high energy flare of PKS 1725+123 in August 2025

Authors: L. Marcotulli, A. Gokus, L. Heckmann, E. Visentin, I. Jaroschewski, J. Otero-Santos, M. Böttcher, M. Zacharias, R. Takeishi, S. Nozaki, Y. Suda, I. Sadeh, P. Boorman, E. de Oña Wilhelmi and D. Berge. Paper: arXiv:2611.03829v1, submitted 2 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: submitted to A&A; X-ray observations and spectral interpretation.

Question and data. Did the high synchrotron peak of this z=0.586 flat-spectrum radio quasar persist after its August 2025 TeV flare? The study analyses approximately 50 ks of XMM-Newton and 180 ks of NuSTAR observations beginning on 22 August, three days after the VHE announcement. Bayesian spectral comparisons use absorbed single- and double-power-law models, with Galactic absorption fixed and calibration constants allowed to vary. Solar-background filtering leaves only about an hour of useful overlap between the instruments: the coverage is quasi-simultaneous and samples the aftermath of the flare.

Results. In the first four XMM-Newton time intervals, two power laws are strongly preferred, with differences in log evidence of roughly 16–58. In the first interval their photon indices are 1.79−0.13+0.09 and 2.87−0.17+0.30, with 68% highest-density intervals; their crossover is near 2 keV. NuSTAR sees a comparatively stable hard slope, about 1.6–1.8, while its 3–79 keV flux rises by approximately 50% over four days.

The soft branch is consistent with a synchrotron tail retreating through the X-ray band, while the hard branch can be inverse Compton emission. Individual soft-index changes have limited significance, and a two-zone interpretation remains possible. The paper does not perform a broadband physical fit that selects leptonic over hadronic emission. Nor does the previously discussed positional connection with IceCube-201021A establish a neutrino association.

History

Liu et al. (2026) reported SVOM-VT observations of the same outburst, including minute-scale optical variability and a high synchrotron peak. Their broadband interpretation used a compact spine plus an extended sheath, allowing photons from one region to be scattered in the other. Thus, an unusually high synchrotron peak in this FSRQ and multizone modelling predate the present paper.

The new contribution is the soft-to-hard X-ray decomposition and its evolution during the post-flare interval. It supplies a useful test of that earlier SED interpretation, rather than a separate flare discovery. Connecting these observations to the TeV peak still requires time-dependent modelling that respects the observing gaps; a single simultaneous SED cannot be assumed across the whole interval.

06Electromagnetic signatures of gradual magnetic dissipation in jets from supermassive black holesStavros Dimitrakoudis, Maria Petropoulou, Stamatios I. Stathopoulos, Dimitrios Giannios

Electromagnetic signatures of gradual magnetic dissipation in jets from supermassive black holes

Authors: Stavros Dimitrakoudis, Maria Petropoulou, Stamatios I. Stathopoulos and Dimitrios Giannios. Paper: arXiv:2611.05322v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: submitted to A&A; theoretical radiation calculation.

Question and method. Can smooth dissipation of alternating magnetic-field stripes produce the gamma-ray-bright SEDs of blazars? The authors couple an accelerating, conical striped jet to the LeHaMoC kinetic code, following synchrotron emission, self-absorption, inverse Compton scattering and pair production along many jet segments. An iterative calculation includes photons exchanged between segments. The flow contains electron–proton plasma, but the radiation calculation is leptonic; it does not predict a hadronic neutrino spectrum.

Results. A representative model uses a 108M⊙ black hole, jet power 5×1045 ergs−1, asymptotic Lorentz factor 50, opening angle 3° and minimum stripe width 400Rg. Dissipation peaks around 24 pc. Intersegment illumination increases the inverse-Compton output by about a factor of three, yet the Compton-to-synchrotron luminosity ratio remains only about 10−3 in the baseline model. Magnetic energy density dominates the radiating-electron energy density by roughly three orders of magnitude even near their closest approach.

The central finding is a difficulty for this particular smoothly dissipating, strongly magnetized jet: it does not naturally produce Compton-dominated blazar emission. External broad-line-region and torus photons, and variations of the tested parameters, do not generally remove that difficulty. This is neither an observational exclusion of reconnection nor a bound on all striped jets. The treatment is steady, uses approximate photon angular distributions, and omits full electron transport between zones in its default implementation; intermittent plasmoids or localized dissipation can behave differently.

History

Giannios and Uzdensky developed the link between a distribution of magnetic-stripe widths, gradual reconnection, bulk acceleration and a broad dissipation maximum. Zhang and Giannios then argued that a striped flow could accommodate large-scale jet emission and the blazar zone within a common model, including substantial external-Compton radiation for suitable conditions.

The new work revisits that radiative conclusion with explicitly local energy injection and coupled kinetic calculations along the jet. The authors identify the conversion from a dissipation profile to locally injected particle power as an important difference from earlier estimates. Photon exchange does enhance Compton emission, but in their calculation it cannot compensate for the low electron energy density. The disagreement is therefore about radiative implementation and physical assumptions, not evidence that magnetic energy cannot power jets.

07Radiative relativistic reconnection powering the hard-state emission of X-ray binaries: synchrotron-driven pair loading, MeV tail, and seed photonsValeriia Rohoza, Maria Petropoulou, Hayk Hakobyan, Despina Karavola, Lorenzo Sironi

Radiative relativistic reconnection powering the hard-state emission of X-ray binaries: synchrotron-driven pair loading, MeV tail, and seed photons

Authors: Valeriia Rohoza, Maria Petropoulou, Hayk Hakobyan, Despina Karavola and Lorenzo Sironi. Paper: arXiv:2611.05307v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: submitted to ApJ; kinetic simulations and radiative modelling.

Question and method. Can reconnection supply the hard X-rays, MeV tail, electron–positron pairs and soft seed photons of a black-hole corona within one physical picture? Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations follow radiatively cooled, magnetically dominated pair plasma. A separate kinetic radiation calculation propagates synchrotron and Compton photons through a compact corona and includes photon–photon pair production.

Results. In the simulated configuration, about 40% of the incoming magnetic power goes into inverse-Compton radiation and 10–20% into synchrotron radiation. Particles escaping the flux ropes can reach energies inaccessible to the confined population and generate a synchrotron MeV tail. For a Cyg X-1-inspired example with B=108 G, radius R=2.5×106 cm and injected synchrotron/Compton luminosity ratio 0.5, photon–photon absorption becomes important near 1.5 MeV. The escaping tail has approximately ϵLϵ∝ϵ−1.2. Secondary pairs radiate near 0.13 keV, supplying a substantial soft-photon population.

These numbers are simulation outputs and chosen physical examples, not fitted confidence intervals. The PIC runs use magnetization σ=10, far below the value estimated for the illustrative real corona. Pair creation is handled in post-processing rather than fed back self-consistently into the reconnection simulation; the homogeneous radiation model also omits pair annihilation. The study establishes a plausible mechanism, not a unique observational identification of the corona or a statistically preferred fit over jet-origin MeV models.

History

Sridhar, Sironi and Beloborodov (2021) showed that Comptonization by reconnection plasmoids can reproduce a hard-state spectrum peaking near 100 keV. Their two-dimensional calculations emphasized bulk motions in a cooled plasmoid chain. The new study adds a three-dimensional route to much more energetic particles and examines how their synchrotron photons feed pair production and seed-photon supply.

Synchrotron seed photons and self-absorption as a thermalizing “boiler” are older ideas: Poutanen and Vurm (2009) solved coupled photon/electron kinetic equations and modelled hard-state spectra without requiring disk feedback. Their particular SSC fits preferred relatively weak magnetization. The present proposal changes the acceleration mechanism and attributes much of the soft synchrotron component to secondary pairs in a strongly magnetized corona. A fully coupled calculation is still needed to determine whether this feedback reaches a stable state with the required optical depth.

08Multimuon Events from Cosmic Rays with the ALICE DetectorMario Sitta, for the ALICE Collaboration

Multimuon Events from Cosmic Rays with the ALICE Detector

Author: Mario Sitta, for the ALICE Collaboration. Paper: arXiv:2611.04677v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: ISVHECRI 2026 conference proceedings, summarizing an already published measurement; not a new independent data set.

Question and data. Can contemporary hadronic-interaction models reproduce underground muon bundles, and what does the comparison imply for the primary cosmic-ray composition? ALICE accumulated 62.5 live days during 2015–2018, recording approximately 165 million events with a reconstructed muon and 15,702 events with at least five muons. The overburden imposes a vertical muon threshold near 16 GeV. Detector-level multiplicity distributions are compared with CORSIKA showers using QGSJET-II-04, EPOS-LHC and SIBYLL 2.3d, with pure proton and pure iron as limiting compositions.

Results. Thirteen events contain more than 100 muons, corresponding to a rate of (2.4±0.7)×10−6 Hz; the uncertainty combines statistical and systematic contributions. The largest bundle contains 287 reconstructed muons. Simulations associate these high-multiplicity events predominantly with primaries around 1017 eV. Iron predictions from QGSJET-II-04 and SIBYLL 2.3d are compatible with the rate, while EPOS-LHC is low by more than about two standard deviations. QGSJET-II-04 gives the best overall description across the lower/intermediate multiplicities studied.

The result favours a heavy composition within these model comparisons. It is not a measurement of a pure-iron flux: composition and hadronic modelling remain entangled, and thirteen extreme events provide limited statistical discrimination. These underground muons and primary energies also differ from those driving the highest-energy air-shower muon discrepancy.

History

The underlying Run-2 analysis is ALICE, arXiv:2410.17771, published in JCAP 04 (2025) 009. It already reported the same exposure, multiplicity sample, high-multiplicity rate and hadronic-model comparisons. The November preprint is a new arXiv entry conveying those results at ISVHECRI, rather than a new resolution of the muon puzzle.

The important baseline is a comparison performed at reconstructed detector level, including overburden and tracking, rather than directly comparing an observed muon count with a ground-level shower prediction. The proceedings offer a concise account of this complementary hadronic test; improved composition inference requires both larger samples and control of interaction-model differences.

09First application of the optimised next neighbour cleaning to data from imaging atmospheric Cherenkov telescopesMaria Kherlakian, Gernot Maier

First application of the optimised next neighbour cleaning to data from imaging atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes

Authors: Maria Kherlakian and Gernot Maier. Paper: arXiv:2611.05123v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: accepted by A&A; instrument-analysis method validated on VERITAS data.

Question and method. How much faint shower information can be retained by using pixel timing as well as charge? Optimised next-neighbour cleaning selects adjacent groups of two to four pixels using charge–coincidence-time contours tied to an allowed accidental-noise rate. The analysis compares it with standard charge-threshold cleaning in simulations spanning zenith angle and night-sky background, and in 121 hours of Crab observations. Gamma/hadron classifiers are trained separately for the two cleaning methods.

Results. Depending on observing conditions, the simulated energy threshold—defined at 10% of maximum effective area—falls by 5.1–22.3%. In a 19.8-hour Crab comparison using boosted-decision-tree selection, the gamma-ray excess rises from about 13,400 to 15,500 events, or roughly 16%, while detection significance changes from 189.1σ to 194.6σ. The lowest reconstructed energy represented in that comparison falls from about 90 to 71 GeV.

The gain is not uniform across analysis choices. Keeping simple box cuts increases residual background enough to reduce significance by about 12%, despite retaining more gamma rays. Angular resolution can worsen at low energies, and the very large effective-area ratios occur near threshold where the original acceptance is small. Consequently, a threshold reduction or an excess-count ratio should not be advertised as the same fractional improvement in point-source sensitivity. Recovered spectra are consistent within the stated simulation/calibration limitations.

History

Shayduk (2013) introduced this charge–time neighbour optimization and tested it with CTA simulations. The principle is that short, locally coherent Cherenkov flashes can be separated from night-sky and detector noise without enforcing an unnecessarily restrictive time window across the entire image.

The new contribution is its detailed application to an operating telescope array, including real source data, reconstruction and retrained background rejection. It is not the invention of timing-assisted image cleaning. The mixed outcomes with box cuts and machine learning make the practical lesson clear: retaining additional pixels changes both signal and background populations, so the downstream classifier and instrument response must be recalibrated together.

10Constraining tau-sterile neutrino mixing with high-energy astrophysical neutrinosMauricio Bustamante, Qinrui Liu

Constraining tau-sterile neutrino mixing with high-energy astrophysical neutrinos

Authors: Mauricio Bustamante and Qinrui Liu. Paper: arXiv:2611.05170v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; reanalysis of a measured flavour likelihood plus future forecasts.

Question and method. Can the flavour composition of cosmic neutrinos constrain the poorly tested tau component of a sterile mass eigenstate? The baseline is a unitary 3+1 model with Ue4=Uμ4=0, while u=|Uτ4|2 varies. Oscillations are averaged over cosmic baselines. The unknown source electron fraction is profiled over, with the source composition restricted to (fe,1−fe,0,0); standard oscillation parameters have NuFIT-based constraints. In this limit the active-normalized tau fraction is

fτ,⊕=(1−u)fτ,⊕(3ν)1−ufτ,⊕(3ν).

Current result. Using the IceCube 11.4-year, 9,888-event, 5 TeV–10 PeV medium-energy starting-event flavour analysis, the paper obtains u<0.88 at 90% confidence, using a broken-power-law astrophysical spectrum and Δχ2=2.71. It finds no evidence for mixing. The bound becomes 0.65 with a single-power-law spectrum, illustrating its spectral dependence. Allowing a source tau fraction as small as 5% removes the current bound.

The mass-splitting independence applies only when oscillations are averaged, approximately Δm412≫8×10−17 eV2(E/PeV)(Gpc/L). Earth-matter effects are neglected in the baseline and can matter in part of the eV-scale range. Forecasts using assumed future detector volumes and operation dates give limits of 0.29/0.24 in 2040 and 0.14/0.12 in 2050 for pion-decay/muon-damped sources. These are conditional projections, not measurements or guaranteed detector performance.

History

Sterile-neutrino effects on astrophysical flavour ratios were already explored by Brdar, Kopp and Wang (2017), including the possibility of altered source production. The present advance is a quantitative tau-only limit using a substantially improved IceCube flavour measurement, with explicit source and spectral tests.

It does not improve the strongest terrestrial constraints in their applicable mass ranges. IceCube DeepCore's 7.5-year analysis used 5–150 GeV atmospheric events and obtained |Uτ4|2<0.0574 at 90% confidence for Δm412≳1 eV2, allowing muon mixing and nuisance parameters to vary. ORCA6 obtained 0.076 at 90% confidence at Δm412=1 eV2, profiling the other mixing element; NOvA's dual-baseline search covers complementary smaller splittings. These conditions differ from the tau-only astrophysical fit.

Hagstotz et al. found a marginalized 95% cosmological bound log10|Uτ4|2<−3.18 for their logarithmic mass-splitting prior on 10−2–102 eV2, using CMB+BAO, standard sterile production and an extended ΛCDM model, with logarithmic priors on each mixing between 10−6 and 10−1. That much stronger result depends on thermal history and priors. The astrophysical method's value is its different baseline, energy and assumptions, not a competitive present-day upper limit.

11Electroweak Constraints on Nuclear Neutron Distributions and the Low-Energy Weak Mixing AngleXu-Run Huang, Tong-Gang Yue, Zhen Zhang, Lie-Wen Chen

Electroweak Constraints on Nuclear Neutron Distributions and the Low-Energy Weak Mixing Angle

Authors: Xu-Run Huang, Tong-Gang Yue, Zhen Zhang and Lie-Wen Chen. Paper: arXiv:2611.03751v1, submitted 2 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; nuclear-model inference and CEνNS reanalysis. The arXiv record states that supplemental material will accompany the published version; it is not included in this v1.

Question and method. Can parity-violating electron scattering constrain the nuclear response sufficiently to sharpen weak-interaction tests with coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering? An extended Skyrme energy-density-functional family maps the measured CREX and PREX-II charge–weak form-factor differences in calcium and lead onto neutron distributions in other nuclei. Regression and experimental uncertainties are propagated, and the inferred form factors enter a joint fit to COHERENT CsI, Ge and Ar data.

Results. The inferred neutron rms radii include 3.417 fm for argon, 4.118 fm for germanium-74, 4.879 fm for xenon-132 and 4.880 fm for cesium-133, with quoted relative uncertainties of approximately 0.46%, 0.60%, 0.72% and 0.68%, respectively. These are conditional nuclear-model inferences, not independent subpercent measurements of each nucleus. Varying the symmetry-energy slope within the selected functional family gives only a small residual spread; that does not establish equally small uncertainty across all possible nuclear models.

The combined CEνNS extraction yields sin2θW=0.231−0.015+0.016 at one standard deviation, at momentum transfer of order 50 MeV, with electroweak radiative corrections. It agrees with the Standard Model. The new germanium information is an important part of the precision gain. The available article does not supply its promised supplemental detail, so the full nuisance-parameter implementation and all auxiliary nuclear fits cannot yet be independently audited from v1.

History

Piekarewicz (2025) already used the calcium–argon correlation and CREX to infer an argon weak-charge radius, 3.452±0.028(stat)±0.022(syst) fm. That is not the same observable as a neutron point radius, so the numerical difference from 3.417 fm is not a discrepancy. The November paper extends this strategy to more targets within a different nuclear framework. The CREX/PREX modelling tension remains a reason to test functional dependence rather than treating one regression family as a model-independent error budget.

Earlier combined germanium work profiled the Lindhard quenching parameter and obtained 0.237−0.022+0.027 at 1σ using a Klein–Nystrand form factor and radiative corrections. CONUS+ run 2 reported 0.225±0.022 at roughly 10 MeV. These results use different data and nuclear/quenching assumptions; their error-bar ratios are not a clean measure of the gain due solely to the new nuclear priors. Electron-sector precision tests remain more precise, while CEνNS supplies a complementary neutrino-sector test and better response modelling for dark-matter experiments.

12Constraints on a Long-Lived X17 Vector Boson with FASERFASER Collaboration

Constraints on a Long-Lived X17 Vector Boson with FASER

Authors: FASER Collaboration. Paper: arXiv:2611.03904v1, submitted 2 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; reinterpretation of existing displaced-decay searches.

Question and data. Does a long-lived vector interpretation of the nuclear X17 anomaly survive FASER's forward LHC searches? The analysis combines orthogonal selections from its 27 fb⁻¹ dark-photon and 57.7 fb⁻¹ axion-like-particle searches at 13.6 TeV. The former contains zero candidates against 0.0023±0.0023 expected background; the latter has one against 0.44±0.39. The integrated luminosities overlap in time and must not simply be added.

Model and result. Fermion couplings are defined by eεfXμf¯γμf. In the principal benchmark, the proton coupling is zero, the neutron coupling is fixed to εn=0.01, neutrino couplings vanish and Br(X→e+e−)=1. At mX=17 MeV, a profiled 90% CLs analysis excludes approximately

2×10−8<εe<3×10−4.

This covers the remaining low-electron-coupling window in that benchmark. The larger-coupling region decays too promptly for the same reach. Production includes meson decays and nucleon bremsstrahlung, with a substantial uncertainty from the off-shell treatment. A zero static proton coupling does not eliminate finite-momentum-transfer bremsstrahlung. Changing the fixed neutron coupling, decay branching ratio or vector structure changes the exclusion; this is not a model-independent rejection of every X17 interpretation.

History

The nuclear anomaly concerns unexpected electron–positron angular distributions, which motivated a roughly 17 MeV mediator. The key recent development was Celentano, Marini and Marsicano's E141 reanalysis, which treated production and experimental acceptance more precisely. In its revised analysis, a remaining interval around 6.5×10−5≲εe≲1.1×10−4 survived electron-coupling searches. FASER's proton-collision production supplies a complementary test, conditional on its specified quark couplings.

MEG II found no significant X17 evidence in excited-beryllium decays and placed 90% limits on the ratios of X17 to photon emission, R18.1<1.2×10−5 and R17.6<1.8×10−6 across 16.5–17.1 MeV. Those nuclear-transition limits are not directly comparable to a FASER electron-coupling interval without a production model. Together these studies constrain interpretations of an unresolved anomaly; none of the FASER candidates constitutes an X17 detection.

13Search for heavy neutral leptons in top quark decays with same-sign, same-flavor dilepton final states in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeVCMS Collaboration

Search for heavy neutral leptons in top quark decays with same-sign, same-flavor dilepton final states in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV

Authors: CMS Collaboration. Paper: arXiv:2611.04904v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: submitted to JHEP; collider search and exclusion limits.

Question and method. Can top-pair events reveal a Majorana heavy neutral lepton through t→bW→bℓN, followed by a same-sign lepton from the decay of N? The search uses 138 fb⁻¹ of CMS data at 13 TeV, selecting same-sign electron or muon pairs with jets and a b tag. Boosted decision trees separate signal from top-related backgrounds; nonprompt leptons are constrained from data. Rate and shape systematics enter a simultaneous profile-likelihood fit across the data-taking years.

Results and assumptions. No significant excess is found. For a prompt Majorana state with only one flavour mixing nonzero, the observed 95% CLs branching-fraction limits span 1.1×10−5–4.8×10−5 for t→beN and 3.7×10−6–2.4×10−5 for t→bμN, over the mass hypotheses between 20 and 100 GeV that are tested. The corresponding mixing limits range from 1.6×10−4 to 0.11 for |VeN|2, and 4.2×10−5 to 0.039 for |VμN|2.

These are ranges along mass-dependent curves, not uniform bounds across the entire interval. The analysis omits 75–85 GeV, where kinematics near the W mass strongly reduce acceptance; interpolation across that gap would be misleading. Prompt-decay and single-flavour assumptions also prevent direct application to displaced particles, Dirac states or arbitrary multi-flavour models. The sensitivity is limited by nonprompt-lepton backgrounds and by the small fraction of signal events in which all decay jets can be reconstructed.

History

ATLAS's earlier top-decay search, using 140 fb⁻¹, established this channel over 15–75 GeV. Its best observed electron and muon mixing limits were 2.0×10−4 and 6.8×10−5 at 95% confidence. Those are minima of mass-dependent curves; comparing only the minima does not define a universal improvement factor. CMS provides an independent implementation and extends the tested mass range above the W threshold.

The stronger direct-production baseline is CMS's trilepton search, which examined 10 GeV–1.5 TeV and considered separate Majorana and Dirac hypotheses. Below the W mass it is generally more sensitive to the same single-flavour mixing. The top channel therefore broadens the experimental test and supplies a complementary production environment, rather than setting the strongest overall heavy-neutral-lepton limit.

14DAMA/LIBRA dark matter signal: a global analysisMatti Heikinheimo, Sebastian Sassi, Alex Stendahl, Kimmo Tuominen, Niklas Zimmermann

DAMA/LIBRA dark matter signal: a global analysis

Authors: Matti Heikinheimo, Sebastian Sassi, Alex Stendahl, Kimmo Tuominen and Niklas Zimmermann. Paper: arXiv:2611.04890v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; multi-experiment phenomenological reanalysis.

Question and method. Can the DAMA/LIBRA annual modulation be reconciled with xenon, fluorine, iodine and germanium null results by broadening the interaction and halo model? The study scans nonrelativistic effective operators in separate leading-, next-to-leading- and next-to-next-to-leading-order groups, allows inelastic scattering and variable Na/I quenching, and compares with LZ, PICO and CDMSlite. Halo freedom consists of a colder rescaling of a Maxwellian distribution: the velocity dispersion is α220/2 kms−1, with 0.07≤α≤1 and escape speed fixed to 544 km s⁻¹. This is not an arbitrary halo reconstruction.

Results. Within the tested families, fitting DAMA tends to overproduce events in other detectors. For example, one next-to-leading-order fit using the reconstructed-count likelihood predicts approximately 316 LZ events where the background expectation used in that analysis is 18, and approximately 877 PICO-CF₃I events in its zero-background treatment. These are illustrative incompatible model predictions, not measured excesses.

The statistical treatment is a central limitation. A Skellam likelihood built from reconstructed DAMA counts gives errors smaller than DAMA's published errors and overweights the modulation data. A Gaussian likelihood using the published errors instead drives the allowed dark-matter signal towards zero. Modulation amplitudes and time residuals are treated as independent despite unavailable covariance information. The paper therefore reinforces tension within its specified model space, but its nominal discrepancy significance should not be interpreted as a universal exclusion of every dark-matter explanation. Operator groups are fitted separately and the full halo/model space is not exhausted.

History

Catena, Ibarra and Wild (2016) already tested general Galilean-invariant elastic interactions, including operator interference and several target materials, and found strong tension under the standard halo model. The November study revisits the problem with newer null data and additional inelastic, quenching and cold-halo freedom; the general observation that changing a single operator rarely reconciles all targets is not new.

An especially useful independent comparison comes from the same detector material. The ANAIS-112/COSINE-100 combined study reported, for a simple combination of their six-year results, a 1–6 keV modulation amplitude of 0.0005±0.0019 countsday−1kg−1keV−1 with one-year period and phase fixed to 2 June. It differs from the DAMA amplitude by 4.7σ in that comparison. This tests the modulation more directly than translating between xenon and NaI interaction models, although background treatment, calibration and quenching remain relevant to physical interpretation. It is not the same statistic as the global EFT fit.

15SBi3PCF: cosmology with Dark Energy Survey Year 3 cosmic shearDavid Gebauer, Anik Halder, Zhengyangguang Gong, Cora Uhlemann

SBi3PCF: cosmology with Dark Energy Survey Year 3 cosmic shear

Authors: David Gebauer, Anik Halder, Zhengyangguang Gong and Cora Uhlemann. Paper: arXiv:2611.05367v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; cosmological inference from observed shear data.

Question and method. How much information beyond two-point cosmic shear can be extracted from the correlation between aperture mass and locally measured shear correlations? The study applies an integrated three-point statistic to the DES Year 3 catalogue, containing approximately 100 million galaxies over 4,143 deg². It compresses a 1,440-component joint data vector into 17 components and learns its likelihood from CosmoGrid simulations. The analysis fits flat wCDM, with baryonic-feedback and intrinsic-alignment parameters and marginalized shear/photo-z uncertainties; the summed neutrino mass is fixed to 0.06 eV.

Results. The joint posterior gives

Ωm=0.272−0.033+0.038,S8=0.784−0.021+0.024,w=−1.04−0.19+0.25,

where S8=σ8Ωm/0.3 and the intervals are marginalized 16th–84th percentiles. Relative to its own two-point-only analysis, the joint (Ωm,S8,w) figure of merit improves by 1.59. This is a result from survey data, rather than the forecast of that improvement.

The posterior depends on the simulation domain and priors, including −2<w<−1/3, and on a restricted intrinsic-alignment model. Coverage tests on held-out simulations pass, but validation against an independent N-body implementation is absent. Full-data posterior-predictive checks give a marginal p = 0.021, and a test omitting the third redshift bin gives p = 0.001. The authors trace these difficulties to nuisance-related compressed directions and base their consistency criterion on cosmological components, which pass. Removing that bin shifts S8 by about 2.2 times the original posterior half-width; this is not itself a 2.2σ inconsistency test. These qualifications matter when interpreting the modest differences from other cosmological analyses.

History

Secco et al.'s DES Y3 two-point analysis established the cosmic-shear baseline and its sensitivity to intrinsic alignments, baryons and scale cuts. Gomes et al. subsequently combined two-point shear with aperture-mass skewness, obtaining S8=0.749−0.026+0.027 and w=−1.39±0.31 in their wCDM analysis. These use the same underlying DES catalogue, different third-order summaries and different modelling choices; their posteriors are correlated and cannot be treated as independent measurements in a simple tension calculation.

The first SBi3PCF paper developed and tested the likelihood-learning pipeline on simulations, showing why the integrated three-point likelihood need not be Gaussian. The November paper advances from that validation to real-data cosmology. It demonstrates additional non-Gaussian information without establishing evolving dark energy or a resolution of the clustering-amplitude discrepancy. Its quoted comparison with Planck+BAO is a multidimensional wCDM comparison, not a standalone significance for an S8 discrepancy.

16A New Precise Measurement of the e+e−→π+π−(γ) Cross Section with the Initial-State Radiation Method at BaBarJ. P. Lees et al. (BaBar Collaboration)

A New Precise Measurement of the e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻(γ) Cross Section with the Initial-State Radiation Method at BaBar

Authors: J. P. Lees et al. (BaBar Collaboration). Paper: arXiv:2611.04596v1, submitted 3 November 2026; first announced 4 November. Status: preprint; experimental cross-section measurement. The detailed companion, 2611.04621v1, reports the same measurement and is linked here rather than counted as a second independent result.

Question and method. Can an alternative separation of pion and muon pairs clarify the inconsistent cross-section inputs to the Standard Model prediction for the muon magnetic anomaly? BaBar uses 460 fb⁻¹ collected near the Υ(4S), selecting initial-state-radiation events to measure the lower-energy two-pion cross section. Unlike its earlier particle-identification analysis, it statistically separates pions and muons through their different angular distributions. Their ratio cancels common luminosity, photon-efficiency and vacuum-polarization effects. Data-derived corrections, unfolding and bootstrap covariance propagation are applied before integrating the bare cross section, including final-state radiation.

Results. Over 0.3–1.4 GeV, the inferred leading-order two-pion hadronic-vacuum-polarization contribution is

aμππ=(525.9±3.5stat±3.8syst)×10−10.

Combining the new and 2009 BaBar measurements, accounting for correlations and enlarging uncertainties where local disagreement warrants it, gives (517.8±2.4stat±3.3syst)×10−10 over the same interval. These are contributions to aμ=(gμ−2)/2, not measurements of the complete anomaly. Near the ρ resonance the new cross section is about 2% higher than BaBar's earlier result. In the shared 0.6–0.8832 GeV comparison interval, the new integral is compatible with CMD-3 at 0.9σ, but differs from KLOE by 4.2σ and BESIII by 3.0σ, using the paper's stated comparison uncertainties.

The alternative method provides a valuable systematic cross-check, but it loses statistical discrimination where muons overwhelm pions near threshold and at higher masses. The overall integral is not simply more precise because the luminosity doubled. The muon-to-QED validation agrees at 0.69% total precision; it does not eliminate every relative pion/muon systematic. This one channel cannot by itself establish the significance of any full Standard Model–experiment discrepancy.

History

BaBar's 2009 measurement used 232 fb⁻¹, an ISR pion/muon ratio and particle identification. It already reduced several common systematics, so the new ingredient is primarily angular separation, fuller data usage and treatment of additional radiation—not the ratio method itself. KLOE's ratio measurement supplied another independent implementation, while CMD-3 used an energy scan and obtained a higher two-pion cross section than several earlier experiments.

The new BaBar result moves toward the higher CMD-3 normalization while leaving disagreement with lower measurements unresolved. Earlier published integrals often use different energy limits and must not be subtracted directly. The collaboration's matched-range comparisons and explicit correlation study are therefore more informative than a comparison of headline aμππ numbers.

A stem-cell model connects early trophectoderm to a three-dimensional human chorion

Miaoci Zhang and colleagues, led by Mijo Simunovic, published Reconstructing the emergence of the human chorion via Hippo-mediated trophoblast induction in Nature Cell Biology on 3 November 2026. The peer-reviewed work is classified by the journal as a Technical Report.

The human chorion is an early fetal membrane and structural precursor of the placenta. Starting with transiently reset human embryonic stem cells, the researchers inhibited LATS kinases to activate the Hippo–YAP route toward trophectoderm without adding external morphogens. Time-resolved bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing mapped an ordered trajectory against integrated embryo and first-trimester chorion references. Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing found an initially hypomethylated state followed by remethylation, while the tested placenta-associated imprinting regions largely retained methylation and allelic bias.

Agitating cell aggregates in suspension produced three-dimensional structures containing trophoblast and stromal-like compartments, outward-facing trophoblast polarity and an inside-out topology relative to the in-vivo chorion. Endogenous WNT signalling was unnecessary for the initial trophectoderm induction but changed the later balance of chorionic lineages. Sorting CLDN6-positive, ITGA6-negative cells isolated a proposed intermediate with villous and extravillous trophoblast potential.

This is a model of developmental transitions, not a synthetic placenta or an embryo capable of implantation. Its identities are inferred through transcriptomic projection, markers, epigenetic measurements and in-vitro lineage potential; the topology differs from native tissue, and maternal interactions, vasculature and full placental function are absent. Its value is experimental access to a poorly observable human developmental interval, with the usual limits of stem-cell models. Article, methods, extended data and deposited datasets.
