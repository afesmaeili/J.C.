01Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulationsRyo Higuchi, Eiji Kido and Shigehiro Nagataki

Isotropy above 100 EeV disfavors a pure-proton sky—but only inside a demanding propagation model

Paper: Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulations — Ryo Higuchi, Eiji Kido and Shigehiro Nagataki. arXiv:2611.05783v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 00:42:11 UTC and first listed 5 November. The arXiv record identifies a 2026 publication in Physical Review D 114, 083040. Classification: composition and source-population inference from public arrival directions plus simulated propagation; not a direct mass measurement.

Question and data. If particles above 100 EeV are mainly protons, their short energy-loss horizon and small magnetic deflections should make nearby large-scale structure visible as close arrival-direction pairs. The authors take the 35 Pierre Auger Phase-I catalogue events above 100 EeV and count pairs separated by at most (3^circ). They find two pairs. In (10^5) isotropic skies with the same event count and Auger directional exposure, that value lies inside the expected distribution; the data therefore show no significant small-scale anisotropy by this statistic.

Propagation and mock skies. The interpretation compares deliberately pure proton, iron and uranium injections. CRPropa supplies proton and iron losses; uranium photodisintegration is calculated with TALYS 2.0 on cosmic-microwave and infrared backgrounds. Sources are sampled from the Millennium Run semi-analytic galaxy catalogue, repeated to cover distances to roughly 500 Mpc. The source density is scanned from (10^{-6}) to (10^{-3} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}). Turbulent deflection is summarized by

α=(λ1 Mpc)1/2(B1 nG),

where (B) is the rms extragalactic field and (lambda) its coherence length. For each grid point and composition, 1,000 mock catalogues of 35 events are tested with the same pair count. A point is called allowed at the paper's one-sided 95% criterion when its median pair count lies inside the corresponding isotropic range. This construction is a simulation acceptance rule, not a likelihood-based confidence interval on composition.

Result. With no magnetic deflection, none of the tested proton or iron source densities reproduces the observed isotropy; uranium can do so for high densities around (10^{-3})–(10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}), because nuclei with smaller Lorentz factor can arrive from a smoother, more distant source distribution. With fields, iron and uranium acquire broad allowed regions. A uranium example becomes compatible near (\rho_{\rm source}sim10^{-4.5} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) for a sufficiently strong turbulent field. Pure protons remain rejected over almost all scanned parameter combinations and survive only for the paper's extreme (alpha=10) field case.

The inference is therefore conditional: the observed statistic is simply consistent with isotropy, while the preference against protons emerges after fixing pure compositions, injection spectra, a galaxy-to-source prescription, energy losses and magnetic transport. It does not measure a uranium fraction or exclude mixed compositions.

Limitations. Thirty-five events give only two close pairs, so the statistic has little resolving power once strong fields erase angular structure. Uranium is an intentionally extreme proxy for nuclei beyond iron; its abundance and acceleration were not fitted. The mock universe uses repeated simulation boxes, one observer realization, a simplified exposure mapping and turbulent fields not tied to filaments, clusters and voids. The regular Galactic field changes inferred source-density limits by about an order of magnitude in the authors' test, and modern Galactic-field models still disagree. Energy-scale uncertainty, hadronic shower modelling and unmodelled mixed composition add further ambiguity.

History

The absence of clustering has constrained source populations before. The Pierre Auger Collaboration's 2013 analysis, arXiv:1305.1576 used events above 60–80 EeV and obtained 95% confidence lower bounds of roughly ((0.06\text{–}5)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) for equal-luminosity, uniformly distributed sources, depending strongly on magnetic smearing; local-matter-tracing cases gave a similar ((0.2\text{–}7)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) range. Those are not directly interchangeable with the new allowed regions because the event threshold, propagation horizon, catalogue and composition hypotheses differ. The new work's useful addition is to ask how much of the isotropy can arise from the longer propagation distance of nuclei heavier than iron, rather than only from a high source density or large deflection.

Independent composition measurements point in the same qualitative direction without identifying uranium. Auger's deep-learning reconstruction of about 50,000 surface-detector showers found the mean mass becoming heavier and its fluctuations narrowing up to 100 EeV (arXiv:2406.06319), subject to hadronic-interaction models. The Telescope Array's large-scale-structure comparison also inferred a very heavy injected composition above 100 EeV even when allowing strong extragalactic fields (arXiv:2406.19287). The present pair-count study is consistent with those baselines, but is less composition-direct than (X_{max}): its remaining uncertainty is the degeneracy among charge, magnetic field, source density and the actual nearby source distribution.

02A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger ObservatoryPierre Auger Collaboration

Auger turns Earth-skimming air-shower data into a limit on MeV sterile neutrinos

Paper: A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory — Pierre Auger Collaboration. arXiv:2611.06284v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 10:23:29 UTC and first listed 5 November. v1 collaboration preprint; no journal reference is listed. Classification: model-dependent reinterpretation and 90% confidence flux limit; no sterile-neutrino candidate or detection.

Parameter and signal. The minimal (3+1) benchmark contains a predominantly sterile mass eigenstate (N_1) with mass from the MeV scale to 100 MeV, mixing only with the tau flavour through (sin^2\theta_N). Its weak interaction cross section is suppressed by that factor. At ultrahigh energy, an (N_1) can therefore cross more Earth than an active neutrino, interact through its active component, create a tau lepton, and yield an upward or Earth-skimming air shower when the tau exits and decays.

Method and observations. A Monte Carlo propagates the sterile state through Earth, samples neutrino interactions, follows tau energy loss and decay, and folds the resulting shower through the fluorescence-detector upward-going and surface-detector Earth-skimming exposures. The upward-going sample, covering 1 January 2004–31 December 2018, contains one candidate against (0.27\pm0.12) expected misreconstructed cosmic-ray showers. The Earth-skimming search through 31 December 2021 contains zero candidates. The corresponding 90% signal-count limits are 4.09 and 2.44 events. Tau energy loss dominates the exposure systematic at (+34%/-28%); the published limit incorporates the conservative exposure treatment.

For (sin^2\theta_N\lesssim0.1), an assumed isotropic (E^{-2}) spectrum gives

E2ΦN≤2×10−9sin−2θNGeVcm−2s−1sr−1

at 90% confidence over 0.3–200 EeV. Thus the numerical bound is (2\times10^{-7}) in these units at (sin^2\theta_N=10^{-2}), and (2\times10^{-6}) at (10^{-3}). The inverse mixing dependence reflects transparency: weaker mixing lets more particles cross Earth but makes the final interaction less likely. The Earth-skimming channel supplies most of the sensitivity; sterile fluxes generated only by standard source neutrinos oscillating into (N_1) remain several orders below it.

Conditional dark-matter interpretation. The paper also assumes a (10^{13})-GeV superheavy particle (chi) that decays exclusively into one or several sterile states. At (m_N=10) MeV and (sin^2\theta_N\sim10^{-2}), the (k=1) benchmark gives (\tau_\chi\gtrsim10^{16}) yr at 90% confidence. This is far weaker than typical (sim10^{23})-yr limits on visible superheavy-dark-matter channels because the assumed products are mostly sterile and have low multiplicity. At (m_N=100) MeV, sterile decays transfer energy into active tau neutrinos before arrival. These curves are conditional exclusions in ((sin^2\theta_N,\tau_\chi)), not general dark-matter lifetime bounds.

Limitations. The reported flux bound fixes isotropy and an (E^{-2}) spectrum; differential limits are less assumption-heavy but weaker. The model allows only tau mixing, uses vacuum mixing and ignores additional sterile interactions. The upward-going event is background-compatible, while the zero-event Earth-skimming sample drives the result. Cosmogenic predictions depend on the UHECR source evolution and composition; the dark-matter result additionally fixes mass, halo profile, decay channel and multiplicity. For (m_N) above the pion threshold, photon searches can dominate once (N_1\to\pi^0\nu_\tau) opens.

History

Auger's air-shower technique was established for active neutrinos. Using 2004–2013 data, the collaboration found no candidates and set a single-flavour 90% confidence (E^{-2}) limit of (E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu<6.4\times10^{-9} {\rm GeV,cm^{-2},s^{-1},sr^{-1}}) over (10^{17})–(2.5\times10^{19}) eV (arXiv:1504.05397). The new sterile limit reuses the same physical signatures but recalculates transmission and conversion for a suppressed cross section. Its larger numerical normalization at small mixing is therefore not a weaker repeat of the active-neutrino result; it constrains a different incident particle and explicitly carries the (sin^{-2}\theta_N) penalty.

Sterile propagation through Earth was proposed as an explanation of anomalous upward ANITA showers by Cherry and Shoemaker, who calculated the acceptance for EeV sterile states and showed how reduced mixing could evade Earth opacity (Physical Review D 99, 063016 (2019)). The present collaboration analysis moves from an event-motivated scenario to detector-specific Auger exposure and observed-count limits over a defined energy interval.

Other bounds probe different assumptions. CHARM's direct beam-dump reinterpretation constrains tau-mixed heavy neutral leptons only for roughly 290 MeV–1.6 GeV, above the main mass range here (Physical Review D 104, 095019 (2021)). Big-bang nucleosynthesis, CMB and supernova bounds can be much stronger at 10–100 MeV, but depend on reheating temperature, early-Universe production and stellar-core transport. The Auger limit is less restrictive yet astrophysically independent; the open question is whether nature supplies any ultrahigh-energy sterile flux near its reach.

03DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky WayDESI Collaboration

DESI's halo stars put the Milky Way near 1.2 trillion solar masses

Paper: DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky Way — DESI Collaboration. arXiv:2611.05518v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 19:15:56 UTC, after the relevant cutoff, and first listed 5 November. v1 DESI Key Project preprint, submitted to The Astrophysical Journal. Classification: dynamical mass measurement with mock-calibrated systematic uncertainties.

Parameter, data and methods. (M_{200c}) is the mass inside the radius where the mean enclosed density is 200 times the critical density. DESI DR2 supplies six-dimensional phase space for 5,198 blue horizontal-branch stars and 4,838 RR Lyrae, 10,036 tracers in total, spanning about 10–95 kpc from the Galactic centre. Known globular clusters, dwarf galaxies and the Sagittarius stream are removed, and angular selection functions are modelled separately for the two tracers.

Four dynamical estimators—emPDF, GME and two variants of the non-parametric Jeans method NIMBLE—fit the enclosed mass profile or halo parameters. Their biases and correlations are tested on 24 AuriDESI mock surveys: six cosmological Milky-Way analogues viewed from four observer positions, populated and selected to resemble the real samples. The joint hierarchy corrects the measured biases, imposes method-dependent error floors and retains residual correlations rather than treating eight method–tracer estimates as independent.

Result. The joint 68% intervals are

M(<80 kpc)=6.36−0.48+0.53×1011 M⊙,M200c=1.19−0.12+0.14×1012 M⊙.

RR Lyrae alone and blue horizontal-branch stars alone both give (M_{200c}\simeq1.20\times10^{12} M_\odot), an important tracer cross-check. The enclosed mass continues rising from 20 to 80 kpc. Within that range the joint uncertainty stays below about 8%, but the virial value extrapolates beyond most tracers and is more model-sensitive.

The apparently tight virial interval needs context. GME receives about 75% of the joint (M_{200c}) weight because it performs especially well on the mocks. Removing it shifts the combination to (1.52^{+0.35}{-0.30}\times10^{12} M\odot) and doubles the relative uncertainty from roughly 11% to 22%. The paper therefore treats method disagreement as part of the result, rather than presenting the central value as a model-free census.

Limitations. Every estimator assumes approximate equilibrium and spherical symmetry. The six mock haloes sample only a small set of assembly histories, and transfer of their bias calibration to the real Galaxy is untestable. The mocks do not contain an LMC analogue at its present close position; published experiments indicate that the LMC's reflex motion and halo distortion can bias equilibrium estimates by up to about 20% over the radial range used. Proper-motion errors and sparse tracers become limiting beyond 70 kpc. The analysis does not infer the local dark-matter density, which would require a large inward extrapolation and assumptions about baryonic contraction.

History

The trillion-solar-mass scale is consistent with several independent tracers. A simulation-calibrated analysis of ten classical satellite galaxies found (M_{200}=1.17^{+0.21}{-0.15}\times10^{12} M\odot) at 68% confidence (Callingham et al., arXiv:1808.10456). A joint Gaia DR2 rotation-curve and outer-tracer fit, including baryonic contraction, obtained (M_{200}^{\rm total}=1.08^{+0.20}{-0.14}\times10^{12} M\odot) (Cautun et al., arXiv:1911.04557). DESI does not dramatically move that established baseline; its advance is the size of the distant standard-candle samples, the use of four methods on common mocks, and an explicit correlated combination.

The live dispute is with low masses inferred by extrapolating the declining Gaia DR3 disc rotation curve. Ou et al. fit a cored Einasto halo to data reaching about 30 kpc and obtained a dark-halo virial mass (1.81^{+0.06}{-0.05}\times10^{11} M\odot), while warning that the low value is driven by the chosen extrapolating profile (arXiv:2303.12838). Jiao et al. reported a roughly 30 km s⁻¹ decline from 19.5 to 26.5 kpc and a total mass (2.06^{+0.24}{-0.13}\times10^{11} M\odot) (arXiv:2309.00048). DESI's mass continues rising well beyond that disc range and strongly favours (M_{200c}\gtrsim10^{12} M_\odot), suggesting that disequilibrium, asymmetric-drift or extrapolation systematics affect the low-mass solutions. It does not by itself identify which correction is responsible; LMC dynamics and non-spherical structure remain the central uncertainties.

04A Laplace-Transform Green's Function for Two-Zone Diffusion with Energy Losses: Application to the Geminga Pulsar HaloFurong Li, Wei Liu, Yali Shao, Yiqing Guo

A flux-matched Green's function for the Geminga two-zone halo

Paper: A Laplace-Transform Green's Function for Two-Zone Diffusion with Energy Losses: Application to the Geminga Pulsar Halo — Furong Li, Wei Liu, Yali Shao, Yiqing Guo. arXiv:2611.05808v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 01:34:00 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. v1 preprint; no journal reference is listed in the checked arXiv record. Classification: methods · cosmic-ray transport.

Question and method. Can analytic transport retain the diffusion boundary around a pulsar while including electron energy losses? The calculation solves spherical two-zone diffusion with a Laplace transform, imposing continuity of both particle density and diffusive flux at the interface. The inner and outer diffusion coefficients must have the same energy dependence; losses are spatially uniform. The solution recovers homogeneous diffusion when the two coefficients coincide.

Result. The Geminga illustration fixes the inner coefficient at D1(100TeV)=4.5×1027cm2s−1, the boundary at 60 pc, the distance at 250 pc, and the magnetic field at 3 μG. Single and broken injection spectra produce substantially different positron contributions. The boundary tests give maximum relative residuals of approximately 7.2×10−14 in density and 8.5×10−7 in flux for the tested configurations. These measure numerical matching, not agreement with observations.

Limitations. This is a transport-method paper with illustrative applications, not a joint fit establishing Geminga as the AMS-02 positron source. In particular, the plotted 5–50 TeV HAWC profile is multiplied by 3.5 for normalization. Injection, spin-down history, field strength and zone size remain degenerate; anisotropic diffusion, proper motion and spatially varying losses are outside this derivation.

History

The physical motivation predates this solution: slow diffusion inferred locally from a TeV halo need not describe the entire interstellar medium. Fang et al. (2018) used a flux-conserving numerical two-zone model and showed how a compact slow-diffusion region could coexist with a substantial positron flux at Earth. Their favorable examples depended on the injection efficiency and spectrum, rather than uniquely identifying a source.

Tang and Piran (2019) examined the connection between Geminga's gamma rays, positrons and a broken injection spectrum. The present application inherits their benchmark parameters. Its contribution is an explicit analytic treatment of interface matching with cooling, not the introduction of two-zone diffusion or a new observational determination of Geminga's positron fraction. Whether one source can jointly reproduce the gamma-ray morphology, spectrum and positron data still requires a consistent likelihood analysis.

05Can Magnetic Reconnection Accelerate Energetic Particles in the Heliosheath?Jeongbhin Seo, Teresa Boland, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Giulia Murtas

Compressive reconnection falls short of the anomalous-cosmic-ray spectrum

Paper: Can Magnetic Reconnection Accelerate Energetic Particles in the Heliosheath? — Jeongbhin Seo, Teresa Boland, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Giulia Murtas. arXiv:2611.06735v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 16:19:29 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. Accepted for The Astrophysical Journal, according to the arXiv record. Classification: simulation · particle acceleration.

Question and method. Can reconnection in the heliosheath accelerate pickup ions into anomalous cosmic rays? Two-dimensional resistive-MHD simulations are coupled to stochastic solutions of the Parker transport equation, with energetic-particle pressure fed back into the fluid. The modeled energization is compressive first-order Fermi acceleration; diffusion and injection are prescribed.

Result. Runs inject 1 keV protons and compare conditions near the termination shock, middle heliosheath and heliopause. The heliopause benchmark gives a differential energy spectrum approximately J(E)∝E−5.4; a wider 3.3 AU current-sheet configuration hardens it to approximately E−4.4. Both are substantially steeper than the approximately E−1.5 anomalous-cosmic-ray spectrum discussed in the paper. Saturation times of roughly 12, 3.7 and 1.6 years also exceed the adopted residence times near the shock and in the middle region, making those saturated spectra optimistic limits.

Limitations. This tests a specific compressive model, not every reconnection mechanism. Prescribed diffusion, two-dimensional geometry and the absence of explicit pitch-angle dynamics, magnetic-mirror acceleration and shear terms restrict the conclusion. The wider-sheet result in the body is harder than the fiducial spectrum emphasized in the abstract; neither reproduces the target spectrum.

History

Drake et al. (2010) proposed acceleration in contracting magnetic islands as an alternative to acceleration solely at the termination shock. Their kinetic picture included pressure-anisotropy feedback and firehose-limited contraction; its low-beta limit could yield the hard spectrum relevant to anomalous cosmic rays. That mechanism is not identical to an isotropic Parker equation containing only compression.

Seo et al. (2024) developed the coupled MHD–transport feedback approach and showed that energetic-particle pressure reduces compression and steepens spectra. The new study applies that framework to heliosheath conditions and residence times. The discrepancy with the hard observed spectrum is therefore informative about this implementation, while the role of kinetic island dynamics and three-dimensional transport remains unresolved.

06Energetic Nonthermal Electrons within the Above-the-looptop Regions in Solar Flares: Acceleration, Feedback, and Quasi-Periodic PulsationsJeongbhin Seo, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Bin Chen, Chengcai Shen, Hui Li

Particle feedback shapes above-loop-top flare acceleration

Paper: Energetic Nonthermal Electrons within the Above-the-looptop Regions in Solar Flares: Acceleration, Feedback, and Quasi-Periodic Pulsations — Jeongbhin Seo, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Bin Chen, Chengcai Shen, Hui Li. arXiv:2611.06763v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 16:40:58 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. Published in The Astrophysical Journal 997, 313 (2026). Classification: simulation · particle acceleration.

Question and method. An eruptive solar-flare simulation couples 2.5-dimensional MHD to stochastic electron transport, including the electrons' pressure and energy exchange with the fluid. It tests whether compression near reconnection outflows and above-loop-top structures can produce spatially localized nonthermal emission.

Result. Electrons injected at 10 keV are followed for initial nonthermal number fractions of 1%, 10% and 20%. In the 10% benchmark, the above-loop-top energy spectrum is approximately E−4, compared with approximately E−4.7 in the current sheet. Electrons above 20 keV reach energy densities of about 10–30 erg cm⁻³, roughly 10–30% of the local magnetic energy density. Greater feedback weakens compression and softens the spectrum. Plasmoid impacts produce modulation on a characteristic approximately 14 s timescale for the adopted normalization.

Limitations. These are simulation outputs, not a fit to a measured flare spectrum or a detection of the proposed pulsation mechanism. Injection and diffusion are prescribed; the model omits thermal conduction and explicit pitch-angle/mirroring physics. Its two-dimensional structure and selected magnetic and density scales limit direct extrapolation to individual solar events.

History

Li et al. (2022) combined flare MHD and Parker transport to study compressive electron acceleration and confinement, including structures motivated by the 10 September 2017 flare. Their energetic particles were treated without the self-consistent pressure feedback emphasized here; unresolved scattering physics already limited interpretation of spectral and spatial agreement.

The feedback formalism was subsequently tested by Seo et al. (2024), who found that a larger energetic-particle fraction suppresses compressibility. The new calculation combines that backreaction with eruptive flare geometry and resolves how the electron spectra vary across the above-loop-top region. It strengthens the case for testing compression and feedback together, while leaving competing acceleration processes and event-specific validation open.

07A high-frequency turnover in the persistent radio source of FRB20201124AG. Bruni, G. Gianfagna, L. Piro, A. L. Thakur, L. Nicastro, E. Palazzi, A. Rossi, S. Savaglio, A. Gardini, Y.-P. Yang, B. Zhang

A tentative millimetre turnover in FRB 20201124A's persistent source

Paper: A high-frequency turnover in the persistent radio source of FRB20201124A — G. Bruni, G. Gianfagna, L. Piro, A. L. Thakur, L. Nicastro, E. Palazzi, A. Rossi, S. Savaglio, A. Gardini, Y.-P. Yang, B. Zhang. arXiv:2611.05694v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 22:22:19 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. Preprint submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics Letters. Classification: observation · tentative spectral turnover.

Question and data. Archival ALMA observations from 31 December 2023, near 97 GHz, are combined with VLA measurements from 2021 to test the synchrotron spectrum of the persistent source associated with FRB 20201124A. Separating the burst position from diffuse host-galaxy emission is essential.

Result. After deblending, the FRB-associated component is 20 ± 11 μJy, only about 1.8σ; the higher significance before separation includes host emission. A slow-cooling broken synchrotron spectrum, with the electron index fixed at 2.5 and log-uniform priors on peak frequency and flux, gives νm=63−33+100GHz and Fm=28±9μJy. These are the reported posterior intervals. In this model the break is the characteristic injection frequency, not the synchrotron self-absorption frequency.

Limitations. The radio and millimetre observations are separated by approximately two years, so variability can mimic spectral curvature. The weak deblended ALMA signal and broad frequency interval make the turnover tentative. A young rotation-powered magnetar is an interpretation, not a measured age or a unique engine identification. The 2025 high-resolution radio non-detection is insufficiently sensitive to impose a useful source-size constraint.

History

Bruni et al. (2024) established the low-luminosity persistent-source candidate using high-frequency radio imaging that separates compact emission near the FRB from extended star formation. Its rising radio spectrum motivated a nebular interpretation, but the angular-size bound did not establish a parsec-scale magnetar wind nebula.

Rahaman et al. (2025) modeled magnetar wind nebulae and found that this comparatively faint persistent source could be rotation powered at an age of a few decades; their model predicted a turnover below approximately 180 GHz. The ALMA point begins to test that pre-existing prediction. It does not yet discriminate robustly between nebular engines or eliminate contamination and time variability, so simultaneous broadband observations remain the missing empirical test.

08Dynamics of self-gravitating halos with decaying dark matterAnna Bencke, Marc Kamionkowski, Annika H. G. Peter, Juniper Morales

Decaying dark matter reshapes halos through kicks and orbital anisotropy

Paper: Dynamics of self-gravitating halos with decaying dark matter — Anna Bencke, Marc Kamionkowski, Annika H. G. Peter, Juniper Morales. arXiv:2611.05474v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 19:00:02 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. v1 preprint; no journal reference is listed in the checked arXiv record. Classification: simulation · decaying dark matter.

Question and method. In χ→Υ+ a massless noninteracting particle, a small daughter–parent mass difference gives the massive daughter a recoil kick. Spherical shell simulations follow the self-consistent response of initially Hernquist halos, rather than fitting an observed halo or a decay signal.

Result. The main grid spans vkick=0.1–0.5vs and lifetimes τ=0.5–10ts, where vs=GM/rs and ts=rs/vs. A Gaussian-process emulator over 0.1–7.5 scale radii has approximately 1% median interpolation error on the test halos. This is separate from numerical integration error, which can reach several percent or more. For a kick of 0.25vs, changing the initial orbital anisotropy from tangential to radial produces appreciably stronger central depletion: the quoted inner-density retention changes from about 74% to 62% after 5ts.

Limitations. The emulator is calibrated to isolated, spherical, collisionless halos and a finite kick/lifetime grid. Baryons, assembly, subhalo tides and nonspherical structure are absent. No lifetime confidence limit or preferred recoil velocity is inferred; the results describe conditional dynamics.

History

Peter, Moody and Kamionkowski (2010) already showed with N-body simulations that decay-induced kicks can expand halos, reduce their concentrations and remove mass. Their NFW models explored recoil speeds and lifetimes against halo dynamical scales, establishing the heating and escape mechanisms underlying this calculation.

Nadler and Benson (2025) developed a fast semianalytic description using daughter escape and the energy retained by bound particles, including applications to subhalos. The present paper supplies a complementary dynamical calculation, explicitly explores initial orbital anisotropy, and packages its results in an emulator. Its percent-level interpolation performance should not be confused with percent-level predictions for real galaxies: numerical convergence and the omitted baryonic and assembly physics remain separate uncertainties.

09Photon-Counting Readout via Electro-Optic Microwave-to-Optical Transduction for Axion HaloscopesJing Wu, Raphael Cervantes, Doga Murat Kurkcuoglu, Zheshen Zhang, Silvia Zorzetti

Optical photon counting as a proposed axion-haloscope readout

Paper: Photon-Counting Readout via Electro-Optic Microwave-to-Optical Transduction for Axion Haloscopes — Jing Wu, Raphael Cervantes, Doga Murat Kurkcuoglu, Zheshen Zhang, Silvia Zorzetti. arXiv:2611.05444v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 16:18:54 UTC; first arXiv listing 5 November 2026 UTC. Preprint, Fermilab report FERMILAB-PUB-26-0790-LDRD-SQMS; the report number is not a journal reference. Classification: forecast · axion detection.

Question and method. Can electro-optic conversion move an axion-induced microwave photon to an optical detector without losing the advantage of photon counting? The model includes conversion efficiency, thermally generated photons, detector dark counts and pump heating. For cooperativity C, conversion scales as ηT=ζoζm4C/(1+C)2, with ζo and ζm the optical and microwave extraction fractions.

Result. At 9 GHz, for a 50 mK sensing cavity, loaded quality factor 105, assumed 98% optical detection efficiency and 1 Hz dark-count rate, optimized continuous operation gives a 5.13-fold projected scan-rate gain, despite only 1.34% conversion efficiency. The comparison uses a 3 kHz homodyne bandwidth and 100 mK additional amplifier noise. A pulsed benchmark gives 10.1-fold gain and 26.2% conversion efficiency. The two benchmarks have different thermal populations, coupling settings and time windows.

Limitations. This is a proposal and sensitivity calculation, not a demonstrated integrated axion detector or a new exclusion. The gains depend on the baseline receiver noise, pump-induced heating, achievable electro-optic coupling and optical collection. More ambitious gains require simultaneous advances in parameters that are not independent experimentally. No general improvement factor over all photon-counting or quantum-enhanced searches follows.

History

Lamoreaux et al. (2013) showed why counting photons can outperform phase-preserving linear detection at sufficiently high frequency and low temperature: counting avoids vacuum-amplifier noise but still pays for thermal counts and the cavity bandwidth. Their idealized comparison already identified the physical opportunity; the new paper studies a particular conversion architecture with additional technical noise.

Dixit et al. (2021) experimentally used a superconducting qubit for repeated nondemolition measurements of microwave-cavity photons, suppressing readout errors until real residual photons dominated. That result demonstrates a different route to counting, without optical transduction. The new proposal trades direct microwave counting for optical-detector capability, making conversion loss and heating decisive. Its forecast must therefore be compared through a common end-to-end noise and bandwidth model, not through isolated detector efficiencies.

10Near-Quantum-Limited Readout for the Princeton Axion Search using a sub-GHz Kinetic Inductance Traveling Wave Parametric AmplifierJoe Wiedemann, Roman Kolevatov, Jianyang Fu, Joelle-Marie Begin, Nate Otto, Felix St-Amour, Lyman Page, Henry LeDuc, Peter Day, Saptarshi Chaudhuri

A measured sub-GHz amplifier for the Princeton Axion Search

Paper: Near-Quantum-Limited Readout for the Princeton Axion Search using a sub-GHz Kinetic Inductance Traveling Wave Parametric Amplifier — Joe Wiedemann, Roman Kolevatov, Jianyang Fu, Joelle-Marie Begin, Nate Otto, Felix St-Amour, Lyman Page, Henry LeDuc, Peter Day, Saptarshi Chaudhuri. arXiv:2611.06761v1, full text. Leopoldo Staiano, Marialuisa Testa and colleagues, led by Maria Antonietta De Matteis, published An actionable GPCR restores phosphoinositide homeostasis and kidney function in Lowe syndrome in Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy on 4 November 2026. This is a peer-reviewed, open-access preclinical study; it does not report a patient treatment result.

Lowe syndrome is an X-linked disorder caused by loss of the phosphatase OCRL. PI(4,5)P₂ then accumulates on endosomes, traps the megalin receptor and impairs proximal-tubule protein reabsorption, producing low-molecular-weight proteinuria and progressive kidney injury. A high-content screen tested 1,280 pharmacologically active compounds at 10 μM in engineered OCRL-deficient cells. Of 113 primary trafficking hits, 18 advanced to kidney-cell assays and four corrected multiple phenotypes. The team selected IB-MECA, or piclidenoson, an oral adenosine A3-receptor agonist with prior human safety data.

Mechanistically, A3-receptor activation drives phospholipase C to consume plasma-membrane PI(4,5)P₂, indirectly draining the abnormal endosomal pool. The drug restored receptor trafficking, albumin uptake, actin organization and autophagic flux in engineered cells and patient-derived proximal-tubule cells. In OCRL-knockout human kidney organoids, 10 μM for seven days improved albumin uptake without changing the broad nephron-cell composition measured by single-cell RNA sequencing.

The in-vivo test used 15 humanized Lowe-model mice per arm, beginning at eight weeks of age. Drug-containing chow supplied about 100 μg kg⁻¹ day⁻¹ for five months. Treated mice showed sustained reductions in urinary low-molecular-weight protein markers, improved uptake of labelled β-lactoglobulin by proximal tubules and less collagen accumulation, with no reported loss of response or detected adverse phenotype. These endpoints link the cellular mechanism to kidney function, but the model does not reproduce the full human neurological and ocular disease.

The translational step remains deliberately small. The EU register lists an authorised, recruitment-pending phase II study with five planned participants, using six-month renal reabsorption as its primary endpoint (PICLILOWE, 2026-525495-26-00). The paper's extensive cross-model replication is unusually strong for a rare-disease preclinical programme, but mouse proteinuria and organoid uptake cannot establish clinical efficacy, optimal dose or long-term safety in children. The trial will test renal function; whether the approach affects cataracts, neurodevelopment or established chronic kidney damage remains unknown.
