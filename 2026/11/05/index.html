ArXiv Highlights

Isotropy above 100 EeV disfavors a pure-proton sky—but only inside a demanding propagation model

Paper: Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulations — Ryo Higuchi, Eiji Kido and Shigehiro Nagataki. arXiv:2611.05783v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 00:42:11 UTC and first listed 5 November. The arXiv record identifies a 2026 publication in Physical Review D 114, 083040. Classification: composition and source-population inference from public arrival directions plus simulated propagation; not a direct mass measurement.

Question and data. If particles above 100 EeV are mainly protons, their short energy-loss horizon and small magnetic deflections should make nearby large-scale structure visible as close arrival-direction pairs. The authors take the 35 Pierre Auger Phase-I catalogue events above 100 EeV and count pairs separated by at most (3^circ). They find two pairs. In (10^5) isotropic skies with the same event count and Auger directional exposure, that value lies inside the expected distribution; the data therefore show no significant small-scale anisotropy by this statistic.

Propagation and mock skies. The interpretation compares deliberately pure proton, iron and uranium injections. CRPropa supplies proton and iron losses; uranium photodisintegration is calculated with TALYS 2.0 on cosmic-microwave and infrared backgrounds. Sources are sampled from the Millennium Run semi-analytic galaxy catalogue, repeated to cover distances to roughly 500 Mpc. The source density is scanned from (10^{-6}) to (10^{-3} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}). Turbulent deflection is summarized by

α=(λ1 Mpc)1/2(B1 nG),

where (B) is the rms extragalactic field and (lambda) its coherence length. For each grid point and composition, 1,000 mock catalogues of 35 events are tested with the same pair count. A point is called allowed at the paper's one-sided 95% criterion when its median pair count lies inside the corresponding isotropic range. This construction is a simulation acceptance rule, not a likelihood-based confidence interval on composition.

Result. With no magnetic deflection, none of the tested proton or iron source densities reproduces the observed isotropy; uranium can do so for high densities around (10^{-3})–(10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}), because nuclei with smaller Lorentz factor can arrive from a smoother, more distant source distribution. With fields, iron and uranium acquire broad allowed regions. A uranium example becomes compatible near (\rho_{\rm source}sim10^{-4.5} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) for a sufficiently strong turbulent field. Pure protons remain rejected over almost all scanned parameter combinations and survive only for the paper's extreme (alpha=10) field case.

The inference is therefore conditional: the observed statistic is simply consistent with isotropy, while the preference against protons emerges after fixing pure compositions, injection spectra, a galaxy-to-source prescription, energy losses and magnetic transport. It does not measure a uranium fraction or exclude mixed compositions.

Limitations. Thirty-five events give only two close pairs, so the statistic has little resolving power once strong fields erase angular structure. Uranium is an intentionally extreme proxy for nuclei beyond iron; its abundance and acceleration were not fitted. The mock universe uses repeated simulation boxes, one observer realization, a simplified exposure mapping and turbulent fields not tied to filaments, clusters and voids. The regular Galactic field changes inferred source-density limits by about an order of magnitude in the authors' test, and modern Galactic-field models still disagree. Energy-scale uncertainty, hadronic shower modelling and unmodelled mixed composition add further ambiguity.

History

The absence of clustering has constrained source populations before. The Pierre Auger Collaboration's 2013 analysis, arXiv:1305.1576 used events above 60–80 EeV and obtained 95% confidence lower bounds of roughly ((0.06\text{–}5)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) for equal-luminosity, uniformly distributed sources, depending strongly on magnetic smearing; local-matter-tracing cases gave a similar ((0.2\text{–}7)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) range. Those are not directly interchangeable with the new allowed regions because the event threshold, propagation horizon, catalogue and composition hypotheses differ. The new work's useful addition is to ask how much of the isotropy can arise from the longer propagation distance of nuclei heavier than iron, rather than only from a high source density or large deflection.

Independent composition measurements point in the same qualitative direction without identifying uranium. Auger's deep-learning reconstruction of about 50,000 surface-detector showers found the mean mass becoming heavier and its fluctuations narrowing up to 100 EeV (arXiv:2406.06319), subject to hadronic-interaction models. The Telescope Array's large-scale-structure comparison also inferred a very heavy injected composition above 100 EeV even when allowing strong extragalactic fields (arXiv:2406.19287). The present pair-count study is consistent with those baselines, but is less composition-direct than (X_{max}): its remaining uncertainty is the degeneracy among charge, magnetic field, source density and the actual nearby source distribution.

Auger turns Earth-skimming air-shower data into a limit on MeV sterile neutrinos

Paper: A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory — Pierre Auger Collaboration. arXiv:2611.06284v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 10:23:29 UTC and first listed 5 November. v1 collaboration preprint; no journal reference is listed. Classification: model-dependent reinterpretation and 90% confidence flux limit; no sterile-neutrino candidate or detection.

Parameter and signal. The minimal (3+1) benchmark contains a predominantly sterile mass eigenstate (N_1) with mass from the MeV scale to 100 MeV, mixing only with the tau flavour through (sin^2\theta_N). Its weak interaction cross section is suppressed by that factor. At ultrahigh energy, an (N_1) can therefore cross more Earth than an active neutrino, interact through its active component, create a tau lepton, and yield an upward or Earth-skimming air shower when the tau exits and decays.

Method and observations. A Monte Carlo propagates the sterile state through Earth, samples neutrino interactions, follows tau energy loss and decay, and folds the resulting shower through the fluorescence-detector upward-going and surface-detector Earth-skimming exposures. The upward-going sample, covering 1 January 2004–31 December 2018, contains one candidate against (0.27\pm0.12) expected misreconstructed cosmic-ray showers. The Earth-skimming search through 31 December 2021 contains zero candidates. The corresponding 90% signal-count limits are 4.09 and 2.44 events. Tau energy loss dominates the exposure systematic at (+34%/-28%); the published limit incorporates the conservative exposure treatment.

For (sin^2\theta_N\lesssim0.1), an assumed isotropic (E^{-2}) spectrum gives

E2ΦN≤2×10−9sin−2θNGeVcm−2s−1sr−1

at 90% confidence over 0.3–200 EeV. Thus the numerical bound is (2\times10^{-7}) in these units at (sin^2\theta_N=10^{-2}), and (2\times10^{-6}) at (10^{-3}). The inverse mixing dependence reflects transparency: weaker mixing lets more particles cross Earth but makes the final interaction less likely. The Earth-skimming channel supplies most of the sensitivity; sterile fluxes generated only by standard source neutrinos oscillating into (N_1) remain several orders below it.

Conditional dark-matter interpretation. The paper also assumes a (10^{13})-GeV superheavy particle (chi) that decays exclusively into one or several sterile states. At (m_N=10) MeV and (sin^2\theta_N\sim10^{-2}), the (k=1) benchmark gives (\tau_\chi\gtrsim10^{16}) yr at 90% confidence. This is far weaker than typical (sim10^{23})-yr limits on visible superheavy-dark-matter channels because the assumed products are mostly sterile and have low multiplicity. At (m_N=100) MeV, sterile decays transfer energy into active tau neutrinos before arrival. These curves are conditional exclusions in ((sin^2\theta_N,\tau_\chi)), not general dark-matter lifetime bounds.

Limitations. The reported flux bound fixes isotropy and an (E^{-2}) spectrum; differential limits are less assumption-heavy but weaker. The model allows only tau mixing, uses vacuum mixing and ignores additional sterile interactions. The upward-going event is background-compatible, while the zero-event Earth-skimming sample drives the result. Cosmogenic predictions depend on the UHECR source evolution and composition; the dark-matter result additionally fixes mass, halo profile, decay channel and multiplicity. For (m_N) above the pion threshold, photon searches can dominate once (N_1\to\pi^0\nu_\tau) opens.

History

Auger's air-shower technique was established for active neutrinos. Using 2004–2013 data, the collaboration found no candidates and set a single-flavour 90% confidence (E^{-2}) limit of (E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu<6.4\times10^{-9} {\rm GeV,cm^{-2},s^{-1},sr^{-1}}) over (10^{17})–(2.5\times10^{19}) eV (arXiv:1504.05397). The new sterile limit reuses the same physical signatures but recalculates transmission and conversion for a suppressed cross section. Its larger numerical normalization at small mixing is therefore not a weaker repeat of the active-neutrino result; it constrains a different incident particle and explicitly carries the (sin^{-2}\theta_N) penalty.

Sterile propagation through Earth was proposed as an explanation of anomalous upward ANITA showers by Cherry and Shoemaker, who calculated the acceptance for EeV sterile states and showed how reduced mixing could evade Earth opacity (Physical Review D 99, 063016 (2019)). The present collaboration analysis moves from an event-motivated scenario to detector-specific Auger exposure and observed-count limits over a defined energy interval.

Other bounds probe different assumptions. CHARM's direct beam-dump reinterpretation constrains tau-mixed heavy neutral leptons only for roughly 290 MeV–1.6 GeV, above the main mass range here (Physical Review D 104, 095019 (2021)). Big-bang nucleosynthesis, CMB and supernova bounds can be much stronger at 10–100 MeV, but depend on reheating temperature, early-Universe production and stellar-core transport. The Auger limit is less restrictive yet astrophysically independent; the open question is whether nature supplies any ultrahigh-energy sterile flux near its reach.

DESI's halo stars put the Milky Way near 1.2 trillion solar masses

Paper: DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky Way — DESI Collaboration. arXiv:2611.05518v1, full text. Submitted 3 November 2026 at 19:15:56 UTC, after the relevant cutoff, and first listed 5 November. v1 DESI Key Project preprint, submitted to The Astrophysical Journal. Classification: dynamical mass measurement with mock-calibrated systematic uncertainties.

Parameter, data and methods. (M_{200c}) is the mass inside the radius where the mean enclosed density is 200 times the critical density. DESI DR2 supplies six-dimensional phase space for 5,198 blue horizontal-branch stars and 4,838 RR Lyrae, 10,036 tracers in total, spanning about 10–95 kpc from the Galactic centre. Known globular clusters, dwarf galaxies and the Sagittarius stream are removed, and angular selection functions are modelled separately for the two tracers.

Four dynamical estimators—emPDF, GME and two variants of the non-parametric Jeans method NIMBLE—fit the enclosed mass profile or halo parameters. Their biases and correlations are tested on 24 AuriDESI mock surveys: six cosmological Milky-Way analogues viewed from four observer positions, populated and selected to resemble the real samples. The joint hierarchy corrects the measured biases, imposes method-dependent error floors and retains residual correlations rather than treating eight method–tracer estimates as independent.

Result. The joint 68% intervals are

M(<80 kpc)=6.36−0.48+0.53×1011 M⊙,M200c=1.19−0.12+0.14×1012 M⊙.

RR Lyrae alone and blue horizontal-branch stars alone both give (M_{200c}\simeq1.20\times10^{12} M_\odot), an important tracer cross-check. The enclosed mass continues rising from 20 to 80 kpc. Within that range the joint uncertainty stays below about 8%, but the virial value extrapolates beyond most tracers and is more model-sensitive.

The apparently tight virial interval needs context. GME receives about 75% of the joint (M_{200c}) weight because it performs especially well on the mocks. Removing it shifts the combination to (1.52^{+0.35}{-0.30}\times10^{12} M\odot) and doubles the relative uncertainty from roughly 11% to 22%. The paper therefore treats method disagreement as part of the result, rather than presenting the central value as a model-free census.

Limitations. Every estimator assumes approximate equilibrium and spherical symmetry. The six mock haloes sample only a small set of assembly histories, and transfer of their bias calibration to the real Galaxy is untestable. The mocks do not contain an LMC analogue at its present close position; published experiments indicate that the LMC's reflex motion and halo distortion can bias equilibrium estimates by up to about 20% over the radial range used. Proper-motion errors and sparse tracers become limiting beyond 70 kpc. The analysis does not infer the local dark-matter density, which would require a large inward extrapolation and assumptions about baryonic contraction.

History

The trillion-solar-mass scale is consistent with several independent tracers. A simulation-calibrated analysis of ten classical satellite galaxies found (M_{200}=1.17^{+0.21}{-0.15}\times10^{12} M\odot) at 68% confidence (Callingham et al., arXiv:1808.10456). A joint Gaia DR2 rotation-curve and outer-tracer fit, including baryonic contraction, obtained (M_{200}^{\rm total}=1.08^{+0.20}{-0.14}\times10^{12} M\odot) (Cautun et al., arXiv:1911.04557). DESI does not dramatically move that established baseline; its advance is the size of the distant standard-candle samples, the use of four methods on common mocks, and an explicit correlated combination.

The live dispute is with low masses inferred by extrapolating the declining Gaia DR3 disc rotation curve. Ou et al. fit a cored Einasto halo to data reaching about 30 kpc and obtained a dark-halo virial mass (1.81^{+0.06}{-0.05}\times10^{11} M\odot), while warning that the low value is driven by the chosen extrapolating profile (arXiv:2303.12838). Jiao et al. reported a roughly 30 km s⁻¹ decline from 19.5 to 26.5 kpc and a total mass (2.06^{+0.24}{-0.13}\times10^{11} M\odot) (arXiv:2309.00048). DESI's mass continues rising well beyond that disc range and strongly favours (M_{200c}\gtrsim10^{12} M_\odot), suggesting that disequilibrium, asymmetric-drift or extrapolation systematics affect the low-mass solutions. It does not by itself identify which correction is responsible; LMC dynamics and non-spherical structure remain the central uncertainties.

EP261103a fades in red light, but its physical class remains open

The first follow-ups after the previous cutoff sharpen the optical behaviour of EP261103a, the Einstein Probe transient triggered on 3 November at 20:18:26 UTC. BOOTES-6 began at 20:24:58 UTC, 396 s after the trigger, and found no source to 15.0 mag in 10 s or 18.8 mag in a later 420-s clear-band stack; those limits are too shallow to conflict with the faint counterpart. GCN 46144.

SVOM/VT detected the candidate only in its red channel at a midpoint 2.19 h after trigger, measuring VT_R = 23.4 ± 0.3 AB mag and VT_B > 23.6 AB mag (3σ). GCN 46145. NTT/EFOSC2 later measured r = 24.10 ± 0.13 AB at 8.67 h and z > 23 AB (3σ) at 9.3 h. GCN 46148. Together with the earlier NOT value (r=23.86\pm0.25), the red-band observations are consistent with modest fading, although filters and calibration differ. A fading, red optical transient strengthens the counterpart case; without a spectrum, secure host, X-ray evolution or chance-coincidence calculation, it does not establish a GRB, tidal-disruption event or other source class.

GRB 261104A: a 40-second SVOM burst with no optical counterpart to r = 22.31

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on GRB 261104A at 4 November, 20:36:44 UTC. The best 5–20 keV image reached S/N 8.02 over 20.48 s and localized the burst to RA 6.2999°, Dec −54.4612°, with a 9.81-arcmin 90% confidence radius including a 2-arcmin systematic. The light curve shows one broad peak with (T_{90}\approx40) s, making it duration-consistent with the long-burst class; refined spectroscopy was not yet supplied. GCN 46150, version 2.

MASTER-SAAO began 2,228 s after the trigger and reached clear-band limits of 19.5–19.7 mag. GCN 46152. LCO/Sinistro started 1.24 h after trigger and found no new source in three 200-s exposures, giving r > 22.31 AB mag at 5σ and 1.33 h. GCN 46154. These are optical non-detections over the reported error box, not evidence that the burst had no afterglow; extinction, redshift, cadence and position-dependent depth remain unconstrained.

EP261015a / SN 2026ahoa continues brightening at the tentative z = 0.31

MMT/Binospec imaged the proposed optical counterpart of EP261015a on 3 November at 06:21 UTC, 18.7 d after the beginning of the Einstein Probe light curve. Difference imaging gives r = 21.56 ± 0.03 AB mag, continuing the previously reported rise. If the tentative spectroscopic redshift (z=0.31) is correct, the authors infer (M_r\sim-19.4) at a rest-frame age of 14.3 d, comparable at that epoch to the broad-lined Type Ic SN 1998bw, and a projected offset 3.8 ± 0.3 kpc from the host centre. GCN 46153.

This strengthens the supernova interpretation of the optical transient, already spectroscopically classified as a likely pre-peak Type Ic-BL. The redshift remains tentative, and temporal and positional consistency do not alone prove that the supernova produced the earlier X-ray transient.

A repurposed GPCR agonist corrects Lowe-syndrome kidney defects from cells to mice

Leopoldo Staiano, Marialuisa Testa and colleagues, led by Maria Antonietta De Matteis, published An actionable GPCR restores phosphoinositide homeostasis and kidney function in Lowe syndrome in Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy on 4 November 2026. This is a peer-reviewed, open-access preclinical study; it does not report a patient treatment result.

Lowe syndrome is an X-linked disorder caused by loss of the phosphatase OCRL. PI(4,5)P₂ then accumulates on endosomes, traps the megalin receptor and impairs proximal-tubule protein reabsorption, producing low-molecular-weight proteinuria and progressive kidney injury. A high-content screen tested 1,280 pharmacologically active compounds at 10 μM in engineered OCRL-deficient cells. Of 113 primary trafficking hits, 18 advanced to kidney-cell assays and four corrected multiple phenotypes. The team selected IB-MECA, or piclidenoson, an oral adenosine A3-receptor agonist with prior human safety data.

Mechanistically, A3-receptor activation drives phospholipase C to consume plasma-membrane PI(4,5)P₂, indirectly draining the abnormal endosomal pool. The drug restored receptor trafficking, albumin uptake, actin organization and autophagic flux in engineered cells and patient-derived proximal-tubule cells. In OCRL-knockout human kidney organoids, 10 μM for seven days improved albumin uptake without changing the broad nephron-cell composition measured by single-cell RNA sequencing.

The in-vivo test used 15 humanized Lowe-model mice per arm, beginning at eight weeks of age. Drug-containing chow supplied about 100 μg kg⁻¹ day⁻¹ for five months. Treated mice showed sustained reductions in urinary low-molecular-weight protein markers, improved uptake of labelled β-lactoglobulin by proximal tubules and less collagen accumulation, with no reported loss of response or detected adverse phenotype. These endpoints link the cellular mechanism to kidney function, but the model does not reproduce the full human neurological and ocular disease.

The translational step remains deliberately small. The EU register lists an authorised, recruitment-pending phase II study with five planned participants, using six-month renal reabsorption as its primary endpoint (PICLILOWE, 2026-525495-26-00). The paper's extensive cross-model replication is unusually strong for a rare-disease preclinical programme, but mouse proteinuria and organoid uptake cannot establish clinical efficacy, optimal dose or long-term safety in children. The trial will test renal function; whether the approach affects cataracts, neurodevelopment or established chronic kidney damage remains unknown.
