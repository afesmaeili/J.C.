01First broadband search for fixed-polarization dark photon dark matter with GigaBREADJialin Yu, Stefan Knirck, Gabe Hoshino, Rakshya Khatiwada, Alex Lapuente, David W. Miller and Andrew Sonnenschein

GigaBREAD uses Earth's rotation to search for a fixed dark-photon polarization

Paper: First broadband search for fixed-polarization dark photon dark matter with GigaBREAD — Jialin Yu, Stefan Knirck, Gabe Hoshino, Rakshya Khatiwada, Alex Lapuente, David W. Miller and Andrew Sonnenschein. arXiv:2611.07426v1, full text. Submitted 5 November 2026 at 04:20:25 UTC and first listed 6 November. v1 Fermilab preprint, report FERMILAB-PUB-26-0792-ETD-PPD; no journal publication is listed. Classification: experimental reanalysis with Bayesian credible and frequentist confidence limits; no dark-photon detection.

Parameter and signal. A dark photon of mass (m_{\rm DP}) kinetically mixed with the ordinary photon by the dimensionless parameter (chi) can convert into a radio photon at a conducting surface. Unlike axions, this conversion needs no applied magnetic field. If vector dark matter was produced by a misalignment-like mechanism, its polarization may remain approximately fixed in celestial coordinates. The signal in a detector sensitive along the axis D^(t) is then

Pdet(t)=P0|X^·D^(t)|2,

so Earth's sidereal rotation supplies a predictable modulation rather than the constant factor ⟨cos2ϑ⟩=1/3 used for random polarization.

Data and analysis. GigaBREAD converts dark photons on a vertical cylindrical conductor of effective area about 0.5 m² and focuses the emitted 10.7–12.5 GHz radiation into a horn. The reanalysis combines a 24-day, zenith-aligned University of Chicago run with 12,970 spectra and a 27-day, horizontally aligned Argonne run with 12,481 spectra. Each spectrum contains 230,392 bins at 7.8 kHz resolution. The reference conversion assumes a local dark-matter density of 0.45 GeV cm⁻³.

The likelihood retains each spectrum's time stamp and matches both the frequency profile and the sidereal polarization pattern. Because absolute timing was not preserved across the 2023 sub-runs, the polarization azimuth is marginalized independently for each sub-run, while the celestial latitude remains common. Bayesian evidence and a profile-likelihood statistic are calibrated with 300 full noise simulations, covering 1,151,965 mass templates. The effective frequentist trials factor is about 5×105. Limits incorporate a 23% power systematic inherited from the detector calibration.

Result. The observed global Bayesian statistic is k0global=−2.5, below the paper's 90% candidate threshold of −2.3; the frequentist scan likewise contains no globally significant mass. The authors report conservative limits by taking the weaker of their Bayesian 90% credible and frequentist 90% confidence upper limits at every mass. Over 44–52 μeV, the resulting fixed-polarization limit is at the level of

χ~10−12.

It is approximately 20% lower in χ than a naive fixed-polarization rescaling of the earlier GigaBREAD result and is comparable to, or slightly stronger than, the earlier random-polarization limit. The improvement comes from using the timing information and a second detector orientation. It applies to a fixed but unknown polarization; it is not a general improvement for every dark-photon production model.

Limitations. The result assumes that polarization stays coherent and fixed over the observation span and that the Standard Halo Model describes the spectral line. The missing absolute phase across 2023 sub-runs removes some directional information. Instrumental baselines are removed with a Savitzky–Golay filter, and the analysis relies on Monte Carlo calibration of a non-Wilks statistic. The constraint also scales with the assumed local density and conversion efficiency. No mass exceeded the candidate threshold, so the work supplies exclusions rather than evidence for a modulated signal.

History

The original GigaBREAD search used the 24-day 2023 data set and treated dark-photon polarization as random. It excluded roughly χ≳10−12 at 90% confidence over the same 44–52 μeV interval and improved previous bounds by about two orders of magnitude below 49 μeV (Knirck et al., arXiv:2310.13891, Physical Review Letters 132, 131004). The new paper does not gain an order of magnitude or open a new mass range. Its substantive advance is a statistically consistent reinterpretation for fixed polarization, plus the combination of vertical and horizontal runs.

Dish searches such as SHUKET, QUALIPHIDE, DOSUE and BRASS-p constrain overlapping or neighboring masses with different receivers, geometries and polarization conventions. Resonant haloscopes including HAYSTAC, ADMX and ORGAN can also constrain dark photons after a polarization assumption is chosen, but their scan strategy and resonant response are different. Improvement factors should therefore be quoted only against the previous GigaBREAD fixed-polarization rescaling. The main remaining uncertainty is physical: structure formation may partially randomize a primordial vector direction, in which case neither the perfectly fixed nor perfectly isotropic limit is the complete description.

02Systematic Study of Cosmic Rays in Star-Forming and Starburst Galaxies using Fermi-LAT and Multiwavelength DataHinako Ochi, Tsunefumi Mizuno, Yasushi Fukazawa, Noemi Barnier, Susumu Inoue, Wataru Iwakiri, Junling Chen and Tomonori Totani

Fifteen years of Fermi data put starbursts at or below the cosmic-ray calorimetric limit

Paper: Systematic Study of Cosmic Rays in Star-Forming and Starburst Galaxies using Fermi-LAT and Multiwavelength Data — Hinako Ochi, Tsunefumi Mizuno, Yasushi Fukazawa, Noemi Barnier, Susumu Inoue, Wataru Iwakiri, Junling Chen and Tomonori Totani. arXiv:2611.07189v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 22:25:01 UTC and first listed 6 November. The manuscript is accepted by the Publications of the Astronomical Society of Japan following peer review. Classification: observational gamma-ray analysis with conditional one-zone cosmic-ray modelling.

Question and data. Star-forming galaxies accelerate cosmic rays, whose hadronic interactions with gas produce gamma rays and neutrinos. A proton calorimeter loses nearly all of the relevant cosmic-ray power through collisions before escape. The authors ask how close ordinary star-forming galaxies and compact starbursts come to this limit, and whether their radio-to-TeV spectra require unusual electron-to-proton ratios.

The analysis uses 15 years of Fermi-LAT data and updated Galactic diffuse and point-source models for seven galaxies: M82, NGC 253, M31, Arp 220, NGC 2146, Arp 299 and M33. M31 is decomposed into central and southeast components. Spectra are fitted from 50 MeV to about 100 GeV, then combined with published radio and TeV measurements in NAIMA-based one-zone models.

Results. M82 and NGC 253 are detected with test statistics 1702 and 899 and show mild curvature. Their 0.1–100 GeV energy fluxes are 8.19×10−12 and 6.31×10−12 ergcm−2s−1. Both spectra turn down below a few hundred MeV, reducing the electron bremsstrahlung contribution relative to earlier models. The inferred electron-to-proton ratios are 1/25–1/30 at 1 GeV and 1/120–1/150 at 10 GeV, close to canonical Milky-Way values.

The proton energy densities are about 190 eV cm⁻³ in M82 and 470 eV cm⁻³ in NGC 253, respectively around eight and 2.5 times below the magnetic energy densities used in the fits. This argues against cosmic-ray–magnetic equipartition inside these one-zone starburst models.

For M31, a compact central component has a soft curved spectrum and a 95% upper limit of 0.33° on its radius. Unresolved sources can therefore contribute substantially, so its luminosity is treated as an upper limit on interstellar emission. M33 is similarly complicated by an offset toward the young region NGC 604.

Revised fluxes for NGC 2146 and Arp 220 are approximately a factor of two lower than in the 2020 comparison analysis. Across the starburst sample, the observed ratio of gamma-ray luminosity to the calorimetric prediction is 0.33–1.20; the value slightly above unity is within the combined modelling and source uncertainties, rather than proof of super-calorimetry. Ordinary star-forming galaxies lie around the 10% level, except for the SMC, where point sources are likely important.

Limitations. Calorimetric efficiency depends on infrared luminosity, gas density, source subtraction and the assumed supernova-to-cosmic-ray conversion. Several weak galaxies have TS~50, making their spectra sensitive to the diffuse foreground. M31 and M33 contain unresolved or localized components that are not clean measurements of diffuse cosmic-ray interactions. The multiwavelength fits assume one-zone geometry, adopted magnetic fields and published gas masses; the apparent departure from equipartition is consequently model-dependent. The work characterizes detected emission but does not directly measure the neutrino output or isolate hadronic photons event by event.

History

Fermi-LAT first reported M82 and NGC 253 at 6.8σ and 4.8σ with 11 months of data in 2010, establishing a connection between massive-star formation and GeV emission. A later uniform study using about ten years of LAT data detected 11 bona-fide galaxies and two candidates, plus a stacked below-threshold population, and estimated that star-forming galaxies contribute about 5% of the extragalactic gamma-ray background and 3% of the IceCube astrophysical-neutrino flux (Ajello et al., arXiv:2003.05493). Those population fractions are model estimates, not direct source associations.

The new analysis updates individual nearby systems rather than remeasuring the total background. Its most consequential changes are the sub-GeV downturn in M82 and NGC 253, the factor-of-two downward revisions for NGC 2146 and Arp 220, and explicit source-confusion cautions for M31 and M33. It supports the established picture that compact starbursts are efficient hadronic targets, while removing the need for several systems to sit clearly above the calorimetric relation. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by gas, magnetic-field and unresolved-source modelling, which also propagates into forecasts of the diffuse neutrino intensity.

03General-relativistic neutrino-radiation hydrodynamics of crust ejection in magnetar giant flaresTetyana Pitik, David Radice, Jacob Fields and Yi Qiu

General-relativistic simulations sharply reduce magnetar-flare r-process yields

Paper: General-relativistic neutrino-radiation hydrodynamics of crust ejection in magnetar giant flares — Tetyana Pitik, David Radice, Jacob Fields and Yi Qiu. arXiv:2611.07022v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 19:00:06 UTC and first listed 6 November. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: one-dimensional numerical simulation and conditional nucleosynthesis forecast, not an observed yield.

Question and method. The radio afterglow of the 27 December 2004 giant flare from SGR 1806−20 implies at least about 10−9M⊙ of baryonic ejecta. Previous hydrodynamic and analytic calculations proposed that a magnetospheric fireball drives a shock into the neutron-star crust, ejecting neutron-rich matter that undergoes r-process nucleosynthesis. This paper tests that chain with general-relativistic hydrodynamics, a tabulated HS(DD2) nuclear equation of state, M1 neutrino transport and a SkyNet reaction network.

The simulations use a fixed spherical neutron-star spacetime and a one-kilometre, whole-surface pressure layer. Its peak pressure is labelled by the equivalent field B=1015–1016.5 G through PGF=B2/8π. Every model is repeated without neutrino interactions, and the layer width, position and shape are varied. These labels describe the imposed pressure; they are not measurements of an actual flare field.

Results. The models unbind approximately

10−10−5×10−8M⊙

of outer crust with mass-weighted asymptotic velocities 0.44–0.52 c. Only about 10−3 of the imposed fireball energy becomes ejecta kinetic energy. Neutrino cooling matters strongly only at the most extreme 1016.5-G pressure: it removes about a tenth of the layer energy while the shock is active and halves the ejecta mass. The ejecta retain Ye≃0.44–0.47.

The rapid, high-entropy expansion makes very few seed nuclei. Roughly 90% of the ejecta becomes alpha particles, while 7–10% remains as free neutrons. Even the strongest model produces at most 3.5×10−11M⊙ in nuclei with A≥90, three to four orders of magnitude below the earlier analytic calculation. Lanthanides and actinides remain below about 3×10−5 of the ejecta mass.

For the extreme model at 10 kpc, the authors forecast about two Super-Kamiokande and 15 Hyper-Kamiokande inverse-beta events, but this flare is far more energetic than the 2004 event. Flares with observed energies should yield no detectable neutrino count. Free-neutron decay instead powers a predicted ultraviolet/optical flash peaking after roughly 40–55 seconds; this is a model forecast, not an observed nova brevis.

Connection to the 2004 flare. Models with B=1015.5–1016 G eject enough mass at about 0.45 c to resemble the radio afterglow, but their whole-surface fireball energies are 60–600 times the roughly 3×1046 erg prompt spike. Scaling to that observed energy gives only (2–3.5)×10−11M⊙, 50–100 times less than the radio afterglow requires. The authors therefore conclude that a one-dimensional crust shock cannot by itself supply the observed baryons unless the flare released far more total energy or the true magnetic geometry couples energy much more efficiently.

Limitations. Spherical symmetry, instantaneous whole-surface heating and omission of magnetic forces probably overestimate ejection. Real flares are localized, laterally expanding and threaded by open and closed field lines. The deepest, marginally unbound shells dominate the already small heavy-element yield, leaving it uncertain by about a factor of two for A≥90 and more for lanthanides. The fixed spacetime, one-millisecond evolution and analytic continuation of trajectories add further approximations. Multidimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamics is required before treating these values as physical rate predictions.

History

Cehula, Thompson and Metzger modelled the same shock-ejection mechanism with special-relativistic hydrodynamics and a simplified crust. For pressures around 1030–1031 ergcm−3, they obtained 1025–1026 g of ejecta at 0.3–0.6 c, sufficient for the 2004 radio afterglow, and estimated a Galactic r-process contribution near 10−9M⊙yr−1 (arXiv:2311.05681, MNRAS 528, 5323).

Patel et al. then applied an analytic ejecta profile and reaction network, predicting moderate third-peak production, a stronger light r-process and a 10–15 minute, 1039–1040 ergs−1 nova brevis for an SGR 1806−20-like ejecta mass (arXiv:2501.17253). A separate analysis interpreted delayed MeV emission from the 2004 event as decay of roughly 10−6M⊙ of fresh r-process material (arXiv:2501.09181).

The new calculation directly conflicts with that high-yield interpretation within its tested mechanism: its largest mass in nuclei heavier than alpha particles is about 8×10−11M⊙, four orders of magnitude below the delayed-MeV requirement. The discrepancy comes mainly from hydrodynamics: deep, cooler crust assumed to escape in the analytic profile falls back in the general-relativistic simulations. This does not disprove that the 2004 MeV component was real or radioactive; it shows that the proposed spherical crust-shock model cannot produce the inferred mass under the new transport and equation-of-state treatment. Geometry, magnetic stresses and an alternative baryon-loading channel remain open.

04DIS-secting the universe with high-energy neutrinosRhorry Gauld, Lorenzo Guerra, Ulrich Haisch, Alessio Vorona

Electroweak radiation changes the inelasticity of high-energy neutrino interactions

Paper: DIS-secting the universe with high-energy neutrinos — Rhorry Gauld, Lorenzo Guerra, Ulrich Haisch, Alessio Vorona. arXiv:2611.08221v1, full text. Submitted 5 November 2026 at 16:09:18 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: precision interaction calculation and event-generator study.

Question and method. Neutrino telescopes infer energy and interaction inelasticity from the division between an outgoing charged lepton and the hadronic cascade. Photons radiated by the lepton change this division even when the inclusive interaction rate changes little. Gauld and collaborators calculate fully differential charged- and neutral-current deep-inelastic scattering with next-to-leading-order QCD and electroweak corrections, finite charged-lepton masses and higher-order lepton radiation. Photon-initiated processes, including coherent nuclear emission and resonant W production, are included. The calculation spans incident energies from about 40 GeV to 109 GeV and is compared with POWHEG interfaced to three Pythia shower prescriptions.

Results. For a bare outgoing muon, y=1−Eμ/Eν. At Eν=108 GeV, electroweak effects increase the mean inelasticity by approximately 10% without photon-initiated channels and 15% when they are included. The inclusive cross-section correction is only a few percent: inclusiveness cancels large radiative effects that survive in the energy partition. In the paper's atmospheric-flux-weighted neutrino/antineutrino illustration, the mean-inelasticity change reaches roughly 20% at 109 GeV, whereas it remains below about 3% below 104 GeV. These are theoretical predictions, not measured IceCube energy biases.

The shower comparison is useful operationally. Agreement is good over much of the lower-energy range, but different showers and missing high-energy channels can produce larger differences in the tails; deviations above 10% occur at high inelasticity in some comparisons. The authors provide a prescription that preserves DIS variables when matching radiation and offer differential grids on request.

Limitations. The baseline uses NNPDF3.1 NLO partons and a low-scale cutoff Q0=1.65 GeV. Free-nucleon structure and an oxygen coherent-photon contribution do not constitute a complete nuclear-PDF treatment. Small-x resummation, nuclear modifications and the missing neutron photon input remain separate uncertainties. No full detector simulation, reconstruction efficiency or electromagnetic-versus-hadronic light-yield response is included. The largest relative shifts occur at energies with very few observed events; the illustrative falling astrophysical spectrum is dominated by much lower energies. Neither a universal 20% correction to existing measurements nor percent-level total theoretical accuracy follows.

History

Plestid and Zhou, arXiv:2403.07984v1, already showed that final-state radiation moves energy from tracks into showers and can substantially distort telescope inelasticity distributions. Their leading-logarithmic treatment used a lepton splitting function and an inclusive hadronic cascade; it also explicitly left detector efficiencies and differing light yields outside the calculation. The new work supplies the full electroweak calculation and photon-initiated channels behind that qualitative warning. It finds that a logarithm based on the full neutrino–nucleon energy can overestimate the radiation contribution relative to the appropriate partonic scale: in the stated 108-GeV comparison the earlier approximation gives about 15% instead of approximately 10%. This is a comparison of specified theoretical observables, not a revision to an observed neutrino flux. The outstanding task is to propagate these corrections consistently through each experiment's event simulation and likelihood.

05Testing the Pseudo-Dirac Nature of Atmospheric NeutrinosOlivia M. Bitter, André de Gouvêa, Srubabati Goswami, Supriya Pan

Atmospheric neutrinos test a pseudo-Dirac third mass eigenstate

Paper: Testing the Pseudo-Dirac Nature of Atmospheric Neutrinos — Olivia M. Bitter, André de Gouvêa, Srubabati Goswami, Supriya Pan. arXiv:2611.07281v1, full text. Submitted 5 November 2026 at 01:03:33 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: oscillation-sensitivity forecast.

Physical question. A small Majorana component can split an otherwise Dirac neutrino into two nearly degenerate states. The paper allows only the third active state to have a maximally mixed sterile partner, with

Δm33′2=2ϵΔm312≃5×10−3ϵeV2.
Atmospheric neutrinos span many Earth baselines and therefore test matter-modified active–sterile oscillations unavailable to a single accelerator baseline.

Method and result. The forecast adopts a DUNE-like 200 kt yr exposure, Honda atmospheric fluxes for Homestake, argon interactions in GLoBES, a five-layer Earth-density approximation and reconstructed energy/zenith bins spanning 0.2–10.5 GeV. Energy smearing and strongly energy-dependent angular resolution are included. Under the more conservative 50° low-energy angular resolution, the projected 3σ sensitivity is ϵ≲0.04, or Δm33′2≲2×10−4 meV2. With an optimistic 15° resolution the corresponding numbers are about 0.012 and 6×10−5 meV2. This is simulated sensitivity, not a DUNE exclusion.

Assumptions. The headline curves fix normal ordering and standard oscillation parameters, including θ23=43.3∘ and δCP=0. Flux normalization, zenith dependence, cross section, general normalization and spectral tilt enter through systematic pulls. Separate profiling exercises for θ23 or Δm312 weaken sensitivity; they are not a simultaneous marginalization over the full oscillation and detector model. Atmospheric direction reconstruction below 1 GeV is particularly influential. The other two pseudo-Dirac splittings are fixed to zero, and the setup is not an official collaboration sensitivity forecast.

History

The relevant baseline depends on which mass eigenstate is split. Ansarifard and Farzan, arXiv:2211.09105v1, fitted Borexino and Super-Kamiokande solar data, profiling the solar mixing angle and including flux uncertainties: their two-sigma upper scales are approximately 1.5×10−12 and 2×10−11 meV2 for the first and second pairs. Solar data have little leverage on the third pair, so those numbers cannot be advertised as directly comparable exclusions of this paper's parameter.

Astrophysical baselines probe much smaller splittings but introduce source assumptions. Rink and Sen, arXiv:2211.16520v1, obtained a nominal 90% NGC 1068 bound near 1.1×10−18 meV2 in a common-splitting setup, using a power-law spectrum and a correlated normalization/index constraint tied to the IceCube fit. That assumption about the unoscillated source is crucial and differs from a freely varying third-pair atmospheric analysis. Anamiati et al., arXiv:1907.00980v1, already demonstrated quasi-Dirac degeneracies in DUNE/JUNO forecasts. The new contribution is the atmospheric energy–zenith calculation and its reconstruction requirements; it does not establish the strongest existing bound on pseudo-Dirac neutrinos.

06Probing Dark Matter-Electron Scattering with the Central Molecular ZoneThong T.Q. Nguyen, Shyam Balaji, Carlos Blanco, Pedro De la Torre Luque

Dense Galactic-centre gas as a conditional test of dark-matter–electron scattering

Paper: Probing Dark Matter-Electron Scattering with the Central Molecular Zone — Thong T.Q. Nguyen, Shyam Balaji, Carlos Blanco, Pedro De la Torre Luque. arXiv:2611.07008v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 19:00:01 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: phenomenological rate constraints with assumed cloud properties.

Parameter and method. The target is the reference electron-scattering cross section σ¯e, in cm², defined at momentum transfer q=αme. The calculation follows dark-matter ionization of molecular hydrogen near the Galactic centre for heavy-mediator, electric-dipole and ultralight-mediator form factors FDM=1,αme/q,(αme/q)2. A dense cloud associated with IRS 13E3, at an assumed distance of roughly 0.4 pc from Sgr A, provides a high-density, high-velocity dark-matter environment. The adopted molecular ionization threshold is 15.43 eV*.

What is constrained. The paper requires the predicted ionization rate not to exceed 2×10−15 ms−1. Crucially, this is an assumed ceiling, not an H₃⁺ ionization measurement of IRS 13E3. The nominal cloud column, density, extent and Galactic dark-matter profiles are also fixed inputs. At 0.4 pc, the adopted NFW, Einasto and Moore profiles give dark-matter densities of about 1.1×104, 730 and 4.8×104 mGeVcm−3. The exclusion curves therefore have no frequentist or Bayesian confidence level attached to a cloud-data likelihood.

Results and qualifications. For an ultralight mediator and a 0.05% dark-matter fraction, the fiducial calculation reaches a region above the shielding-limited upper edge of the JWST direct-search band. At 0.4%, JWST already covers the relevant proposed region more strongly. A dominant heavy-dark-photon component is constrained more strongly by cosmology; the paper finds no new allowed-region coverage for its fiducial subdominant heavy leptophilic example. Electric-dipole curves reach reference cross sections around 10−25 mcm2 at masses of tens of MeV, but the seemingly new region can require a dipole interaction outside the simple point-particle effective theory's validity.

Changing the adopted ionization ceiling to 7×10−15 ms−1 weakens a rate-linear bound by 3.5; moving the cloud to 1 pc weakens the fiducial reach by roughly an order of magnitude. Cloud attenuation and the finite incident dark-matter energy budget invalidate portions of otherwise attractive large-cross-section curves. The H₂ ionization approximation itself carries order-unity uncertainty. These sensitivities are physical assumptions, not marginalized error bands.

History

Molecular-cloud constraints, arXiv:2211.05787v1, used chemical tracers in cold clouds such as L1551 and L63 to constrain an added ionization source. Their very low inferred rates depend on molecular abundances, depletion and grain chemistry; the conservative dark-matter requirement is a rate cap rather than a formal confidence interval. The Galactic-centre proposal trades those low rates for a much higher assumed density and velocity distribution, introducing a different set of dominant uncertainties.

The JWST electron-scattering analysis, arXiv:2412.13131v1, instead fitted NIRSpec detector backgrounds with a 95% profile-likelihood construction, marginalizing detector nuisance parameters and modelling spacecraft shielding. Its assumed local density, material response and subcomponent fractions must be matched before comparison. The present cloud curves do not supersede the strongest laboratory or cosmological bounds generally: their distinctive opportunity is a specific large-cross-section, subdominant-dark-matter regime that requires both a suitable cloud and valid propagation modelling.

07Latest νs from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWSTRanjini Mondol, Priyank Parashari, Anirban Das, Vera Gluscevic, Ranjan Laha

JWST ultraviolet galaxies constrain delayed neutrino free streaming

Paper: Latest νs from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWST — Ranjini Mondol, Priyank Parashari, Anirban Das, Vera Gluscevic, Ranjan Laha. arXiv:2611.07013v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 19:00:02 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: cosmological parameter inference.

Question and method. A contact neutrino self-interaction with strength Geff, in MeV⁻², delays neutrino free streaming and changes the matter power spectrum. The authors connect this to the abundance of early galaxies, using a modified Boltzmann calculation, a halo mass function and a five-parameter star-formation prescription calibrated with Thesan-Zoom simulations. The likelihood combines high-redshift JWST ultraviolet luminosity functions with Planck 2018 CMB data; the methods also describe a Pantheon supernova likelihood. The galaxy analysis uses z≥9, including very-high-redshift photometric candidates that are not all spectroscopically confirmed.

Results. In the stated Bayesian model, the reported 95% upper limits are

log10(Geff/MeV−2)<−2.75(one interacting flavour),<−3.41(three interacting flavours).
The posterior samples six cosmological parameters, the summed neutrino mass and astrophysical nuisance parameters. The neutrino mass is restricted to 0.06–1 eV and the coupling has a log-uniform prior from −6 to −2. For the three-flavour case, the quoted Planck-only result is approximately −3.29; the one-flavour Planck-only limit approaches the upper prior boundary. The useful new information is sensitivity to the small-scale matter distribution traced by high-redshift galaxies, around k~1–10 mMpc−1.

Limitations. Converting luminosity into halo mass requires a star-formation efficiency, scatter, dust/selection assumptions and a model for halo growth. Here the efficiency parameters are redshift-independent and parts of the calibration come from cold-dark-matter simulations. The supplement lists Gaussian priors on As and ns derived from CMB information alongside the Planck likelihood; this raises a correlation/double-counting concern unless their independence is established. A table and the methods do not describe the supernova combination identically. Consequently, the numerical limit should be attached to the stated implementation, not interpreted as an assumption-free JWST measurement. The four-fermion description also requires the mediator to be sufficiently heavy over the relevant epoch.

History

JWST is not the first small-scale probe of this interaction. He et al., arXiv:2503.15592v1, combined Planck and CMB lensing with an effective-field-theory analysis of the BOSS/eBOSS Lyman-α forest and obtained log10(Geff/MeV−2)<−4.4 at 95% confidence in their three-flavour contact model. Their calculation fixes Neff=3.046, varies the neutrino mass and uses simulation-informed bias relations; treatments of intergalactic gas and reionization affect the inference. This is numerically stronger than −3.41, although the different likelihoods and astrophysical priors prevent a clean improvement-factor comparison. The useful feature of the JWST result is an independent tracer with different systematics.

Laboratory and BBN analysis, arXiv:1905.02727v1, shows why translating Geff~g2/mϕ2 into particle parameters is flavour- and mediator-dependent: electron and muon couplings face different decay constraints, while a tau-dominated interaction can evade some of them. The galaxy limit does not remove these ultraviolet-completion requirements or establish a detection of self-interacting neutrinos.

08Signatures of Neutrinophilic Pion Dark MatterClaire Chevallier, Ji-Heng Guo, Giacomo Landini

Neutrinophilic dark pions connect relic abundance to MeV-neutrino searches

Paper: Signatures of Neutrinophilic Pion Dark Matter — Claire Chevallier, Ji-Heng Guo, Giacomo Landini. arXiv:2611.08440v1, full text. Submitted 5 November 2026 at 18:31:52 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: particle model and approximate data reinterpretation.

Model. A confining dark sector produces pion dark matter and communicates with neutrinos through an inverse-seesaw construction. The simplest annihilation coupling is tied to the small neutrino mass and is insufficient to meet both the thermal-relic and self-interaction requirements at low pion masses. The authors introduce an additional light pseudoscalar, allowing semi-annihilation ππ→πa followed by neutrino decays, or an off-shell three-body process. Its near-degeneracy with the pion determines the spectral shape. This requires a tuned light state; it is not a generic prediction of all dark-pion theories.

Super-Kamiokande reinterpretation. Using more than 6000 days of published SK data above 17.3 MeV, a reconstructed binned likelihood and a Galactic annihilation J factor, the paper obtains local preferences of 2.49σ or 2.52σ for its two illustrative mass splittings. These are weak, model-dependent fits, not evidence for dark matter, and the best-fitting points do not reproduce the abundance through standard thermal freeze-out.

A separate thermal benchmark near 50 MeV uses a dimensionless all-sky J=17, normalized to 4πR0ρ02 with R0=8.277 kpc and ρ0=0.4 mGeVcm−3, and J⟨σv⟩≃1.4×10−24 mcm3s−1. It yields a local preference near 2.05σ. The interval J≈4–17 comes from a separate rotation-curve halo fit; choosing its upper end is a benchmark assumption, not marginalizing the SK fit over halo uncertainty.

Forecast and limitations. The inference varies cosmological parameters and two intrinsic-alignment parameters within the stated flat priors. PBH priors are log10(M/M⊙)∈[8,18] and log10f∈[−11,−3]; the baryon density, acoustic angular scale, scalar amplitude and tilt have relatively narrow permitted intervals. The halo growth, profile and compensating radius are fixed rather than marginalized. The likelihood description does not demonstrate full marginalization over all DES photometric-redshift and shear-calibration nuisance parameters.

Limitations. The conclusions depend on the existence and survival of the assumed isolated haloes, their compensation and the nonlinear mapping used for the added spectrum. Halo overlap, tidal stripping and the sparse sampling/non-Gaussian covariance of the most massive objects require more work. At the upper mass end, the idealized halo prescription extrapolates to extraordinarily large structures. This is a test of a specified PBH-plus-halo population, not a formation-independent abundance exclusion with all astrophysical uncertainty included.

History

Carr, Kühnel and Visinelli, arXiv:2008.08077v1, assembled constraints on extremely massive black holes from dynamics, lensing, large-scale structure, CMB dipoles and accretion. Each has different environmental assumptions, and they emphasized difficulties in extending ordinary Poisson constraints into the rare-object regime. Ivanov and Trifinopoulos, arXiv:2508.04767v1, used MIKE/HIRES Lyman-α data and effective-field-theory modelling to exclude broad massive-PBH regions with fPBH≳10−3; their analysis deliberately restricts itself to the Poisson regime, fixes standard cosmology and calibrates several nuisance parameters under ΛCDM. The new seed-halo calculation targets lower abundances where that treatment is inapplicable, so a simple ratio of bound strengths would be misleading.

CMB spectral-distortion arguments, arXiv:1405.5999v1, can already strongly disfavor sufficiently massive PBH formation from enhanced Gaussian primordial perturbations. Those are constraints on a formation model, and can change with non-Gaussianity; they do not provide a universal substitute for the present-day abundance test. The weak-lensing result is complementary, with its own strong halo-model dependence.

Paper: Analytic Marginalization Over Calibration Uncertainties in Gravitational Wave Inference and Application to Black Hole Ringdown — David Dzingeleski, Aaron Zimmerman. arXiv:2611.07023v1, full text. Submitted 4 November 2026 at 19:00:06 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: inference method and gravitational-wave reanalysis.

Method. Black-hole spectroscopy compares the damped oscillation frequencies after merger with the Kerr prediction. A frequency-dependent error in the detector response can mimic changes in those modes. The authors expand amplitude and phase calibration errors in cubic splines and, after linearizing the response and imposing Gaussian coefficient priors, integrate them analytically out of the likelihood. The effective data covariance becomes

B=C+MΣMT,
where C is the noise covariance, Σ the calibration-prior covariance and M the waveform's response to the spline coefficients. Calibration uncertainty is therefore correlated across time samples and depends on the signal model; it is not just an extra independent noise term.

Applications. For GW250114, whose adopted calibration errors are approximately 2% in amplitude and 2° in phase, evidence for the first overtone persists over the useful early-ringdown interval. At a start time of six remnant-mass time units, a representative 90% interval for the fractional 221-mode frequency departure is δf221=−0.1−0.1+0.4, consistent with general relativity. A third mode is not robustly established.

For GW250207, broad Hanford calibration priors of approximately 20% and 20° matter much more. Marginalization restores agreement between ringdown-inferred remnant properties and the inspiral–merger–ringdown reference. The first-overtone amplitude then remains compatible with zero at 90% after only a short post-peak interval; many deviation posteriors largely reproduce their uniform priors. A full-signal SNR near 69 does not guarantee multi-mode ringdown information: the later ringdown SNR can be only about 16 in the stated window.

Limitations. These are conditional ringdown analyses with fixed sky location/reference timing, specified start times, Gaussian noise and chosen mode content. The analytic response expansion assumes small calibration errors; the widest GW250207 priors make higher-order terms a relevant unquantified issue. The broad deviation prior [−1,1] also matters when data are weak. An unconstrained overtone is not evidence that the physical mode is absent, and neither event establishes a violation of general relativity.

History

Calibration marginalization is established in full-waveform inference. Nathan et al., arXiv:2610.11052v1, already extended ringdown rational filtering to account for calibration and applied it to GW250207. Their filter removes candidate quasinormal modes without fitting their amplitudes, and they explicitly tracked the same detector response acting on both signal and noise. Thus the November paper is not the first calibration-aware ringdown analysis.

Its contribution is an analytic marginalization for the direct mode-fitting likelihood, together with an examination of overtone evidence and frequency-deviation posteriors. Agreement between methods on remnant properties is useful; a numerical bound from one method cannot be ranked without matching start time, mode model, noise treatment and calibration priors. The work illustrates how instrumental uncertainty can weaken a gravity test before any new physics is invoked.

Paper: Can Two-Point Correlations Determine Gravitational Waves from Sound Waves? — Guangshang Chen, Jianpeng Dai, Yang Xiao. arXiv:2611.07877v1, full text. Submitted 5 November 2026 at 11:58:30 UTC; first announced in the 6 November 2026 UTC listing. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. Classification: analytic and numerical method study.

Physical point. Gravitational waves generated by fluid motions depend on the unequal-time correlator of the transverse-traceless stress. Since that stress is quadratic in velocity, its power contains a four-point velocity correlator. Replacing the four-point function by products of two-point functions is exact for a Gaussian field, but need not be justified for a finite or correlated population of sound shells.

Results. The paper separates two cases often conflated. For independent, spherically symmetric shells with Poisson-distributed centres, the connected contribution can vanish after the tensor projection even though the velocity field itself is non-Gaussian. Hence Gaussianity is sufficient, but not necessary, for the familiar reduction. Conversely, a deliberately constructed three-site ensemble has the same two-point statistics in two realizations but different higher correlations: their low-wavenumber gravitational-wave spectra approach a ratio of two. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a universal factor-two correction to a cosmological phase transition.

For exponentially nucleated bubbles in a finite acoustic window, the authors derive conditions under which connected terms become relatively small. Their asymptotic suppression behaves schematically as log2(1+βE)/(βE), where β sets the nucleation timescale and E is an expansion-limited effective source duration. A long source duration helps, but Hubble expansion and nonlinear steepening limit how far that asymptotic argument can be taken.

Limitations. Linear sound propagation, prescribed nucleation statistics and idealized shell profiles do not describe the later shock/turbulent stage of a strong transition. The counterexample establishes nonuniqueness from two-point data; it does not calculate the error for all realistic transitions. No detection forecast, particle-parameter exclusion or observed gravitational-wave background is produced.

History

Hindmarsh and Hijazi's sound-shell model, arXiv:1909.10040v1, expresses the wave spectrum through the stress unequal-time correlator and then a convolution of velocity correlations, providing the established semi-analytic framework. The new derivation identifies when the statistical reduction underlying that framework is valid and when information is missing.

Independent strong-transition simulations, arXiv:2505.17824v1, already found that a Gaussian velocity-convolution treatment could reproduce spectral shapes more faithfully than amplitudes, with shocks, enthalpy weighting and time decorrelation important. That nonlinear discrepancy is not identical to the present idealized connected-correlator effect. Together, the studies argue for checking source statistics and hydrodynamic evolution separately, rather than assuming a successful velocity power spectrum automatically guarantees a precise gravitational-wave amplitude.
