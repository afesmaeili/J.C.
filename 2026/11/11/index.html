Fixed-rigidity abundances point to metal-rich UHECR sources

Paper: Ultra-high-energy cosmic rays require metal-rich sources — E. Podlesnyi, F. Oikonomou, D. Ehlert and M. Unger. arXiv:2611.14038v1, full text. Submitted 10 November 2026 at 13:05:49 UTC and first listed 11 November; primary category astro-ph.HE. No journal publication is listed. Classification: model-dependent source-composition inference, not a direct elemental-abundance measurement.

Physical question. Air-shower data indicate that the ultra-high-energy cosmic-ray mixture becomes heavier above the ankle, but a source abundance must be compared with a candidate seed population at the same magnetic rigidity R=E/Z, because acceleration and confinement depend on rigidity. For a source spectrum dNi/dE∝εiE−γ, the rigidity-space fraction is

ρi=εiZi1−γ∑jεjZj1−γ.

This conversion matters enormously: changing the spectral index from a Galactic-like γ≃2.6 to the very hard UHECR value γ≃0.8 can move the hydrogen-to-iron ratio at fixed energy by roughly 261.8≃350, even when the fixed-rigidity fractions are unchanged.

Method and data. The authors fit the Pierre Auger Observatory all-particle spectrum, mean shower maximum ⟨Xmax⟩ and shower-to-shower dispersion σ\(Xmax) above 1018.7 eV, together with the inferred hydrogen spectrum between 1017.8 and 1018.7 eV. Sources inject five groups—H, He, CNO, Si and Fe—with a common power law and a super-exponential rigidity cutoff Ei,cut=ZiRcut. CRPropa3 propagates the nuclei through photon backgrounds; an MCMC profiles the spectral slope, cutoff, cutoff shape and composition.

The main fit uses EPOS-LHC-R shower modeling and star-formation-rate-like source evolution, with alternatives spanning (1+z)m, m=−4 to 5, and Sibyll 2.3e. The Galactic comparison uses γGCR=2.6 and a deliberately broad ±30% abundance band.

Results. For the nominal star-formation-history fit, the posterior modes are

γ=0.81−0.05+0.06,Rcut=8.1−0.5+0.3×1018 V,λ=2.9−0.2+0.3,

with fixed-rigidity fractions ρH=0.38−0.08+0.05, ρCNO=0.41−0.05+0.07, ρSi=0.160±0.018 and ρFe=0.0041−0.0024+0.0027. Helium is negligible at the posterior mode. The authors define metallicity as Zρ=ρCNO+ρSi+ρFe; every source-evolution scenario examined requires at least about three times the Galactic value, with a factor near four for star-formation-rate evolution and more than an order of magnitude for strongly positive evolution.

Two fit classes survive. In the nominal EPOS solution, secondary protons from photodisintegration dominate the hydrogen flux at Earth, while CNO and silicon source fractions exceed the Galactic fixed-rigidity values by about factors 30 and 50. If the Auger Xmax scale is shifted downward by one quoted systematic standard deviation, a primary-hydrogen-rich branch appears: hydrogen and CNO can then resemble the Galactic mixture, but silicon remains about 50 times larger and iron about three times larger. The silicon excess remains above an order of magnitude across the EPOS/Sibyll and Xmax variations studied.

Limitations. The nominal fit has deviance per degree of freedom about 3.34; the shifted-Xmax solution gives about 2.69. These are phenomenological fits, not statistically pristine descriptions. Results depend on hadronic-interaction models, the absolute Xmax scale, extragalactic background light, assumed homogeneous identical sources, common source evolution, five broad mass groups and one-dimensional propagation. Negative source evolution fits poorly because it produces too few secondary protons. The analysis identifies a robust heavy-seed tension inside this model family, but it does not identify the accelerator or explain how the metal-rich material is supplied.

History

The Pierre Auger Collaboration's 2017 combined fit already established the modern baseline: above 5×1018 eV, a simple homogeneous-source model prefers a hard injection spectrum, low maximum rigidity and a mixed, relatively heavy composition. Later two-population fits extending toward 1017 eV retained a hard, heavy high-energy component while adding a softer, lighter low-energy population. Magnetic-horizon models can soften the inferred injection spectrum, but still require an additional low-energy component under their field and source-density assumptions.

The new step is the fixed-rigidity comparison with measured Galactic seed abundances and the inclusion of the sub-ankle hydrogen spectrum to separate primary from secondary protons. It strengthens the composition problem into a chemical one: ordinary Galactic cosmic-ray material does not reproduce the inferred silicon-group fraction. It neither proves that every UHECR source is metal-rich nor excludes structured environments, multiple source classes, magnetic horizons or altered propagation. The main unresolved uncertainty remains whether shower-model and source-population systematics can mimic the apparent heavy-seed excess.

A 3.7 PeV photon tests superluminal photon decay and splitting

Paper: Constraints on Lorentz Invariance Violation from PeV Gamma Rays of Cygnus X-3 Observed by LHAASO — LHAASO Collaboration. arXiv:2611.14028v1, full text. Submitted 10 November 2026 at 12:58:14 UTC and first listed 11 November; primary category astro-ph.HE, cross-listed in hep-ph. No journal publication is listed. Classification: photon-stability constraints; no Lorentz-invariance violation is detected.

Physical question. A superluminal photon term in a modified dispersion relation can make normally forbidden processes kinematically possible. Linear or quadratic corrections can permit γ→e+e−; a quadratic term can also permit photon splitting. Detecting photons above the corresponding threshold constrains the Lorentz-violation energy scale ELIV(n). These are photon-survival tests and should not be equated with arrival-time tests, which constrain energy-dependent propagation delays under different assumptions.

Data and method. LHAASO analyzes 692.8 days of KM2A exposure across four Cygnus X-3 flare windows from 26 December 2019 to 31 July 2024, adopting a distance of 8.95 kpc. KM2A's stated energy resolution improves from about 17% at 0.1 PeV to 10% at 1 PeV, while hadron rejection is about 104 at 100 TeV. A binned three-dimensional spatial–spectral likelihood and an unbinned cross-check fit the Cygnus region. The baseline intrinsic spectrum is a physically motivated pγX+pγUV model; a power law probes spectral-model systematics. Low-count coverage is calibrated with Monte Carlo CLs, rather than a naive asymptotic cutoff test.

Results. The highest reconstructed photon candidate has

Eγ=3.735±0.411 PeV.

The spectral fit places a 95% confidence lower limit on an exponential cutoff of

Ecut>3.05−0.29+0.29 PeV,

with the unbinned analysis giving 3.06 PeV. Interpreted in the paper's superluminal photon-sector effective model, the corresponding limits are

ELIV(1)>2.72−0.70+0.85×1034 eV,
ELIV(2)>9.09−1.65+1.81×1024 eV

for photon decay, and

ELIV,split(2)>1.73−0.30+0.33×1026 eV

for photon splitting. The latter is roughly 10−2MPl. The quoted total systematic uncertainty is about 9.6%, dominated by the intrinsic-spectrum choice at 9.5%; atmosphere contributes about 1.5%, and high-energy background is negligible in the fitted PeV tail.

Limitations. The bounds apply to the particular superluminal photon dispersion and effective photon-splitting framework used. They assume that the intrinsic Cygnus X-3 spectrum has no astrophysical cutoff below the inferred limit and that the spatial/spectral source model correctly separates the crowded Cygnus emission. Energy scale and sparse-event statistics matter directly. A surviving PeV photon does not constrain every Lorentz-violating operator, and it is not evidence for a quantum-gravity scale.

History

LHAASO's first PeV-source analysis, published as Physical Review Letters 128, 051102, used photons up to 1.4 PeV and reported photon-stability limits around ELIV(2)>10−3MPl, alongside a linear scale above about 105MPl. The present Cygnus X-3 data push the quadratic photon-decay/splitting reach toward 10−2MPl, close to an order of magnitude within the same class of photon-stability tests.

Time-of-flight analyses are complementary. LHAASO's GRB 221009A timing study constrained linear and quadratic dispersion from energy-dependent delays, but those values cannot be divided into the present limits to claim a universal improvement: source distance, observables, operator sign and threshold physics differ. The remaining uncertainty is whether intrinsic PeV spectral structure in Cygnus X-3 can be separated cleanly enough from the propagation effect the stability test is designed to exclude.

SPHEREx places its first real-data limits on decaying eV-mass dark matter

Paper: First constraints on axion-like particles with SPHEREx data — Connor Hainje, Elena Pinetti and David W. Hogg. arXiv:2611.13090v1, full text. Submitted 9 November 2026 at 19:00:02 UTC and first listed 11 November; primary category hep-ph, cross-listed in astro-ph.CO. No journal publication is listed. Classification: 95% power-constrained upper limits under the assumption that all dark matter is the decaying axion-like particle; no unknown decay line is detected.

Physical question. An axion-like particle of mass (m_a) coupled to two photons decays at rate

Γa→γγ=gaγγ2ma364π,

producing a narrow line at photon energy (m_a/2). SPHEREx covers 0.744–5.002 μm at resolving power (R=35)–128, corresponding to (0.496<m_a<3.32) eV. The analysis asks whether the Milky Way dark-matter column produces such a line in the mission's first deep-field data.

Data and method. The study uses the first 16.435 months of Quick Release data over 213 deg² around the North and South Deep Fields. A 6.3-trillion-row pixel table is filtered to 4.70×1011 valid, source-masked measurements; the known-source flag removes about half the measurements. The signal assumes an NFW Milky Way halo with rs=14.46 kpc and ρs=0.566 GeVcm−3, an isotropic Maxwellian velocity width of 160 km s⁻¹, and that ALPs constitute all dark matter.

For each sky-pixel/detector pair, an independent fourth-order polynomial models the continuum inside a window of ±5 instrumental resolution elements. The analysis profiles those nuisance coefficients analytically at 408 trial masses. One-sided 95% limits use Δχ2=2.71, a power-constraint threshold of 0.1587 to prevent over-exclusion after downward fluctuations, and three data-driven error inflations that account for local scatter and coherent residual structure.

Results. The strongest coupling limit is

gaγγ<2.10×10−11 GeV−1

at ma=0.606 eV; the median over the scanned range is 4.16×10−11 GeV−1. Recast without the axion coupling relation, the median lower limit on the two-photon dark-matter lifetime is τ>6.5×1025 s. These are 95% power-constrained limits for the adopted halo and full-dark-matter fraction.

Fourteen wavelength regions have nominal two-sided (z>1.96), but every excursion above about 4σ aligns with known atmospheric He I, PAH 3.3 μm, Paα, Brα or He-I leakage. Sixteen regions fluctuate below −1.96, consistent with substantial structured residuals and look-elsewhere expectations. The authors therefore report no unidentified line. Scaling exposure from 16.435 to 25 months improves the coupling forecast by only about 10%, whereas the median gap to the Cramér–Rao bound is a factor 39, pointing to foreground and calibration modeling as the main opportunity.

Limitations. Coupling limits scale with the assumed dark-matter fraction and halo column; a subdominant ALP component weakens them. The independent local continua are conservative but discard spatial coherence. Detector boundaries and the bright 1.083 μm atmospheric He I line create sensitivity holes, and the current quick-release calibration lacks some planned foreground templates. Different published line searches use different halos and confidence constructions, so point-by-point comparisons are safer than a single improvement factor.

History

The 2025 SPHEREx forecast by Regis, Taoso and Terol Calvo predicted strong eV-mass sensitivity from Milky Way, dwarf-galaxy and Magellanic-Cloud sightlines. The new work is the first search using actual SPHEREx measurements. It also corrects a channel-width mismatch in the journal-version forecast: after discussion, the forecast's arXiv v3 and the data analysis agree.

Earlier real-data baselines include optical MUSE-Faint dwarf-galaxy searches, the WINERED first result and several JWST infrared analyses, including Janish and Pinetti. Globular-cluster energy-loss bounds constrain the same photon coupling but do not assume the particle is dark matter; conversely, decay-line limits depend directly on its abundance and halo distribution. SPHEREx is competitive across parts of 0.5–3.3 eV, not uniformly the strongest at every mass. The unresolved issue is whether all-sky morphology and improved calibration can recover the order-of-magnitude statistical potential without foreground mismodeling.

The present neutrino-ordering hint is a balance of small, partly opposing pulls

Paper: Where does the neutrino Mass Ordering preference come from? — Pedro A. N. Machado and João Paulo Pinheiro. arXiv:2611.13629v1, full text. Submitted 10 November 2026 at 06:16:18 UTC and first listed 11 November; primary category hep-ph. No journal publication is listed. Classification: diagnostic reanalysis of published likelihood information, not a new oscillation measurement.

Physical question. Reactor disappearance measures Δmee2, while muon-neutrino disappearance measures Δmμμ2. Their relation to the fundamental atmospheric splitting depends weakly on whether m3 lies above or below the solar pair. The paper asks which data sets actually generate the current normal-ordering preference after JUNO's first 207 days, while deliberately removing standalone ordering information from accelerator appearance and matter effects.

Data and construction. The fit combines JUNO with Daya Bay and one-dimensional Δχ2(|Δm312|) profiles from T2K, NOvA, MINOS and 9.3 years of IceCube/DeepCore data as compiled in NuFIT 6.1. The accelerator and atmospheric profiles have θ23 and δCP marginalized. Super-Kamiokande is not included. For every ordering, the external curves are re-zeroed at their own minima, removing their direct ordering preference and isolating consistency of the measured splittings. JUNO confidence behavior is treated with Feldman–Cousins and pseudo-experiments because its likelihood has multiple local minima.

Results. JUNO alone contributes Δχ2=1.84 toward normal ordering, but its preferred splitting is in tension with Daya Bay; after combining the two, the ordering statistic falls to 0.21, effectively no reactor-only preference. Adding accelerator and IceCube disappearance yields

TMO=5.91.

From 12,000 toys per ordering, the inverted-ordering tail probability is pIO=0.013, or 2.2σ one-sided. Because the observed statistic is not unusually central under every normal-ordering toy, the CLs value is 0.027, or 1.9σ. T2K and NOvA prefer atmospheric splittings differing by about 3.5%, comparable to the ordering-dependent offset; reactor+T2K and reactor+NOvA therefore pull toward opposite orderings.

The same non-Gaussianity weakens apparent cross-experiment tensions. For the full combination, naive T gives 2.4σ tension under normal ordering and 3.1σ under inverted ordering, whereas pseudo-experiments give only 1.0σ and 1.8σ. In an Asimov projection with 1,500 additional JUNO days but today's external data, inverted-ordering rejection reaches only about 2.8σ; even perfect reactor precision gives about 2.9σ. The limiting error then moves to accelerator disappearance and percent-level neutrino-energy reconstruction.

Limitations. This combination intentionally discards appearance and matter-effect ordering information, so it should not replace a full global fit. It inherits NuFIT's one-dimensional profiles and their nuisance marginalization, approximates some external fluctuations by shifted profiles, and omits Super-Kamiokande. The projection freezes non-JUNO data rather than forecasting DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande or updated cross-section constraints.

History

The mass ordering was first probed indirectly through atmospheric and long-baseline matter effects; the recent change is JUNO's precise reactor spectrum. JUNO's first oscillation measurement, published in Nature in 2026, made the Δmee2 comparison competitive after only 207 days. T2K, NOvA and IceCube/DeepCore already measured νμ disappearance precisely, while their full analyses also contain ordering-sensitive appearance or Earth-matter information.

The new paper explains rather than strengthens that experimental record. Its 1.9σ CLs preference is weaker and conceptually narrower than some full global-ordering summaries because direct appearance and matter-effect evidence is removed by construction. The key historical advance is diagnostic: a few-percent difference between T2K and NOvA central values can reverse the disappearance-only ordering pull. The outstanding uncertainty is experimental control of reconstructed neutrino energy, including neutrino–nucleus modeling, alongside statistically independent combination of future reactor, accelerator and atmospheric likelihoods.

GRB 261110A acquired a fading optical-counterpart candidate after an SVOM trigger

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on GRB 261110A at 11:50:00 UTC on 10 November. The best image trigger had signal-to-noise ratio 7.60 in 5–20 keV over 40.96 s, with (T_{90}=21.9^{+8.7}_{-8.7}) s. Its position was RA 350.7538°, Dec +33.0385°, with a 10.33-arcmin 90% confidence radius, including a 2-arcmin systematic term. SVOM slewed immediately. GCN 46215.

The Xinglong 2.16-m telescope began observing 22.2 minutes after the trigger and found a candidate at RA 350.8530709°, Dec +33.0630451°, at approximately R=21.4; a much fainter catalogued Legacy Survey source lies at the same position. GCN 46218. SVOM/VT had already detected that position from 306 s after trigger, measuring 22.3±0.2 AB in VT_B and 22.0±0.2 AB in VT_R around 17 minutes, with a shallow two-hour decay. GCN 46219.

By 8.62 h, SAO RAS measured Rc=23.0±0.2 Vega, supporting fading. GCN 46220. LCO did not recover it at 13 h, to r>22.3 AB and z>20.2 AB at 3σ. GCN 46221. The positional match and fading make it the leading afterglow candidate, but no redshift or spectrum had been reported by the cutoff. The early GWAC-F60 non-detection, R>20.15 at 16 minutes, was shallower than the later candidate and is not contradictory.

ASKAP revealed delayed radio brightening from the nearby tidal-disruption event 2025aarm

An Astronomer's Telegram posted 10 November at 20:29 UTC reports previously unpublished 0.887-GHz ASKAP measurements of TDE 2025aarm, at (z=0.01368) and an adopted distance of 61.5 Mpc. ATel 18150.

ASKAP images from 29 September 2025 through 10 March 2026 show no significant excess above the approximately 0.3 mJy map noise. The first >3σ detection appears on 10 April 2026 at a preliminary 1 mJy; the flux then rises almost linearly to 4.5 mJy on 27 August, corresponding to spectral luminosity about 2×1028 ergs−1Hz−1. A VLA observation on 4 November 2025 had already detected 36±10 μJy at 15.1 GHz.

The delayed low-frequency rise is consistent with an expanding outflow becoming visible as synchrotron self-absorption or external absorption decreases, but the telegram does not fit a jet velocity, energy or circumnuclear density. Multi-frequency contemporaneous monitoring is needed before selecting among an off-axis jet, a slower wind or changing absorption.

Count-based single-cell eQTL mapping finds more regulatory genes without the mixed-model cost

Zixuan Eleanor Zhang, Artem Kim, Noah Suboc, Nicholas Mancuso and Steven Gazal published Efficient count-based models improve power and robustness for large-scale single-cell eQTL mapping in Nature Genetics on 10 November 2026. The open-source framework, jaxQTL, aggregates single-cell RNA counts into pseudobulk samples and fits negative-binomial models accelerated with JAX.

Applied to the OneK1K data set—14 peripheral-blood cell types from 982 people—the negative-binomial model identified 18,907 eGenes, 14% more than its linear implementation. In the software comparison at the specified coverage threshold, jaxQTL found 19,115 eGenes, 16% more than tensorQTL and statistically similar to SAIGE-QTL's 19,424. Its extra discoveries concentrate among lower-expression genes.

Fine-mapped single-cell eQTLs were farther from transcription start sites than bulk eQTLs P=10−4 and more enriched in cell-type-specific enhancers P=3×10−10. For a simulated 10,000-person scale, the reported GPU implementation was 646 times faster than SAIGE-QTL in the dominant T-cell type, although tensorQTL remains faster when a linear model is adequate. The method improves power and scaling; it does not establish that every additional association is causal, and differential cell abundance still drives large power differences across cell types.

A nine-country survey finds AI use surging while trust remains conditional

The Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism published Generative AI and News Report 2026 on 29 October 2026; Nature highlighted its science-trust findings on 10 November. The YouGov survey covers nearly 19,000 adults in Argentina, Australia, Denmark, Finland, France, Japan, Norway, the United Kingdom and the United States.

Across the six countries with a three-year series, weekly use of a generative-AI system rose from 34% in 2025 to 51% in 2026, and daily use from 16% to 28%. Use for getting information rose from 11% in 2024 to 24% in 2025 and 38% in 2026. Scientists were the group most often expected to improve society through AI use: 46% of respondents anticipated a positive effect.

The optimism is selective. Across nine countries, only 12% were comfortable with news produced entirely by AI and 19% when some human oversight was present. Respondents expected AI to make news cheaper and more current, but less transparent and less trustworthy. This is a repeated cross-sectional survey of attitudes, not a controlled test of AI's social impact; country sampling, question wording and rapidly changing product familiarity limit causal interpretation.
