01Revisiting the Neutrino Emission and Physical Parameter Constraints from Time-Resolved Spectra of Gamma-Ray BurstsYang-Dong-Jun Ou, Hou-Jun Lü, Hao-Yu Yuan, Si-Xue Ren, Xue-Zhao Chang, Yan-Qiu Zhang, Shao-Lin Xiong, En-Wei Liang

Revisiting the Neutrino Emission and Physical Parameter Constraints from Time-Resolved Spectra of Gamma-Ray Bursts

Yang-Dong-Jun Ou, Hou-Jun Lü, Hao-Yu Yuan et al. arXiv:2611.14902v1; submitted 2026-11-11; first announced 13 November 2026; primary astro-ph.HE. Accepted for publication in ApJ.

This recalculation asks how prompt variability changes photohadronic neutrino expectations for four bright GRBs, especially 221009A. It sums independently modelled time bins, including proton synchrotron and pion/muon cooling, then folds the muon-neutrino fluence through IceCube effective area: Nν=∫ΦνμAeffdE. The adopted zero-event criterion is Nν<2.3 at 90% confidence; this is a source-model reinterpretation, not a new event-level IceCube search.

For the BOAT, fixing R=1.07×1015 cm and baryon loading ϵp/ϵe=1,3,10 gives Γ>254,371,537, compared with 188,273,395 in the matched time-integrated calculation. In internal shocks with variability time 0.01 s, Γ=400 gives loading below 0.66 versus 1.30; at Γ=700, below 6.98 versus 19.20. These are conditional limits, not joint marginalized bounds. Time resolution can either increase or decrease the yield: the ratio spans roughly 0.6–3 because increased target density competes with secondary cooling. Proton spectral assumptions change absolute yields by orders of magnitude. The useful result is therefore a correction to the photon-field treatment, rather than a model-independent baryon-loading measurement.

History

IceCube's dedicated BOAT search established the underlying nondetection. Veres et al. had already used time-resolved Fermi spectra and obtained strong, model-dependent constraints. This paper extends that line using unsaturated GECAM-C spectra and a different cooling prescription; its quoted improvement is against its own time-integrated baseline. It does not establish a universal improvement over Veres, whose magnetic partition, radii and statistical threshold differ.

02New Physics in Ultra-High-Energy Neutrino FlavorSamiur R. Mir, Carlos A. Argüelles, Vedran Brdar

New Physics in Ultra-High-Energy Neutrino Flavor

Samiur R. Mir, Carlos A. Argüelles and Vedran Brdar. arXiv:2611.14624v1, submitted 10 November 2026, first announced in the 13 November UTC batch; hep-ph; preprint. Classification: forecast.

The study asks how flavor measurements between 100 PeV and 10 EeV could test isotropic Lorentz-invariance violation (LIV). It adds one real flavor coefficient at a time to the propagation Hamiltonian, HLIV∝Ed−3κ˚αβ(d), where the coefficient has units GeV4−d. SimProp supplies the production-redshift distribution of cosmogenic neutrinos; propagation is folded into projected flavor likelihoods combining radio-in-ice and Earth-skimming experiments. For the moderate-flux, GRAND50k configuration, Table S1 gives a projected 95% confidence sensitivity of 1.6×10−73GeV−4 for the dimension-eight eμ coefficient, compared with 4.0×10−73GeV−4 using Earth-skimming channels alone. The corresponding eτ values are 3.0×10−71 and 2.6×10−70GeV−4.

These are conditional sensitivities, not observed bounds. They assume a cosmogenic production model, detector deployments and sufficient event statistics, fixed best-fit PMNS parameters, and one nonzero real LIV coefficient; ee and μτ structures remain weakly constrained. The forecast inherits detector-response simplifications from the underlying flavor study, including its omission of systematic errors where statistics were assumed dominant.

History

IceCube's astrophysical-flavor analysis already constrained isotropic LIV with 7.5 years of observed HESE data, spanning reconstructed energies of 60 TeV–2 PeV. Its source-composition-independent dimension-six ττ bound is 3×10−42GeV−2 using Bayes factor greater than 10, a criterion different from the new forecast's 95% contours. Brdar and Mir's earlier UHE tau study established the Earth-skimming opportunity; Burgos-Mondéjar, Thompson and Argüelles supplied the complementary flavor forecast. The advance is the explicit three-flavor LIV assessment with production redshifts. The paper's large cross-sector improvement claims compare different photon/neutrino operators and statistical conventions; they are not a universally comparable improvement in one measured parameter. The clearest like-for-like comparison is the channel combination within this forecast.

03Earth-sized tests of long-range neutrino interactionsDyer F. Cardenas, Mauricio Bustamante

Earth-sized tests of long-range neutrino interactions

Dyer F. Cardenas and Mauricio Bustamante. arXiv:2611.14642v1, submitted 10 November 2026, first announced in the 13 November UTC batch; hep-ph; preprint. Classification: observed constraint and improved propagation treatment.

A flavor-dependent ultralight vector force changes atmospheric-neutrino oscillations through a Yukawa potential sourced by terrestrial electrons and nucleons. The authors integrate that potential throughout a 12-layer Earth, instead of assigning its detector value to the whole trajectory, and fit 21,914 DeepCore events from 2011–2019, with 2,724 live days and reconstructed energies 6.31–158.49 GeV. Their Hamiltonian includes vacuum, standard matter and new-force terms; all six oscillation parameters and 18 nuisance parameters are profiled, with NuFIT 6.1 priors on four oscillation parameters and normal mass ordering assumed.

For Le−Lμ, the dimensionless gauge coupling has a nominal one-sided 90% confidence upper limit g′<3.54×10−26 at mediator mass m′=10−15 eV, weakening to 6.59×10−25 at 10−12 eV and 6.56×10−22 at 10−9 eV (machine-readable results). The scan constrains eight of 14 symmetries; forces acting equally on muon and tau flavors have no comparable upper limit. Sensitivity comes chiefly from the disappearance minimum near 24 GeV. The stated 90% intervals cover 87.0±1.4% of the tested pseudoexperiments; thresholds restoring nominal coverage would weaken coupling limits by 1.4–2.3%. This statistical caveat is material.

History

The same public sample was used in Garg et al.'s earlier DeepCore search, with a constant long-range potential, some oscillation parameters fixed, and a different confidence threshold. Consequently, a blanket ratio between the two published curves is not a controlled improvement factor. Holding all other choices fixed in the new analysis, following the spatial potential strengthens the coupling limit by 1.2 at 10−15 eV, rising to 2.7 at 10−9 eV. The result implements a physical effect already developed in Smirnov and Xu's potential calculation and Wise and Zhang's depth-dependent study, rather than discovering the possibility of depth dependence.

The independent terrestrial baseline includes MICROSCOPE's final equivalence-principle test, which found ηTi,Pt=(−1.5±2.3stat±1.5syst)×10−15, and Eöt-Wash torsion-balance measurements. Converting those accelerations into g′ depends on each symmetry's matter charges. Under those specified conversions, the new neutrino constraints dominate below mediator masses of roughly 1.7×10−11–2.1×10−10 eV, depending on symmetry. Longer-range solar/Galactic constraints outside this mass interval are not displaced by this Earth-only calculation.

Solar Neutrinos at the ESSνSB experiment

Authors and status: ESSnuSB Collaboration (J. Aguilar et al.; 100 authors), v1 submitted Thu 12 Nov 2026 13:44:31 UTC; first announced Fri 13 Nov; new primary hep-ph, cross hep-ex. Preprint, 18 pages/12 figures. Abstract, full text.

Study and results: The ESSνSB far detector model has two 270-kt fiducial water-Cherenkov tanks at Zinkgruvan, about 1 km underground (roughly 2700 m water equivalent). It folds an adiabatic three-flavor MSW survival probability and neutrino–electron elastic scattering with 90% efficiency and Gaussian recoil-energy response. The upturn fit uses reconstructed 6.5–15 MeV recoils; the hep analysis uses 14–25 MeV, with 1-MeV bins. Reference fractional energy resolutions are 10% and 5%, respectively. An Asimov spectral-shape comparison floats the overall normalization: the upturn null is constant survival probability, while the hep null has only boron-8 signal. A covariance term represents a correlated 10% normalization uncertainty, alongside response and background uncertainties. Spallation is extrapolated from Super-Kamiokande isotope yields using the site muon-flux scaling; radon is also modeled.

The ideal statistics-only upturn reaches 5σ in roughly half a year, but normalization and energy response move this to approximately 6.2 years before environmental backgrounds. At the adopted thresholds, 90% spallation rejection prevents either upturn or hep from reaching 5σ even in 20 years; approximately 99% rejection is required for discovery-level sensitivity over that duration. The hep window contains about 1077 boron-8 events/year and 126 hep events/year (about 1204 combined, rounding), given the adopted solar model. With normalization and response uncertainties but no environmental background, hep reaches 5σ in about four years. The ten-year hep flux precision changes from roughly 6% statistics-only to 12% with those systematics; residual spallation at 90% rejection degrades it to worse than a factor of two at 1σ. Figures scanning veto efficiency versus threshold carry the useful design message: fiducial mass alone does not solve the background problem.

Limitations: All significances are projected Asimov sensitivities, not detections or measured limits. The muon/spallation model is a scaled estimate, not a detector-specific simulation; mountain profile, reconstruction, veto dead time and energy-dependent rejection need validation. The electron-scattering cross section is tree level; radiative corrections cited as about 2% in total and up to 4% in spectral shape are omitted, material for a precision upturn test. Assumptions about threshold, response and solar flux matter. Different solar observables and confidence conventions should not be collapsed into a single sensitivity number.

History

The Super-Kamiokande-IV solar analysis, Secs. IX–X, measured 2970 days of SK-IV data, combined with earlier SK phases, and found only 1.2σ preference for the spectral upturn; adding SNO spectral data raised the distorted-spectrum preference to 2.1σ. That is why a larger detector can add real physics, but only if its low-energy backgrounds are controlled. The existing SNO three-phase hep search used 2.47 kt-years, remained compatible with both zero and the solar-model flux, and quoted a 90% credible upper limit of 30,000 cm⁻² s⁻¹. This is a measured bound, not the same statistic as ESSνSB’s forecast discovery significance. The new paper advances these questions by turning a proposed detector into explicit joint threshold/veto requirements rather than claiming an observed upturn or hep flux.

Search for gravitational wave coincident neutrinos in DEAP-3600

P. Adhikari, P. -A. Amaudruz, D. J. Auty et al. arXiv:2611.15821v1; submitted 2026-11-11; first announced 13 November 2026; primary astro-ph.HE. Preprint; 11 pages, 10 figures.

The liquid-argon dark-matter detector searches for νe+40Ar→40K*+e− within ±500 s of seven binary-neutron-star or neutron-star–black-hole candidate mergers. Its 3269±24 kg target and characteristic prompt/delayed scintillation structure provide a distinct flavor-sensitive channel. Empirical sidebands account separately for changing calibration-source backgrounds and data-taking conditions; MARLEY and detector simulations determine signal acceptance.

All windows are consistent with background. Feldman–Cousins 90% fluence limits are given for several assumed spectra. Quoted GW170817 monoenergetic benchmarks are 6.8×1012cm−2 at 10 MeV and 1.8×1010cm−2 at 100 MeV. These values depend on the charged-current cross section and energy-dependent efficiency, so they cannot be compared with other experiments' differently shaped or differently flavored limits by a single improvement factor. Above 10 MeV, nuclear states leading to particle emission cannot be fully optically simulated; the authors bracket acceptance by rejecting or accepting these states. Their fraction reaches about 60% at 100 MeV, making that end of the limit especially conditional. This is a demonstrated additional search channel with a null result, not evidence of merger neutrinos.

History

Super-Kamiokande's GW170817 search already covered a ±500 s window and found no signal across low- and high-energy channels. Separately, DEAP's May 2026 solar-neutrino paper reported 4.0σ evidence for argon absorption. This study's contribution applies that detector/channel capability to gravitational-wave timing; it is neither the first merger-neutrino search nor the first argon-absorption evidence.

06Resolving Radiogenic Heterogeneity in the Earth's Mantle with Multi-Site Geo-neutrino MeasurementsMichael Leyton, Stephen T. Dye

Resolving Radiogenic Heterogeneity in the Earth's Mantle with Multi-Site Geo-neutrino Measurements

Michael Leyton, Stephen T. Dye. arXiv:2611.16715v1; submitted 2026-11-12T12:33:20Z; first announced 13 November 2026; primary physics.geo-ph; preprint.

Leyton and Dye combine KamLAND, Borexino, SNO+ and JUNO geoneutrino rates with site-specific lithosphere predictions. A common mantle signal fits acceptably (χ2=3.78 for three degrees of freedom; p=0.286), giving 14.8±4.5 neutrino-interaction units and 18.5±5.6 TW of mantle radiogenic heating, conditional on Th/U=3.77 and K/U=13,000. The approximately 3.2σ result concerns a nonzero mantle contribution, not spatial heterogeneity.

After lithosphere subtraction, Jiangmen exceeds Kamioka by 24.6±13.8 interaction units: only 1.8σ, with this contrast selected after inspecting the data. The authors test nearly one million configurations across seismic anomalies, deep mantle provinces, slabs, lithosphere rescaling and local prescriptions. Plausible large-scale structures produce only small contrasts because the two detectors sample overlapping mantle volumes; reproducing the central difference requires an explicitly local contribution or offset. This is a useful diagnostic for improved JUNO exposure and local crust modeling, not evidence for a radioactive mantle anomaly. Its limitations include four nondirectional integrated rates, approximate reservoir geometry and composition, and geological priors; the inferred size of deep mantle provinces is driven almost entirely by the tomography prior.

History

Šrámek et al. (2013) predicted geographically varying geoneutrino signals from compositionally enriched mantle structures. Borexino's 2020 analysis demonstrated how local-crust subtraction constrains mantle radioactivity. The new paper extends that logic to four sites. Its JUNO input, 73±11 TNU, is independently reported in the September 2026 JUNO analysis, based on 207.2 live days with the geoneutrino rate free and Th/U constrained. A TNU is one inverse-beta event per year per 1032 free protons; the paper calls the equivalent unit NIU.

07A Dark-Sector Mass Flip-Flop Mechanism for the ANITA-IV EventsDibya S. Chattopadhyay, Carlos A. Argüelles and Vedran Brdar

A Dark-Sector Mass Flip-Flop Mechanism for the ANITA-IV Events

Dibya S. Chattopadhyay, Carlos A. Argüelles and Vedran Brdar. arXiv:2611.17328v1, submitted 12 November 2026, 18:59:33 UTC; first announced 13 November. Primary category: hep-ph. Preprint. Classification: speculative astroparticle model and forecast.

An ultralight scalar raises one dark fermion's mass inside Earth, reversing the vacuum ordering. Underground N1→N2Z′ conversion is followed by atmospheric N2→N1Z′ decay and a hadronic shower. The inequality |M1−M2|≤MZ′ defines a layer engineered to block both decays across IceCube.

For a monoenergetic, isotropic 20 EeV flux and g12=10−5, two benchmarks are normalized to four ANITA-IV candidates. Their required integrated fluxes are 1.1×10−16 and 3.5×10−17 cm−2 s−1 sr−1. Angular goodness-of-fit probabilities are 0.098 and 0.023, conditional on four events; these are not discovery significances. Predicted PUEO yields are 2.7 and 3.0 events for 12.9 live days, using an approximate 1.5 response improvement.

The effective area is semi-analytic, rescaling tau-shower response with a sharp 700 g cm−2 development requirement. A full radio-detector simulation and quantitative Auger prediction for high-altitude decays remain absent. The construction demonstrates possible consistency, not observation of a dark-sector mass reversal.

History

The ANITA-IV collaboration's original analysis reported approximately 3.3±0.5σ against its combined background scenarios, explicitly insufficient to exclude a background combination. These near-horizon candidates are distinct from the steep events in earlier flights.

Chattopadhyay, Argüelles and Brdar's July analysis quantified the poor consistency of a common ultra-high-energy neutrino flux across ANITA, IceCube and KM3NeT. Its large inter-experiment tension tests a specified neutrino interpretation; it does not establish particles beyond the Standard Model. The present mechanism changes propagation and decay geography instead of increasing that neutrino flux.

The independent Pierre Auger search found one candidate against 0.27±0.12 expected background events, using upward showers more than 20° below the horizon and simulated starting heights up to 9 km above ground. Different angles and high-altitude decay thresholds prevent a direct transfer of its exclusions to these benchmarks. That limitation requires a dedicated response calculation; it is not proof that the model evades Auger.

08Cosmic Ray Reacceleration and Gamma-Ray Emission via the Cloud-Wind Interaction in the Galactic HaloRyohei Inamoto, Jiro Shimoda, Katsuaki Asano

Cosmic Ray Reacceleration and Gamma-Ray Emission via the Cloud-Wind Interaction in the Galactic Halo

Ryohei Inamoto, Jiro Shimoda, Katsuaki Asano. arXiv:2611.15120v1; submitted 2026-11-11; first announced 13 November 2026; primary astro-ph.HE. Preprint; 13 pages, 13 figures, submitted to ApJ.

Two-dimensional axisymmetric simulations couple gas dynamics to a momentum-dependent cosmic-ray fluid as a 300 km/s Galactic-halo wind strikes a 50 pc cloud. They include radiative cooling and conduction, with imposed isotropic diffusion D=1027[p/(1GeV/c)]1/3cm2s−1. Initial cosmic-ray/gas pressure ratios of 0.01 and 1 isolate how particle backreaction changes shocks, compression and vorticity.

Compression hardens the 1–3.16 GeV gamma-ray photon index from 2.66 to 2.29 in the cosmic-ray-poor run, but only to 2.57 in the rich run, where pressure smooths the shock. Bright simulated clumps reach 1–10 GeV intensities of 10−9–10−8ergcm−2s−1sr−1, above typical bubble intensities; a roughly 0.1 filling factor is an illustrative reconciliation, not a fitted measurement. The cloud is also more massive than typical observed halo clouds. The calculation establishes a local reacceleration mechanism, while magnetic geometry, anisotropic transport, wave heating, electrons and a global bubble model remain absent. In particular, a narrow-band hardening result does not itself demonstrate agreement with the full observed bubble spectrum.

History

Fermi's 2014 bubble analysis found that both inverse-Compton and hadronic interpretations could describe the data. Shimoda and Asano's 2024 disk–halo model explored persistent cosmic-ray/gas interactions on global scales. The new calculation supplies their missing local nonlinear cloud interaction and shows that cosmic-ray pressure can suppress, rather than simply amplify, the resulting hardening; it does not select a hadronic origin observationally.

09Energetic Particle Confinement In Black Hole Accretion DisksDaniele Villa, Frank Rieger, Frank Jenko, James Stone

Energetic Particle Confinement In Black Hole Accretion Disks

Daniele Villa, Frank Rieger, Frank Jenko and James Stone. arXiv:2611.15397v1, astro-ph.HE. Submitted 11 November 2026, 12:18 UTC; first announced 13 November 2026. Preprint; no journal reference verified. Classification: numerical transport and acceleration study.

How long do energetic protons remain near an accreting black hole, and which structures energize them? The authors track approximately 1.5 million passive protons per run in evolving three-dimensional, ideal-GRMHD AthenaK simulations: magnetically arrested disks (MADs) with dimensionless spin a=0 and 0.9375, and a less strongly magnetized SANE flow with a=0.9375. A single injection near 8rg samples initial Larmor radii rL/rg=5–25, where rg=GM/c2; the particles exert no feedback on the plasma.

For the high-spin MAD, regional mean escape times from the central cylinder R≤64rg, |z|≤64rg span 72–302 tg in the lower sampled Lorentz-factor interval and 66–90 tg in the higher interval, with tg=GM/c3. These are spatial ranges of model averages, not confidence intervals. The jet sheath, turbulent flow and magnetic geometry produce strongly nonuniform residence times; neither constant isotropic diffusion nor simple advection describes the whole domain. Correlations favor turbulence and velocity shear as principal energization channels in these simulations. Some repeatedly reflected particles gain approximately two orders of magnitude in energy, but distant reflections are sensitive to grid resolution and numerical density floors; this does not establish a steady accelerated spectrum.

The rigidity range is crucial: protons relevant to the TeV-neutrino interpretation of NGC 1068 have much smaller rL/rg, and its luminous accretion flow differs from the simulated radiatively inefficient disks. The work supplies transport diagnostics, not a neutrino-flux prediction or an observational confinement measurement. Ideal MHD and unresolved kinetic dissipation also prevent a general exclusion of reconnection.

History

The physical baseline is competition between acceleration, losses and escape: a large acceleration rate is insufficient if particles promptly leave the interaction region. Kimura, Tomida and Murase (2019) already tracked particles in global MRI-turbulent hot flows and found hard-sphere-like energy diffusion together with superdiffusive radial transport and outward drift. Their fluid treatment used Newtonian gravity and particle trajectories used MHD snapshots; the present work follows an evolving relativistic disk and explicitly compares magnetic state and spin. Their 2019 erratum corrects figure axes without changing the conclusions.

Lemoine and Rieger (2025) showed that turbulent escape, systematic advection and particle feedback materially alter coronal proton spectra; phenomenological fits cannot determine these independently. The new simulations strengthen the case for spatially structured transport, but their passive, high-rigidity particles do not yet calibrate the lower-rigidity, backreacting coronal regime assumed in those neutrino models. No directly comparable improvement factor is established.

10Self-Regulated Cosmic-Ray Electron Acceleration in High-Mach-Number MHD-PIC ShocksAlisa Galishnikova, Siddhartha Gupta, Anatoly Spitkovsky, Xiaochen Sun

Self-Regulated Cosmic-Ray Electron Acceleration in High-Mach-Number MHD-PIC Shocks

Alisa Galishnikova, Siddhartha Gupta, Anatoly Spitkovsky, Xiaochen Sun. arXiv:2611.15879v1; submitted 2026-11-11; first announced 13 November 2026; primary astro-ph.HE. Preprint; 14 pages, 8 figures. Submitted to ApJ.

Hybrid MHD–particle simulations follow kinetic nonthermal electrons interacting with thermal plasma near strong nonrelativistic shocks. The one- and two-dimensional runs use sonic and Alfvénic Mach numbers of 50, an oblique field, reduced mass/light-speed ratios and imposed seed-electron abundances. Electron current excites Bell-type magnetic turbulence, which develops large structures that isotropize the electrons and reduce the escaping current driving further amplification.

Magnetic amplitudes reach roughly δB/B~10 in localized structures, while far-upstream current becomes comparatively insensitive to injection abundance. One-dimensional high-momentum distributions approach f(p)∝p−4; the two-dimensional tail remains steeper over the limited resolved range, so its asymptotic slope is unsettled. Effective electron pressure around 0.3ρ0v02 is measured over a finite momentum interval. It is not a claim that 30% of a supernova's total energy becomes electrons. Escape, radiative losses, finite age and three-dimensional geometry are not included, and the largest artificial seed abundance exceeds the background electron charge density.

The result identifies a feedback loop that can cap the upstream current and magnetic amplification despite increasing injected particles. Its relevance to real remnants requires physical injection, ion-dominated acceleration and larger multidimensional dynamical ranges.

History

Gupta et al. had already demonstrated lepton-driven nonresonant magnetic amplification in kinetic simulations. Diesing and Caprioli showed how downstream scattering-center drift can steepen accelerated spectra. The present upstream current-regulation mechanism is complementary to that downstream effect; it neither establishes the final multidimensional spectrum nor overturns nonlinear shock-acceleration theory.

11LHAASO's Peanut source is a laboratory for studying magnetic fields in the Milky WayMaksim Kleimenov, Yuan Li, Gwenael Giacinti, Dmitri Semikoz

LHAASO's Peanut source is a laboratory for studying magnetic fields in the Milky Way

Maksim Kleimenov, Yuan Li, Gwenael Giacinti and Dmitri Semikoz. arXiv:2611.15473v1 · astro-ph.HE · submitted 11 November 2026; first announced 13 November 2026 · preprint; no journal reference verified. Classification: transport modelling and conditional magnetic-field inference.

Can magnetic structure explain the narrow, two-hotspot, one-sided LHAASO Peanut without introducing multiple accelerators? The authors assume that PSR J0218+4232 supplies electrons that inverse-Compton scatter the cosmic microwave background, adopting a distance near 3 kpc. They compare direct particle trajectories in isotropic Kolmogorov turbulence with guiding-centre transport in anisotropic Goldreich–Sridhar turbulence, including synchrotron/Compton cooling and instrumental angular smoothing.

Both reproduce the morphology, by different mechanisms. In the diffusive example, magnetic bottles increase electron residence times; its one-sided appearance additionally requires a reflecting boundary at the pulsar. In the approximately ballistic model, relativistic beaming selects electrons travelling toward us, while field-line projection produces the hotspots. The two SIDM choices respectively emphasize core growth and eventual gravothermal collapse.

Irregular galaxies' K-band half-light radii track their dark-matter cores approximately one-to-one: core expansion enlarges the stellar system, while collapse can reverse that growth. Merger-driven disk formation breaks the correspondence. High-angular-momentum gas fuels star formation at larger radii, allowing the stellar disk to keep growing while its dark-matter core remains fixed or shrinks. Morphology therefore conditions any inference from galaxy size to dark-sector microphysics.

The result is a population-prediction framework, not a measured interaction strength. The adopted core-resolution floor is approximately 0.45 kpc; the collapsing SI50 sample is compared only through z=0.3 because later central-density evolution is not time-step converged. Resolution, feedback prescriptions and merger history require further testing before extracting precision cross-section limits. Full text, including convergence tests.

Adrian Ka-Wai Chung. arXiv:2611.15914v1, submitted 11 November 2026; first announced 13 November; gr-qc. Preprint. Gravitational-wave constraint.

A massive scalar coupled to the Gauss–Bonnet invariant (sGB) or gravitational Pontryagin density (dynamical Chern–Simons, dCS) changes black-hole quasinormal frequencies. In this paper's normalization, 16πL contains ℓ2θI−12(∇θ)2−12μ2θ2 and the frequency correction scales with ζ=(ℓ/M)4. The author fits one theory at a time to the ringdowns of GW150914, GW190521_074359, GW200129_065458, GW231226_101520 and GW250114_082203. The model resolves axial and polar gravitational modes; GW250114 additionally includes the first overtone. Scalar-led modes are omitted.

The joint, scalar-mass-marginalized 90% credible upper limits are ℓdCS<60.4 km and ℓsGB<59.8 km. Priors are uniform in ℓ∈[0,300] km and μ∈[0,10−8] eV, with validity restrictions ℓ<M, Mμ<1 and damped modes. A central methodological point is correction for the parameter-dependent volume these restrictions remove from each event's nuisance prior. Without that correction the combined endpoints would be 48.5 and 47.1 km, an artificial strengthening. The posterior's apparent upper scalar-mass scale near 3×10−12 eV is set chiefly by model validity; it is not an observational scalar-mass bound. Results also depend on ringdown start times, the perturbative mode model and the retained mode content.

History

The author's earlier massless-scalar analysis used these five events and obtained 90% limits of 45.2 km for dCS and 44.4 km for sGB in the same coupling-length convention, with a different coupling prior. Introducing and marginalizing the scalar mass weakens the endpoint; this is broader parameter coverage rather than a stronger bound. Massive-sGB inspiral tests already reached αGB≲1 km at 90% credibility for masses μ≲10−13 eV, using dipole radiation and a different action normalization and prior. An sGB inspiral–merger–ringdown model gave αGB≲0.31 km from GW230529 in the massless theory. For massless dCS, NICER plus GW170817 tests of neutron-star structure constrain α≲8.5 km at 90% confidence under their action convention and equation-of-state-insensitive relation. These are important complementary baselines, not interchangeable numerical limits: scalar mass, coupling normalization, stellar versus black-hole physics and inference assumptions differ. The new ringdown analysis extends access toward heavier scalars and explicitly exposes prior-volume bias.

53From Matched Black-Hole Scalar Charges to Gravitational-Wave Inspirals in a Massive Scalar–Gauss–Bonnet Effective TheoryJie Wu, Mengfei Sun, Jin Li

From Matched Black-Hole Scalar Charges to Gravitational-Wave Inspirals in a Massive Scalar–Gauss–Bonnet Effective Theory

Jie Wu, Mengfei Sun and Jin Li. arXiv:2611.14934v1, submitted 11 November 2026; first announced 13 November; gr-qc. Preprint. Effective-theory derivation.

A massive scalar sourced by black-hole curvature has both a locally induced charge and a far-field profile. These are not interchangeable: in the action 16πL=R−12(∇ϕ)2−12μ2ϕ2+β0ϕG, with β0 a length squared, the large-radius solution contains a forced 48β0M2/(μ2r6) tail as well as a homogeneous Yukawa term. The coefficient of the exponential therefore cannot simply be declared the worldline charge.

For a weakly coupled nonspinning black hole and ϵ=2μM≪1, matched asymptotics give the local coefficient Cloc=4−43ϵ2+O(ϵ3logpϵ), with no linear mass correction. The renormalized worldline charge also contains scale-dependent logarithms that cancel corresponding terms in the field. Numerical reconstruction finds the quadratic coefficient −1.3333334 in the stated fitting window; independent shooting and boundary-value solutions agree in the decaying-mode coefficient to better than 1.51×10−8 in the reported small-mass tests. These are numerical consistency checks, not observational errors.

The paper then derives leading-PN circular-inspiral dynamics and scalar radiation while retaining the finite-range Yukawa factor. Dipole radiation opens only at orbital frequency Ω>μ, with phase-space suppression (1−μ2/Ω2)3/2; scalar quadrupole radiation opens earlier, at 2Ω>μ, with the corresponding fifth-power suppression. Equal scalar charge-to-mass ratios remove the dipole. Scalar breathing and longitudinal strains satisfy hl/hb=μ2/(nΩ)2 for a propagating harmonic. Source phasing is kept distinct from propagation dispersion.

The illustrative binary is 30+20M⊙, with β0=0.02(20M⊙)2 and a small local scalar-mass parameter. Importantly, those binary examples still use the leading massless charge QA=2β0/mA: the full matched correction has not yet been combined with all conservative and radiative terms at a consistent higher order. Spin, merger, ringdown, cosmological propagation and a detector likelihood are absent; the stationary-phase approximation is nonuniform at radiation thresholds. No new observational bound follows.

History

Van Gemeren, Hinderer and Vandoren recently derived massive-sGB conservative dynamics through 1PN and quadratic scalar-mass order for circular and eccentric binaries. Their Section 4.4.1 explicitly left the massive black-hole charge matching unresolved and used massless charges for illustrative systems. The new local-versus-far-field distinction addresses that open ingredient, while its leading-PN radiation model complements rather than replaces the earlier higher-order conservative calculation. Existing massive-sGB inspiral constraints already reach αGB≲1 km at 90% credibility for μ≲10−13 eV under their action normalization, dipole model and priors. The present paper offers ingredients for improving such waveform tests, not a more stringent coupling limit. A consistently matched, higher-PN waveform with data inference remains to be demonstrated.

54Rapid Multiparameter Gravitational-Wave Tests of General Relativity with Neural Posterior EstimationHong Su, Tao Yang, He Wang

Rapid Multiparameter Gravitational-Wave Tests of General Relativity with Neural Posterior Estimation

Hong Su, Tao Yang and He Wang. arXiv:2611.17038v1, submitted 12 November 2026; first announced 13 November; gr-qc. Preprint. Inference method and reanalysis.

Simultaneously varying several post-Newtonian phase coefficients creates strong degeneracies and expensive likelihood calculations. DINGO-TGR learns a 21-dimensional posterior: 15 source parameters and six fractional phase deviations from 1.5PN through 3.5PN, each with a uniform [−5,5] prior. The precessing, higher-mode IMRPhenomXPHM waveform retains a general-relativistic merger and ringdown. Exact likelihood importance weights correct the neural proposal, and a weighted principal-component analysis isolates informative combinations of the deviations.

Tests include 16 zero-noise GR and 12 non-GR injections, followed by GW150914 and GW250114 data. For GW250114 the best principal component is 0.012−0.055+0.051, a 90% equal-tailed interval consistent with GR. The component is an event- and prior-dependent linear combination, not a universal bound on a single PN coefficient. Repeated Gaussian-noise tests for a fixed GW150914-like source show that 115 of 1,000 GR injections have a discrepancy at least as large as the observed chosen statistic: a calibrated tail probability of 11.5%±1.98% (approximately 95% sampling uncertainty), not evidence for a violation.

After training, reported inference takes 38 minutes for GW150914 and 4.4 hours for GW250114, compared with 176.9 and 237.6 hours for the stated Bilby runs. These timings use different GPU/CPU resources and exclude roughly 67 GPU-hours of training per event configuration; they should not be advertised as a hardware-independent speedup. Importance-sampling efficiencies are 1.21% and 0.041%, yielding effective sample sizes of 4,231 and 1,037. The low efficiency for the louder event matters for scaling. Calibration under Gaussian noise and the chosen waveform does not remove real-detector nonstationarity, waveform error or unmodeled eccentricity.

History

Saleem and collaborators already used principal components to overcome degeneracies among the same six higher-PN deformation parameters; their combined five-event analysis was consistent with GR. DINGO's neural importance-sampling work established likelihood reweighting and effective-sample-size diagnostics for validating learned gravitational-wave posteriors. The advance here is their integration into rapid multiparameter tests, accompanied by explicit noise realizations that calibrate the apparent principal-component offset. Earlier PCA studies including eccentric injections show why omitted source physics can mimic deviations, a limitation the new Gaussian-noise calibration does not settle. The LVK study of GW250114 also found consistency with GR; the present result is a methodological cross-check and efficiency study, not the first test of that event or a new GR anomaly.

Lurkers at the Threshold: GWTC-4.0's near-threshold events

Authors: Daniel Williams and Michael J. Williams. Classification: public-data reanalysis of marginal GW candidates. Primary category: gr-qc. arXiv:2611.16985v1 · full text. Submitted 12 November 2026, 15:50 UTC; first announced in the 13 November UTC listing. Preprint; no journal reference is listed in the inspected record. The analyzed strain is from O4a in 2023–2024, not new November 2026 events.

Question and method. What astrophysical information can be recovered from candidates that entered GWTC-4.0 but did not meet its stricter parameter-estimation threshold? The sample contains 42 candidates with pastro≥0.5 in at least one search pipeline but false-alarm rate FAR≥1yr−1. These are different statistics: FAR measures the rate of noise triggers at least as highly ranked; pastro depends additionally on population and pipeline assumptions.

The authors analyze public GWOSC strain with on-source BayesWave noise spectra, Bilby/Dynesty inference and the precessing, higher-mode IMRPhenomXPHM-SpinTaylor waveform. Automated posterior checks are combined with inspection of detector time–frequency data. Priors are uniform in detector-frame component masses, with 0.05≤q≤1; spin magnitudes are uniform between 0 and 0.99, orientations isotropic, and distance is uniform in comoving volume and source-frame time between 10 and 20,000 Mpc. Source-frame masses assume Planck15 cosmology. Distance is marginalized in the likelihood and reconstructed afterward.

Results. 29 candidates yield accepted parameter posteriors; two are consistent with noise, five are glitch affected, five retain unconstrained chirp masses and one has unresolved multimodality. Acceptance is a statement about parameter-estimation quality, not confirmation of astrophysical origin. Indeed, summing the catalog's terrestrial probabilities within the accepted subset still gives an expectation of about nine noise-origin candidates, conditional on calibration of those probabilities.

The most informative low-mass candidate is GW230920_064709, observed on 20 September 2023. Conditional on the compact-binary signal model, its source-frame chirp mass is 3.88−0.21+0.15M⊙ and its secondary mass lies in 1.7–2.8M⊙, with 90% highest-posterior-density intervals. Its median network SNR is 9.1. This is compatible with a neutron-star–black-hole binary, but the waveform omits tidal effects and the mass estimate alone does not establish a neutron star.

Two candidates have informative effective-spin posteriors. GW230902_172430 has χeff=0.15, a 90% HPD interval of 0.05–0.28, and 99.6% posterior probability above zero. GW230823_142524 has median χeff=−0.41, a 90% HPD interval of −0.71 to −0.02, and 6.7% probability above zero. These posterior probabilities are conditional parameter statements, not independent event significances. The latter's precession indication remains provisional. Across the sample, median information gains are only 0.24 bit in χeff and 0.09 bit in the precession parameter χp.

Limitations. Only one waveform family is used, calibration uncertainty is omitted, and no deglitching is performed. Several broad distance/mass posteriors remain prior sensitive. Rejecting a posterior does not prove the candidate terrestrial, just as accepting it does not validate a detection. The released samples are valuable for follow-up and careful population studies, but require treatment of contamination and selection effects.

History

The GWTC-4.0 catalog deliberately separated its candidate list from a higher-purity subset with detailed source inference: it reported 128 new candidates, with parameter estimates for 86. Its methods explicitly documented pipeline-dependent FAR and pastro, and its search classification already identified GW230920_064709 as a possible NSBH. Thus the new paper adds posterior-level information and a reproducible validation workflow; it does not newly discover these triggers or independently settle their authenticity.

Williams's earlier Beyond GWTC-3 analysis, published in 2025, applied a comparable public-data workflow to 57 candidates from community catalogs. Its comparison exposed meaningful disagreements between inferred parameters, especially spins, and emphasized that extreme spins can also arise when a binary waveform fits a glitch. The present study extends that approach to a well-defined GWTC-4.0 threshold gap, adds systematic automated checks and quantifies information gain. Its most useful lesson is that useful mass information and weak detection significance can coexist; robust classification still requires noise validation and waveform checks beyond a plausible posterior.

Applied to banded iron formations, the measured partitioning suggests that some Archean waters could retain more molybdenum relative to vanadium than previously assumed. This provides a plausible environmental explanation for early molybdenum-dependent nitrogen fixation; it does not directly date the enzyme’s origin.

The published peer-review record challenged whether the reconstruction represents the global ocean. The final article explicitly allows elevated molybdenum to have been confined to particular hydrothermally influenced water masses, reconciling it with contrasting shale and pyrite records. Extrapolation also depends on mineral precursors, pH, competing ions, redox chemistry and alteration after deposition. The published article-in-press PDF, including methods and discussion, was reviewed; the earlier September 2025 preprint was not treated as the final paper.

Kelp retains a conditional asexual pathway when mates are absent

A peer-reviewed PLOS Biology study published 12 November combines developmental experiments, gene-expression analysis and population genetics in Laminaria pallida. Both male and female laboratory cultures produced sporophytes without fertilisation. Male development involved enlarged, egg-like cells and extensive changes in expression away from the usual male programme; asexual offspring showed genome duplication and poorer growth and developmental performance than sexually produced offspring.

Separating potential partners with a 5 µm mesh reduced asexual development when the opposite sex was present, consistent with a diffusible mate-recognition signal. The signal remains unidentified, and the authors could not completely exclude sperm passage through the mesh. Screening 523 wild sporophytes from Namibia and South Africa found no unambiguous population-genetic evidence that asexual reproduction contributes substantially to natural recruitment. The study supports a latent, context-dependent reproductive option, while leaving its importance in sparse or warming wild populations unresolved. The published PDF’s results, experimental descriptions and discussion were reviewed.
