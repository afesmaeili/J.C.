01Magnetic kink instability of jet-wind outflows. I. Static column configurationKrishna Kumar, Serguei S. Komissarov

Magnetic kink instability of jet-wind outflows. I. Static column configuration

Authors: Krishna Kumar, Serguei S. Komissarov.

arXiv:2611.18268v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: numerical plasma dynamics. Can a magnetically dominated jet acquire baryons through a current-driven kink at its interface with a dense disk wind? Three-dimensional relativistic-MHD calculations with PERT follow a static, periodic column with central magnetization $\sigma_j=10^3$, surrounded by a wind with $\sigma_w=10^{-1}$ or $10^{-3}$. The equilibrium has constant magnetic pitch and is approximately force-free away from the transition layer. It deliberately excludes relative bulk motion, rotation and radial expansion.

The linear instability develops at wavelengths longer than $2\pi r_c$ and grows slightly faster than the reference uniform-column maximum $\Gamma_{\max}\simeq0.133v_A/r_c$, where $r_c$ is the characteristic column radius. In the nonlinear phase, the interface coils into a corkscrew and wind material occupies the spaces between its turns. High-resolution runs reveal additional field-aligned fingers, tentatively interpreted as a magnetic Rayleigh–Taylor instability. In the weakly magnetized-wind case, the magnetization averaged over the volume initially occupied by the jet falls from approximately $10^3$ to approximately 20 after the linear stage, then relaxes toward approximately 10. This is a volume-average diagnostic, not a uniform final magnetization of every jet fluid element.

The result identifies a possible route to baryon loading in otherwise highly magnetized outflows, which matters for the composition available to hadronic emission models. It does not calculate a cosmic-ray spectrum, acceleration efficiency or neutrino luminosity. Resolution tests show that secondary structure can disappear on coarse grids; the authors explicitly identify numerical diffusion as an important contributor to the final mixing and magnetization. In a moving jet, the helix would drive waves or shocks into the wind, potentially pushing material away and reducing entrainment. Rotation, expansion, different pitch profiles and open boundaries could also change the outcome. The simulation therefore establishes a mechanism in a controlled configuration, rather than a quantitative baryon-loading prescription for AGN jets.

History

Mizuno, Lyubarsky, Nishikawa and Hardee (2009), arXiv:0903.5358v1, already studied current-driven kinks in three-dimensional relativistic-MHD simulations of static, force-free columns. Their nonlinear helical distortions and strong dependence on magnetic-pitch profile established that kink growth and saturation are not universal: an outward-increasing pitch can slow or saturate the instability, whereas a decreasing pitch promotes continued growth and thin structures. The present paper's new emphasis is the interaction between a highly magnetized central column and an explicitly denser, weakly magnetized surrounding wind, including the resulting intrusion and reduction of volume-averaged magnetization. It is not the first simulation of a relativistic magnetic kink.

A complementary baseline is Barniol Duran, Tchekhovskoy and Giannios (2017), whose global jet calculations connected confinement and recollimation to kink-driven dissipation. Those calculations addressed propagating jets and their conversion of magnetic energy, whereas the new static-column experiment isolates interface mixing. Dissipation and baryon entrainment need not track one another, so neither the earlier dissipation efficiencies nor the new final magnetizations should be transferred unchanged into realistic source models.

02Resonant Trapping of Neutrinophilic Dark Matter in SupernovaeChristopher V. Cappiello, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Amol V. Patwardhan

Resonant Trapping of Neutrinophilic Dark Matter in Supernovae

Authors: Christopher V. Cappiello, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Amol V. Patwardhan.

arXiv:2611.17604v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: model-dependent supernova cooling constraint. The paper considers fermionic dark matter $\chi$ coupled to neutrinos through a complex scalar mediator $\phi$, with an interaction of the form $-g\phi^*\bar\chi P_L\nu+\mathrm{h.c.}$, $m_\phi>m_\chi$, and flavor-universal coupling. Neutrino–antineutrino annihilation produces dark matter through a nonresonant $t$-channel process, while scattering on the ambient neutrino bath can encounter an $s$-channel resonance at $s=m_\phi^2$. The question is whether that resonance prevents the produced particles from escaping and carrying away energy.

Using a Garching 20-solar-mass progenitor profile at one second after core bounce, the authors integrate production over the proto-neutron star and weight escaping particles by an energy-dependent radial attenuation factor, $\exp[-\int dr/\lambda(E,r)]$. Neutrino phase-space blocking is included. Emission is concentrated near a radius of approximately 8 km, while the adopted neutrinosphere is around 40 km. An escaping dark-matter luminosity above $5\times10^{52}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ is treated as excessive cooling. This is a conventional luminosity criterion, not a likelihood-derived 90% or 95% confidence limit.

For the benchmark $m_\phi=3m_\chi$, resonant opacity can open a substantial gap in the usual intermediate-coupling cooling exclusion for mediator masses of roughly 5–100 MeV. At a heavy mediator benchmark of 10 GeV, the more familiar progression from inefficient production to excessive cooling and then strong trapping reappears. The scalar model can consequently evade exclusions that would be inferred by transferring results from a different interaction structure.

Only particles that escape without scattering contribute to the cooling estimate. Scattered particles are not followed through a full transport calculation, making the escape treatment conservative but incomplete. Feedback on the supernova evolution and neutrino signal is not simulated, and the result depends on the adopted progenitor snapshot and mass ratio. External cosmological bounds displayed for comparison are not all derived in precisely this scalar model. The work demonstrates a resonance-induced change in opacity, not evidence for dark matter or a model-independent exclusion of a mass interval.

History

The immediate baseline is Cappiello, Dev and Patwardhan, New Supernova Constraints on Neutrinophilic Dark Sector, arXiv:2503.09691v3. That study examined a vector mediator coupling separately to neutrinos and dark matter. There, neutrino annihilation can resonantly produce dark matter, whereas the scattering responsible for trapping has a different channel structure. In the new scalar interaction the resonance instead enhances scattering, so a similar mediator mass does not imply a similar cooling constraint. The change is physical rather than simply a stronger data set or a numerical tightening of an existing limit; no general improvement factor is meaningful across the two Lagrangians.

Trapping itself has a longer history. For example, Sung, Guo and Wu (2021), Supernova constraint on self-interacting dark sector particles, examined how dark-sector interactions alter supernova energy transport and weaken naive free-streaming limits. That establishes why enhanced production alone cannot determine the exclusion. The distinctive mechanism here is resonant scattering on the abundant ambient neutrinos, rather than a claim that dark-sector trapping was previously unknown.

03Improved Bounds on the Dark Matter-Neutrino Cross Section using LSBGsAkari Suezaki, Shunsaku Horiuchi

Improved Bounds on the Dark Matter-Neutrino Cross Section using LSBGs

Authors: Akari Suezaki, Shunsaku Horiuchi.

arXiv:2611.17922v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: astrophysical, model-dependent scattering bound. Supernova neutrinos can transfer energy to halo dark matter and potentially turn a central cusp into a core. This study asks how much scattering is compatible with the observed rotation curves of low-surface-brightness galaxies. It uses 29 data-set entries—10 from de Blok, four from DensePak and 15 from SPARC—with overlapping galaxies, rather than 29 statistically independent systems. An initial NFW halo is compared with a cored profile matched to the rotation-curve fit. The integrated core-collapse-supernova population is inferred from the present stellar mass with a Kroupa IMF; each explosion supplies $3\times10^{53}$ erg in neutrinos with mean energy 15 MeV, pinching parameter 2.3 and identical flavor spectra.

A key correction is to use the probability that a dark-matter particle scatters, $1-e^{-\lambda}$, rather than count every expected encounter as a newly removed particle. Here $\lambda=\sigma_{\nu\chi}N_{\nu,\mathrm{tot}}/(4\pi r^2)$ in the central, isotropic-supernova approximation. This avoids repeatedly removing the same particle from the inner halo when the expected number of encounters is large.

The strongest low-mass limit comes from F579-V1: $\sigma_{\nu\chi}\lesssim8.5\times10^{-25}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ at $m_\chi=1$ GeV, with the approximately constant branch extending to about 35 GeV. UGC06628 yields approximately $1.5\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ up to about 82 GeV and becomes more competitive at larger masses. These bounds refer to the adopted supernova-neutrino spectrum; an energy-dependent cross section cannot be compared directly with high-energy neutrino limits.

The conservative construction uses a core radius shifted by its quoted $+1\sigma$ error, but this does not define a globally calibrated 90% or 95% confidence bound. Stellar mass, IMF, supernova history, halo structure and the central-source approximation are adopted inputs, not nuisance parameters jointly marginalized in a full likelihood. Maximum recoil-energy transfer in a single collision helps set the mass-dependent regimes. Changing the high-mass IMF slope by $\pm0.3$ changes the estimated supernova count by approximately $-45\%$ or $+59\%$. Baryonic contributions to core formation and uncertainties in the original halo further limit the interpretation.

History

Heston, Horiuchi and Shirai (2024), arXiv:2402.08718v2 / Physical Review D 110, 023004, introduced the corresponding dwarf-spheroidal argument. The published version gives a low-mass Fornax bound near $3.4\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ for masses below approximately 130 GeV under its adopted 15-MeV neutrino setup. The new probability treatment recalculates Fornax to $1.56\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, approximately 46 times weaker: repeated scattering had previously been counted as removal of additional particles. This correction is central to understanding the comparison. Relative to that recalculated Fornax baseline under the same new prescription, F579-V1 is approximately 1,800 times more restrictive on the shared low-mass branch. Comparing only the new galaxy bound with the old published curve would conflate the changed target and changed calculation.

An independent probe is Chauhan et al., Dark Matter Diffused Supernova Neutrino Background, arXiv:2505.03882v1. Its Super-Kamiokande-IV diffuse-neutrino comparison gives a characteristic bound $\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi\lesssim2.4\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm{cm^2\,GeV^{-1}}$ for masses above approximately 1 GeV, using a delayed Galactic supernova-neutrino population, a 16-MeV mean spectrum and an assumed Galactic supernova history. Although the LSBG number is smaller at 1 GeV, differences in the signal, spectrum and astrophysical assumptions preclude presenting their ratio as a clean statistical improvement.

04Self-Consistent Subtracted Second Quasiparticle Random-Phase Approximation for Dipole Responses in Neutron-Rich Oxygen IsotopesM.J. Yang, X. Lian, C.L. Bai, C.L. Zhang, H. Sagawa, H. Q. Zhang

Self-Consistent Subtracted Second Quasiparticle Random-Phase Approximation for Dipole Responses in Neutron-Rich Oxygen Isotopes

Authors: M.J. Yang, X. Lian, C.L. Bai, C.L. Zhang, H. Sagawa, H. Q. Zhang.

arXiv:2611.17721v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: nuclear-response method and calculation. Dipole response controls how nuclei absorb photons, but ordinary quasiparticle random-phase approximation can miss fragmentation and place low-energy states too high. This paper develops a subtracted second quasiparticle RPA, coupling two-quasiparticle and four-quasiparticle configurations, and applies it to $^{18,20,22}$O. The calculation uses a SkM* energy-density functional, HF+BCS reference states and volume pairing, with a harmonic-oscillator basis truncated at $N_{\max}=12$ and $J_{\max}=11/2$, two-quasiparticle energies up to 60 MeV and a stated four-quasiparticle selection cutoff of 0.01.

Subtraction removes the static part of the additional energy-dependent response, preserving the reference static limit while reducing double counting. The implementation includes forward and backward amplitudes, but its retained four-quasiparticle space is restricted to two-particle–two-hole-like configurations: four-particle–four-hole, three-particle–one-hole and three-hole–one-particle contributions are omitted. Particle–particle residual terms are retained in the two-quasiparticle sector rather than throughout the enlarged space. The results should therefore not be read as an untruncated solution of all four-quasiparticle correlations.

The dominant isovector dipole strength shifts down by approximately 4 MeV into the 15–16 MeV region. Isoscalar compressional strength is fragmented and redistributed mainly toward 23–27 MeV. The rms error of the four lowest $1^-$ excitation energies in $^{18}$O falls from 5.31 MeV in QRPA to 0.35 MeV; for two measured states in $^{20}$O it falls from 4.07 to 0.74 MeV.

Better excitation energies do not yet imply reliable absolute photonuclear rates. The calculated low-lying dipole strengths remain one to two orders of magnitude below experimental values. Missing continuum coupling or higher correlations may matter. Consequently, this is a useful development in the microscopic response calculation, not a validated replacement for photodisintegration cross sections or a complete nuclear-cascade library. The six-page main paper was reviewed in full; the separately referenced supplemental derivations were not available in the retrieved main PDF and were not independently checked.

History

Tselyaev (2013), arXiv:1308.1429v1, established the subtraction strategy for extended RPA: replacing the additional interaction by its dynamical part, schematically $W(\omega)-W(0)$, preserves the static response and restores stability under the theorem's assumptions. Thus subtraction and its double-counting motivation predate this calculation.

Gambacurta, Grasso and Engel (2015), arXiv:1506.05975v1, implemented subtracted second RPA in $^{16}$O, including monopole and quadrupole response. Their calculations demonstrated removal of excessive downward shifts, improved cutoff behavior and redistribution of strength through more complex configurations. The present advance is its paired, open-shell quasiparticle formulation and application to dipole states in neutron-rich oxygen. The improved low-lying energies extend that earlier program; the persistent deficit in absolute transition strengths identifies a remaining limitation rather than evidence that the full dipole response has been solved.

058B Solar Neutrino Measurements of Solar Core Size and Temperature from 5805 Days of Super-KamiokandeThe Super-Kamiokande Collaboration (Y. Jiang et al.)

$^8$B Solar Neutrino Measurements of Solar Core Size and Temperature from 5805 Days of Super-Kamiokande

Authors: The Super-Kamiokande Collaboration (Y. Jiang et al.).

arXiv:2611.17651v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: solar-neutrino inference, conditional on solar and oscillation models. The analysis uses 5,805 days of Super-Kamiokande-I–IV solar-neutrino data, combining recoil-electron spectra and day–night information to constrain the spatial distribution of $^8$B neutrino production. It compares four reference solar models, including B16 high- and low-metallicity models and K2 models with accretion. The spectral analysis uses 23 recoil-energy bins spanning 3.49–19.49 MeV with the phase-dependent thresholds. JUNO oscillation priors are imported assuming CPT symmetry; $\sin^2\theta_{12}$ and $\Delta m^2_{21}$ are marginalized while $\theta_{13}$ is fixed.

The reported core radius is defined through the reference radius enclosing 90% of all solar-neutrino production, with a parameterized rescaling of the $^8$B production profile. It is not a direct angular image of the solar core. For the quoted radius limits the independent density-deformation parameter is fixed to zero; the paper also examines the radius–density degeneracy. In the high-metallicity GS98 model, the 90% upper limit on this radius changes from $0.499R_\odot$ using SK alone to $0.268R_\odot$ with SNO. For the K2-MZ reference the corresponding values are $0.381R_\odot$ and $0.215R_\odot$. These limits therefore depend on the reference profile and on which density degrees of freedom are held fixed.

The paper also quotes a central temperature of $(15.599\pm0.0016)\times10^6$ K for its nominal high-metallicity calibration. That very small conditional error is not the total uncertainty of stellar modeling: changing the metallicity prescription shifts the result by approximately $0.16\times10^6$ K.

The v1 temperature presentation requires clarification. The neutrino-production-zone temperature is printed as $15.492\times10^6$ K in the abstract, $14.492\times10^6$ K in the main text/conclusion, and $14.942\times10^6$ K in Table V. Moreover, the displayed flux–temperature scaling places the temperature sensitivity exponent in the inverse direction to the usual relation $\Phi_{^8\mathrm{B}}\propto T^n$. These internally inconsistent numbers and equations should not be silently reconciled or treated as an independently validated precision temperature measurement. They do not by themselves negate the separate spectral radius analysis, whose model dependence remains explicit.

History

Zaidel and Beacom (2025), arXiv:2504.10583v1, developed a related neutrino-based probe of the solar interior: the $^8$B flux carries strong temperature sensitivity, while matter effects in its survival spectrum constrain the production-region density. Their study emphasized reference-model dependence and the benefit of improving oscillation parameters. The present paper takes the spectral idea into a long-exposure SK analysis with external oscillation information and additional SNO data. It does not remove the distinction between an inference conditional on a solar profile and a model-independent reconstruction of that profile.

Davis (2016), Physical Review Letters 117, 211101, had instead proposed using neutrino–electron recoil directions to probe the angular extent of the solar production region. That was a forecast for a different observable and radius construction. Its sensitivity cannot be compared numerically to these 90% profile-based bounds as if both measured the same parameter with the same data and confidence prescription. The new analysis is scientifically distinct from that angular-imaging proposal, and its temperature discrepancies must be resolved before assessing a precision improvement there.

06Discovery of a Short-Duration Fast X-ray Transient Associated with a Short Gamma-Ray BurstYi-Han Iris Yin, Run-Duo Liang, Hui Sun, Jun Yang, Haiwu Pan, Yong-Feng Huang, Jirong Mao, Bin-Bin Zhang, Cui-Yuan Dai, Xiang-Yu Wang, Weimin Yuan, Bing Zhang

Discovery of a Short-Duration Fast X-ray Transient Associated with a Short Gamma-Ray Burst

Authors: Yi-Han Iris Yin, Run-Duo Liang, Hui Sun, Jun Yang, Haiwu Pan, Yong-Feng Huang, Jirong Mao, Bin-Bin Zhang, Cui-Yuan Dai, Xiang-Yu Wang, Weimin Yuan, Bing Zhang.

arXiv:2611.18210v1 · Full text. Submitted 13 November 2026; first announced in the 16 November 2026 UTC batch. Status: arXiv v1 preprint; journal publication not verified.

Study type: archival transient analysis and association. A targeted search for Einstein Probe/WXT counterparts to short gamma-ray bursts identifies EP260411a as a candidate X-ray counterpart to GRB260411A. The event occurred on 11 April 2026 at 06:36:28 UTC; this is a new paper about an earlier observation, not a 16 November event alert. The parent search covers 166 short GRBs from January 2024 through June 2026. Of 133 poorly localized events, six had the required WXT coverage over the relevant 20-second interval.

The WXT source region contains ten photons. The quoted local source-detection significance is $5.26\sigma$, obtained with the source/background-region comparison; it is not a $5.26\sigma$ significance for the X-ray/GRB association. A control search over 1,000 separate 20-second intervals finds no analogous candidate. With stationary Poisson event rates and a spatial-uniformity approximation, the authors derive a local chance-coincidence upper bound below $2.73\times10^{-3}$ and a six-trial global upper bound below $1.62\times10^{-2}$.

The X-ray duration is $T_{90}=6.1^{+0.7}{-1.1}$ s in 0.5–4 keV, compared with $1.3^{+0.2}}$ s in 50–300 keV. Both display a single pulse with energy-dependent timing. A joint WXT/GBM smoothly broken power law with an exponential cutoff and Galactic absorption fixed at $N_H=1.87\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ gives a low-energy break $E_b=7^{+6{-3}$ keV and peak $E_p=386^{+288}}$ keV. These are quoted $1\sigma$ uncertainties. A cutoff power law with free absorption also fits, with $N_H=5.25^{+5.67{-3.83}\times10^{21}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ and $E_p=252^{+296}$ keV. The BIC values, 80.66 and 86.50, favor the broken model but do not uniquely establish a break mechanism.

There is no measured host redshift, confirmed kilonova or gravitational-wave counterpart. The source's track on the energy–peak-energy plane is calculated over assumed redshifts, not a distance measurement. A short central-engine activity time is compatible with a promptly formed black hole, but does not prove the remnant identity. Low photon counts, background assumptions, absorption–spectral-shape degeneracy and the limited counterpart sample bound the interpretation.

History

Sun et al., arXiv:2307.05689v1, later published in National Science Review, analyzed soft X-rays from the merger-associated GRB230307A. Its gamma-ray duration was approximately 41.5 s and its X-ray emission lasted approximately 200 s, with a soft component interpreted as consistent with a magnetar-powered contribution. That work showed why long soft emission and merger origin cannot be classified solely from the conventional short/long gamma-ray division; the engine interpretation was still model dependent.

More directly, Li et al., arXiv:2601.14137v1, studied EP250704a/GRB250704B: an approximately 0.35-s prompt X-ray spike accompanied a 0.37-s gamma-ray event, followed by a soft bump lasting roughly 540 s. Short prompt X-ray emission associated with a short GRB therefore already had a precedent. The new object adds a largely isolated short X-ray transient without a prominent long soft component. Its value is the extension of the observed phenomenology and joint prompt spectral coverage, not a demonstration that all short-GRB remnants are black holes or that a brief X-ray spike was previously unknown.

Astrophysical Alerts

IceCube-261113A: a Gold track with no significant Fermi-LAT counterpart

IceCube detected a track-like neutrino candidate on 13 November 2026 at 16:48:28.48 UTC. The offline reconstruction gives RA 165.45° (+0.22°/−0.16°), Dec −14.94° (+0.20°/−0.19°), with the quoted asymmetric intervals describing the 90% point-spread-function containment. It entered the Gold alert stream and has an estimated atmospheric-background false-alarm rate of 0.2215 events yr⁻¹. No FL16Y gamma-ray source lies inside the 90% region. This is a high-purity neutrino candidate, not a source association. The event was reported in GCN on 14 November at 16:08 UTC. IceCube circular.

Fermi-LAT reported on 15 November at 13:06 UTC that its survey data show no new >5σ excess above 100 MeV within the IceCube 90% region. For a point source fixed at the neutrino best-fit direction with photon index 2.0, the preliminary 95% confidence photon-flux limits above 100 MeV are <2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ cm⁻² s⁻¹ over the approximately 18-year mission interval, <4.9 × 10⁻⁹ cm⁻² s⁻¹ over the month before the event, and <9.1 × 10⁻⁸ cm⁻² s⁻¹ over the preceding day. These limits constrain one assumed spectral model; the non-detection does not establish that the neutrino lacks an electromagnetic counterpart. Fermi-LAT follow-up.

GRB 261113A: bright prompt emission, an optical flash and a secure redshift

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on 13 November at 14:31:02 UTC. The initial 8–120 keV image had S/N 56.88, and the 90% localization radius was 2.42 arcmin, including a 2-arcmin systematic term. MXT began 131 s later and found an uncatalogued X-ray source. Initial SVOM alert. The refined ECLAIRs analysis gives T90 = 7.8 ± 0.1 s in 4–120 keV. A cutoff power law fitted over 5–120 keV has index −0.61 ± 0.05 and observed peak energy 108 ± 10 keV; the 4–120 keV fluence is (2.81 ± 0.09) × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻². At the measured redshift, the reported standard-cosmology estimates are Eiso = (1.10 ± 0.09) × 10⁵³ erg and rest-frame Ep = 495 keV; all quoted ECLAIRs errors are 68% intervals. Its position on the Amati plane is consistent with the authors’ Type-II/collapsar-like comparison, but that consistency is not by itself a progenitor identification. Refined prompt analysis.

The earliest reported optical observations began 74 s after trigger; MASTER measured an unfiltered magnitude of approximately 15.7 at 79 s, while NUTTelA-TAO measured i′ = 16.3 ± 0.07 and g′ = 17.2 ± 0.08 AB at 174 s. MASTER, NUTTelA-TAO. VLT/X-shooter spectroscopy beginning 14 November at 00:42:17 UTC, 10.18 h after trigger, detected the Lyman break and C II, Si II, C IV and Fe II absorption at z = 3.580; the acquisition image gave r = 22.00 ± 0.06 AB at 9.98 h. VLT redshift. The multi-instrument sequence establishes a rapidly fading afterglow and a secure absorption redshift; detailed energetics still depend on calibration and cosmology, and no contemporaneous neutrino or gravitational-wave association was reported.

GRB 261114A: a long SVOM burst at z = 3.716

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on 14 November at 09:33:13 UTC. The 8–120 keV image had S/N 13.84, the initial 90% radius was 5.91 arcmin, and the broad single pulse had T90 ≈ 46⁺⁶₋₉ s. MXT found no onboard X-ray source; that instrumental non-detection does not exclude an afterglow. Initial alert. VT identified a fading optical candidate at RA 345.81339°, Dec −32.31699° with a 0.50-arcsec uncertainty: at 6.45 min it measured VT_B = 19.54 ± 0.02 and VT_R = 18.09 ± 0.01 AB, fading by roughly half a magnitude by 14.55 min. The photometry was not corrected for Galactic reddening. VT follow-up.

VLT/FORS2 spectra obtained from 15 November at 03:44:31 UTC, 18.19 h after trigger, show Lyα and narrow Si II, Si IV and C IV absorption at z = 3.716. The acquisition image measured i = 22.6 ± 0.2 AB at 18.07 h. This is a spectroscopic redshift for the afterglow; the circular does not yet provide refined prompt energetics. VLT/FORS2.

GRB 261113B: a particle-classified trigger recovered as a GRB

Fermi/GBM triggered on 13 November at 19:45:55.10 UTC, but the flight software initially classified the event as distant particles. Ground analysis instead identifies a likely long GRB at RA 168.2°, Dec +42.4°, with a 2.76° statistical localization uncertainty; the event was 147° from the LAT boresight. The reclassification was reported on 14 November at 23:55 UTC. Fermi/GBM final localization.

Insight-HXMT/HE independently detected a multi-pulsed event at the same time. Its CsI data give T90 = 8.0⁺³·⁵₋₆·⁰ s, 1-s peak rate 811 counts s⁻¹ from T0+0.25 s, and 1434 total counts in the detector’s approximately 60–900 keV deposited-energy band. These are instrumental count measurements, not a calibrated fluence. The independent detection supports the GRB reclassification, but no counterpart or redshift was reported. Insight-HXMT.

EP261115a: an uncatalogued X-ray transient without an optical identification

Einstein Probe/WXT triggered on 15 November at 00:22:13 UTC, localizing EP261115a to RA 58.644°, Dec −44.311° with a 3-arcmin 90% radius. FXT found an uncatalogued source at RA 58.6217°, Dec −44.2950°, with a 20-arcsec 90% radius including statistical and systematic uncertainty. No X-ray flux or duration was supplied in the initial report. Einstein Probe detection.

FRAM-Auger began at 01:25:27 UTC, about 63 min after trigger, and found no new source in the FXT circle, reaching R_C > 18.9 Vega at 3σ in a 48-frame stack near 92 min. FRAM-Auger. SVOM/VT began at 01:59:00 UTC and obtained deeper 3σ limits at 2.43 h of VT_B > 23.6 and VT_R > 23.3 AB. SVOM/VT. These limits exclude bright optical emission at the stated times and bands; they do not identify the X-ray source or prove the absence of an optical counterpart at other epochs or wavelengths.

EP261114a is reclassified as a likely stellar flare

WXT triggered on 14 November at 19:40:35.649 UTC, initially reporting an uncatalogued transient at RA 68.336°, Dec −3.541° with a 3-arcmin 90% radius; FXT found a source inside that region. Initial alert. Telemetry analysis refined the FXT position to RA 68.3436°, Dec −3.50425°, with an approximately 10-arcsec 90% radius, and associated it with the high-proper-motion star UCAC4 433-006766. The estimated 0.5–4 keV flux was 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, corresponding to a quoted luminosity of 5.6 × 10³¹ erg s⁻¹. The team described the event as likely a stellar flare; no association probability or uncertainty on the flux was supplied. Classification update.

EP261006a: a delayed, brightening nuclear radio source

VLA follow-up of the 6 October X-ray transient was reported on 13 November at 19:35 UTC. No significant 10-GHz emission appeared at the X-ray/optical position on 9, 12 or 20 October, with a representative 3σ limit of 26 μJy; the 20 October epoch contained a marginal approximately 3σ signal. On 31 October, VLA securely detected 42.2 ± 4.5 μJy at RA 01:27:10.006, Dec +12:53:26.67, with 0.1-arcsec uncertainty, coincident with the host-galaxy nucleus. The sequence indicates a brightening radio source, but the nuclear coincidence alone does not determine the transient mechanism. VLA follow-up.

EP-WXT trigger 01709322455: the G 6-43 flare correction resolves the prior notice mismatch

A correction issued on 15 November at 14:41 UTC confirms that the optical observations reported in GCN 46243 concerned EP-WXT trigger 01709322455 and the star G 6-43. SND@LHC prepares an all-silicon upgrade for the high-luminosity LHC

CERN announced on 13 November that SND@LHC will replace its nuclear-emulsion target with a fully electronic detector for high-luminosity LHC running. The experiment sits about 480 m downstream of ATLAS and recorded Run 3 data from July 2022 through June 2026. Its upgrade will reuse 1,680 silicon-microstrip modules removed from the CMS outer tracker; a prototype is now in beam tests. Real-time readout will replace the integrated exposure delivered by emulsions, which cannot tolerate the future particle flux, although the silicon system has coarser intrinsic position resolution.

The design also includes a magnet intended to distinguish neutrino from antineutrino interactions. CERN identifies direct detection of the tau antineutrino as a central target: it is the remaining Standard Model particle whose interaction has not been observed directly. This is a detector-development milestone, not a tau-antineutrino observation or a demonstrated sensitivity. CERN did not publish a complete construction schedule, exposure forecast or expected event yield in the announcement.

A compact high-rate quantum code gets a fault-tolerant encoder

Naoyuki Kanomata and Hayato Goto report a fault-tolerant state-preparation method for a symplectic-double quantum code in a peer-reviewed Science Advances paper published 13 November. The code encodes six logical qubits in 30 physical qubits. According to the RIKEN and JST technical announcement dated 14 November, the new all-plus-state circuit reduces the number of CNOT gates from 108 to 67 and adds a procedure for detecting damaging faults during encoding.

Numerical simulations reported by the team retain a logical error rate comparable to the earlier construction while reducing the total number of required physical qubits by more than half; the authors also construct arbitrary-state encoding for all six logical qubits. The advance addresses preparation overhead for a specific high-rate code. It is a design and simulation result, not an experimental demonstration of logical qubits, and its practical value will depend on hardware connectivity, gate errors, syndrome extraction and the cost of the remaining fault-tolerant operations.

A metal-organic framework creates a sharply polarized triplet radical pair

A peer-reviewed Nature Communications study published 13 November places porphyrin electron donors and naphthalene-diimide acceptors roughly 10 Å apart in a metal-organic framework. Transient spectroscopy gives charge separation on a 33 ps timescale, much faster than the approximately 1.8 ns intersystem-crossing pathway in the control system. The apparent charge-separation yield is close to 0.98, but the authors estimate that the long-lived triplet charge-separated population is only about 0.1%, corresponding to roughly 1.6 × 10⁻³ spins per pore.

Electron-paramagnetic-resonance measurements show selective population of the triplet radical pair's T0 sublevel, with relaxation that changes when guest solvents occupy the pores. This gives a chemically tunable route to spin hyperpolarization in a crystalline scaffold. The work does not demonstrate long transverse coherence, quantum sensing or transfer of polarization to an analyte; those are potential applications rather than measured capabilities.

An end-to-end neural system forecasts the eddy-resolving global ocean

Xiang Zhang and colleagues present WenHai-E2E in a peer-reviewed Science Advances article published 13 November. Unlike many machine-learning ocean models that still require initial conditions from an operational numerical forecast, the system ingests multisource ocean observations and produces a global eddy-resolving forecast without that numerical initialization pipeline. The publisher-indexed abstract reports higher forecast accuracy than a state-of-the-art numerical global ocean forecast system.

The architecture is scientifically relevant because the initialization step is a major barrier to independent deployment of data-driven ocean forecasts. Evidence access: the article page was inaccessible at the cutoff, so only the bibliographic record and abstract were reviewed; no unverified skill score, resolution, lead time or compute-saving factor is quoted here. Performance on the authors' test distribution is not yet evidence of operational robustness under sparse observations, regime shifts or extreme events, and the comparison needs the full methods and evaluation protocol for independent assessment.

High-precision krypton-81 dating reaches the Late Pleistocene radiocarbon overlap

A peer-reviewed Science Advances study published 13 November reports an Atom Trap Trace Analysis workflow that lowers the practical young-age boundary of krypton-81 groundwater dating from roughly 50,000 years to about 10,000 years. In North China Plain samples, paired carbon-14 and krypton-81 ages agree for approximately 10–30 ka after carbon-geochemical correction; older samples retain an absolute krypton clock after radiocarbon corrections become increasingly uncertain. The dual tracer also helps identify mixed groundwater, and the screened oxygen-isotope sequence is consistent with polar-ice palaeoclimate records.

Krypton-81 is chemically inert and has a half-life of about 229,000 years, so the improved atom counting closes a useful gap between radiocarbon and conventional krypton dating. Evidence access: the quantitative claims above come from the indexed abstract; the full article could not be retrieved at the cutoff. The demonstration is in one aquifer system, and age interpretation still depends on sampling, mixing and the atmospheric isotope baseline. The result extends an analytical method; it does not make every old groundwater sample uniquely dateable.

Reconstructed mountain-belt thickness rises in two deep-time steps

Matthijs Smit and colleagues compiled metamorphic peak pressures over the past 3 billion years to infer maximum crustal thickness in a peer-reviewed Science Advances paper published 13 November. Their reconstruction shows two broad increases: from roughly 30 to 60 km around 2.7–2.2 billion years ago, and from roughly 60 to 90 km around 0.8–0.7 billion years ago. The timing overlaps changes in terrigenous element delivery, sulfur metabolism and surface oxygenation.

The proposed mechanism is that taller, more extensive mountain belts exposed more rock to weathering and erosion, increasing nutrient flux and helping alter ocean-atmosphere chemistry. The UBC research summary published 13 November correctly presents this as a mechanistic proposal. Correlation across coarse geological timescales does not establish that mountain growth caused oxygenation or biological diversification. Maximum preserved pressure is also an indirect proxy for mountain-belt height and is vulnerable to preservation, sampling and tectonic-model assumptions.

Enhancer-targeted CRISPR activation rescues cellular phenotypes in autism-gene models

A peer-reviewed, open-access Nature Neuroscience study published 13 November maps regulatory enhancers for CHD8 and SCN2A, two genes in which loss of one functional copy can cause neurodevelopmental disorders. The researchers used dCas9-p300 to activate selected enhancers of the remaining intact allele in human-pluripotent-stem-cell-derived excitatory neurons and cerebral forebrain organoids. Expression gains persisted for three to five months, depending on the construct.

Untreated CHD8 mutant organoids showed 5,933 differentially expressed genes relative to controls; enhancer activation shifted transcriptomes toward the control state and improved proliferation and maturation markers. In SCN2A models, treatment improved neuronal maturation and several intrinsic-excitability and action-potential measures, although resting membrane potential was not fully normalized. Organoid-to-organoid variability was substantial, and in one configuration only about a quarter of cells expressed the activator. This is preclinical cell and organoid evidence, not an autism treatment. Delivery to the developing human brain, dose control, off-target regulation, timing and long-term safety remain unresolved.

Tirzepatide produces large 72-week BMI reductions in an adolescent trial

The phase 3 SURMOUNT-ADOLESCENTS trial was published online in JAMA on 15 November. It randomized 160 participants aged 12 to under 18 with obesity, or overweight plus an obesity-related complication, in a 2:1 ratio to once-weekly tirzepatide or placebo alongside lifestyle intervention. At 72 weeks, mean BMI changed by −18.5% with tirzepatide and +0.5% with placebo, a treatment difference of −19.0 percentage points (95% CI, −23.9 to −14.1; P < .001). At least 5% BMI reduction occurred in 79.6% versus 28.5% of participants.

Gastrointestinal adverse events occurred in 81.5% of the treatment group and 54.9% of placebo recipients; 9.3% receiving tirzepatide discontinued because of adverse events, compared with none receiving placebo. Serious adverse events occurred in 7.4% and 3.9%, respectively, and no deaths were reported. The sponsor-supported trial is small and lasts 72 weeks, has limited racial and ethnic diversity, and includes very few participants with type 2 diabetes or merely overweight. It establishes efficacy over the trial period; it does not resolve durability after stopping treatment, rare harms, effects on growth and maturation, or general effectiveness in routine adolescent care.

A tau fragment distinguishes mixed 3R/4R pathology in a small autopsy-linked cohort

A peer-reviewed JAMA study published online 13 November evaluated the fluid marker eMTBR-tau243 in 229 participants, including cerebrospinal-fluid samples from 196 and plasma from 55. In autopsy-confirmed Alzheimer disease, median cerebrospinal-fluid concentration was 0.459 fmol/mL (10 cases), compared with 0.002 fmol/mL in 29 cognitively unimpaired participants and 0.015 fmol/mL across 52 non-Alzheimer tauopathies. Among symptomatic MAPT mutation carriers, the six mixed 3-repeat/4-repeat cases had a median of 0.140 fmol/mL, versus 0.004 fmol/mL in 15 four-repeat cases (P = .001).

The result supports eMTBR-tau243 as a marker of mixed 3R/4R tau pathology and suggests possible value beyond Alzheimer disease. It is not a validated standalone diagnostic test: the rare-disease subgroups are very small, samples came from heterogeneous retrospective biobanks, plasma data were more limited, and biofluid-to-autopsy intervals were not systematically modeled. The paper also reports relevant intellectual-property and industry relationships, including a licensed MTBR-tau243 patent held by the authors' institution.
