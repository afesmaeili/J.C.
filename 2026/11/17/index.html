01The imprint of neutron escape on non-thermal protons in AGN coronaeAntonio Ambrosone and Damiano F. G. Fiorillo

The imprint of neutron escape on non-thermal protons in AGN coronae

Authors: Antonio Ambrosone and Damiano F. G. Fiorillo.

arXiv:2611.21047v1 · Full text. Submitted 16 November 2026 at 12:59:46 UTC; first announced in the 17 November listing. Status: preprint, intended for submission to JCAP; no journal acceptance verified.

Question and method. Can proton-to-neutron conversion substantially alter the energetics inferred for neutrino-producing AGN coronae? The authors solve coupled, steady-state proton and neutron transport, including stochastic acceleration, continuous Bethe–Heitler and synchrotron losses, and discrete photohadronic and hadronuclear interactions. Their “trickle-down” treatment removes a parent nucleon and reinjects a leading proton or neutron at lower energy, using channel-conditioned mean energy fractions and the corresponding energy-mapping Jacobian. SOPHIA through Chromo supplies photohadronic inputs; Sibyll supplies hadronuclear inputs. This is a transport-model comparison, not a new IceCube measurement or statistical exclusion.

The turbulent-corona benchmark adopts a black-hole mass of $6.7\times10^6M_\odot$, radius $R_c=10GM/c^2$, 2–10-keV luminosity $L_X=3\times10^{43}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$, Thomson depth 0.5, magnetization 1 and coherence length $0.5R_c$. These imply $B\simeq3.8\times10^4$ G and an acceleration time near $1.65\times10^3$ s. Two radiation geometries bracket the influence of optical–UV disk photons: disk plus coronal radiation, and X-rays alone. Both continuous-loss and discrete-interaction solutions are normalized to the same stationary proton energy density, $u_p=0.25u_{B,\mathrm{tur}}$.

Results. Magnetically confined protons can convert to neutrons that leave on a light-crossing time, long before beta decay. Sustaining the prescribed nonthermal population then requires approximately two orders of magnitude more seed-proton injection than in the continuous-loss treatment for the adopted confinement law. This is an injection-rate comparison at fixed stationary energy density; it should not be read as a measured hundredfold increase in a source luminosity.

In the X-ray-only turbulent benchmark, the neutrino spectral maximum shifts downward by roughly a factor of four, from several tens of TeV to below 10 TeV. The main calculation gives an integrated neutrino luminosity approximately 35% lower than the continuous-loss result. The appendix's alternative Sim-B production treatment gives about 39% without secondary cooling and about 30% with pion and muon synchrotron cooling. The distinction between these numbers reflects both cooling and different production parametrizations. When disk photons make Bethe–Heitler losses dominant, the spectral change is smaller.

A separate reconnection benchmark uses $R_c=2GM/c^2$, $B\simeq1.4\times10^5$ G and a prescribed broken-power-law injection with a 25-TeV break. Here both treatments use the same injected proton power, and protons already escape ballistically. The neutrino change remains at the percent level, even though neutrons carry about 25% of the stationary nucleon energy density. Thus the large turbulent-corona correction is not universal across acceleration and escape prescriptions.

Limitations. This is a one-zone, fixed-background calculation with selected benchmarks rather than a fit marginalizing over source parameters. Daughter energies use channel means, not full redistribution distributions. Neutron-induced yields invoke approximate isospin symmetry; CEL and discrete photopion coefficients do not use exactly the same leading-baryon observable. The appendix's cooling calculation neglects the subdominant hadronuclear contribution and uses simplified photopion channels. Faster hydrodynamic escape would reduce the injection-rate enhancement. Emission from neutrons after they leave the corona is not modeled. The quoted effects therefore quantify the sensitivity to charge exchange and confinement, not a universal correction factor for every Seyfert model.

History

Neutron escape from magnetized accretion flows is established physics. Toma and Takahara (2012), Baryon Loading of AGN Jets Mediated by Neutrons, explicitly compared proton acceleration, photohadronic and hadronuclear losses, neutron escape and decay, then studied how escaping neutrons deposit mass and energy in a polar jet. Their treatment already made the basic distinction between confined charged particles and freely escaping neutral particles. The new paper's contribution is the quantitative feedback of that conversion on the accelerating coronal proton distribution and its neutrino emission; it does not discover neutral-particle escape.

The more immediate baseline is Fiorillo et al. (2024), A magnetized strongly turbulent corona as the source of neutrinos from NGC 1068. Its nonresonant stochastic-acceleration model linked the available turbulent magnetic power, proton cooling and the neutrino spectrum. In its disk-photon benchmark, Bethe–Heitler losses were important near 20 TeV and diffusive escape much slower. The present calculation retains that competition but adds explicit charge exchange, regeneration and neutron escape. This explains why the largest spectral shift occurs in the new X-ray-only case, while the injection requirement can change strongly even when the neutrino spectrum looks similar. The two papers use different benchmarks, so the factor-of-four shift is an internal comparison of the new paper's treatments, not a direct revision factor for every result in the 2024 study.

The new paper also acknowledges contemporaneous nuclear-transport work in its note added in preparation. That comparison was not independently completed here; no claim of exclusive priority or exhaustive novelty is made. Its most secure advance is the demonstrated importance of coupling nucleon conversion and regeneration directly to ongoing acceleration when inferring the required seed population.

Astrophysical Alerts

IceCube-261113A: no additional track-like neutrinos in either prompt search window

IceCube reported on 16 November 2026 at 15:43 UTC a follow-up to the Gold-stream track candidate detected on 13 November at 16:48:28.482 UTC. Excluding the alert event itself, the collaboration found zero additional track-like events inside the candidate's 90% localization in a 1,000-s window (16:40:08.482–16:56:48.482 UTC) and in a separate two-day window (12 November 16:48:28.482 to 14 November 16:48:28.482 UTC). Both searches returned p = 1.00, consistent with no additional-event excess. IceCube follow-up; initial event report.

For an assumed E−2.5 point-source spectrum, IceCube quotes a sensitivity in E2dN/dE at 1 TeV of 3.4–3.5 GeV cm⁻² across the 90% region for the 1,000-s search and 3.3–3.4 GeV cm⁻² for the two-day search. For the stated declination and spectrum, 90% of detectable events would lie at approximately 10⁴–2 × 10⁶ GeV. These are model-dependent sensitivities to an additional neutrino component. They neither retract the original Gold candidate nor establish or exclude an astrophysical source association.

GRB 261113A: independent prompt measurements and very early optical photometry

The burst occurred on 13 November at 14:31:02 UTC. GECAM-A and GECAM-B follow-ups issued on 16 November independently resolve the multi-pulsed prompt emission in approximately 70–6000 keV. GECAM-A reports T90 = 8.2⁺¹·⁰₋₀·₈ s and, over T0−2.7 to T0+6.9 s, a power-law index −2.44⁺⁰·²²₋₀·²¹ with a 10–1000 keV fluence of (2.85⁺⁰·⁵⁹₋₀·⁷⁴) × 10⁻⁵ erg cm⁻². GECAM-B reports T90 = 7.4⁺⁰·⁶₋₀·⁴ s and, over T0−1.7 to T0+6.5 s, index −2.55⁺⁰·¹⁹₋₀·²⁰ with fluence (1.07⁺⁰·³⁰₋₀·²⁰) × 10⁻⁵ erg cm⁻². GECAM-A; GECAM-B. The reported fluences are not identical measurements: the satellites used different time intervals and responses, so their numerical difference should not be read as physical variability.

MITSuME Akeno began three-colour imaging 1.58 min after trigger. A 540-s stack centred at T0+400 s measured Rc = 17.2 ± 0.2 and Ic = 16.9 ± 0.2 AB, with g′ > 17.6 AB (5σ). This independently confirms the early optical afterglow at the previously reported position. MITSuME. Glowbug on the International Space Station also detected multi-peaked emission from about T0−1 to T0+7 s, but its report is explicitly preliminary: the observation occurred during high radiation-belt background and the response lacks a detailed model of surrounding ISS passive material. Glowbug. These additions strengthen the multi-instrument prompt and afterglow record; they do not change the secure z = 3.580 redshift reported earlier.

GRB 261114A: a long burst at z = 3.716 with refined prompt energetics and a late optical limit

For the burst triggered by SVOM/ECLAIRs on 14 November at 09:33:13 UTC, the refined ECLAIRs event data give an imaging-defined T90 = 73.9⁺⁷·⁸₋₉·₃ s in 4–120 keV. From T0−17.68 to T0+56.70 s, a power law with index −1.58 ± 0.07 yields a 4–120 keV fluence of (2.14 ± 0.20) × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻² and photon flux (9.31 ± 0.43) × 10⁻¹ ph cm⁻² s⁻¹; the reported uncertainties are 68% intervals. SVOM/ECLAIRs.

Konus-Wind independently found a roughly 7σ count-rate excess from T0+11.011 to T0+22.787 s. In 20 keV–10 MeV it reports fluence 1.64⁺⁰·⁶²₋₀·⁴⁴ × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻², a 2.944-s peak energy flux of 1.61⁺⁰·⁷¹₋₀·⁵⁵ × 10⁻⁷ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, and photon index −2.22⁺⁰·¹⁹₋₀·²⁴. Assuming the measured z = 3.716 and the stated Planck-like cosmology, the team estimates Eiso = 4.70⁺¹·⁷⁹₋₁·²⁵ × 10⁵² erg and Liso = 2.18⁺⁰·⁹⁵₋₀·⁷⁵ × 10⁵² erg s⁻¹; all are preliminary 68% results. Konus-Wind.

VST/OmegaCAM observed the field on 16 November from 00:34:57 UTC, about 39 h after trigger, and did not recover the afterglow in stacked images: r > 24.1 AB at 39.43 h and g > 24.5 AB at 39.13 h (3σ, 1-arcsec aperture; not corrected for Galactic extinction). At z = 3.716 the g band lies blueward of Lyα and adds little constraint; the r limit is consistent with extrapolating the earlier SVOM/VT decay, which predicts r ≈ 24.0. The non-detection therefore tracks ordinary fading rather than demonstrating an abrupt disappearance. VST/OmegaCAM.

GRB 261115A: GECAM-B measures a short multi-pulsed prompt phase

GECAM-B independently triggered on the Fermi/GBM event on 15 November at 22:10:54 UTC. Its approximately 70–6000 keV light curve gives T90 = 4.0⁺³·⁵₋₀·⁵ s. A power-law fit from T0−1.25 to T0+3.25 s has index −2.23⁺⁰·¹⁷₋₀·¹⁵ and a 10–1000 keV fluence of (6.8⁺¹·¹₋₀·⁸) × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻². The on-ground localization is RA 3.3°, Dec −26.3°, with a 5.0° statistical uncertainty. GECAM-B.

This is a calibrated prompt-emission characterization, but the localization remains broad and the circular reports no afterglow, host or redshift. The measurement therefore confirms the gamma-ray event and its approximate duration; it does not by itself identify the source environment.

Science News

A diamond maser amplifies microwaves near the quantum-noise limit at millikelvin temperature

A peer-reviewed Nature Communications article published 16 November reports the first non-superconducting maser amplifier operated near the quantum-noise limit at millikelvin temperature. The device uses nitrogen impurity spins in diamond and microwave pumping, with four-spin cross-relaxation creating population inversion. At 6.595 GHz it reaches more than 30 dB maximum power gain; the measured minimum input-referred noise is 3.05 ± 0.23 quanta, or about 2.55 quanta above the standard half-quantum limit, corresponding to a noise temperature of approximately 0.96 K.

The gain profile can be shifted by more than 10 MHz with a static magnetic field. At 20 dB gain the input and output 1 dB compression points are approximately −85 dBm and −65 dBm, respectively. The result supplies a non-superconducting route to cryogenic microwave amplification, but the noise, bandwidth and tunability remain behind leading superconducting parametric amplifiers. The authors identify imperfect spin inversion, insertion loss and pump power as practical limitations; this is a laboratory amplifier demonstration, not yet a drop-in quantum-computing readout system.

US Supreme Court leaves restoration of terminated research grants in place

On 16 November, the US Supreme Court declined to hear the federal government's appeal in the University of California research-grant case, leaving the lower-court injunction in force. The dispute concerns competitive grants terminated by the National Science Foundation and other agencies because the research was considered inconsistent with the administration's policies on diversity, equity and inclusion. The Supreme Court's action contained no opinion or merits ruling.

The lower courts found that the researchers had shown a substantial likelihood that the cancellations constituted unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination, and ordered the affected grants restored while litigation continues. Reports differ on the relevant monetary total because they describe different subsets of the wider grant dispute; the specific appeal covered roughly $59 million according to Reuters, while broader University of California terminations were much larger. The denial preserves the existing injunction but does not finally resolve federal authority over research funding or establish a nationwide rule for all cancelled grants.

Cryo-electron tomography resolves a functional pore in the poxvirus core

Ye Hong and colleagues used cryo-electron tomography and subtomogram averaging to determine the vaccinia-virus portal complex at 6.7 Å resolution and its central pore at 4.4 Å, in a peer-reviewed Nature paper published 16 November. Structural prediction and experimental density identify the viral proteins E8, E6 and L3 as the main pore components. A narrow, positively charged constriction is formed by conserved lysines in L3; replacing these residues impaired viral gene expression and propagation.

Poxviruses transcribe early messenger RNA inside an intact viral core, so they require a route for molecular exchange with the cytoplasm. The channel's dimensions, charge and mutational sensitivity support a portal function, but the study does not directly visualize RNA passing through the pore. A related independent structure published in July likewise identified a conserved hexameric E6/E8/L3 complex and connected it to assembly, early-mRNA release and genome uncoating. The new work strengthens the structural model and identifies a functional constriction; therapeutic targeting remains a proposal rather than a demonstrated antiviral strategy.

A scalable plasma immunoassay tracks advanced insoluble tau pathology

A cross-sectional JAMA study published online 16 November reports a high-sensitivity immunoassay for endogenously cleaved microtubule-binding-region tau, using 35 μL of plasma per sample. The analysis included 728 participants: 521 brain donors from two autopsy cohorts and 207 participants from two tau-PET cohorts. In the autopsy cohorts, adjusted geometric-mean eMTBR-tau concentrations were 25.2-fold and 50.5-fold higher at Braak stage VI than at stages I–II.

In one imaging cohort, levels were 11.0-fold higher at tau-PET stages III–IV and 59.9-fold higher at stages V–VI than at stage 0. Immunoassay measurements correlated strongly with mass-spectrometry eMTBR-tau243 measurements (Spearman ρ = 0.93 across 108 participants), and both approaches discriminated regional tau-PET positivity with AUCs of 0.96–0.99 in the comparison sample.

This provides proof of principle for a more scalable blood measure of advanced neurofibrillary-tangle burden. It is not a prospective clinical diagnostic validation: the cohorts were cross-sectional, many low-stage measurements fell below the quantification limit, autopsy and imaging cohorts differed, and performance across laboratories and representative clinical populations remains unestablished. Relevant assay patents and industry relationships are disclosed in the article.

A Neandertal-derived variant is associated with occipital volume and schizophrenia risk

Hugo Zeberg and Svante Pääbo report in a peer-reviewed PNAS article published online 16 November that a common Neandertal-derived variant near DAAM1 is associated with altered DAAM1 expression and an approximately 0.85 cm³ smaller occipital-lobe volume per copy in present-day humans. Homozygous carriers had an almost 10% lower relative risk of schizophrenia, while associations with general cognitive ability and educational attainment were small or absent.

The archaic origin is supported by the haplotype's similarity to Neandertal sequence and its population distribution. The anatomical and disease results are association analyses: they do not show that reduced occipital volume protects against schizophrenia, that DAAM1 alone mediates the association, or that the variant conveys a general cognitive advantage. Independent expert commentary also cautions against interpreting a small present-day imaging association as a reconstruction of Neandertal cognition. Evidence access: the indexed abstract and independent expert commentary were reviewed; the complete methods and supplementary analyses were not accessible at the cutoff.

Distinct mouse prefrontal circuits separate sensory and affective pain responses

A peer-reviewed, open-access Nature Neuroscience study published 16 November combines longitudinal two-photon imaging, electrophysiology, viral tracing, fibre photometry, optogenetics and chemogenetics in a mouse spared-nerve-injury model. Peripheral nerve injury sensitized several classes of inhibitory neurons in the prelimbic cortex and shifted their responses from predominantly single-modality to polymodal.

Circuit perturbations separated two components of neuropathic pain. PV⁺ inhibitory neurons gating Rbp4⁺ excitatory output were linked primarily to sensory hypersensitivity, whereas Prox1⁺ inhibitory neurons interacting with Cux2⁺ and Ntsr1⁺ excitatory populations influenced avoidance and affective behaviour through different projections. Several intervention groups contained only 6–9 mice, although the study used converging anatomical, physiological and behavioural assays. The work maps causal circuitry in this mouse model; it does not establish an equivalent circuit organization in humans or demonstrate a treatment for chronic pain.

An aromatic cyclopentadienide ring is embedded in a bowl-shaped nanocarbon

A peer-reviewed Nature Chemistry paper published 16 November reports pentabenzocorannulenide, a curved conjugated carbon framework containing an anionic aromatic cyclopentadienide ring. The five-membered ring forms quantitatively through aromatization-driven nucleophilic dealkylation of a pentabenzocorannulene precursor. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction and quantum-chemical calculations show a bonding and electronic structure distinct from corannulene, fullerenes and carbon-nanotube end caps.

The anion undergoes reversible covalent functionalization and η⁵ metal coordination, demonstrating that an aromatic cyclopentadienide motif can retain useful reactivity when incorporated into a non-planar sp²-carbon network. This is a fundamental synthesis and structure result. Possible applications in molecular electronics, catalysis or functional materials were not demonstrated. Evidence access: the publisher abstract and indexed structural conclusions were reviewed; complete experimental yields, spectra and computational sensitivity analyses were not accessible.
