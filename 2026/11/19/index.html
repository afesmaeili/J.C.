01A lepto-hadronic perspective on the properties of the candidate neutrino-emitter blazar 5BZQ J0808+4950Margot Boughelilba, Leonard Pfeiffer, Jose Maria Sanchez Zaballa, Alessandra Azzollini, Annette Bremer, Sara Buson, Massimiliano Lincetto

A lepto-hadronic perspective on the properties of the candidate neutrino-emitter blazar 5BZQ J0808+4950

Authors: Margot Boughelilba, Leonard Pfeiffer, Jose Maria Sanchez Zaballa, Alessandra Azzollini, Annette Bremer, Sara Buson and Massimiliano Lincetto. Paper: arXiv:2611.24418v1 · full text. Dates/status: submitted to arXiv 18 November 2026; first announced 19 November; submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics, under review. The observations cover 2008–2024; IC110929A was detected on 29 September 2011.

Question and method. Can the broadband emission of this flat-spectrum radio quasar at redshift 1.4344 accommodate a physically consistent neutrino component, and which photon band follows it? The authors combine 16.4 years of Fermi-LAT data with optical/UV, radio, Swift-XRT and NuSTAR observations into six epochs. AM3 evolves electrons, protons and their secondaries in a non-expanding spherical emission region moving along the jet, including synchrotron radiation, Compton scattering, photomeson and Bethe–Heitler interactions, photon–photon pair production and escape. Accretion-disk, broad-line-region and torus photons evolve in importance as the region moves. The calculation averages the evolving emission over each observational interval; it is not an instantaneous flare fit.

Results. Representative solutions have comoving radii of (1–3) × 10¹⁶ cm, magnetic fields of about 0.5–5 G, Doppler factors around 5, and maximum injected proton energies of approximately 4–9 PeV in the blob frame. The proton injection index is tied to the electron index, approximately 2, and the minimum proton Lorentz factor is fixed to 1. The inferred injected jet powers span about 2–20 times the adopted Eddington luminosity, 8.75 × 10⁴⁶ erg s⁻¹. This is an energetic tension, not a violation of a strict Eddington bound on jets.

Folding the predicted muon-neutrino plus antineutrino flux through the public IceCube point-source effective area yields about 0.001–0.101 events per epoch and 0.17 events over all 16.4 years. For the 4.9-year interval containing IC110929A, the mean is 0.101, corresponding to a Poisson probability of approximately 9.6% for at least one event. These are model expectations, not measured source counts or an association significance. In particular, the blazar lies just outside the event's quoted 90% localization contour. A broad Poisson interval containing one event does not establish that this blazar produced it.

Across the six fitted solutions, the predicted neutrino luminosity follows the modeled 0.3–10 keV luminosity more closely than the modeled 0.1–100 GeV luminosity: fitted slopes are 1.12 ± 0.19 and 0.22 ± 0.79, respectively. This is a correlation among model outputs, not an observed neutrino–X-ray correlation. Three epochs lack X-ray coverage and have fewer fitted data points than free parameters. The authors explicitly caution that their independently varied parameter ranges are not joint statistical confidence regions; the figure's “1σ” shading should not be read as a marginalized uncertainty. Other solutions remain possible, and two epochs allow zero proton injection. The apparently significant ∼129 GeV photon is assigned only ∼1% source probability by the LAT analysis and is not evidence for a hard source component.

History

Keivani et al. (2018) already demonstrated, for TXS 0506+056, that photohadronic cascades fill the X-ray/MeV spectral valley and therefore restrict the neutrino output. Their multiwavelength cascade calculation showed why raising the proton luminosity cannot independently raise neutrinos without risking excess photons. The present study extends that physical test to several activity states of a different quasar; it does not newly establish the general cascade constraint.

Rodrigues et al. (2024) modeled 324 gamma-ray blazars with external photon fields. Their population-level neutrino–gamma correlation used broadband data without a consistent temporal selection, and their fixed hard proton index differed from the approximately 2 used here. Consequently, a weaker within-source correlation across six epochs neither contradicts the population trend nor supplies a directly comparable improvement in neutrino sensitivity. The earlier paper also warned that acceptable leptohadronic fits need not be unique.

A relevant earlier study absent from this paper's bibliography is Pfeiffer et al. (September 2026), which modeled six other candidate counterparts while preferentially maximizing neutrino output and constraining jet power. It found external photons important even for weakly accreting systems and predicted small event rates. The different source selection and power assumptions preclude a direct rate comparison. Together, these studies support testing source physics with joint photon and neutrino predictions while retaining the distinction between a viable emission model and a statistically established counterpart.

02Isotropic diffuse gamma-ray flux limits from Carpet-3 experiment data using neural networks with domain adaptationD. D. Dzhappuev, I. M. Dzaparova, T. A. Dzhatdoev, E. A. Gorbacheva, I. S. Karpikov, M. M. Khadzhiev, A. U. Kudzhaev, A. N. Kurenya, A. S. Lidvansky, O. I. Mikhailova, V. B. Petkov, N. A. Pozdnukhov, V. S. Romanenko, G. I. Rubtsov, S. V. Troitsky, I. B. Unatlokov, N. A. Vasiliev, A. F. Yanin, K. V. Zhuravleva

Isotropic diffuse gamma-ray flux limits from Carpet-3 experiment data using neural networks with domain adaptation

Authors: D. D. Dzhappuev, I. M. Dzaparova, T. A. Dzhatdoev, E. A. Gorbacheva, I. S. Karpikov, M. M. Khadzhiev, A. U. Kudzhaev, A. N. Kurenya, A. S. Lidvansky, O. I. Mikhailova, V. B. Petkov, N. A. Pozdnukhov, V. S. Romanenko, G. I. Rubtsov, S. V. Troitsky, I. B. Unatlokov, N. A. Vasiliev, A. F. Yanin and K. V. Zhuravleva. Paper: arXiv:2611.23389v1 · full text. Dates/status: submitted 17 November 2026 at 19:00 UTC; first announced 19 November; preprint, with institutional report number INR-TH-2026-020 rather than a journal publication.

Question and method. How tightly can an isotropic photon component above 100 TeV be constrained when simulated hadronic showers do not perfectly reproduce the data? The analysis uses 1,078 live days between 17 June 2022 and 7 April 2026, with 161,076 quality-selected showers and the enlarged 410 m² muon detector. A neural network combines surface-array images with reconstructed shower quantities. A domain-adaptation branch uses unlabeled measured showers to reduce simulation–data discrepancies in the learned features. Training uses CORSIKA/QGSJET/FLUKA simulations of photons and protons; the domain-alignment procedure is not a measurement of the cosmic-ray mass composition.

Measured constraints. The reported limits use the Feldman–Cousins construction at 90% confidence, with simulated background and photon acceptance, assuming an isotropic E⁻² photon spectrum. Integral intensity bounds are 3.2 × 10⁻¹³ cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ above 100 TeV, 3.54 × 10⁻¹⁴ above 1 PeV and 2.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ above 3.16 PeV. For the latter two thresholds, no candidates survive the integral-analysis cuts, against expected backgrounds of 1.3 and 0.9 events. Differential quarter-decade bins, also assuming E⁻² within each bin, yield E²dN/dE < 134 and 107 eV cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ at logarithmic bin centers 1.33 and 2.37 PeV, respectively. Integral and differential selections differ and their candidate counts should not be interchanged.

Limitations and interpretation. At the 100 TeV integral threshold, the total photon efficiency is only 0.004; the spectrum assumed above threshold materially affects that result. Domain adaptation reduces a classifier-distribution discrepancy but does not eliminate detector, composition or hadronic-interaction uncertainties, nor independently calibrate the photon response. Several differential bins below 1 PeV contain more photon candidates than the modeled background. The paper explicitly leaves their physical interpretation to a dedicated analysis: these excesses are not an established diffuse-photon detection. The measurement supplies a photon-intensity constraint; it does not itself fit a neutrino-source population or derive a dark-matter lifetime or Lorentz-invariance bound.

History

The Carpet-2 analysis (2022) used 1999–2011 and 2018–2022 data and a statistical decomposition of the muon-number distribution, designed to avoid reliance on a simulated hadronic tail. It reported an E⁻², 90%-confidence integral bound EₘᵢₙI(>Eₘᵢₙ) < 160 eV cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ at 1 PeV, equivalent to I < 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹. The corresponding Carpet-3 value is smaller by approximately 4.5, comparing the same threshold, spectrum and confidence level. This is a comparison of reported bounds, not a matched analysis isolating the gain from domain adaptation; detector area, exposure and statistical treatment all changed.

HAWC's diffuse-isotropic search provides a complementary lower-energy baseline, using 535 days and a likelihood analysis with a baseline E⁻²·⁵ spectrum and one-sided 95% limits. Its detector-response systematics and energy bins differ. Those results cannot be divided directly by Carpet-3's E⁻², 90% limits to obtain a meaningful sensitivity-improvement factor. The new result's clearest advance is relative to Carpet-2 in the PeV regime. Photon attenuation and the distinction between isotropic and Galactic-plane emission remain essential when using any of these bounds in multimessenger source models.

03Strongly Suppressed Cosmic-Ray Diffusion in a Wind-Blown Cavity Environment of SNR G21.8-3.0Yuan Li, Gwenael Giacinti, Yang Su, Xiao Zhang, Hui Li, Yang Chen

Strongly Suppressed Cosmic-Ray Diffusion in a Wind-Blown Cavity Environment of SNR G21.8-3.0

Authors: Yuan Li, Gwenael Giacinti, Yang Su, Xiao Zhang, Hui Li and Yang Chen. Paper: arXiv:2611.23744v1 · full text. Dates/status: submitted 18 November 2026; first announced 19 November; submitted manuscript, no journal publication recorded. Fermi-LAT observations span 4 August 2008–4 August 2024.

Question and data. Does a progenitor's low-density wind cavity let escaping cosmic rays travel freely, or can its magnetic environment inhibit transport? The paper associates the existing catalog source 4FGL J1840.4−1139 with molecular material outside the radio shell of G21.8−3.0, using 16 years of 0.1–1,000 GeV LAT data and MWISP CO observations. Thus, the new claim is the physical association and interpretation, rather than discovery of a previously uncataloged gamma-ray source. The source remains consistent with a point source: extension test statistics are only about 2–3. A log-parabola improves the spectral fit over a power law by TS = 46.7; that statistic measures curvature, not source-detection significance. The fitted photon flux is (1.35 ± 0.27) × 10⁻⁹ cm⁻² s⁻¹.

Transport model and results. A spherical, expanding shock continuously releases protons with an assumed E⁻² injection spectrum. Their accumulated diffusive distribution illuminates a nearby cloud through proton–proton interactions. Adopting a distance of 0.8 kpc, an SNR age of about 3 kyr and a cloud mass of 101 M☉ with mean density 253 cm⁻³, the authors explore D(E) = χ × 10²⁸(E/10 GeV)^δ cm² s⁻¹. The illustrated δ = 1 solution has χ = 0.1, or D(10 GeV) = 10²⁷ cm² s⁻¹, roughly 30 times below the comparison Galactic value 3 × 10²⁸ cm² s⁻¹. A δ = 0.5 solution with χ = 0.3 also describes the data, corresponding to a factor of 10 at that reference energy. Different energy dependences mean that “30-fold suppression” is not a universal factor at all proton energies.

The inferred energy in cloud-interacting protons is approximately 3 × 10⁴⁶ erg. Its smallness relative to an assumed 10⁵⁰ erg cosmic-ray budget is consistent with only a small intercepted population, but does not by itself measure the fraction confined in the remnant. The ∼0.9 Myr timescale estimated for the progenitor's wind bubble is distinct from the ∼3 kyr SNR age.

Limitations. The transport parameters come from illustrative model grids, not a marginalized posterior or a stated confidence interval on D. The gas distance, cloud geometry, SNR age and association are assumptions. In particular, using the unresolved gamma-ray source's localization scale to characterize the target cloud does not turn it into a measured physical emission radius. Possible CO broadening and the cavity alignment support an association without uniquely establishing it. Neither a spiral wind field nor self-generated turbulence is directly measured here. The quoted low variability does not exclude every alternative source class.

History

Li and Chen (2010) constructed the accumulated-escape model for W28 by integrating diffusion from the expanding shock over its history; their 2012 extension explicitly incorporated finite molecular-cloud volumes. The established lesson was that cloud distance, source age and diffusion can create spectral breaks without inserting an arbitrary break into the accelerator's proton spectrum. G21.8−3.0 applies that framework to a candidate wind-cavity environment. Its significance is the environmental example, not a new escape formalism or the first inference of locally inhibited transport.

An independently located earlier comparison, absent from the new bibliography, is Giacinti, Kachelrieß and Semikoz (2012). Their individual-particle calculations showed strongly anisotropic near-source propagation even in turbulence without a globally ordered field, before particles sample enough coherence volumes to approach ensemble-averaged diffusion. That does not numerically refute the G21.8 model: the energy and field setups differ. It does show why a scalar diffusion coefficient inferred under spherical symmetry need not uniquely identify microscopic scattering suppression. Distinguishing confinement from field geometry requires spatial and spectral information beyond the present unresolved gamma-ray source.

04Positron escape from dark matter spikes in black hole X-ray binariesMpho Kgoadi, Geoff Beck

Positron escape from dark matter spikes in black hole X-ray binaries

Authors: Mpho Kgoadi and Geoff Beck. Paper: arXiv:2611.23983v1 · full text. Dates/status: submitted 18 November 2026; first announced 19 November; preprint, no journal publication recorded.

Question and method. Can positrons produced in steep dark-matter concentrations around A0620−00 and XTE J1118+480 escape the near-black-hole environment with enough energy to explain the AMS-02 excess? The authors re-examine an 8 TeV particle annihilating into W⁺W⁻, separating spike emission from an extended ambient-halo contribution. They then propagate positrons through a radially declining magnetic field, comparing Bohm-like and Kolmogorov transport with the ballistic limit. The diffusion coefficient is capped at cr/3 so that the escape prescription does not imply faster-than-light transport. Synchrotron cooling, inverse-Compton losses and annihilation-induced depletion of the spike are treated separately.

Results. In the reproduced earlier calculation, approximately 97.9% of the peak positron flux comes from the extended ambient contribution and only 2.1% from the spikes. Removing the ambient term changes the required normalization from ⟨σv⟩ = 8.2 × 10⁻²² to 3.06 × 10⁻²⁰ cm³ s⁻¹ before near-source cooling. For the adopted fields, B(10R_g) = 4.2 × 10⁵ G and 2.3 × 10⁵ G, cooling suppresses the spike-only positron peak by factors of about 473–1,374 across the tested transport prescriptions. A proportional cross-section rescaling cannot be taken arbitrarily far: increasing annihilation also erodes the very density enhancement producing the signal. These are conditional model comparisons, not confidence-level exclusions of all dark-matter explanations of AMS-02.

Radio constraints. A separate synchrotron calculation uses XTE J1118+480's 5.3 GHz flux, 4.79 ± 1.45 μJy. With the adopted spike, magnetic field, age and diffusion prescription fixed, the authors define a one-sided 95% threshold through Δχ² = 2.71, allowing ordinary source emission to supply any remaining flux. For annihilation into b b̄, the first excluded cross sections are 3.29 × 10⁻²⁸ cm³ s⁻¹ at 100 GeV and 9.75 × 10⁻²⁷ cm³ s⁻¹ at 500 GeV. The spike slopes, field normalization, density profile and ages are not jointly marginalized. The inferred radio signal is non-monotonic in cross section once annihilation depletes the spike; these are lower boundaries of excluded intervals, not unconditional upper bounds excluding every larger cross section. The low-cross-section branch changes around 750 GeV for b b̄. The quoted subthermal reach is therefore conditional on both particle mass and the astrophysical model.

Limitations. The spike profiles are assumed, not independently detected. Their adopted slopes are 1.71 and 1.85, with radii near 1.1 pc; saturation uses assumed ages of 47 and 7.73 Myr. Magnetic normalization, inner transport and the persistence of the spike are major physical uncertainties. The radio calculation's local escape treatment also omits radiation emitted after particles leave their shell. The strongest conclusion is that a uniform, weak magnetic environment cannot safely be extrapolated down to the spike's emitting region.

History

Chan and Lee (2025) proposed these binaries as a dark-matter origin for the positron excess using a spatially uniform transport environment. The present paper changes two important pieces of that baseline: attributing the signal to the compact spikes rather than the extended ambient integral, and following energy losses before interstellar propagation. It is a critique of that specific normalization and escape scenario, not evidence that the AMS-02 excess itself is spurious.

Kar et al. (2024) already obtained stringent radio constraints from XTE J1118+480. Their paper explicitly identified magnetic equipartition as a major assumption and allowed diffusion and spike-slope variations. The new bounds are not generically stronger: different spike radii and accretion-rate-dependent fields explain much of the apparent discrepancy. Once those inputs are aligned, the new authors report agreement at approximately the 15% level at 100 GeV and 4% at 500 GeV. This is a model-reproduction comparison; the earlier comparison-to-flux procedure should not be silently assigned the new paper's Δχ² confidence construction.

A separate, relevant critique absent from the new bibliography is Scarcella and Kavanagh, v2 (March 2026). Their N-body calculations showed that the companion star dynamically depletes shallow spikes, requiring substantially steeper initial profiles, roughly γ ≳ 2.15–2.2 for these two systems, to sustain the proposed orbital-decay explanation over the simulated intervals. Long-term equilibrium was not established. This challenges the physical persistence of the adopted 1.71/1.85 profiles independently of positron cooling and reinforces why the radio numbers cannot be promoted to astrophysics-independent particle limits.

Astrophysical Alerts

M87: LHAASO-WCDA measures a strong multi-day TeV flare

LHAASO reported on 19 November 2026 at 04:02 UTC that its WCDA blind-search monitor flagged very-high-energy emission from M87 on 16 November with TS = 119 in a two-day integration. A dedicated daily analysis finds enhanced emission from 15–17 November, peaking on 16 November at TS = 92.7 (about 9.6σ); the three-day stack gives TS = 122 (about 11σ) at a position consistent with M87. The preliminary 0.5–20 TeV intrinsic spectrum is a power law with photon index 1.68 ± 0.29, with statistical uncertainty only. The integral flux above 1 TeV is about 0.5 Crab, roughly seven times the 1 October–14 November baseline, and the signal remains significant to about 15 TeV. LHAASO alert.

This is a high-significance flare from a known variable TeV radio galaxy, not a new source association. The spectrum and flux are preliminary, the quoted index excludes systematic uncertainty, and the telegram reports no significant cutoff rather than proving that none exists. Contemporaneous imaging and multiwavelength data are needed to locate the emitting zone within M87's unresolved TeV source region.

3C 138: MAGIC reports the first VHE detection of a compact steep-spectrum quasar

MAGIC reported on 18 November at 18:02 UTC that observations on the nights of 16 and 17 November detected the compact steep-spectrum quasar 3C 138 at about 9σ after 3.4 h. The average flux above 100 GeV was about 8% of the Crab Nebula flux. A hint on 16 November prompted a target-of-opportunity observation on 17 November, which established the reported signal. The source position is RA 80.292°, Dec +16.639° (J2000) and its redshift is z = 0.759. MAGIC alert.

The collaboration describes this as the first VHE detection of 3C 138 and of the compact steep-spectrum quasar class. That is a preliminary rapid-report claim: no spectrum, energy-dependent significance, trials accounting or systematic uncertainty is supplied, and the LST-1 data were still awaiting a combined analysis. The result establishes an observational target for follow-up; it does not by itself identify the emitting zone or show that compact steep-spectrum quasars form a general VHE population.

EP261115a: FXT narrows the position and measures the fading X-ray spectrum

Einstein Probe issued a refined FXT analysis on 18 November at 11:01 UTC for the transient first detected by WXT at 2026-11-15 00:22:13 UTC. FXT began its autonomous follow-up at 00:25:52 UTC, 295 s after the trigger, and observed for 3477 s. It found an uncatalogued source at RA 58.6230°, Dec −44.2932° (J2000) with a 10-arcsec radius uncertainty at 90% confidence, inside the WXT region. Refined FXT analysis; event thread.

An absorbed power law gives Galactic hydrogen column density 1.20⁺¹·¹⁴₋₁·₀₃ × 10²¹ cm⁻², photon index 2.01⁺⁰·³⁹₋₀·³⁵, and average unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux 5.73⁺⁰·⁷⁶₋₀·⁵⁷ × 10⁻¹² erg s⁻¹ cm⁻²; all intervals are 90%. This substantially improves the position and adds an X-ray spectrum, but the telegram does not provide a secure optical counterpart, distance or physical classification. The refined result therefore characterizes a fading X-ray transient without establishing its nature.

GRB 261118A: Fermi/GBM identifies a likely short burst with a broad localization

Fermi/GBM triggered at 2026-11-18 13:17:49 UTC and issued its final real-time localization at 13:28 UTC. The team classifies GRB 261118A as a likely short GRB and reports RA 172.9°, Dec +51.0° (J2000) with a 7.7° statistical uncertainty. The event was 165° from the LAT boresight. GBM circular; public trigger record.

The circular supplies no duration, spectrum, fluence, false-alarm rate or independent counterpart. “Likely short” is therefore the GBM team's prompt classification, not a secure progenitor identification. The broad statistical region also requires the HEALPix map, including the estimated localization systematic, for quantitative follow-up.

GRB 261118B: GBM upgrades the earlier candidate to a likely long burst

The Fermi trigger at 2026-11-18 01:50:36.86 UTC, previously reported only as a broad candidate, received a final localization and likely long-GRB classification on 18 November at 17:23 UTC. GBM places GRB 261118B at RA 272.2°, Dec +60.7° (J2000) with a 14.7° statistical uncertainty; it was 14° from the LAT boresight. Final GBM localization; public trigger record.

The new circular resolves the prompt pipeline's earlier ambiguity between a GRB and Cyg X-1, but it gives no duration, spectrum, fluence, LAT counterpart or false-alarm probability. The long-burst label remains a preliminary instrument classification, and the very broad localization prevents a unique source association.

GRB 261113B: Fermi/GBM adds prompt spectral parameters and fluence

Fermi/GBM reported on 18 November at 20:47 UTC its analysis of the burst at 2026-11-13 19:45:55.10 UTC, already detected by Insight-HXMT/HE, GECAM-B and SVOM/GRM. GBM measures a single episode with T90 ≈ 17.7 s in 50–300 keV. From T0−5.9 to T0+5.4 s, a cutoff power law gives index −0.54 ± 0.05 and Epeak = 212 ± 7 keV. The 10–1000 keV fluence over that fit interval is (6.0 ± 0.1) × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻², and the 1-s peak photon flux from T0+0.6 s is 8.4 ± 0.8 ph s⁻¹ cm⁻². Fermi/GBM analysis; event thread.

The on-ground GBM region is consistent with the possible SVOM/GWAC optical counterpart, but positional consistency is not proof of association. The spectrum is preliminary, and its integration interval is shorter than the quoted T90. These data strengthen the prompt GRB characterization and complement the earlier off-axis SVOM/GRM measurement; final catalog calibration may change the parameters.

V488 Sge: Chandra finds a line-dominated, variable nova spectrum; a cited-reference error was corrected

Chandra observed the active nova V488 Sge for about 14 ks on 10 November and 13 ks on 11 November; the analysis was reported on 18 November at 16:08 UTC. The first-order 0.2–10 keV count rate was 1.058 ± 0.0127 counts s⁻¹ on 10 November and 0.920 ± 0.0115 counts s⁻¹ on 11 November. The first exposure included a 2500-s flare, while the second also showed strong irregular variability. The spectrum is dominated by thermal-plasma emission lines, including H-like and He-like oxygen and nitrogen, with several radiative-recombination continua and little continuum flux. Chandra report.

The authors state that the data rule out the previously hypothesized 40 eV blackbody-like atmospheric component. A correction issued on 19 November at 01:38 UTC clarifies that this hypothesis came from the earlier XMM-Newton report, ATel 18135, rather than ATel 18138 as first written. Correction; XMM-Newton report. The telegram provides count rates and qualitative spectral identifications, but no plasma-temperature fit, line fluxes or confidence interval for the rejected continuum component; its physical interpretation remains preliminary.

Science News

Sentinel-3C returns its first thermal-infrared Earth images

ESA reported on 18 November 2026 that the Sea and Land Surface Temperature Radiometer aboard Copernicus Sentinel-3C has produced its first thermal-infrared and shortwave-infrared images. The satellite launched on 15 September; the displayed scenes were acquired over southwestern Europe and North Africa on 2 November and Western Australia on 4 November. SLSTR has nine visible, shortwave-infrared and thermal-infrared bands, including two channels intended for detecting and monitoring wildfire intensity. ESA commissioning report.

The Western Australia scene resolves top-of-atmosphere brightness temperatures above 40 °C over parts of the land, around 20 °C over the eastern Indian Ocean, vegetation structure and recent burn scars. Brightness temperature is not identical to physical surface temperature, and the instrument was still being commissioned. These are engineering and calibration images demonstrating that the channels operate, not a new climate trend measurement; validated continuity with Sentinel-3A/3B records still requires full calibration.

Device-independent quantum position verification beats equal-latency classical localization

Gautam Kavuri and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed Nature article published 18 November a remote position-verification experiment whose security depends on observed Bell correlations and relativistic timing rather than trusted quantum hardware. A loophole-free Bell test across a quantum network was used against adversaries that could be weakly entangled before each trial. The demonstrated one-dimensional localization interval was 2.47(2) times smaller than the best necessarily non-remote classical protocol; against classical protocols with identical latency, the localization volume was 4.53(5) times smaller. Research article.

The experiment establishes a certifiable quantum advantage for its threat model and network geometry. It is not yet a field-ready geolocation service: the demonstrated protocol treats a restricted experimental adversary, while general security depends on stated assumptions about entanglement resources, timing, loss and station separation. Deployment would also need authenticated infrastructure and robust long-distance quantum links.

A two-dimensional Bose gas simulates a massive relativistic quantum field

Yansheng Zhang and colleagues report in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature article published 18 November an analogue simulation of a massive relativistic field in 2+1 dimensions. Two coherently coupled spin components in a uniform two-dimensional atomic Bose–Einstein condensate encode the long-wavelength sine-Gordon field in their relative phase. In the perturbative regime the excitations show relativistic dispersion with a tunable mass gap; outside that regime the experiment directly observes topological domain walls across which the phase winds by 2π. Research article.

The platform makes real-time, non-perturbative dynamics experimentally accessible in more than one spatial dimension and could emulate ingredients of preheating, defect dynamics and false-vacuum decay. It is an analogue quantum simulator, however: the condensate implements the sine-Gordon universality class over a finite momentum and parameter range, not the Standard Model or an actual cosmological spacetime. Quantitative conclusions about cosmology require a controlled mapping of scales, initial states and observables.

Cluster-based sampling outperforms active learning across a large materials benchmark

Quinn Gallagher, Sonia Hasko and Michael Webb compared 100 space-filling and active-learning algorithms across 60 chemical and materials property-prediction tasks in a peer-reviewed Nature Computational Science analysis published 18 November. When training examples had to be chosen from a fixed candidate library, cluster-based selection consistently performed best across representation dimensionality, fixed and learned representations, and training-set size. A Gaussian-process-variance argument identifies cluster-based selection as optimal in the low-data limit. Research article.

This challenges the common assumption that uncertainty-driven active learning is automatically the most data-efficient acquisition strategy. The benchmark concerns selection from an already defined library; adaptive experiments that can generate candidates outside that library may behave differently. Performance also remains dependent on dataset coverage, property smoothness and representation quality. Evidence access: the abstract, figure descriptions and data/code statement were reviewed; the subscription-only full methods and supplementary comparisons were inaccessible at the cutoff.

A human hypothalamus atlas maps selective vulnerability in ageing and Alzheimer’s disease

Dan Yu and colleagues present HypoAD in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature article published 18 November. The atlas combines high-resolution MRI from 458 people with single-nucleus RNA sequencing of 614,403 nuclei from young adults, people with Alzheimer’s disease and age-matched non-dementia controls. Subregions governing metabolism, stress and circadian function showed particularly strong age- and Alzheimer-associated differences in volume and gene expression. Research article.

The work links regional anatomy to cell-type-resolved transcription and supplies public sequencing and analysis resources. Cross-sectional tissue and imaging comparisons cannot determine whether the hypothalamic changes cause neurodegeneration, result from it or reflect comorbid ageing processes. Post-mortem sampling, cohort composition and the correspondence between donor tissue and living MRI cohorts also limit direct clinical inference. Evidence access: the full abstract, data statement and indexed article metadata were reviewed; detailed supplementary analyses were not accessible.

Amazon leaf shedding is widespread and often decoupled from canopy greenness

Guangqin Song and colleagues combined 10-m Sentinel-2 observations with machine learning in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature article published 18 November. Across 70.0% of the Amazon, inferred leaf shedding precedes leaf flushing by at least three months, contradicting the usual representation of the forest as uniformly evergreen. Hydroclimate was the dominant correlate of this asynchrony across 51.6% of the basin, followed by soil properties (33.2%) and light availability (15.2%). Research article.

Adding deciduousness-constrained turnover and leaf-age structure improved seasonal agreement between modelled gross primary productivity and satellite-derived photosynthetic seasonality across 79.9% of the basin. These percentages describe spatial model attribution and agreement, not controlled causal effects. Cloud contamination, optical signals from multilayer canopies and sparse ground validation remain limitations; the result argues for revising carbon-cycle phenology models rather than supplying a direct new estimate of total Amazon carbon uptake.

Late Homo erectus occupied southern Chinese rainforest environments

Kira Westaway and colleagues report in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature article published 18 November nine hominin teeth from the Naxian and Baxian caves in Guangxi, eight identified morphologically and genetically as Homo erectus. The team integrated 43 radiometric ages from five techniques with two Bayesian age models, placing the occupation between 324 and 184 thousand years ago. Faunal evidence, stable and clumped isotopes, trace elements and strontium mapping indicate closed-canopy rainforest conditions and possible movement beyond the local karst. Research article.

The result supplies the youngest securely dated H. erectus occurrence yet reported from China and weakens the view that the species was restricted to open woodland or grassland mosaics. The behavioural inference is more tentative than the dating: mobility estimates rely on limited strontium measurements, cave assemblages can mix material, and the environmental proxies do not reveal subsistence technology or continuous occupation. The authors’ possible range of roughly 250 km should therefore be read as a hypothesis from provenance modelling, not a directly tracked migration.

Kuiper-belt dust preserves evidence for an isotopically heavy primordial water reservoir

Catch-up — newly highlighted by NASA on 18 November; peer-reviewed paper published online 11 November and in Science Advances on 13 November. Lindsay Keller and colleagues analysed 12 hydrated interplanetary dust particles, roughly 10–30 μm across, collected in Earth’s stratosphere. Very high solar-energetic-particle track densities support an Edgeworth–Kuiper-belt origin. Electron microscopy and ion-probe measurements show water-altered minerals whose oxygen isotopes require ice enriched in ¹⁷O and ¹⁸O relative to inner-solar-system reservoirs. Research article; NASA announcement.

The isotopic and mineralogical differences from CI chondrites and Bennu/Ryugu material support limited mixing between outer and inner formation regions, possibly because Saturn or the ice giants impeded inward drift. That barrier explanation is an inference from the samples and dynamical models. The particles’ provenance rests on exposure-track arguments, and twelve microscopic grains cannot measure the reservoir’s total mass or establish that all Kuiper-belt bodies shared the same water composition.

A plasma immunoassay measures a marker of advanced insoluble tau pathology

Catch-up — peer-reviewed JAMA article published online 16 November and indexed in the 18 November research update. Wagner Brum and colleagues tested a new plasma immunoassay for endogenously cleaved microtubule-binding-region tau in 728 participants: 521 brain donors from two autopsy cohorts and 207 participants from two tau-PET cohorts in Canada and Sweden. Plasma eMTBR-tau rose with advanced neuropathological and PET Braak stage, identified regional tau-PET positivity and correlated strongly with mass-spectrometry eMTBR-tau243. Research article.

The assay could make measurement of a candidate blood marker for neocortical insoluble tau burden more scalable than mass spectrometry. This is a cross-sectional proof of principle, not a validated screening or diagnostic test: cohort-specific thresholds, longitudinal prediction, performance against clinical mimics and the effect of kidney or other systemic conditions still require prospective study. It should not be conflated with plasma p-tau217, which tracks amyloid-associated soluble tau biology more closely.
