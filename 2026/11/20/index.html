01Direct simulation Monte Carlo for dark matter production and phase-space evolutionFelix Kahlhoefer, Ann Cathrin Wesener

Direct simulation Monte Carlo for dark matter production and phase-space evolution

Felix Kahlhoefer and Ann Cathrin Wesener · arXiv:2611.25812v1 · Full paper. Submitted 19 November 2026; first announced 20 November. Preprint, prepared for JHEP; no journal publication recorded in the checked arXiv record. Numerical transport method and illustrative dark-sector models.

The question is how to follow a dark-matter population whose distribution is neither thermal nor well represented by a single temperature, while production, redshifting and self-interactions act together. The authors solve the homogeneous, isotropic Boltzmann equation with an ensemble of particles and stochastic collisions, rather than a fixed momentum grid. The implementation covers annihilation/freeze-out, decays, elastic scattering and number-changing 2↔3 reactions. A no-time-counter algorithm samples candidate pairs or triples using upper bounds on interaction probabilities; multiparticle final states are sampled in phase space and boosted back while conserving four-momentum. Expansion redshifts momenta between interaction steps. This is an energy-distribution calculation with isotropic directions, not spatially resolved source transport.

The illustrative model has a thermal bath ψ, a heavy scalar φ that freezes out and decays into two dark-matter particles ρ, and subsequent ρ self-scattering. With mφ = 1 TeV, mρ = 10 keV and decay width Γφ = 10⁻¹⁷ GeV, early in-equilibrium and late out-of-equilibrium decays produce separate momentum populations, near p/T ≈ 2.5 and of order 100. Elastic scattering with constant squared matrix element 2 × 10⁻¹⁴, corresponding to a nonrelativistic cross section per mass of approximately 10⁻⁵ cm²/g, first equilibrates the low-momentum component; the late, energetic population takes longer. A single effective temperature can therefore hide substantial structure during the evolution.

Number-changing reactions alter both the distribution and the abundance. For constant squared 2↔3 matrix elements of 6 × 10⁻¹², 2 × 10⁻¹¹ and 4 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻², underpopulated sectors initially undergo particle multiplication and cooling, while inverse reactions become important nearer chemical equilibrium. In the displayed examples, doubling the matrix-element square from 2 × 10⁻¹¹ to 4 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻² changes the eventual yield by roughly an order of magnitude. This is sensitivity within a specified toy model, not an observational constraint or a universal scaling. Figures 11–12 show why: the high-momentum peak can remain unthermalized, partly thermalize, or disappear depending on interaction strength.

Validation includes freeze-out calculations with early kinetic decoupling, decay distributions and static-box equilibration. Ten independent runs at each of three ensemble sizes, ending with approximately 45,000, 180,000 and 405,000 particles, exhibit the expected approximate inverse-square-root convergence; the largest ensembles reach percent-scale abundance precision in this test. Branching and collisions generate correlations, so naïve independent-particle Poisson errors are insufficient.

Limits. The benchmarks fix relativistic degrees of freedom to g = 3.36 and gs = 3.91, use simple constant matrix elements and neglect quantum occupation factors. The elastic-only 10-keV example remains relativistic until bath temperatures below roughly 0.1 keV and is too warm to be presented as a viable structure-formation fit. No matter-power-spectrum likelihood, dark-matter detection or new confidence bound is supplied. Realistic amplitudes, stiff collision rates and particle splitting/merging remain implementation challenges; the paper does not establish a universal speed advantage over deterministic solvers.

History

Binder et al. (2017) already showed that early kinetic decoupling can invalidate the usual number-density-only freeze-out calculation. Their coupled abundance/temperature equations and direct phase-space solution produced order-of-magnitude departures in a resonant scalar-singlet example. Thus, the need for phase-space evolution is established. The 2021 freeze-in/freeze-out study explicitly followed distributions from annihilations and decays, including multimodal populations and their modification by elastic scattering; bimodality is also not new here.

Stochastic collision methods have an equally important prior history. The 2024 neutrino DSMC work adapted stochastic ensembles to an expanding early universe, including decays and quantum-statistical complications. Independently, Rocha et al. (2012), not cited in this manuscript, derived pairwise Monte Carlo self-scattering for cosmological N-body halos from the Boltzmann collision term. That spatial, gravitational application differs from primordial particle production and should not be conflated with it. The present advance is the tested combination of production, decay and elastic and number-changing dark-sector transport without imposing a thermal distribution, with an explicit stochastic treatment of three-particle initial states. It extends the computational toolkit; it does not overturn the preceding phase-space results.

02Aggregating soft-photon recoil with a positive finite-step samplerNina Elkina, Alexander Pukhov

Aggregating soft-photon recoil with a positive finite-step sampler

Nina Elkina and Alexander Pukhov · arXiv:2611.26402v1 · Full paper and supplements. Submitted 19 November 2026; first announced 20 November. Preprint; primary category physics.plasm-ph, encountered through the hep-th cross-list. Stochastic radiation-reaction method.

Resolving every soft synchrotron photon can be expensive, but replacing many emissions by Gaussian noise allows some electrons to gain energy from a radiation-loss step. The proposed δ-Gamma sampler preserves a finite probability of no emission and otherwise draws a positive aggregate recoil. For a frozen emission rate W and step h, the zero-loss probability is exp(−Wh). The nonzero Gamma distribution matches the unconditional mean loss M and variance V of the compound-Poisson emission process: if q = 1 − exp(−Wh), its conditional mean is M/q and its variance is V/q − (1−q)M²/q². Matching two moments alone would not enforce the sign of recoil; the support of the chosen distribution does.

The authors also address a separate finite-step bias: cooling changes the emitter and hence the rate and spectrum during the step. With drift A and jump generator L, the mean is corrected to hA + h²LA/2, with a corresponding variance correction. This improves aggregation relative to a frozen-state approximation; it is not an exact transition law or a proof of globally second-order integration. All comparisons use the same spin-averaged, ultrarelativistic, collinear locally constant crossed-field emission kernel, with the single-photon energy restricted by the electron's available kinetic energy.

In a uniform-field cooling test starting at Lorentz factor γ₀ = 2000 and quantum parameter χ₀ = 0.002, 50 aggregate steps initially encompass an average 21.6 photons per step. The final mean Lorentz factor is 996.76 ± 0.13 for frozen aggregation and 1003.95 ± 0.13 after generator correction, compared with 1003.732 from an independent small-χ expansion and 1003.667 ± 0.100 from state-updated explicit emission. The empirical cumulative-distribution discrepancy falls from 0.08755 to 0.00568 in this comparison. These are finite-ensemble benchmark diagnostics, not general error guarantees. A separate magnetic-orbit test at γ₀ = 1000 and χ₀ = 0.04 finds about 14.55% of a Langevin ensemble above its initial energy at an early checkpoint despite the absence of magnetic work; reducing the step does not remove this diffusion-support effect.

Limits and use in cascades. Positivity does not enforce the upper bound on available kinetic energy: the Gamma tail is unbounded. A strong focused-laser stress test is stopped after 93 excessive-loss draws among 10,496 trials, consistent with its predicted risk; straightforward step reduction does not eliminate that failure mode. The reported approximately 1.24-fold dynamics-only speedup belongs to a separate frozen-Gamma ion-channel benchmark, excludes compilation, table construction and spectral postprocessing, and is not a performance measurement of every corrected variant. The aggregate recoil does not specify individual emitted photons. The accompanying spectral estimator represents a conditional mean source and conserves energy in expectation, not event by event; treating each aggregate draw as one physical photon would therefore give an unjustified cascade injection prescription. Extensions to pion, muon or proton synchrotron transport are not demonstrated.

History

Neitz and Di Piazza (2013) derived stochastic broadening from a photon-emission kinetic equation and its small-χ drift–diffusion limit. The competition between deterministic cooling and stochastic energy spread is therefore established; this paper concerns a finite-step representation of that physics. Ridgers et al. (2017), independently retrieved and absent from the present reference list, separated quantum suppression of the mean radiated power from changes in the energy variance. Their analysis showed why the mean energy alone cannot diagnose all stochastic effects.

The present work adds an explicit no-emission atom, positive nonzero loss and a state-feedback correction, then tests both electron distributions and failure at the available-energy boundary. Its comparison with a state-updated emission reference is more informative than agreement between two frozen-state samplers. It does not make Fokker–Planck transport generally invalid: diffusion remains an approximation with a restricted regime and loses information about jump support. Nor does aggregation introduce new multiphoton QED dynamics; it approximates repeated emissions governed by the specified single-photon kernel.

03Intra-day variability of the gamma-ray/optical emission of 3C 454.3: the complexity beyond the long-term correlationGabriele Panebianco, Marco Tavani, Valerio Vittorini, Paolo Coppi, Andrea Bulgarelli, Claudia M. Raiteri, Massimo Villata

Intra-day variability of the gamma-ray/optical emission of 3C 454.3: the complexity beyond the long-term correlation

Gabriele Panebianco, Marco Tavani, Valerio Vittorini, Paolo Coppi, Andrea Bulgarelli, Claudia M. Raiteri and Massimo Villata · arXiv:2611.25125v1 · Full paper. Submitted 18 November 2026; first announced 20 November. Manuscript received by A&A on 7 October and accepted on 18 November; journal publication date not verified. Archival multiwavelength measurements and source interpretation.

Does the long-term gamma-ray/optical correlation of the flat-spectrum radio quasar 3C 454.3 describe individual flares? The study combines AGILE and Fermi-LAT gamma-ray observations with R-band WEBT and public optical photometry, examining approximately 17 years of activity at redshift z = 0.859. AGILE coverage runs from April 2007 to January 2024; Fermi data extend from August 2008 to the start of January 2025. Light curves use 0.1–10 GeV photons, while spectral fits extend to 30 GeV. Fourteen well-covered, gamma-ray-selected flaring epochs are compared with global samples using 24-, 12-, 6- and, for the brightest episode, 3-hour bins.

The global Spearman correlation is strong, ρS = 0.87 at 24 hours and 0.80 at 6 hours. Power-law relations between gamma-ray and optical flux have slopes around 1.6–1.7 and intrinsic scatter near 0.24–0.25 dex. Version-specific inconsistency: §5 assigns η = 1.67 ± 0.02 to 6-hour bins and 1.60 ± 0.02 to 24-hour bins, whereas Figure 2 reverses those assignments. Both fitted values are reported here without choosing an unverified bin assignment. This does not erase the broader contrast: individual epochs have ρS ≈ 0.20–0.81 and slopes spanning approximately 0.40 ± 0.20 to 2.00 ± 0.20.

The reported “Compton dominance” is specifically the proxy νFν at 1 GeV divided by νFν in R band, not an integrated luminosity ratio or the ratio of measured SED peaks. It is typically about 5–10 in quiet intervals, commonly 10–30 during elevated emission, and can reach approximately 40–120. In the November 2010 superflare, 3-hour resolution reveals subflares and excursions from roughly 100 to 60 and back to 100 that are smoothed in daily averages. Spectral evolution is similarly heterogeneous: the log-parabola index changes from 2.55 ± 0.10 to 1.85 ± 0.06 in the 2013 event despite relatively low dominance, whereas the much brighter 2010 event changes from 2.60 ± 0.06 to 2.20 ± 0.03. The data contain both optical and gamma-ray orphan or partly decoupled flares.

These results disfavor treating all epochs as the same one-zone configuration with only electron density varying. They do not statistically exclude all leptonic models: magnetic field, Doppler factor, external seed-photon density and multiple zones can change the relation. Orphan gamma-ray activity alone is not evidence for protons or neutrino production. For illustration, the standard causality relation R′ ≲ cδtobs/(1+z) gives R′ ≲ 1.74 × 10¹⁵ cm for a 3-hour observed variation and assumed δ = 10. This is a conditional size estimate, not a confidence limit or a black-hole mass measurement; converting jet variability into a black-hole mass requires additional geometry and timescale assumptions.

Limits. Gamma-ray selection and incomplete simultaneous optical sampling can suppress optical-only events. Bins with gamma-ray TS < 9 are represented by upper limits and excluded from correlation fits, introducing a flux-dependent selection. Thermal-disc light can dilute the faint optical jet signal. Proposed mirror/cloud interactions and geometrical explanations remain interpretations rather than unique parameter fits.

History

Coogan, Brown and Chadwick (2016) already measured an intrinsic gamma-ray doubling time of 0.68 ± 0.01 hours during the 2014 flare and discussed compact emission beyond the broad-line region, with localization depending on opacity and geometry assumptions. The present three-hour bins are therefore not a new shortest-variability record. Kushwaha et al. (2017) found strongly correlated 2014 multiwavelength variability, with the optical/infrared-to-gamma lag changing from near zero to about three days; evolving magnetic or external-radiation fields were already plausible explanations.

An independent Maidanak optical study published in September 2026, not cited here, also found state-dependent colours and intranight variability in long-baseline monitoring. Its optical-only diagnostics cannot establish gamma-ray coupling, but reinforce the need to separate long-term trends from local behaviour. The new contribution is the systematic, common-analysis comparison across many epochs at intraday resolution, exposing how a strong global correlation conceals distinct radiative states. It strengthens the case for variable or structured emitting regions without uniquely selecting one acceleration mechanism.

04Gamma-Ray Pulsar Discovery Using a Lattice-Based MethodDaniel Pook-Kolb, Dotan Gazith, Colin J. Clark, Erik Agrell, Barak Zackay, Bruce Allen

Gamma-Ray Pulsar Discovery Using a Lattice-Based Method

Daniel Pook-Kolb, Dotan Gazith, Colin J. Clark, Erik Agrell, Barak Zackay and Bruce Allen · arXiv:2611.26425v1 · Full paper · Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 211201. Submitted to arXiv 19 November 2026; first announced 20 November. Peer-reviewed journal article published 19 November, accepted 22 September. Pulsar discovery and demonstrated search method.

The authors discover the isolated millisecond pulsar PSR J1622−7204 in the previously unassociated source 4FGL J1622.2−7202. Sparse gamma rays make the missing integer rotation counts, rather than time-tag precision, the central obstacle: photons can be separated by millions of unobserved turns. The method converts a timing solution into a short-vector problem in a high-dimensional lattice. It analytically eliminates five continuous parameters—phase, frequency, frequency derivative and two sky offsets—leaving integer rotation counts for 80 photon arrival times. Lattice reduction and sieving generate many candidate vectors, which are checked with a weighted H statistic using the 1,000 highest-weight photons. The correct solution is not necessarily the shortest vector.

The search draws random subsets of 80 photons from the 120 with highest source-association probabilities, using Fermi Pass 8 SOURCE photons above 100 MeV. The full timing data span MJD 54694–60284, approximately 15 years. Gaussian search-prior widths include 300 Hz in frequency and 10⁻¹⁴ Hz/s in frequency derivative, with an assumed pulse width of 0.1 rotation and a sky region tied to the catalog localization. These are search regularizers, not measured parameter uncertainties. A trial takes about 90 seconds on an otherwise idle CPU core, rising to roughly five minutes under load. Empirically only about 1% of subsets recover this pulsar; about 300 trials are needed for 95% recovery probability under that measured per-trial success rate.

The successful discovery candidate has H = 231; the paper estimates a single-template tail probability near 10⁻⁴⁰ and compares it with an order-10²² effective search-space trials scale. This supports a highly significant detection, although the approximate trials argument is not an exhaustive end-to-end false-alarm calibration of every implementation choice. Subsequent likelihood timing uses 15,905 photons with weights above 0.01, corresponding to an effective source-photon sum near 828. It yields f = 260.89620807072(2) Hz and ḟ = −1.5845(4) × 10⁻¹⁵ Hz/s, or a period of 3.8329418713856(3) ms; parentheses denote the paper's quoted last-digit uncertainties. The pulse profile has two peaks despite the simpler Gaussian profile used to construct the search.

The paper reports approximately ten CPU hours for discovery versus roughly 600 CPU years spent by an unsuccessful earlier semicoherent search. This is a striking source-specific comparison of different searches, not a matched sensitivity-versus-cost benchmark proving that every source becomes half a million times cheaper. Background photons, pulse shape and subset luck strongly affect performance. Derived characteristic age ≈2.6 Gyr, surface field ≈3 × 10⁸ G and spin-down power ≈1.6 × 10³⁴ erg/s inherit conventional pulsar-model assumptions and distance-dependent kinematic corrections. They are not direct measurements of the emission efficiency.

Outlook versus result. The demonstrated discovery is isolated. Blind binary-pulsar searches are proposed to require roughly 10⁶ additional orbital linearizations and a higher detection threshold; their feasibility is a forecast, not a binary discovery in this paper. Search and timing notebooks are linked in the journal supplement; the scientific assessment here is based on the article, not an independent rerun of those notebooks.

History

Pletsch and Clark (2014), independently retrieved and not directly cited in the new article, optimized semicoherent gamma-ray searches at fixed computing cost. Their metric-based grids and staged coherent follow-up explain why the relevant baseline is an optimized search, not naïve enumeration of all phase histories. Gazith, Pearlman and Zackay's lattice study, first posted in 2024 and published in 2025, already transformed sparse timing into a lattice problem and recovered the known pulsar PSR J0318+0253. It also identified the sensitivity to background contamination and non-Gaussian pulse profiles, and advocated ranking many short vectors with a pulsation statistic.

The new paper advances that proposal by analytically removing the continuous timing parameters, employing repeated photon subsets, and delivering a previously unknown pulsar with a subsequently refined timing solution. The precedent prevents calling lattice recovery itself new, while the successful blind discovery is a substantive empirical step beyond recovering an already known system. The potential for finding hidden gamma-ray pulsar populations is credible, but its impact on Galactic diffuse emission or dark-matter interpretations requires a population-level selection function and further discoveries.

05Measurements of the total and differential inclusive electron neutrino charged-current cross sections on argon with SBNDSBND Collaboration (L. Aliaga-Soplin et al.)

Measurements of the total and differential inclusive electron neutrino charged-current cross sections on argon with SBND

SBND Collaboration (L. Aliaga-Soplin et al.; complete author list in the linked paper) · arXiv:2611.25098v1 · Full paper and supplemental material. Submitted 18 November 2026; first announced 20 November. Preprint, FERMILAB-PUB-26-0808-PPD; no journal publication recorded. Neutrino–argon cross-section measurement.

SBND measures inclusive electron-neutrino plus electron-antineutrino charged-current scattering in the Booster Neutrino Beam, using data collected from February to early April 2025. The exposure is 8.81 × 10¹⁹ protons on target. Its liquid-argon detector lies about 110 m from the target; the analysis selects 729 candidates with outgoing electron energy above 500 MeV. The predicted selected signal is 95.2% νe and 4.8% antineutrino, with mean incident energy 1.8 GeV. This is a flux average over a restricted final-state phase space, not the cross section evaluated at a monochromatic 1.8-GeV beam.

The measured total is [3.90 ± 0.18 (statistical) ± 0.41 (systematic)] × 10⁻³⁹ cm² per nucleon, for the stated electron-energy requirement. The analysis uses an average efficiency of 36.7%. A photon-enriched sideband constrains backgrounds, reducing the predicted background from about 166 to 132 events and increasing purity from 80.1% to 84.5%. The flux contribution remains a major uncertainty, about 8.4% for the total; detector and interaction-model terms are approximately 4.6% and 3.8%. These are uncertainties on a measured cross section, not confidence bounds on a new interaction.

Single-differential results are unfolded in five electron-energy bins and five angular-cosine bins using Wiener-SVD regularization. Model predictions must be multiplied by the supplied additional-smearing matrix Ac and compared with the full unfolded covariance, rather than simply overlaid as unsmeared truth or tested with diagonal errors. The publication supplies both matrices. In the total-rate comparison, NuWro, GENIE AR23, NEUT and GiBUU predict respectively 4.37, 4.49, 4.77 and 5.03 in units of 10⁻³⁹ cm²/nucleon, with reported p-values 0.301, 0.198, 0.055 and 0.012. The data prefer the lower-normalization predictions in this set.

GiBUU's electron-energy comparison gives χ² = 16.8 for five degrees of freedom (p = 0.005), and its angular comparison gives 11.8/5 (p = 0.038). The paper finds that much of the discrepancy is normalization driven; floating normalization in the angular test gives 8.2/4, p = 0.085. It would be misleading to turn these generator comparisons into a universal exclusion of a nuclear-transport approach. Flux, detector response, neutrino interaction modeling and the chosen signal phase space remain coupled ingredients.

Scope. These data directly support electron-flavour interaction modeling for oscillation experiments, but the analysis is not an oscillation-parameter fit or a sterile-neutrino detection. The 500-MeV electron threshold also means it is not a direct measurement of the entire low-energy electron-like excess region. The inferred cross section assumes the beam-flux model and negligible relevant short-baseline disappearance; it does not jointly marginalize over arbitrary new-physics modifications to that flux.

History

MicroBooNE's 2021 inclusive measurement established νe + antineutrino scattering on argon with the off-axis NuMI beam, reporting 6.84 ± 1.51 (stat.) ± 2.33 (syst.) × 10⁻³⁹ cm²/nucleon for incident neutrino energy above 250 MeV, with a thresholded flux mean of 905 MeV. Its larger central value cannot be compared directly with SBND's 3.90: beam spectra and the incident-energy versus outgoing-electron cuts differ. MicroBooNE's subsequent differential analysis used 243 candidates, Wiener-SVD unfolding and additional-smearing matrices; differential electron-flavour argon measurements and this comparison machinery therefore predate SBND.

An independently retrieved 2026 MicroBooNE analysis additionally measured electron-neutrino argon scattering without final-state pions, including lepton and proton observables. Its generator tensions depend on topology and, for GiBUU, in-medium hadronic corrections. That is a useful warning against transferring a p-value between inclusive and pionless samples. SBND's advance is the larger inclusive electron-flavour sample in the Booster beam and its total and differential constraints in a clearly specified phase space. No common-flux reweighting is performed here, so no numerical cross-experiment precision-improvement factor or apparent inter-experiment discrepancy is asserted.

Science News

CTAO-South will add two Large-Sized Telescopes

The European Southern Observatory announced on 19 November 2026 that the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory's southern site at Paranal will include two Large-Sized Telescopes, bringing the planned southern array to 53 telescopes. The LSTs complement the site's Medium- and Small-Sized Telescopes by collecting the faint atmospheric Cherenkov flashes produced by lower-energy gamma rays. ESO announcement.

The change is scientifically important for transients and multimessenger targets because it expands CTAO-South toward lower energies while preserving the high-energy reach of its smaller telescopes. It is an approved construction change, not an observing result: final performance, energy threshold and commissioning dates will depend on the completed array and site operations.

ESA selects a seven-spacecraft observatory for multiscale plasma physics

ESA's Science Programme Committee formally selected Plasma Observatory as the agency's M7 medium-class mission on 19 November 2026, after a selection process that began with 27 proposals in 2021. Seven coordinated spacecraft will measure particles and electric and magnetic fields at multiple locations simultaneously, linking kilometre-scale, millisecond processes to magnetospheric changes extending over tens of thousands of kilometres and minutes. Launch is provisionally foreseen for the late 2030s. ESA mission-selection announcement.

The mission is intended to resolve a limitation of the four-spacecraft Cluster mission, which could characterize only one scale at a time. Formal selection does not yet mean construction has been adopted: ESA still distinguishes selection from the later adoption decision that commits the mission to implementation. ESA also advanced four M8 concepts and three fast-class concepts for further study, including GRINTA for transient and multimessenger gamma-ray astronomy.

Calcium shuttles fluoride in a main-group catalytic cycle

Job J. C. Struijs, Agamemnon E. Crumpton, Véronique Gouverneur, Simon Aldridge and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed Nature article published 19 November a calcium complex that catalyses nucleophilic fluorination by capturing and releasing fluoride. A sterically encumbered dianionic ligand stabilizes a terminal Ca–F intermediate; kinetic and computational analysis places late Ca–F bond cleavage in the rate-determining fluoride-delivery transition state. Research article.

The proof-of-concept reactions show that calcium modulation can favour substitution over competing elimination, offering a catalytic role for an abundant main-group element without relying on the oxidative-addition and reductive-elimination chemistry common to transition metals. This is a new catalytic platform rather than an established manufacturing process: substrate breadth, catalyst loading, ligand synthesis, recycling and performance at scale remain to be demonstrated. Evidence access: the accelerated article preview, abstract, crystallographic-data statement and supplementary-information inventory were reviewed; the complete edited version of record was not yet available.

An agentic system designs and experimentally refines functional RNA

Han Huang, Tianqing Zhang and colleagues introduce RhoAgent in a peer-reviewed Nature Biotechnology article published 19 November. The system combines an RNA diffusion model, RDiff, with iterative experimental feedback and constraints on base interactions and functional motifs. It generated active ribozymes sharing only 40–70% sequence identity with wild types, improved cleavage of a structurally uncharacterized hovlinc ribozyme by as much as sixfold relative to a human-expert-truncated construct, and produced a fluorogenic aptamer scaffold with 230% greater fluorescence than the wild type. Research article; released code.

The work goes beyond an in-silico benchmark because designed molecules were experimentally tested, including dual-function scaffolds that preserved two aptamer cores in a Förster-resonance-energy-transfer system. The reported gains are task- and construct-specific; they do not establish general autonomous RNA design, therapeutic efficacy or safety. Training data include experimentally determined structures and large sequence datasets, so performance on genuinely out-of-distribution folds and functions remains an important test.

A nanobody blocks opioid-receptor signalling with cellular and subcellular precision

Zoé Valbret and colleagues report in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Chemical Biology article published 19 November a structure-guided nanobody, Nb64, engineered to bind active opioid receptors with sufficient affinity to compete with intracellular transducers. In cultured cells it inhibited G-protein, GRK and β-arrestin engagement and receptor internalization. A Golgi-targeted form selectively blocked delta-opioid-receptor signalling at that organelle while leaving plasma-membrane responses intact. Research article.

In acute mouse ventral-tegmental-area slices, Nb64 abolished μ-opioid-receptor-driven GIRK currents while sparing GABA-B-receptor currents in the same neurons; expression in GABAergic neurons attenuated fentanyl-evoked dopamine release and behavioural responses. This makes Nb64 a mechanistic tool for separating receptor pools and circuits. It is not an opioid treatment: genetic delivery lacks immediate clinical applicability, and the authors state that cell-penetrating delivery still requires improvement in specificity, tissue penetration and temporal control.

Pediatric imaging radiation is associated with a dose-dependent increase in brain-tumour risk

Diana L. Miglioretti and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed JAMA study published online 19 November a retrospective cohort of 3,704,860 children born from 1996 through 2016 in six US health systems and Ontario, Canada. Over 45,671,641 person-years, 1,536 brain tumours were diagnosed. Cumulative imaging dose to the brain, lagged by two years, was associated with an excess relative risk of 1.27 per 100 mGy (95% CI 0.83–1.77); the association was stronger for aggressive histologies. Research article.

The authors estimate that exposure comparable to one head CT was associated by age 21 with 7.9 excess brain tumours per 10,000 children, or about one excess tumour per 1,266 head CT examinations. The result supports justification and dose optimization when ionizing-radiation imaging is clinically indicated; it does not imply that necessary CT should be withheld. Residual confounding by the medical reasons for imaging, dose reconstruction and limited follow-up into later adulthood remain important limitations of this observational study.

A small autopsy-linked study identifies a candidate blood marker for high-stage CTE

Annalise E. Miner and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed JAMA article published online 19 November measurements of plasma endogenously cleaved microtubule-binding-region tau, or eMTBR-tau, in 21 former male American-football players who later donated their brains. Eighteen had neuropathologically confirmed chronic traumatic encephalopathy; 16 had high-stage disease. Median eMTBR-tau was 0.021 pg/mL in high-stage CTE, compared with 0.001 pg/mL in low-stage or no CTE, and it correlated with a composite tau-burden score across 11 brain regions. Research article.

The association remained after excluding five cases with comorbid Alzheimer pathology, while plasma p-tau217 was not associated with CTE. The study supplies biological proof of concept, not a diagnostic test: only three participants lacked CTE, all participants were male former football players, the groups differed in age, and the convenience sample was enriched for advanced disease. Diagnostic accuracy, specificity against other tauopathies and performance in living clinical populations require much larger prospective and autopsy-validation cohorts.

A layered biosecurity framework targets AI-generated biological sequences

A multidisciplinary group presents an open-access Nature Communications Perspective published 19 November, proposing safeguards across biological training data, sequence-generation models, users and physical DNA synthesis. The framework recommends risk-stratified data access, capability and red-team evaluations, user controls, traceability, and sequence screening at synthesis providers rather than relying on any single checkpoint. Perspective.

The paper carefully distinguishes demonstrated capability from prospective risk. It cites genome-language-model work that generated viable variants of the roughly 5.4-kb ΦX174 bacteriophage—16 viable designs among 285 synthesized candidates—while emphasizing that de-novo design of enhanced human pathogens, deliberate host-range expansion and AI-designed gene drives have not been demonstrated. The recommendations arose from a 20-person workshop held 25–27 November 2025 and have not been validated as an operational security standard; effectiveness, compliance costs, international coverage and independent auditing remain unresolved.

Antarctic zircons point to eroded Gondwanan supermountains

Catch-up — institutional report dated 19 November; the open-access paper appeared online on 17 September and is assigned to the 1 December issue of Earth and Planetary Science Letters. Bei Chen and Ian H. Campbell analysed 1,712 detrital zircon grains recovered from sediments around Antarctica's continental margin, using age and trace-element signatures to infer high-pressure mountain roots hidden beneath the ice. The dominant 650–450 million-year population coincides with Gondwana's assembly. Research article; Australian National University report.

After weighting a global river-zircon database, the authors attribute about 46% of detrital zircons and 55% of high-pressure grains from the interval leading into and through the Cambrian explosion to Antarctica. They propose that erosion of Himalayan-scale mountains supplied ocean nutrients and promoted organic-carbon burial, raising oxygen availability. The mountain-building reconstruction is geochemical and indirect, and the proposed causal connection to animal diversification rests on temporal coincidence plus a biogeochemical mechanism; it is not a direct measurement of Cambrian oxygenation or evolutionary rate.
