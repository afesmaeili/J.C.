01The cosmological lithium problem and how decaying relics still solve itMaksym Ovchynnikov

The cosmological lithium problem and how decaying relics still solve it

Maksym Ovchynnikov. arXiv:2611.26691v1 · Full text. Primary category: hep-ph. Submitted 19 November 2026 at 19:00:07 UTC; first announced in the 23 November UTC listing. Preprint, CERN-TH-2026-305; no journal reference is recorded. Type: numerical transport calculation and conditional BSM interpretation, not a detection.

Question and physical mechanism. Can nonthermal injection reduce primordial lithium without violating the much tighter deuterium, helium and radiation-density measurements? The proposed mechanism separates two epochs. Electron antineutrinos with energies around 100 MeV convert protons into neutrons while beryllium-7 is forming. The reactions Be-7(n,p)Li-7 and Li-7(p,α)He-4 then remove the precursor of most primordial lithium. This also makes unwanted deuterium. Later electromagnetic cascades, powered by injected electrons or photons, destroy that excess once the background is cool enough for cascade photons to survive above the 2.22 MeV deuteron threshold. The approximate cascade cutoff is E_c ≃ m_e²/(22T_γ); the useful deuterium destruction occurs around T_γ ≃ 5 keV. The calculation therefore depends on injection timing as strongly as on the energy budget.

Method and assumptions. The paper couples νDsmc neutrino transport to Acropolis electromagnetic cascade kernels and a nuclear network following n, p, D, T, He-3, He-4, Li-6, Li-7 and Be-7. It evolves neutrinos and antineutrinos separately, includes phase-averaged flavor conversion, muon energy loss and decay, neutrino-induced helium breakup, fragment reactions, photon heating and the relic contribution to expansion. It fixes the final baryon-to-photon ratio to η_B = 6.13 × 10⁻¹⁰ and uses the adopted PRIMAT nuclear rates, rather than marginalizing their uncertainties jointly with the relic parameters. Its standard calculation gives D/H ≃ 2.443 × 10⁻⁵ and Li-7/H ≃ 5.53 × 10⁻¹⁰.

The working observational acceptance intervals are D/H = (2.450–2.566) × 10⁻⁵ and helium mass fraction Y_P = 0.2432–0.2484, from the adopted two-standard-deviation measurement ranges; He-3/H ≤ 1.45 × 10⁻⁵ includes surviving tritium. The broad radiation interval is 2.57 ≤ N_eff ≤ 3.291, formed from the span of recent CMB analyses. The more restrictive set called “lithium solutions” additionally uses Li-7/H = (1.064–2.350) × 10⁻¹⁰ and 2.69 ≤ N_eff ≤ 3.161. These are acceptance cuts assembled from different measurements and analyses, not a single joint confidence region or a posterior marginalized over cosmological and nuclear nuisance parameters. In particular, entering that lithium band is weaker than reaching the PDG stellar range (1.45 ± 0.25) × 10⁻¹⁰ at two standard deviations.

Quantitative results. Single decaying species work in restricted mass, branching-ratio and lifetime windows. One Le−Lτ gauge-boson benchmark has mass 260 MeV, dimensionless coupling g = 7.5 × 10⁻¹⁴, lifetime 8.5 × 10³ s and maximum relic-to-radiation energy-density ratio f = 0.052. It yields Li-7/H = 2.32 × 10⁻¹⁰, Y_P = 0.2481 and N_eff = 3.15, with D/H adjusted to 2.508 × 10⁻⁵. A 265 MeV heavy neutral lepton with electron-neutrino dipole coupling d_e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ GeV⁻¹, lifetime 8.9 × 10³ s and f = 0.044 gives Li-7/H = 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁰. Both alleviate the discrepancy but remain above the upper end, 1.95 × 10⁻¹⁰, of the adopted PDG two-standard-deviation range. These couplings are benchmarks, not measured values or exclusion limits.

Populations of leptonic Q-balls do better because their initial charges produce a distribution of lifetimes and injection energies. For the assumed nearly flat scalar potential, M_Q ∝ Q^(3/4), emitted energy ω_Q ∝ Q^(−1/4), and lifetime τ_Q ∝ Q^(5/4). Smaller solitons release harder antineutrinos earlier; larger ones preserve a later, softer electromagnetic component. Population 1 uses ω_peak = 41 MeV, charged fraction b = 0.75, charge asymmetry ε = −1, τ_peak = 1.238 × 10⁴ s and initial energy-density ratio R_Q = 2.39 × 10⁻⁴ at T_γ = 3 MeV. It gives Li-7/H = 1.937 × 10⁻¹⁰, Y_P ≃ 0.2476 and N_eff ≃ 3.089. Its Li-6/Li-7 prediction spans 0.00573–0.01195 under the two nuclear-recoil prescriptions. Other allowed populations reach Li-7/H near 10⁻¹⁰. These are numerical examples and scanned envelopes, not evidence that Q-balls exist.

Figure S5 makes the compensation particularly clear for population 3: removing photodisintegration leaves D/H about 61% above standard BBN, whereas removing the nonthermal neutrino reactions leaves it about 41% below. Both processes together restore the observed deuterium abundance while reducing lithium. This is a coupled transport result; neither injection channel alone reproduces it.

Limitations and testability. The cancelling lifetime is narrow: D/H changes by roughly 2–3% per 1% change in τ_peak around the fitted solutions. Helium-fragment recoil and weak breakup cross sections produce factor-of-two-to-three uncertainties in Li-6; the particle benchmarks predict much more Li-6 than the stellar upper limits unless substantial stellar depletion is allowed. Q-ball solutions depend on the charge distribution and its early and late tails: some tested lattice distributions lose the solutions, and some recover them only after extending the large-charge tail. The microscopic realization also requires additional assumptions about formation, charge capture, reheating and the superpartner spectrum. The calculation neglects refraction from the injected lepton asymmetry after estimating it to be small for a benchmark.

Supernova limits are sensitive to the core model and detector response. The paper's calibrated one-zone calculations allow the quoted particles for sufficiently cold cores; hotter cores or an expanded treatment of high-energy photons can exclude some heavy-neutral-lepton cases. A future Galactic-supernova gamma-ray signal and shifts in N_eff offer proposed tests, but the quoted comparison with Simons Observatory precision is a forecast. The work does not establish a universal surviving particle solution, nor rule out an astrophysical origin of the lithium discrepancy.

History

The two-stage mechanism has an important direct predecessor. Pospelov and Pradler (2010), especially §2.5 and Figures 8–9, already studied muon-decay antineutrinos followed by electromagnetic destruction of the added deuterium. Their example at τ_X = 8.5 × 10³ s exhibited the same nonmonotonic deuterium evolution. Consequently, neither antineutrino neutron production nor subsequent deuterium cleanup is new here. The present work revisits the balance with updated abundance/radiation constraints, harder direct neutrino spectra, and a resolved population of evaporating solitons. Its claim that the old muon-pair realization fails is conditional on those newer inputs and its updated transport treatment.

Coc, Pospelov, Uzan and Vangioni (2014) varied the time dependence of neutron injection through decay, annihilation, resonant annihilation and mirror-neutron conversion. In their calculations, obtaining Li/H ≤ 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ with neutron injection alone implied D/H ≥ 3.6 × 10⁻⁵. Their conclusion explicitly allowed combinations with later soft electromagnetic injection. The new result therefore addresses an established escape route from the deuterium obstruction; it does not overturn that obstruction for neutron-only models. No improvement factor between the older abundance envelope and the new parameter windows is meaningful because the physical models and observational cuts differ.

There is also a competing stellar baseline. Borisov et al. (2024) reproduced the plateau with stellar transport including atomic diffusion and parametrized turbulence, calibrated at log₁₀(T₀/K) = 6.28. The calibration is a genuine uncertainty, but it prevents interpreting the stellar surface abundance as a model-independent primordial measurement. An independently examined, uncited observational study, “Subgiants in NGC 188 reveal that rotationally induced mixing creates the main sequence Li-Dip” (2025), used spectroscopy of 96 turnoff/subgiant members and evolving convection-zone depths to distinguish subsurface lithium profiles. It favors rotational mixing for that cluster. NGC 188 is metal-rich, so this is not a direct solution for metal-poor primordial-lithium tracers; it is evidence that stellar processing is physically consequential. The new relic calculation expands the viable theoretical alternatives rather than demonstrating that new particles are required.

02Blazars through the eyes of REDSoX and GOSoXSara Capecchiacci, Ioannis Liodakis, Herman L. Marshall, Swati Ravi, Haocheng Zhang

Blazars through the eyes of REDSoX and GOSoX

Sara Capecchiacci, Ioannis Liodakis, Herman L. Marshall, Swati Ravi and Haocheng Zhang. arXiv:2611.26682v1 · Full text. Primary category: astro-ph.HE. Submitted 19 November 2026 at 19:00:02 UTC; first announced in the 23 November UTC listing. Preprint in A&A manuscript format; no journal reference or acceptance is recorded. Type: instrument/source forecast, not a polarization measurement or a hadronic-emission detection.

Question and method. Which blazars are promising for polarimetry in the 0.2–0.4 keV band, and what could simultaneous soft-X-ray and MeV polarization reveal about jet emission? The authors transfer leptonic, hadronic and hybrid polarization templates to a 62-source gamma-ray-loud RoboPol sample, supplemented by Mrk 421 and 3C 273, and to a separate 63-source CAZ–eROSITA BlazEr1 match. The latter contains six low-, six intermediate- and 51 high-synchrotron-peaked sources and is selected above 10⁻¹² erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ in the catalogue's 0.2–0.5 keV band.

Broadband SED fits are shifted to catalogue synchrotron-peak frequencies and X-ray fluxes. The reference polarization spectra come from BL Lac, BZBJ0211+1051 and Mrk 501, shifted in frequency using the SED valley and normalized with optical polarization. For sources without RoboPol monitoring, the optical degree is assigned from a peak-frequency-binned population relation. An alternative high-synchrotron-peaked prediction scales the assigned optical degree by 2.5. The leptonic prescription contains polarized synchrotron self-Compton and unpolarized external Compton radiation; the hadronic prescription contains proton and secondary-pair synchrotron radiation, and the hybrid adds electron SSC.

Sensitivity and results. The adopted background-free threshold is MDP₉₉ = 4.291/(μ√(R_s T)), as a polarization fraction, with μ = 0.95, source count rate R_s from the effective-area response and a flat energy-flux density across the narrow band. Exposure is 300 ks for high-synchrotron-peaked sources and 1 Ms for the other classes. The quoted 99% is the minimum-detectable-polarization convention; it is not a 99% probability of distinguishing emission models.

For Mrk 421, Table B.1 gives band flux (1.94 ± 1.37) × 10⁻¹⁰ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, predicted polarization degrees 8.38%, 6.57% and 8.20% for the leptonic, hadronic and hybrid prescriptions, and MDP₉₉ = 1.19%. For 1ES 1959+650, the corresponding flux is (3.13 ± 1.47) × 10⁻¹⁰ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, polarization approximately 5.98%, 4.48% and 5.89%, and threshold 0.94%. These are source forecasts anchored to catalogue fluxes, not new measured polarization values.

The authors simulate 1,000 flux/polarization realizations per source. Their quoted “duty cycle” is the fraction exceeding the sensitivity threshold: 100% for Mrk 421 and about 99% for 1ES 1959+650 under the table assumptions. It is neither satellite observing efficiency nor a confidence level for a physical model. For some high-synchrotron-peaked templates, a leptonic interpretation gives roughly 11% in the soft-X-ray band but nearly zero in the 0.2–5 MeV COSI band; simultaneous measurements could therefore add information beyond an SED fit. The long exposures describe orbital-mission targeting prospects, not what can be achieved during the roughly five-minute REDSoX science flight described in the paper.

Scientific limitations. The forecasts inherit a small set of template shapes, noncontemporaneous SED/optical information, simplified spectral conversion, neglected background and assumed polarization-angle stability. Independently sampling flux and optical polarization does not preserve their possible correlation. For the unmonitored sample, sampling around a population median is not a measurement of each object's variability. Detecting polarization above MDP does not by itself distinguish the three templates: uncertainty in magnetic ordering, emission zones and component mixtures must enter that inference.

There is also an internal classification inconsistency: §5.1 and the appendix prose assign the synchrotron/high-energy SED components to source classes in a way that conflicts with the high-synchrotron-peaked template shown in Figure A.1 and with the paper's subsequent discussion. The physical interpretation here follows the displayed SEDs and the established distinction between synchrotron-dominated and Compton/hadronic bands, rather than that blanket sentence. In particular, high soft-X-ray polarization from a high-synchrotron-peaked source is not standalone evidence for protons. The linked complete machine-readable catalogue was not independently inspected; the numerical examples above are from the full paper's printed tables.

History

Zhang and Böttcher (2013) calculated frequency-dependent synchrotron and SSC polarization for realistic leptonic and hadronic SED fits. Their fully ordered-field values were upper limits, with optical synchrotron measurements proposed as a normalization. They already found that high-synchrotron-peaked blazars can have essentially identical electron-synchrotron X-ray polarization in both model families, while the gamma-ray polarization can differ strongly. That baseline is crucial: the discriminant is the pattern across physically different spectral components, not simply the existence of X-ray polarization.

The immediate precursor, Capecchiacci et al. (2026), A&A 711, A83 (preprint), already combined RoboPol SED fitting, rescaled three-model templates, multi-band predictions, minimum detectable polarization and Monte Carlo detection fractions. It even included a narrow soft-X-ray band near 0.275 keV. The present paper's advance is a dedicated 0.2–0.4 keV response calculation and target assessment for REDSoX/GOSoX, with the additional CAZ–BlazEr1 population. It extends that programme rather than introducing polarization as a new leptonic/hadronic discriminator. The two studies have correlated modelling assumptions and are not independent empirical confirmations.

An independently checked, uncited observational benchmark is Liodakis et al. (2022) (full preprint). IXPE measured Mrk 501 at 2–8 keV during 8–10 and 26–28 March 2022, obtaining polarization degrees 10 ± 2% and 11 ± 2%, compared with optical values 4 ± 1% and 5 ± 1%. Their multiwavelength analysis favored a shock-accelerated, energy-stratified electron population. This supplies measured support for chromatic magnetic ordering and motivates the approximate X-ray/optical scaling, while also showing why a universal single-zone transfer factor is a limitation. The new soft-band forecasts test extensions of that observational picture; they do not replace its measurements or establish jet composition.

03Is Neutrino Disappearance Sensitive to T-Violation for Asymmetric Matter Profiles ?Gabriela Barenboim, Stephen J. Parke, Alberto Sánchez Vargas

Is Neutrino Disappearance Sensitive to T-Violation for Asymmetric Matter Profiles ?

Gabriela Barenboim, Stephen J. Parke and Alberto Sánchez Vargas. arXiv:2611.27985v1 · Full text. Primary category: hep-ph. Submitted 20 November 2026 at 18:59:54 UTC; first announced in the 23 November UTC listing. Preprint, FERMILAB-PUB-26-0820-T; no journal reference is recorded. Type: exact oscillation formalism and numerical examples, not a measured T asymmetry.

Question. The familiar subtraction P(ν_α→ν_α) − P(ν_α→ν_α) vanishes identically. Does that prove disappearance cannot test time reversal? In an asymmetric medium it does not: exchanging source and detector also reverses the order of matter layers. The relevant comparison is forward survival against survival through the reversed profile, with the same physical mixing parameters. Matter asymmetry can make that comparison sensitive to the intrinsic complex phase for muon and tau flavors.

Derivation. For coherent, unitary three-flavor propagation with the standard real electron matter potential, the Hamiltonian is H(x) = U M² U†/(2E_ν) + diag(W(x),0,0), where W = √2 G_F N_e. Factoring out the 2–3 rotation and Dirac phase gives the exact form

P_μμ = A + B sin δ + C cos δ + D sin 2δ + E cos 2δ.

The coefficients depend on energy, density profile, length and the other mixing parameters, but not on δ. Reversing the profile relates the relevant probability to U→U*, so the proper T asymmetry is ΔP_μμᵀ = 2(B sin δ + D sin 2δ). B and D vanish for a symmetric profile. For an asymmetric profile, the resulting asymmetry can be nonzero when intrinsic CP/T violation is present and the oscillation factors do not cancel. Electron survival is exceptional: P_ee is independent of δ for this standard three-flavor potential. A proper CP comparison also transforms matter into antimatter; comparing neutrinos and antineutrinos in the same terrestrial matter introduces additional medium effects.

Size of the effect. The two-layer calculation fixes Y_e = 0.5, normal ordering, Δm²₂₁ = 7.5 × 10⁻⁵ eV², Δm²₃₁ = 2.5 × 10⁻³ eV² and sin²θ₁₂, sin²θ₁₃, sin²θ₂₃ = 0.31, 0.022, 0.47. It maximizes the phase-odd coefficient over path lengths. Near density–energy products ρ_a E_ν ≃ 0.7 and ρ_b E_ν ≃ 34 GeV g cm⁻³, approximately associated with the solar and atmospheric resonances, |B| can reach about 0.4. At δ = π/2 this permits a probability difference near 0.8. At 1 GeV, the global maximum shown uses lengths approximately 21,000 and 830 km; a shorter illustrative configuration uses 10,500 and 700 km. The required densities and distances make these demonstrations unsuitable as ordinary terrestrial beam designs. They are optimized amplitudes with fixed parameters, not statistical sensitivities or confidence bounds.

For the modeled DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande density profiles, |B| is only of order 10⁻⁷ around their beam peaks, approximately 2.6 and 0.6 GeV. It grows toward lower energy but is still only about 10⁻⁴ at 0.5 GeV for DUNE and 0.1 GeV for Hyper-Kamiokande. Thus the paper finds no practical sensitivity to this phase-odd disappearance mechanism in those experiments. That statement does not eliminate their appearance-channel CP programme or the different possibility of extracting cos δ from disappearance spectra.

Scope of the proposed extensions. Illustrative Earth-to-geostationary-detector and Moon-to-Earth configurations introduce long vacuum segments followed or preceded by Earth matter. They produce phase-odd coefficients of order 0.3 near a few GeV and 0.15 near tens of GeV, respectively; replacing mean Earth density by PREM materially changes the former example. These are propagation calculations. No complete beam-flux, detector-mass, event-rate, energy-resolution, background, engineering or nuisance-parameter analysis establishes an achievable significance. A direct reversed-path test would additionally require exchanging source/detector roles. Merely fitting the phase-odd term in a forward spectrum is a model-dependent inference. The result also assumes standard coherent propagation; absorption, decoherence or complex nonstandard matter interactions require a revised treatment.

History

Yokomakura, Kimura and Takamura (2002) already derived the exact CP-phase dependence for arbitrary matter profiles. Their equations (26)–(27) explicitly include sin δ and sin 2δ in muon and tau survival, and their symmetric-profile relations remove both terms. This is a precise mathematical antecedent, not merely a vaguely related appearance calculation. The new paper confirms and reorganizes that result; it should not be read as the first discovery that asymmetric-matter disappearance can contain phase-odd terms.

Bitter, de Gouvêa and Kelly (2025) (preprint) carefully distinguished proper reversed-trajectory tests from flavor exchanges along one trajectory. Their ordered evolution-matrix construction explains why a nonsymmetric medium can generate an apparent same-direction T-odd difference even without intrinsic T violation, whereas the properly reversed comparison vanishes for a real Hamiltonian. The present work applies that distinction systematically to disappearance, supplies the full coefficient bookkeeping, and explores where the intrinsic effect can become large. The practical smallness found for terrestrial baselines is consistent with the earlier analysis, rather than a new observation of symmetry violation.

A separate route is Denton (2024), “Probing CP Violation with Neutrino Disappearance Alone” (preprint). It constrains the phase-even cos δ dependence by combining electron- and muon-flavor disappearance. Its illustrative forecast used six years of JUNO and 13 years of DUNE, a 40 kt fiducial detector, 1.2 MW beam, external oscillation priors and detector/systematic modelling; the maximum CP-violation discovery sensitivity was about 1.6σ under those assumptions. That forecast does not measure the sign of sin δ and is not the same observable as the new reversed-profile asymmetry. The present contribution is the clarified symmetry test and resonant-profile optimization, not a replacement for an experimental sensitivity study.

Astrophysical Alerts

M87: IceCube limits neutrino emission during the LHAASO TeV flare

An IceCube follow-up published on 23 November at 07:59 UTC searched for track-like muon neutrinos from M87 over 14 November 00:00 to 18 November 00:00 UTC. The result is consistent with atmospheric background, with p = 1.0. For an assumed E⁻² spectrum, the 90% confidence upper limit on time-integrated muon-neutrino emission is E² dN/dE = 3.9 × 10⁻² GeV cm⁻². The central 90% of the expected detected events for that spectrum and declination lie between approximately 930 GeV and 1.5 PeV. These are fluence units, not a per-second flux. ATel 18165.

This follows LHAASO-WCDA's report of enhanced gamma rays on 15–17 November, with a peak on 16 November. Its preliminary spectrum spans roughly 0.5–20 TeV, with photon index 1.68 ± 0.29 (statistical uncertainty), an integral flux above 1 TeV around 0.5 Crab, and a three-day test statistic TS = 122, quoted as about 11σ. The announcement does not specify a global trials correction. ATel 18161.

The neutrino non-detection constrains emission during the stated four-day window under the spectral assumption; it neither excludes all hadronic models nor demonstrates that M87 emits no neutrinos. A model comparison must match flavor, spectrum and time window rather than directly equating the gamma-ray flux with this fluence bound.

GCN 46334.

The notably different durations and peak energies refer to different instruments and integration intervals; they must not be averaged as independent estimates of one fixed spectrum. The reported E_peak values are reproduced as written, pending final catalogue analysis; neither Circular states a confidence convention for its parameter errors. GCN 46333's position matches 46326 despite an erroneous additional citation to 46328. A short initial pulse alone does not establish a compact-binary merger or a gravitational-wave association.

GRB 261119B: ground analysis recovers a burst flagged as unreliable onboard

Fermi/GBM's 19 November 20:06:53.85 UTC trigger 816811618 was initially flagged as an unreliable location by flight software. In a 20 November 21:12:45 UTC report, the GBM team identifies it as a GRB, likely long, with ground position RA 216.6°, Dec +38.1°, J2000, and 13.9° statistical uncertainty. Its LAT boresight separation was 22°. GCN 46323.

This is a revised astrophysical classification of an existing trigger, not a second event. An unreliable initial localization did not establish that the signal was non-astrophysical. The Circular does not provide a duration, fluence, false-alarm probability or identified counterpart.

ESO overhauls real-time quality control for observatory data

The European Southern Observatory reported on 20 November 2026 that it has overhauled its quality-control software and reorganized reduction processing around ESO Data Processing System (EDPS) pipeline recipes. The update is intended to combine real-time science-data assessment and observing-block grading with current calibrations, while producing more uniform quality information for science products. ESO science announcement.

This is an operations and data-provenance improvement rather than a new astronomical measurement. Its scientific value will depend on how consistently the revised workflow identifies instrumental problems, propagates calibration status and exposes quality information to archive users; ESO did not attach a quantitative comparison of old and new rejection rates or downstream parameter accuracy to the announcement.

Antarctica InSync moves from planning toward coordinated observations

ESA reported on 20 November that the Antarctica InSync programme is moving toward implementation after a workshop held at ESA-ESRIN on 10–13 November 2026. More than 130 in-person participants from 29 countries worked on common operating procedures, joint instrument deployments and data-management practices. The initiative plans synchronized observations across Antarctica and the Southern Ocean during 2027–2030, organized around seven themes including sea ice, ice-sheet change, biogeochemical cycles, biodiversity, atmospheric processes and human impacts. ESA report; workshop record.

The result is an international observing framework, not a completed survey or climate finding. Coordinated timing and shared protocols could reduce one of the main limitations of polar research—measurements made by different programmes at incompatible places and times—but the realized coverage will still depend on national logistics, instrument availability, weather, data release and sustained funding.

A multiancestry study expands the genetic map of endometriosis

Lindsay A. Guare, Jagyashila Das, Yuji Yamamoto and colleagues report in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Genetics article published 20 November a meta-analysis of more than one million participants across 14 biobank datasets, with 32% non-European ancestry. Eleven phenotype and ancestry meta-analyses identified 58 associated genomic regions, of which 27 were previously unreported, including ancestry-specific loci in African and admixed-American groups. Research article.

Fine-mapping nominated 37 putative causal variants, while integration of proteomic, splicing, expression, methylation and RNA-editing data implicated 314 genes. Those are statistical and in-silico assignments, not demonstrations that any nominated variant or gene causes endometriosis or is already a therapeutic target. Case definitions ranged from broad electronic records to procedure-confirmed disease; ancestry-specific sample sizes remained unequal, and tissue-specific functional work is needed to resolve mechanisms behind the associations.

Antibody profiling maps candidate links between herpesviruses, autoantigens and chronic disease

Sanghun Lee, Nicole Prince, Qingwen Chen and colleagues present an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Immunology resource published 20 November. They measured IgG reactivity to more than 4,600 herpesvirus peptide tiles and more than 15,000 human proteins in a discovery cohort of 1,289 adults and a replication cohort of 763, then linked the profiles to longitudinal electronic health records. Research article.

The analysis replicated 4,943 FDR-significant virus–autoantigen associations spanning 106 autoantigens; models for 11 autoantigens exceeded 85% accuracy in the training analysis and 80% in the independent test set. Two candidate pairs also showed viral–host sequence similarity consistent with possible molecular mimicry. These are association networks and hypotheses, not evidence that a herpesvirus exposure caused an autoimmune disease. The discovery cohort was selected for asthma, the sample sizes for individual incident diseases varied, antibody reactivity is an imperfect record of infection history and experimental perturbations are needed to test the proposed mechanisms.

Genome-wide imaging separates two mechanisms that tether chromatin to the nuclear lamina

Patrick J. Walsh, Emily B. Kraeutler, Roberto Linares-Saldana and colleagues report the first genome-wide imaging-based siRNA screen of human lamina-associated-domain positioning in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Communications article published 20 November. The screen identified more than 100 genes required for perinuclear localization and highlighted the RNA-binding protein hnRNPK: its depletion displaced nearly two-thirds of lamina-associated domains without erasing the assayed heterochromatin marks or dismantling lamina components. Research article.

The displaced domains were enriched for H3K27me3, whereas hnRNPK-insensitive domains were enriched for H3K9me3, supporting at least two mechanistically distinct classes of peripheral chromatin. hnRNPK loss also dysregulated transcription within detached domains. The work establishes a screening and classification framework in cultured human cells; siRNA off-target effects, cell-type dependence and the molecular bridge between hnRNPK and the lamina still require direct tests.

Neurons route PINK1 through an ER-assisted pathway before mitochondrial quality control

J. Tabitha Hees, Inmaculada Segura and colleagues used live-cell imaging and perturbation experiments to follow local PINK1 synthesis in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Communications article published 20 November. When metabolically regulated Pink1 mRNA detached from mitochondria, it moved to ribosomes near the endoplasmic reticulum and endolysosomes. The ER chaperone DNAJB6 protected the newly translated PINK1 precursor during transfer to mitochondria through an ER-SURF-like pathway. Research article.

Depleting DNAJB6 left persistent PINK1 precursor stores near ER/endolysosomal compartments and impaired mitophagy after mitochondrial damage. The result supplies a cellular mechanism connecting RNA localization, organelle contacts and the PINK1–Parkin quality-control pathway associated with Parkinson's disease. It does not show that manipulating DNAJB6 treats neurodegeneration: the experiments establish trafficking and mitophagy effects in model systems, while the relevant contact machinery, behaviour in ageing neurons and organism-level consequences remain unresolved.

Hybrid molecular nanobelts sustain global antiaromatic ring currents

Leonard S. L. Goh, Lene A. Gödde, Ka Man Cheung and colleagues report in an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Chemistry article published 20 November the synthesis of triple-stranded anthracene–porphyrin nanobelts containing 72-, 96- or 120-π-electron Hückel circuits. Nickel(0)-mediated macrocyclization followed by DDQ/TfOH Scholl oxidation closed and fused the strained belts. Research article.

The authors infer global paratropic ring currents in the neutral molecules from proton NMR shifts and corroborating quantum-chemical calculations, overcoming the localized Clar-sextet behaviour that usually prevents global currents in benzenoid belts. This is a synthesis and electronic-structure result, not yet an electronic device: yields, stability under operating conditions, charge transport and scalable preparation were not demonstrated, and the antiaromatic assignment rests on the agreement of spectroscopic signatures with models rather than a direct measurement of a persistent electrical current.

Climate and glacier geometry explain most mass loss at lake-terminating glaciers

A peer-reviewed Nature Climate Change article published 20 November maps global glacial lakes in 2000 and 2020 using deep learning followed by manual refinement. The inventory finds 3,567 more ice-contact lakes (+35%), with total area increasing by 604 ± 22 km² (+9%) and estimated volume by 66 ± 5 km³ (+12%). Lake-terminating glaciers lost mass at −0.66 ± 0.16 m water equivalent per year, compared with −0.40 ± 0.12 for land-terminating and −0.36 ± 0.13 for marine-terminating glaciers. Research article.

In the authors' attribution model, ice-contact lakes explained 11 ± 7% of mass loss among lake-terminating glaciers, versus 43 ± 10% for climate forcing and 46 ± 10% for glacier geometry. The analysis shows that lakes matter without making them the dominant global driver. The percentages are model-based contributions rather than controlled causal fractions; lake-depth estimates, remote-sensing classification, covariance among climate and geometry, and strong regional heterogeneity limit how directly the global values can be transferred to an individual glacier.
