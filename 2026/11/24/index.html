01Interpreting the γ-Ray Emission of the Cygnus X region with a Combined Cygnus OB2 and Cygnus X-3 ScenarioCaijin Xie, Zhihao Ouyang, Yudong Cui and Lili Yang

Interpreting the γ-Ray Emission of the Cygnus X region with a Combined Cygnus OB2 and Cygnus X-3 Scenario

Caijin Xie, Zhihao Ouyang, Yudong Cui and Lili Yang. arXiv:2611.30284v1 · Full text. Submitted 23 November 2026, 08:40 UTC; new-primary entry in the 24 November UTC listing, astro-ph.HE. Preprint; no journal reference or acceptance recorded on the checked arXiv page.

Question and approach. Can one physically consistent superposition explain the Cygnus region from GeV to PeV energies, and can neutrinos distinguish hadronuclear from photohadronic emission? The paper combines the nearby Cygnus OB2 wind-termination shock, Galactic cosmic rays interacting with surrounding gas, and both compact and extended emission associated with the much more distant Cygnus X-3. These are sources projected onto the same sky region, not members of one physical association: the adopted distances are 1.46 and 9.0 kpc, respectively.

The calculation solves a steady, spherically symmetric diffusion–advection–loss equation for the proton distribution. OB2 has a termination-shock radius of 15 pc, a superbubble radius of 96 pc and a gas extent of 155 pc. The adopted effective gas density is 8.75 cm⁻³, after dilution of the molecular contribution. Inside the bubble the authors compare Kolmogorov, Kraichnan and Bohm transport; outside it they fix D(E)=4.0×1026(E/GeV)1/3cm2s−1. The numerical calculation uses 1,000 spatial and 1,000 energy steps, with an iterated shock boundary condition. Gamma-ray production is computed with GAMERA, and photomeson production with the Hümmer et al. simplified interaction model; absorption on local stellar light and the cosmic microwave background is included.

Results and assumptions. In the tested wind-shock configurations, Kolmogorov and Kraichnan escape limit the high-energy output. Bohm transport with scattering-center drift parameter η=0.11 reproduces the spectrum below roughly 100 TeV but leaves a higher-energy deficit. Adding the Galactic cosmic-ray component helps without filling the PeV shortfall. A Cygnus X-3 core component fills the highest-energy end, while escaped protons supply the remaining several-hundred-TeV emission; in the resulting model Cygnus X-3 becomes dominant above about 200 TeV.

Two compact-source benchmarks fit the gamma-ray spectrum: pp+pγUV and pγX+pγUV. Both fix the proton cutoff at 50 PeV. Their proton indices are 2.4 and 2.3 and their proton energies above 1 TeV are 6.6×1039 and 4.8×1039 erg, respectively, for a 1011 cm emission scale. The first uses a target density of 2.0×1010 cm⁻³. The donor temperature is 3.75×104 K; the X-ray target is based on MAXI observations from 1 January 2020 to 1 January 2026. Extended emission uses an ambient density of 1 cm⁻³ and escape fractions of 0.009 or 0.005. These are adopted model parameters, not measured confidence intervals.

The predicted single-flavor core neutrino energy flux in the pp+pγUV case reaches approximately Eν2dNν/dEν~10−10 GeV cm⁻² s⁻¹ in the GeV band; the photohadronic alternative differs strongly there despite similar absorbed gamma-ray spectra. This is a discriminator proposed for future observations, not a neutrino detection.

Limitations. The spectral agreement is not a statistical rejection of every OB2 accelerator model. The calculation fixes simplified gas and transport geometries, assumes steady injection and a preserved escaping spectrum, and represents the average core flux as half its high-state value. The long-term duty cycle is therefore consequential. Its comparison of extended emission with a point-source IceCube limit and a projected NEON sensitivity is indicative; a spatially matched detector likelihood is needed for a detection forecast. Gamma-ray attenuation is included, but a self-consistent reprocessed electromagnetic cascade is not established here.

History

The extended-halo interpretation predates this paper. Shi et al., arXiv:2607.07100 modeled continuous proton escape from Cygnus X-3 and compared both the spectrum and radial intensity above 400 TeV with LHAASO. Their benchmark fixes an injection index of 2, a 10 PeV cutoff and D(1PeV)=3×1029 cm² s⁻¹, with source ages of 100–400 kyr. With a vertically stratified gas distribution, the required cosmic-ray power was 1.6–3.2% of an assumed 5×1039 erg s⁻¹ kinetic luminosity. Thus a distant microquasar halo was already an energetically plausible alternative to assigning all emission to the nearby stellar association. The present work adds an explicit OB2/Galactic-background/core/halo decomposition across a wider energy range and a low-energy neutrino comparison; it does not originate the microquasar-halo hypothesis.

An independent, relevant comparison is Wan et al., arXiv:2610.11881, absent from the new paper's bibliography. That study jointly models the GeV and ultrahigh-energy spectra and orbital light curves, with anisotropic inverse-Compton emission and escaping protons interacting with stellar photons and competing disk/Wolf–Rayet winds. It finds that the gas encountered by the protons depends strongly on wind geometry, and that sustaining a dominant bubble contribution requires adequate time-averaged proton power or duty cycle. This exposes an important uncertainty in interpreting a successful steady spectral decomposition: compact-source high-state fits do not by themselves establish the long-term injection history of the extended halo.

02Reconstructing the depth of first interaction in extensive air showers from fluorescence detector profiles with a physics-motivated neural networkMarcos Olegario and Vitor de Souza

Reconstructing the depth of first interaction in extensive air showers from fluorescence detector profiles with a physics-motivated neural network

Marcos Olegario and Vitor de Souza. arXiv:2611.30661v1 · Full text. Submitted 23 November 2026, 13:21 UTC; new-primary entry in the 24 November UTC listing, astro-ph.HE. Preprint prepared for JCAP; no acceptance or journal publication was verified.

Question and method. Can fluorescence profiles recover the first-interaction depth Xfirst, rather than only the shower maximum Xmax? For a fixed primary and energy, Xfirst follows an exponential distribution with interaction length λA-air=⟨mair⟩/σA-air. Recovering its distribution would offer a route to hadronic cross sections and composition, although the first interaction itself usually produces too little fluorescence to observe directly.

The authors generate four million CONEX showers: proton and iron libraries for EPOS LHC-R and Sibyll 2.3d, spanning 1018–1020 eV uniformly in log energy. They propagate fluorescence light through an atmosphere and an Auger-like detector response, including triggering, noise and reconstruction. Cuts require an observed track of at least 300 g cm⁻², a contained maximum and at least 200 g cm⁻² before it. A temporal convolutional encoder feeds a Gaisser–Hillas profile decoder and a second branch predicting seven ordered quantiles of Xfirst. The profile-shape parameter X0 is explicitly not identified with the physical first-interaction depth. Conditional distributions, rather than a histogram of point estimates alone, are used to reconstruct the population tail.

Quantitative results. In the 1019.0–1019.2 eV bin, the mixed-model network obtains a proton mean absolute error of 20.78 g cm⁻² and correlation coefficient 0.752 with detector-level profiles, compared with 13.97 g cm⁻² and 0.872 for ideal profiles. Iron has a smaller absolute error, 6.75 g cm⁻², but a much weaker correlation, 0.398: its interaction length is itself only about 10 g cm⁻². Smaller absolute error therefore does not imply better physical reconstruction for iron.

Fitting the deepest 20% of the reconstructed distribution gives interaction lengths within 2% in same-model tests. With mixed-model training, the biases are +4.7% on EPOS LHC-R and −3.1% on Sibyll 2.3d; half their separation supplies the quoted approximately 4% model-spread systematic, not a confidence bound. Single-model cross-tests reach +8.4% or −6.0%. Raising iron contamination to 60% changes the reconstructed proton interaction length by less than 1%. Nominal 90% event intervals have 89.4–90.0% coverage in same-model tests, but cross-model coverage can fall to 86.7% for protons and 85.2% for iron.

Limitations and interpretation. This is a simulation-based method, not a new cross-section measurement. Training/test mixtures contain equal proton and iron fractions; helium and intermediate nuclei are absent. The weak dependence on iron contamination cannot be transferred to helium. Geometries are initially redrawn until every simulated shower passes selection, preserving the input depth distribution rather than reproducing an observed population. Reweighting by realistic selection changes the recovered interaction length by about −12% above 1018.6 eV. Requiring field-of-view coverage beyond 1,000 g cm⁻² and impact parameters below 15 km reduces that bias to about 1%, at a cost of a factor 2–4 in statistics; lower energies need further cuts. The approximately 4% uncertainty samples only two interaction models and is not a complete uncertainty budget for application to data.

History

The established observable is the exponential tail of Xmax, whose slope must be translated into a proton–air cross section using shower simulations. The Pierre Auger Collaboration's 2012 analysis measured σp-airprod=505±22(stat)−36+28(syst) mb at a mean nucleon center-of-mass energy of 57 TeV, using 1018–1018.5 eV events. It selected the deepest 20%, applied fiducial acceptance cuts and compared four hadronic generators; composition, especially helium, was a central uncertainty. That is an actual measurement. The new method aims to infer Xfirst more directly, but transfers part of the simulation dependence into the learned conditional distribution. Its simulation spread cannot be presented as an improvement factor over Auger's total measured uncertainty.

Auger's average-profile measurement, arXiv:1811.04660, established that Gaisser–Hillas profiles describe measured averages to approximately 1% over −300 to +200 g cm⁻² around the maximum, while identifying atmospheric aerosols as an important shape uncertainty. This supports a physically structured decoder without guaranteeing faithful reconstruction of every individual shower. Independently, Buitink et al., arXiv:2607.00135, not cited in the new paper, use simulated anomalous and double-bump showers to show how delayed energetic secondaries encode hadronic interactions beyond the first collision. Their proposed SKA-Low application concerns a different energy range and detector. Together these studies show why full profiles are informative, but also why a restricted smooth-profile model and uncertain early hadronic interactions remain important tests before deployment on data.

03Search for Axion-like Particles using Blazar Observations with the HAWC ObservatoryR. Alfaro et al. (HAWC Collaboration)

Search for Axion-like Particles using Blazar Observations with the HAWC Observatory

R. Alfaro et al. (HAWC Collaboration). arXiv:2611.30818v1 · Full text and complete author list. Submitted 23 November 2026, 15:09 UTC; new-primary entry in the 24 November UTC listing, astro-ph.HE. Preprint; the checked arXiv record gives no journal reference or acceptance.

Question and data. Photon–axion-like-particle conversion can let part of a gamma-ray beam cross intergalactic space without pair-production absorption, then regenerate photons in the Galactic magnetic field. Does the observed multi-TeV flux of distant blazars allow this extra transparency? The analysis combines approximately 3,000 days of HAWC Pass 5 observations from November 2014 to January 2024 with published H.E.S.S., MAGIC and VERITAS spectra for seven BL Lac objects at adopted redshifts 0.14–0.60. HAWC contributes a spatial Poisson likelihood with the full instrument response; the published IACT flux points enter a Gaussian likelihood. This is a constraint from existing observations, not a sensitivity forecast.

Model and statistical treatment. The interaction is Laγ=−(gaγ/4)aFμνF~μν. Transfer matrices propagate an initially unpolarized photon beam through source jets, assigned intracluster fields, EBL attenuation and the regular Jansson–Farrar Galactic field. The baseline EBL is Domínguez et al.; intergalactic conversion is omitted. Intrinsic spectra are simple power laws: each source's normalization and slope are profiled independently at every mass–coupling point. Redshifts, source-field prescriptions and the absence of intrinsic spectral cutoffs are fixed assumptions, not parameters marginalized with broad priors. For PG 1553+113, the adopted redshift is 0.491 and the jet field is 0.5 G with Doppler factor 35.

For four cluster-associated sources the calculation uses 1,000 turbulent-field realizations, adopting the median survival probability and treating its 5th–95th percentile spread as a systematic variation; this is not a Bayesian marginalization over the full field likelihood. The exclusion scan covers masses 0.1–2,000 neV and couplings 10−12–6×10−11 GeV⁻¹ on a 25×25 grid. Two thousand pseudo-experiments calibrate each tested hypothesis, with spectral nuisance parameters refitted. Exclusion requires CLs<0.05; a simple Wilks-theorem threshold is not assumed.

Results. No ALP signal is detected: the largest improvement has global p=0.31 after the scan. The lowest excluded coupling is 5.5×10−12 GeV⁻¹ for masses below approximately 4 neV and reaches 4.6×10−12 GeV⁻¹ around 6–9 neV. Exclusion extends to roughly 600 neV at larger couplings. Crucially, these numbers describe the lower edge of a disconnected 95% CLs excluded set. They do not exclude every larger coupling: below approximately 4 neV only 40% of scanned cells above that edge are excluded. The region becomes contiguous above its lower boundary for masses above roughly 50 neV within the scanned coupling range.

PG 1553+113 dominates: it excludes 178 of 625 tested cells alone, compared with 181 in the joint analysis. Changes of EBL prescription alter the excluded logarithmic parameter-space area by −36.5% to +32.6%; these are area changes, not percentage errors on gaγ. Tested Galactic-field variants reduce the area by up to 15.5%, with smaller detector-response effects.

Limitations. The result depends on extrapolating the IACT-constrained intrinsic power laws into the strongly absorbed multi-TeV band. Intrinsic curvature or cutoffs can mimic a change in transmission. Noncontemporaneous source variability, approximate IACT energy dispersion and the neglect of correlations between published flux points are additional qualifications. Assigned source/cluster fields and a restricted family of Galactic-field alternatives do not span all astrophysical uncertainties. The dominance of one source further limits the interpretation of this as seven independent confirmations.

History

Jacobsen, Linden and Freese, arXiv:2203.04332, already used the same seven nonflaring blazars to test enhanced TeV transparency. Their analysis combined IACT spectra with catalog-level HAWC information near 7 TeV and reported exclusions at greater than 3σ around gaγ≳3×10−11 GeV⁻¹ for masses below roughly 200–300 neV. The new work replaces that compressed flux treatment with more exposure, improved event separation and the forward-folded count likelihood. However, it also changes statistical calibration, confidence convention and the definition of the excluded set. A ratio of the two lower-edge couplings is therefore not a like-for-like sensitivity improvement and is not quoted here.

The strongest relevant comparison at the lower masses is not only the earlier HAWC result. Guo et al., arXiv:2507.07786, a primary study absent from the new bibliography, analyzed 16.5 years of Fermi-LAT observations of NGC 1275 between 100 MeV and 500 GeV. It reported a 95% exclusion reaching approximately 3×10−12 GeV⁻¹ over 0.4–5 neV under a 10 μG central Perseus-field model, Domínguez EBL and Jansson12 Galactic field. It refits log-parabola normalization, index and curvature, fixes its spectral scale, and uses the fifth percentile of fitted χ² across 100 turbulent realizations with a Monte Carlo-calibrated threshold. Thus the new HAWC lower edge is not universally the tightest constraint at overlapping masses; its value is complementary energy and propagation coverage with different systematics. The much smaller couplings discussed for VLAST in that earlier paper are forecasts and are not existing exclusions.

Astrophysical Alerts

GRB 261122B: optical and radio detections establish a multiwavelength afterglow

The SVOM burst occurred on 22 November 2026 at 22:46:55 UTC. Follow-up reported on 23–24 November adds two detections at the corrected GRB 261122B position. Kilonova-Catcher imaging began 3.5 h after the trigger; a 12 × 300 s r-band stack, template-subtracted against Pan-STARRS, measured r_AB = 19.83 ± 0.12 at a midpoint 4.07 h after the trigger. This is a detection of the fading optical counterpart; the observing-program name does not classify the transient as a kilonova. GCN 46348.

ATCA observations beginning 6.6 h after the burst found a preliminary ≈0.2 mJy source at 5 GHz, positionally consistent with the optical counterpart. The report does not quote a flux uncertainty or chance-coincidence probability, and further observations were planned. GCN 46351. Together with the earlier X-ray and optical detections, these data establish a multiwavelength afterglow. They do not by themselves determine the progenitor, redshift or energetics.

GRB 261119A: SVOM recovers a short burst and sharply improves its localization

Fermi/GBM first detected this event on 19 November 2026 at 19:01:35.03 UTC and classified it as a likely short GRB with a broad 13.4° statistical localization uncertainty. An edited SVOM report, issued 23 November and revised 24 November at 06:33:58 UTC, describes an offline ECLAIRs detection beginning at 19:01:35.10 UTC. The best 10–120 keV image trigger has SNR 6.3 over 0.22 s, too faint to trigger onboard. GCN 46349 v2.

ECLAIRs measures T₉₀ = 0.31 (+0.13/−0.12) s in 4–120 keV and localizes the burst to RA 19.059°, Dec +41.392°, with a 12.34 arcmin 90% radius that includes a 2 arcmin systematic contribution in quadrature. A power-law fit from T₀−0.15 to T₀+0.15 s gives photon index −0.85 ± 0.14, 4–120 keV fluence (9.5 ± 1.3) × 10⁻⁸ erg cm⁻², and photon flux 5.2 ± 0.6 photons cm⁻² s⁻¹; the report states that these errors are 68% confidence.

This confirms that the prompt emission is short and reduces the search region dramatically relative to GBM, but short duration alone does not identify a compact-binary progenitor. No counterpart, redshift, gravitational-wave association or trials-corrected false-alarm probability is reported.

PKS 1958−179: renewed Fermi-LAT activity with a harder gamma-ray spectrum

Fermi-LAT reported on 23 November 2026 at 20:00 UTC that the flat-spectrum radio quasar PKS 1958−179 (4FGL J2000.9−1748; z = 0.652) entered a renewed gamma-ray high state on 22 November. The preliminary one-day flux above 100 MeV was (0.3 ± 0.1) × 10⁻⁶ photons cm⁻² s⁻¹, statistical uncertainty only, about ten times the 4FGL-DR4 average. The fitted photon index was 1.7 ± 0.2, harder than the catalogue value 2.24 ± 0.03. ATel 18166.

Two photons were associated with the source at probability >0.999 by the LAT analysis; the highest-energy event was 34 GeV at 22 November 13:18:36 UTC. These probabilities refer to individual photon–source associations, not the global significance of the flare. The telegram supplies no trials-corrected flare significance, contemporaneous neutrino association or causal interpretation.

EP261015a / SN 2026ahoa: a radio upper limit 33 days after the X-ray trigger

Einstein Probe triggered on EP261015a on 15 October 2026 at 13:15:36 UTC; optical follow-up previously reported a broad-lined Type Ic supernova candidate, SN 2026ahoa, at tentative z = 0.31. ATCA observations began 17 November at 14:53 UTC, about 33.1 days after the trigger, with a central frequency of 8.5 GHz. No radio source was detected at the optical position. With a local rms of 8.1 μJy beam⁻¹, the report gives a 3σ upper limit of 24.3 μJy. GCN 46347.

This is a frequency- and epoch-specific upper limit. It constrains radio-emission models for the transient but does not retract the optical association, rule out radio emission at other epochs or frequencies, or establish the physical relation between the X-ray transient and the supernova.

EP261122a: near-infrared follow-up adds two counterpart limits

Einstein Probe/WXT triggered on EP261122a on 22 November 2026 at 06:12:37 UTC, and FXT subsequently localized an uncatalogued X-ray source. MDM/MIRAGE observations started at 10:03:48 UTC in J band, 3.86 h after the trigger, and at 10:27:16 UTC in Hs band, 4.24 h after the trigger. No new source was found inside the FXT error circle. The reported 5σ AB limits are J > 19.93 for 1080 s exposure and Hs > 19.45 for 1620 s exposure. GCN 46350.

These measurements complement the earlier r-band limit but are shallower in AB magnitude and were obtained in different bands and at different times. They do not demonstrate that the transient lacked a counterpart, nor do they determine its source class, distance or extinction.

Science News

JWST dust chemistry reconstructs late-stage mixing in Cassiopeia A's progenitor

Tea Temim and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed Astrophysical Journal Letters article published 23 November 2026 a JWST/MIRI map of dust composition in the Cassiopeia A supernova remnant. Webb resolved individual, roughly Solar-System-scale ejecta clumps that Spitzer could not separate. One reverse-shocked region is dominated by silica dust and another by carbon dust; the circumstellar "Green Monster" instead contains complex magnesium silicates. Research article; NASA release.

Combined with Chandra and XRISM measurements, the spatially distributed chemical pattern supports mixing between oxygen- and carbon-burning shells shortly before core collapse, possibly within hours of the explosion. This is an inference from surviving dust mineralogy and multiwavelength modelling rather than a direct observation of the progenitor's final hours; dust formation, shock processing and clump selection can modify the record. The method is valuable because it connects present-day remnant chemistry to otherwise inaccessible pre-supernova structure.

ESA releases complementary satellite records spanning 28 years of atmospheric SO₂

ESA's Climate Change Initiative announced on 23 November three version-1.0 Level-3 tropospheric sulphur-dioxide products built with a consistent covariance-based retrieval algorithm. The records combine GOME (April 1996–June 2003), OMI (October 2004–December 2022) and TROPOMI (May 2018–December 2024) observations, spanning 28 years in total. GOME is supplied as monthly means on a 1° grid; OMI and TROPOMI provide daily 0.25° and monthly 0.125° products. ESA dataset announcement.

The COBRA retrieval is designed to improve sensitivity to low SO₂ columns and reduce artefacts, which should help studies of point-source emissions, air quality, volcanic activity and aerosol–climate interactions. The instruments remain separate, complementary records with a gap between GOME and OMI and overlapping but non-identical resolution and sampling. A 28-year span therefore does not by itself make a perfectly homogeneous trend series; cross-instrument calibration, cloud screening and changing detection limits remain important.

A graph-based mouse pangenome reduces reference bias across laboratory strains

Juan F. Macias-Velasco, John E. Garza, Ting Wang and colleagues present a peer-reviewed Nature Genetics article published 23 November with three base-level graph references on a telomere-to-telomere C57BL/6J backbone: a 19-strain draft pangenome, an eight-founder Collaborative Cross graph and a graph augmented with diploid haplotypes from 40 recombinant-inbred lines. Research article.

Personalizing the founder graph with short-read whole-genome data reconstructed per-animal diploid genomes with 99.2% mean concordance to expected mosaics. Relative to GRCm38 or the single-strain telomere-to-telomere reference, graph alignment reduced reference-allele bias and improved read placement and variant-calling sensitivity; the framework also supported haplotype-specific methylation and allele-specific expression/chromatin analyses. The resource is a draft built from laboratory-strain diversity, so it cannot represent all wild-mouse variation, and improved computational concordance still needs phenotype-specific validation before it changes biological conclusions.

An evolved artificial photoenzyme controls a difficult 6π electrocyclization

Jonathan S. Trimble, Jordan Berreur, Anthony P. Green and colleagues report an open-access, peer-reviewed Nature Chemistry article published 23 November. Starting from an engineered [2+2]-cyclase, they screened more than 8,600 clones and evolved PhotoElectroCyclase PEC1.3 for light-driven 6π electrocyclization of acrylanilides. Research article.

For the model substrate, PEC1.3 raised yield from 6.9% to 71%, compared with 2% for free benzophenone under the same assay conditions, while reaching 35:1 diastereomeric ratio and >99% enantiomeric excess. A 1.3 Å crystal structure, spectroscopy, simulations and cryogenic activity support a mechanism in which hydrophobic contacts preorganize the substrate next to a genetically encoded benzophenone triplet sensitizer. The result demonstrates precise excited-state control in a laboratory system; it does not yet establish preparative scale, broad industrial robustness or environmental advantage over established syntheses.

Multilingual models expose a shared meaning-related component in the human language network

Andrea Gregor de Varda, Saima Malik-Moraleda, Greta Tuckute and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed Nature Neuroscience article published 23 November an fMRI comparison across speakers of 21 languages. They fitted brain-encoding models using representations from 20 multilingual neural-language models and found that models trained on some languages could predict language-network responses in previously unseen languages. Research article; code and reproducibility materials.

Perturbation and representation-removal analyses point to a transferable component related to meaning rather than language-specific word form. This supports a shared cross-lingual semantic organization, while leaving room for language-specific features. The models are correlational encoders of fMRI responses, not literal replicas of neural computation; per-language samples were modest, stories and scanners differed across datasets, and success of a multilingual artificial model cannot prove that the brain uses the same algorithm.

Proxy records and Earth-system simulations link glacial North Pacific oxygenation to Atlantic circulation

M. Tetard and colleagues report in a peer-reviewed Nature Geoscience article published 23 November a combined proxy–model reconstruction of North Pacific oxygenation over the past 55,000 years. Benthic-foraminifera records and Earth-system simulations indicate that transient weakening of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation during the last glacial period altered global overturning and contracted North Pacific oxygen-minimum zones. Research article; underlying proxy and age-model data.

In the Holocene simulation, biological export production rises by as much as 61% in the North Pacific relative to the Last Glacial Maximum, increasing water-column oxygen consumption and reinforcing deoxygenation during deglaciation. The paper also projects a modest oxygen increase of up to 0.05 ml l⁻¹ in the eastern tropical Pacific by 2100 under its model setup, implying contraction of the most oxygen-depleted core. The historical attribution combines sparse proxies with a model and does not isolate a single mechanism experimentally; the future number is scenario- and model-dependent and should not be read as a general reversal of global ocean deoxygenation.

Neither olanzapine nor droperidol clearly outperformed the other for pediatric emergency sedation

Elyssia M. Bourke, Amit Kochar, Deborah Shellshear and colleagues published the open-label, multicentre PEAChY-M randomized trial in JAMA on 23 November. The study enrolled 348 patients aged 9–17 years at ten Australian emergency departments from November 2021 to February 2026, assigning a single weight-based intramuscular dose of olanzapine or droperidol after nonpharmacological management had failed. Research article.

At one hour, successful sedation without extra medication occurred in 89/167 (53%) participants with outcome data in the olanzapine group and 104/167 (62%) in the droperidol group. The adjusted risk difference was −8.3 percentage points (95% CI −18.6 to 2.1; P = 0.12), so the prespecified superiority of olanzapine was not shown. One serious adverse event, apnea, occurred in the olanzapine group. Because the trial was open-label, 30%–34% of randomized participants did not receive the assigned drug within 30 minutes, and it was not designed to prove equivalence or detect rare harms, the result supports neither a definitive safety ranking nor the claim that the treatments are interchangeable in every clinical setting.
