Cosmic ray protons from 40 GeV to 10 EeV due to star formation?

Timur Dzhatdoev, Ilya Kudryashov and Anatoly Semenov. arXiv:2611.32671v1 · Full text. Primary category: astro-ph.HE. Submitted 24 November 2026 at 14:17:25 UTC; first listed in the 25 November UTC batch. Preprint; no journal publication is recorded on the checked abstract page.

Question and method. Can massive-star endpoints account for the measured proton spectrum across the Galactic–extragalactic transition? This three-page phenomenological study combines DAMPE, LHAASO, IceTop and Pierre Auger information, fits three spectral components, and explores local confinement and cosmological propagation. It is an interpretation of existing measurements, not a detection of three source populations. The Auger proton intensity is reconstructed as the measured proton fraction times an all-particle spectrum, so composition inference enters the high-energy input. The adopted energy calibration factors are 0.93 for LHAASO and IceTop and 0.95 for Auger.

Quantitative result. The two lower-energy components are fixed to the earlier Dzhatdoev–Semenov parameterization. The added component is a smoothly broken power law with pivot 100 TeV, normalization $1.55\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{PeV^{-1}\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}$, indices 2.61 and 3.68, break 1.25 EeV and smoothness parameter 7.6. Figure 1 shows that both proposed transport scenarios can describe the broad proton spectrum. These are quoted fit parameters without reported confidence intervals, not confidence limits or a statistical demonstration that alternative source classes are excluded.

In the local scenario, a magnetized structure of radius 1–20 Mpc confines particles. Equating an assumed $10^9$ yr photodisintegration timescale at iron rigidity 2.9 EV to $H^2/(6D)$, with $H=3$ Mpc, gives $D=4.5\times10^{32}\,\mathrm{cm^2\,s^{-1}}$. With $D\propto R^{1/3}$ this implies $D(1\,\mathrm{GV})=3.2\times10^{29}\,\mathrm{cm^2\,s^{-1}}$. A separate illustrative configuration—8 EeV iron, $H=20$ Mpc, sources at $0.8H$, and continuous injection over $3\times10^9$ yr—produces a 5.6% dipole. Its proximity to the cited Auger dipole does not establish agreement for a mixed composition or for the fitted proton population; the calculation concerns iron and an assumed source geometry.

For the cosmological alternative, CRPropa 3.2 propagates protons from sources extending to $z=6$, following the Madau–Dickinson star-formation history. The calculation includes photopion, Bethe–Heitler and adiabatic losses on the CMB and Gilmore EBL. The injection indices are 2 and 3.52, separated by a 3.16 EeV break with smoothness 4.9. The plotted high-energy suppression above roughly 50 EeV is a propagation prediction, beyond the principal 40 GeV–10 EeV comparison range.

Limitations. The short paper supplies neither posterior intervals for the new component nor a quantitative comparison of the two transport scenarios. Fixed lower-energy components and energy-scale adjustments limit interpretation of the apparent fit. The local magnetic structure is assumed, and the anisotropy example is not a joint spectrum–composition–arrival-direction fit. A full nuclear cascade, source energetics and corresponding multimessenger tests are not provided. The authors explicitly defer detailed photodisintegration calculations for heavier nuclei. Consequently, the result motivates a testable population scenario rather than establishing a common stellar origin of all cosmic rays.

History

The immediate baseline is Dzhatdoev and Semenov, arXiv:2606.02748. Its spectral construction, discussion and Table B1 already contain two Galactic components plus a harder third proton component; it places their proton crossover near 50 PeV. The present paper inherits the first two components and extends the third through an EeV break, adding explicit cosmological proton propagation and a local-confinement alternative. Thus the three-component decomposition itself is not the new result, and the reused lower-energy data do not constitute an independent confirmation.

An independently checked, uncited baseline is Gaisser, Stanev and Tilav, arXiv:1303.3565, especially its population models and Tables II–III. That work already demonstrated three-population descriptions with rigidity-dependent cutoffs, including both mixed and proton-dominated extragalactic alternatives, and explained that overlapping components and energy-scale shifts complicate physical identification. Its Hillas implementation uses characteristic rigidities of 4 PV, 30 PV and 2 EV, whereas its alternative global fit uses 120 TV, 4 PV and 1.3 EV. These are different all-particle/composition constructions, not directly comparable fitted proton break energies. The new paper narrows the emphasis to the proton data and a specific stellar interpretation; it does not supersede those composition-dependent alternatives merely by achieving a similar spectral shape.

The proposed high-energy stellar channel also predates this work. Wang, Razzaque, Mészáros and Dai, arXiv:0705.0027v2 modeled semirelativistic hypernova ejecta in stellar winds, deriving an acceleration scale proportional to $Z\epsilon_B^{1/2}v^2(\dot M/v_w)^{1/2}$ and showing how an ejecta-energy distribution over velocity steepens the integrated cosmic-ray spectrum. That study already connected a cosmological hypernova population to the second-knee region and computed a faint photohadronic neutrino counterpart. Its acceleration and energetics assumptions differ from a phenomenological broken-power-law injection. The 2026 paper adds a broader proton-spectrum comparison and modern propagation treatment, while leaving a similarly explicit source and neutrino calculation as an outstanding test.

Astrophysical Alerts

EP261122b: refined X-ray spectrum and optical limits strengthen the high-redshift-GRB hypothesis

Einstein Probe’s refined analysis, reported 24 November 2026 at 12:50:59 UTC, moves the onset of this fast X-ray transient to 22 November at 08:59:04 UTC. WXT recorded a single pulse lasting about 120 s. With Galactic hydrogen column fixed at 3.0 × 10²⁰ cm⁻², an absorbed power law gives photon index 1.61 (+0.40/−0.44) and unabsorbed 0.5–4 keV flux 6.1 (+2.1/−1.4) × 10⁻¹⁰ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻². GCN 46356.

FXT began its autonomous exposure at 09:05:03 UTC, about six minutes after the refined onset, and localized an uncatalogued source to RA 17.8019°, Dec +1.8008° (J2000), with a 10 arcsec radius at 90% confidence, including statistical and systematic uncertainty. Its average 0.5–10 keV spectrum is much softer: photon index 2.99 ± 0.16 and unabsorbed flux 1.20 (+0.08/−0.07) × 10⁻¹² erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² under the same fixed column. The Circular states that the quoted spectral uncertainties are 1σ.

AZT-33IK R-band imaging, reported 24 November at 23:21:54 UTC, did not recover the earlier z-band optical candidate, giving 3σ Vega limits R > 21.2 at a midpoint 3.62 h after onset and R > 21.3 at 30.06 h. GCN 46361. The authors’ inference z ≳ 5 is conditional on EP261122b being a GRB and on a power-law optical decay before the z-band detection; it is not a spectroscopic redshift or confidence limit. The R-band non-detections are compatible with a high-redshift dropout, but dust extinction and light-curve structure remain alternatives.

GRB 261121A: IPN triangulation reduces the short-burst search region to 6.2 square degrees

The bright burst was detected by Fermi/GBM, GECAM-B and Konus-Wind on 21 November 2026 at about 13:23:56.5 UTC. A joint Konus-Wind/Fermi analysis reported on 24 November at 15:25:05 UTC triangulates it to an annulus centered at RA 251.829°, Dec −20.132°, with radius 36.194° ± 0.089° at 3σ. Intersecting this annulus with the final GBM probability region yields a 6.2 deg² 3σ region, highly elongated: about 38.4° long and 12 arcmin wide. The Sun was approximately 39° away. GCN 46357.

This materially improves the broad GBM-only localization for counterpart searches, although the IPN team notes that the region may improve further. Localization alone does not identify a counterpart, compact-binary progenitor or gravitational-wave association; follow-up limits must also account for the unusually long, narrow geometry.

IceCube-261123A: MASTER reports optical limits for the Bronze track candidate

IceCube detected this track-like event on 23 November 2026 at 06:54:16.99 UTC. The revised Bronze notice gives an energy estimator of 145.04 TeV, signalness 0.45677, atmospheric-background false-alarm rate 1.2048 yr⁻¹, and direction RA 320.45°, Dec +24.58°, with asymmetric 90% PSF-containment uncertainties of roughly 1.4°–1.7°. These quantities make it a candidate, not a confirmed astrophysical neutrino or source association. IceCube notice; GCN 46345.

A MASTER follow-up reported on 24 November at 16:00:35 UTC. MASTER-Tunka began at 23 November 10:30:44 UTC, about 3.61 h after the neutrino, reaching clear-band limits up to about 19.0 mag; additional Tunka observations followed around 27.8 h, and MASTER-Tavrida observed from 24 November 15:47:56 UTC, about 32.9 h after the event, reaching up to 17.1 mag. GCN 46358. The report provides pointing-by-point upper limits and claims no optical transient association. These observations cover finite fields and depths; they do not establish that the neutrino region contained no electromagnetic counterpart.

GRB 261119A: Fermi confirms a 0.4-second prompt episode and resolves its spectral ambiguity

Fermi/GBM detected this event on 19 November 2026 at 19:01:35.03 UTC; SVOM later recovered it offline and provided a much smaller ECLAIRs position. The delayed GBM spectral report was issued 24 November at 21:56:49 UTC. In 50–300 keV, T₉₀ ≈ 0.4 s. Over T₀−0.07 to T₀+0.07 s, a cutoff power law gives the reported index 0.12 ± 0.01 and E_peak = 118 ± 6 keV. The 10–1000 keV fluence is (7.4 ± 0.9) × 10⁻⁸ erg cm⁻², and the 64 ms peak flux is 11 ± 1 photons cm⁻² s⁻¹. GCN 46360.

A Band function fits equally well, with E_peak = 107 ± 3 keV, α = 0.30 ± 0.05 and β = −2.6 ± 0.2. The competing fits and preliminary status matter: these are not a unique physical spectrum, and the report does not provide a redshift, energetics, counterpart or progenitor classification. The burst was 88° from the LAT boresight, limiting the relevance of contemporaneous LAT coverage.

GRB 261121B: SVOM/GRM measures a multi-pulse, long-duration burst

Fermi/GBM triggered on a likely long burst on 21 November 2026 at 20:27:50 UTC, localizing it to RA 255.4°, Dec −60.2° with 6.0° statistical uncertainty. GCN 46328. An SVOM/GRM ground analysis reported 25 November at 06:24:30 UTC, using 20:28:10.500 UTC as its spectral reference time. GRM finds multiple pulses and T₉₀ = 37 ± 9 s in 15–5000 keV. GCN 46362.

From five seconds before to 35 seconds after the GRM reference time, a power law gives index −1.60 (+0.12/−0.13) and 10–1000 keV fluence (4.31 ± 0.98) × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻². The source was about 83° from SVOM’s optical axis, outside ECLAIRs’ field of view. The duration and multipulse structure support the operational long-GRB classification, while the report supplies no counterpart, redshift or physical progenitor determination.

PKS 2232−488: Fermi-LAT records its brightest daily gamma-ray state

Fermi-LAT reported on 24 November 2026 at 20:09 UTC that the flat-spectrum radio quasar PKS 2232−488 (4FGL J2235.3−4836; z = 0.50581 ± 0.00015) was strongly active on 23 November. The preliminary one-day flux above 100 MeV was (0.3 ± 0.1) × 10⁻⁶ photons cm⁻² s⁻¹, with statistical uncertainty only: more than ten times the 4FGL-DR4 average and the highest LAT daily flux yet reported for this source. The fitted photon index was 1.8 ± 0.2, compared with the catalogue value 2.45 ± 0.04. ATel 18167.

LAT associated one 10 GeV photon detected at 12:51:01 UTC with the quasar at probability >0.999. That is a photon–source association probability, not a trials-corrected global significance for the flare. No contemporaneous neutrino association, multiwavelength counterpart change or causal interpretation was reported.

Science News

Euclid identifies a quasar at redshift 8.129

ESA announced the discovery of J1504+6252, or Dún Aonghasa, on 24 November 2026, the same day Julien Wolf and colleagues published the peer-reviewed paper in Astronomy & Astrophysics. Euclid imaging identified the candidate. Follow-up observations included Keck on 18 January, Hubble on 12 May, the Large Binocular Telescope on 15–16 May, and JWST/NIRSpec on 26 June 2026. Narrow Hβ gives a systemic redshift of 8.129 ± 0.005, corresponding to a cosmic age of about 630 million years. ESA announcement · Journal article · Full manuscript

Broad Hβ gives a benchmark black-hole mass of roughly 360 million solar masses, with a 0.43 dex uncertainty in the logarithmic mass. Across the adopted Hβ and Mg II calibrations, the inferred luminosity spans 12–44% of the Eddington luminosity. Building that mass so early challenges growth models, especially those with light initial seeds and sustained accretion at the currently inferred rate. The spectrum constrains the present accretion state. The initial seed mass and earlier growth history remain model-dependent.

Independent Lean checks support the Alpöge–Claude Riemann hypothesis proof

Levent Alpöge released the Lean formalization and an accompanying note on 20 November 2026, covering the classical Riemann hypothesis and its generalization to Dirichlet L-functions. On 25 November, Nature reported mathematicians’ assessments. Andrew Sutherland’s independent verification log, updated 24 November at 05:25 UTC, documents a byte-for-byte rebuild of the proof export, acceptance by separately implemented proof kernels, and independent checks that the proved statements match the challenge. Michael Rubinstein separately reported successful module and kernel checks on 21 November and posted a working exposition of the classical case on 22 November. Alpöge’s note · Sutherland’s verification log · Nature reporting

The checks give strong evidence for the formal theorem placing all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function on the line Re(s) = 1/2. Statement matching is essential because a kernel can certify a valid derivation of whatever proposition it is given. At the timestamp of Sutherland’s log, one newer end-to-end comparator run was still in progress. The release is a publicly scrutinized formal proof, with conventional peer review and a readable account of the full argument still outstanding at the cutoff.

An ecological model tests how well species records predict distributions in held-out regions

Yujing Yan and colleagues published EcoVAE in Nature Communications on 24 November 2026. In the peer-reviewed accepted manuscript, the authors train variational autoencoders on georeferenced occurrence records for plants, butterflies and mammals, with optional climate inputs. For the plant-genus baseline comparison across five geographically held-out folds, the occurrence-only model reached mean AUROC 0.97 and true skill statistic 0.89, ahead of the strongest baseline by 0.10 and 0.19, respectively. Article · Accepted manuscript

The approach can help prioritize surveys where predicted distributions and recorded occurrences diverge. The main records document presences, so an unsampled location cannot establish absence. Shared geography and sampling effort can also generate apparent species associations. Field tests are needed to distinguish ecological interactions from those effects.

Ambulance ceftriaxone showed no clear mortality benefit in a randomized sepsis trial

Damon Scales and colleagues published the peer-reviewed PITSTOP-Antibiotics trial in JAMA on 24 November 2026. Four Ontario paramedic services enrolled adults with suspected infection, fever and hypotension from 23 March 2020 to 20 August 2025. Patients received intramuscular ceftriaxone or placebo in the ambulance, with usual emergency-department care available in both groups. Trial report

Hospital mortality was 18.0% (182/1,009) with ceftriaxone and 18.4% (186/1,011) with placebo, giving a risk ratio of 0.97 (95% CI 0.81–1.17). Ambulance dosing advanced antibiotic treatment by roughly three hours on average. The interval leaves room for smaller clinically important effects, and the findings apply to this screening approach, drug and care setting.

SLAC remembers synchrotron pioneer Herman Winick

In a remembrance published 24 November 2026, SLAC reported that accelerator physicist Herman Winick died on 13 November at age 94. He led technical development of the Stanford synchrotron project’s first beamline, helped develop brighter X-ray sources using wigglers and undulators, and organized early study of the X-ray free-electron laser that became LCLS. With DESY’s Gus Voss, he initiated the project that became SESAME, the international synchrotron facility in Jordan that opened in May 2017. He shared the 2010 APS Andrei Sakharov Prize for defending scientists’ human rights. SLAC remembrance
