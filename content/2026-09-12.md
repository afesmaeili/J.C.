#### 1. ★★★ **IceCube neutrino point-source searches in the direction of the KM3NeT ultra-high-energy event**

**IceCube Collaboration — R. Abbasi et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.10931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10931)

IceCube uses **15 years of data** to search the direction of KM3-230213A, the extraordinary KM3NeT event with reconstructed energy around \(200\) PeV, under three temporal hypotheses: a steady source, a flare coincident with the KM3NeT event, and a transient occurring at an arbitrary time. They find **no evidence for neutrino emission in any search** and derive point-source flux limits. The important conclusion is that if KM3-230213A came from an astrophysical point source, the simplest interpretation in which that source produces an \(E^{-2}\)-like flux at the normalization inferred from the single KM3NeT event is now **strongly constrained**, both for steady and transient scenarios.

**Significance:** this significantly sharpens the astrophysical interpretation problem surrounding the KM3NeT event. The combination of one extraordinarily energetic KM3NeT neutrino and a long IceCube null exposure is becoming an interesting consistency test rather than merely an “extreme-energy neutrino” story.

**Classification:** **genuinely new experimental constraint.**

#### 2. ★★★ **Extended gamma-ray emission in the vicinity of Westerlund 1 and Kes 41 seen by Fermi-LAT**

**L. Tibaldo, R. Bernet, L. Härer, M. Lemoine-Goumard, L. Mohrmann, G. Peron, B. Reville, T. Vieu**  
[arXiv:2609.10176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10176)

This paper appeared in the preceding announcement and is included in this first journal-club report. Using **more than 16 years of Fermi-LAT data above \(0.8\) GeV**, the authors identify **three new extended, soft-spectrum gamma-ray components** in the Westerlund 1/Kes 41 region. One is associated with the natal cloud of Kes 41 and is statistically preferred over the previously used point-source description. Two others overlap neutral gas within roughly \(100\) pc of the edge of the Westerlund 1 superbubble.

There is an important caveat: they cannot yet cleanly distinguish **real locally injected CR populations** from residual mismodelling of the Galactic interstellar emission. If the components are physical, the Kes 41 emission requires \(\lesssim5\%\) of the SNR energy budget in accelerated particles, while the two Wd1 components correspond to a gamma-ray luminosity of roughly \(10^{-4}\) of the cluster-wind mechanical power.

**Significance:** this is precisely the sort of intermediate-scale CR-transport phenomenology that can alter how one interprets TeV–PeV emission and neutrino expectations from stellar-cluster environments.

**Classification:** **new observational result; interpretation not yet unique.**

<!-- paper:paper-2609-11833 -->

#### 3. ★★★ **Solar Neutrino Constraints on Inelastic Dark Matter Scattering in Light of Recent LUX-ZEPLIN Observations**

**Thong T. Q. Nguyen, Tim Linden, Dan Hooper**  
[arXiv:2609.11833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11833)

The recent LZ high-recoil candidate has encouraged DM models in which the recoil spectrum is strongly momentum-dependent or the scattering is inelastic, allowing a large high-energy recoil without producing the many low-energy events expected from conventional elastic WIMPs. Nguyen, Linden and Hooper point out an independent astroparticle constraint: such particles can be captured in the Sun, annihilate there, and generate **high-energy solar neutrinos**. IceCube sees no corresponding excess.

Their conclusion is that IceCube solar-neutrino observations **strongly constrain a substantial part of the inelastic-DM parameter space relevant to the LZ event**. This tests the recoil interpretation through an independent messenger.

For perspective, LZ itself reports only **2.6σ local significance**, approximately a \(0.5\%\) background probability, and explicitly does **not** claim a DM discovery.

**Significance:** a multimessenger connection between direct detection, solar capture and neutrino-telescope observations.

**Classification:** **new phenomenological constraint on interpretations of the LZ candidate.**

#### 4. ★★☆ **Low-Mass Magnetic Monopoles in the Galaxy: Simulations and Comparison with Ultra-High-Energy Cosmic-Ray Data**

**Ala’a AL-Zetoun, Igor Ostrovskiy, Peiwen Mi, Daniele Perri**  
[arXiv:2609.10784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10784)

The authors implement Galactic propagation of low-mass magnetic monopoles in a custom **CRPropa-based simulation with a realistic Galactic magnetic field**. Because magnetic monopoles are accelerated *along* magnetic-field structures rather than deflected in the ordinary \(q\,\mathbf v\times\mathbf B\) sense, their trajectories develop what the authors call **Galactic magnetic funnels**, producing strongly anisotropic arrival directions. They then compare the predicted directional/energy distributions with Pierre Auger data.

The data generally favor **zero monopole contribution**, and the profile-likelihood analysis yields a \(90\%\) C.L. integral flux limit below roughly

\[
2.1\times10^{-23}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}.
\]

**Significance:** beyond monopole phenomenology, the study develops methods for modelling a highly anisotropic exotic charged population propagating through the GMF.

**Classification:** **new simulation + Auger constraint; niche but technically interesting.**

#### 5. ★★☆ **EFT Approaches to Sommerfeld Enhancement and Bound States in Singular Potentials**

**Arindam Bhattacharya, Rashmish K. Mishra, Tracy R. Slatyer**  
[arXiv:2609.10690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10690)

The issue is that singular long-range potentials make the usual Schrödinger boundary-condition treatment of **Sommerfeld enhancement and bound-state formation** ambiguous. They attack the problem both with velocity power counting in nonrelativistic EFT and by explicitly regularizing the potential at short distance.

For a pseudoscalar mediator coupled to massive Dirac DM, an important result is that a **weakly coupled UV-complete theory need not generate Sommerfeld enhancement at all**. Conversely, derivative/higher-dimensional interactions can produce enhancement and bound states at weak coupling when there is a sufficiently large hierarchy between the EFT cutoff and \(m_\chi\). In that case the enhancement retains genuine UV sensitivity.

**Significance:** it is a useful warning against blindly inserting a singular potential into the Schrödinger equation and interpreting the resulting large enhancement as robust DM phenomenology.

**Classification:** **conceptual/methodological advance rather than a new observational result.**

#### 6. ★★☆ **Re-examining the sensitivity of JWST to decaying axion dark matter**

**Caleb Gemmell, Christopher Dessert, Andrea Caputo, Joshua W. Foster**  
[arXiv:2609.10674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10674)

This is a useful corrective paper. Different JWST searches for the infrared line from \(a\rightarrow\gamma\gamma\) had been claiming coupling sensitivities differing by roughly **an order of magnitude**. The authors redo the data reduction and use flexible non-parametric inference, concluding that some of the strongest previously claimed sensitivities are simply not achievable with the datasets used.

Their robust analysis nevertheless excludes QCD-axion DM over approximately

\[
m_a\simeq 0.5-2.5\ {\rm eV},
\]

and gives complementary ALP constraints at \(0.1-0.5\) eV. Importantly, they argue that the present analyses are approaching a **systematics floor**, so simply accumulating more exposure will not give the naive statistical improvement unless JWST instrumental modelling and reduction pipelines improve.

**Significance:** not “new physics discovered,” but exactly the kind of systematic reassessment that determines whether a spectacular exclusion plot is actually trustworthy.

**Classification:** **important re-analysis/systematics paper.**

#### 7. ★☆☆ **Ultralight Axial Dark Matter**

**Anson Hook, Junwu Huang, Kevin Zhou**  
[arXiv:2609.10671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10671)

They explore ultralight DM as an **axial vector field** rather than the usual scalar/axion or vector dark photon. Fermionic couplings generate spin torques that can be parametrically enhanced relative to axion DM. They also construct an analogue of the axion-photon coupling, though its consistent realization requires a photon mass, and discuss signatures involving polarimetry, superconducting cavities, electric fields and the CMB. The massive axial vector description is dual to a massive two-form/Kalb–Ramond field.

**Significance:** an expansion of the ultralight-DM model space with unusual experimental signatures.

**Classification:** **new theoretical framework/model-building.**

#### 8. ★☆☆ **Cosmological Evolution of Primordial Black Holes: UV/IR Decoupling and the KM3NeT 220 PeV Neutrino Anomaly**

**Debdatta Dey, Tausif Parvez, S. Shankaranarayanan**  
[arXiv:2609.11511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11511)

The proposal is that KM3-230213A came from the **terminal Hawking evaporation of a primordial black hole**. The authors embed the PBH in a McVittie cosmological spacetime: early-time cosmological accretion and expansion modify the PBH lifetime and initial mass required to explode today, while the final \(z\simeq0\) phase becomes essentially Schwarzschild. They argue that this can produce a large local \(\sim220\) PeV neutrino signal while avoiding excessive diffuse early-time emission.

This theoretical interpretation is **highly model-dependent**, and today's IceCube point-source paper further constrains source interpretations of KM3-230213A.

**Significance:** the paper explores an exotic origin for the KM3NeT event, with viability dependent on the underlying PBH and source assumptions.

**Classification:** **speculative interpretation.**

### Verified science news

#### ★★★ A genuinely new population of **“hypersoft X-ray sources”**

Muhibullah, Irwin and Di Stefano report in *Nature Astronomy* a previously largely missed population of luminous, point-like, non-nuclear objects emitting predominantly or exclusively **below \(0.3\) keV**. The brightest have narrow-band X-ray luminosities approaching \(10^{38}\) erg/s, while spectral modelling implies even larger bolometric luminosities dominated by the difficult-to-observe extreme-UV band.

NASA reports **84 such sources in six galaxies**, found retrospectively in Chandra archival data. Candidate physical systems include accreting white dwarfs/post-novae and accreting black-hole binaries. Because their EUV luminosity can be enormous, they might also provide a previously underestimated contribution to ionizing galactic gas.

[Nature Astronomy paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02959-7)  
[NASA/Chandra announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/chandra/nasas-chandra-unveils-mysterious-x-ray-objects/)

This one is **real peer-reviewed discovery territory**, not a press-release exaggeration.

#### ★★☆ Magnetars may constitute roughly **half of isolated neutron-star births/population**

A new *Nature Astronomy* population-synthesis study by Celsa Pardo-Araujo, Nanda Rea, Michele Ronchi and Vanessa Graber finds a surprisingly large magnetar fraction. Among observed Galactic neutron stars younger than \(2\) kyr, only **9 of 23** are ordinary rotation-powered pulsars; the remainder are magnetars or central compact objects. Their combined dynamical, spin-down and magneto-thermal population synthesis favors a bimodal birth-field distribution in which magnetars peak around

\[
B_0\sim(1-2.5)\times10^{14}\ {\rm G}
\]

and constitute on average roughly **50% of the isolated neutron-star population**. The model also prefers a Galactic core-collapse rate above two per century.

That is astrophysically nontrivial because magnetar birth rates enter models for **GRBs, superluminous SNe, FRBs and other engine-powered transients**.

[Nature Astronomy paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02915-5)

#### ★★☆ IceCube: no significant neutrino signal from a sample of 89 tidal-disruption events

IceCube has released a dedicated TDE search using **89 optically/UV/X-ray selected TDEs**. They perform both source-by-source time-dependent searches and stacking analyses of jetted and non-jetted populations. **No statistically significant neutrino excess is found**, and the result instead tightens limits on the contribution of these TDE populations to the diffuse astrophysical neutrino intensity.

This is useful negative information: TDEs remain viable in some corners of parameter space, but ordinary optically/X-ray selected TDE populations are increasingly constrained as the dominant origin of IceCube's diffuse flux.

[IceCube collaboration summary](https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/research/2026/09/search-for-neutrino-emission-from-tidal-disruption-events/)  
[arXiv:2609.00657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00657)
