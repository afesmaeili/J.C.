#### ★★★ 1. **Time-Integrated Searches for Sub-TeV Neutrino Sources with IceCube-DeepCore**

**IceCube Collaboration — R. Abbasi et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.12055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12055)

IceCube has developed a dedicated **sub-TeV point-source analysis using 11.1 years of DeepCore data**, extending source searches down into an energy range that ordinary IceCube point-source analyses do not exploit efficiently. The motivation is especially strong for sources with soft spectra.

They perform three searches: X-ray-bright AGN selected with Swift/BAT, including **NGC 1068**; Galactic Fermi-LAT sources whose spectra look compatible with \(\pi^0\) decay; and an all-sky point-source search. They also combine individual source \(p\)-values with a binomial test to look for weak population-level emission. No statistically significant sub-TeV signal appears in any of them.

For NGC 1068, assuming

\[
\frac{d\Phi_\nu}{dE}\propto E^{-3.4},
\]

they obtain

\[
\left.\Phi_{\nu+\bar\nu}\right|_{1\,{\rm TeV}}
<
9.5\times10^{-11}
\ {\rm TeV^{-1}\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}
\]

per flavor at 90% C.L., probing approximately **30–400 GeV**. Importantly, that upper limit is still about a factor of two *above* the extrapolation of IceCube's higher-energy NGC 1068 measurement, so it does **not yet rule out** a simple continuation of the measured soft component.

**Significance:** this provides an observational input for multimessenger modelling of NGC 1068. It begins constraining what the neutrino spectrum can do below the existing IceCube measurement and therefore interfaces naturally with MeV–GeV electromagnetic constraints and source-opacity/cascade models.

**Assessment:** **genuinely new experimental result.**

#### ★★★ 2. **Hidden structures in the UHE sky: unveiling potential sources via spectral signatures**

**Mateus Zeferino Rennó, Cainã de Oliveira**  
[arXiv:2609.12174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12174)

This is conceptually simple but potentially quite useful.

Most UHECR source searches ask whether some angular region contains **more particles than expected**,

\[
N(\hat n)>N_{\rm iso}(\hat n).
\]

But a real source contribution could alter the *local energy distribution* without producing an obvious flux excess, particularly after magnetic smearing.

The authors construct a directional functional \(\mu_E\) of the energy spectrum specifically designed to separate

\[
\text{flux normalization}
\qquad\text{from}\qquad
\text{spectral shape}.
\]

They then generalize the **Li–Ma significance** formalism to such energy-weighted observables and test the statistic in Monte Carlo. Hard-spectrum patches that are essentially invisible in conventional event-count maps become detectable with \(\mu_E\).

That idea is interesting because magnetic deflections can smear angular overdensities while still allowing a direction-dependent spectrum to survive.

**Significance:** it is complementary to conventional UHECR anisotropy statistics and particularly relevant to the general question of how much source information survives GMF/EGMF smearing. Comparison with composition-sensitive and entropy/information-based approaches could help establish the statistic's complementary value.

**Assessment:** **new methodology; potentially useful rather than yet experimentally decisive.**

#### ★★★ 3. **Constraining dark matter using 20-year INTEGRAL/IBIS observations I: Primordial black holes**

**Jordan Koechler, Pedro De la Torre Luque**  
[arXiv:2609.12044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12044)

This is a nice astroparticle-analysis paper.

Low-mass PBHs emit \(e^\pm\) through Hawking evaporation. Those electrons and positrons subsequently inverse-Compton scatter the Galactic radiation field,

\[
e^\pm+\gamma_{\rm ISRF}\rightarrow e^\pm+\gamma ,
\]

creating diffuse hard X-rays.

The authors use **20 years of INTEGRAL/IBIS observations** and, importantly, do not rely only on the energy spectrum. They perform a combined **spectral + morphological analysis**, modelling the conventional Galactic hard-X-ray sky with cosmic-ray-electron IC emission plus unresolved accreting white dwarfs and adding the PBH-induced IC morphology.

They obtain competitive constraints on the PBH DM fraction for monochromatic, extended and rotating-PBH mass functions. Their dominant theoretical uncertainty is not Hawking physics itself but the Galactic transport of the relatively low-energy \(e^\pm\) responsible for the IC emission.

That is an important methodological point:

\[
\text{particle-physics injection}
\longrightarrow e^\pm\ {\rm propagation}
\longrightarrow {\rm IC}
\longrightarrow {\rm diffuse\ morphology}.
\]

**Significance:** besides the PBH result itself, the spectral+morphological separation of signal from Galactic diffuse emission is highly relevant to any MeV/hard-X-ray multimessenger analysis.

**Assessment:** **genuinely new search strategy and observational constraint.**

#### ★★★ 4. **Supernova cooling from neutrinophilic dark matter**

**Yugen Lin**  
[arXiv:2609.12821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12821)

This paper considers light DM whose dominant coupling is to neutrinos and asks whether it can be produced inside a core-collapse SN through neutrino–neutrino reactions.

Because the proto-neutron-star core contains an enormous neutrino density, even extremely weak interactions can efficiently populate a new light sector. If the produced DM escapes, it adds a new cooling channel.

Using modern supernova simulation profiles, the paper derives bounds on effective \(\nu\)-DM interactions for sub-GeV DM. The striking numerical claim is that for

\[
m_\chi\lesssim {\cal O}(100)\ {\rm MeV},
\]

the SN cooling limits on the reference DM–neutrino cross section can improve existing indirect-detection limits by **more than ten orders of magnitude**.

The physics is essentially the usual SN energy-loss argument, but with the very large thermal neutrino bath acting as the production environment rather than nucleons/electrons.

**Significance:** it connects supernova neutrino transport directly to light-dark-sector phenomenology and illustrates how dramatically SN1987A-like arguments can outperform terrestrial/indirect searches for genuinely neutrinophilic states.

**Assessment:** **new BSM constraint; quite interesting.**

#### ★★☆ 5. **Probing Neutrinophilic Axion-Like Particles in Tritium Beta Decay**

**Yi Chung, Florian Goertz, Maya Hager, Joscha Lauer**  
[arXiv:2609.12942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12942)

Here the new particle is a keV-scale ALP coupling preferentially to neutrinos. Such a state modifies the ordinary tritium spectrum measured by KATRIN and potentially by **TRISTAN**.

The authors calculate the spectral distortions for both lepton-number-conserving and lepton-number-violating interactions and derive updated laboratory constraints. Ordinarily cosmology wins over beta-decay experiments, but they emphasize an important assumption behind that comparison: the particle and its interactions must exist in essentially the same form in the early Universe.

They therefore construct an explicit pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone realization with a nontrivial symmetry-breaking thermal history in which the usual cosmological constraints can be evaded. In that case,

\[
{\rm KATRIN/TRISTAN}
\]

can become the leading probe.

**Significance:** good example of the often-overlooked distinction between a powerful cosmological constraint and a genuinely model-independent laboratory constraint.

**Assessment:** **interesting neutrino/ALP phenomenology.**

#### ★★☆ 6. **Leptogenesis Determined By Low Energy Parameters**

**Xiao-Gang He, Zhong-Lv Huang, Raymond R. Volkas, Yu-Qi Xiao**  
[arXiv:2609.12542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12542)

This attacks the usual problem with type-I seesaw leptogenesis: the heavy-neutrino sector normally contains enough freedom that low-energy neutrino observables provide little information about baryogenesis.

They instead impose three predictive relations,

\[
M_D=M_u,\qquad M_D=M_d,\qquad \text{or}\qquad M_D=M_\ell,
\]

so the seesaw equation allows the heavy Majorana matrix to be reconstructed from low-energy parameters.

They evolve the flavored asymmetries using **density-matrix Boltzmann equations**. Viable thermal leptogenesis occurs for normal ordering with nonzero Majorana phases in the down-quark-like and charged-lepton-like constructions. Successful points favor a close heavy-neutrino pair,

\[
\frac{|M_i-M_j|}{M_i}<10^{-3},
\]

although the authors stress that it is not the conventional resonant-leptogenesis limit.

The interesting experimental payoff is \(0\nu\beta\beta\): some successful leptogenesis regions predict

\[
m_{\beta\beta}
\]

within reach of future **sub-10-meV** experiments such as LEGEND-1000, nEXO and CUPID-1T.

**Significance:** this is exactly the kind of seesaw construction where cosmological baryogenesis and measurable low-energy neutrino observables cease to be almost completely disconnected.

**Assessment:** **good neutrino-pheno paper; model-dependent but predictive.**

#### ★★☆ 7. **Constraints on Galactic Dark Photons from a DALI Prototype**

**Javier De Miguel et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.12775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12775)

A straightforward but clean experimental result.

The DALI haloscope prototype analyzed **36 hours** of data around

\[
\nu\simeq6.9~{\rm GHz},
\]

corresponding to a dark-photon mass near

\[
m_{A'}=28.54~\mu{\rm eV}.
\]

No statistically significant excess is observed, giving approximately

\[
\chi\lesssim6.8\times10^{-14},
\]

which the authors report as the strongest laboratory exclusion in that narrow frequency range.

**Significance:** not transformative, but a real experimental improvement in ultralight-vector DM parameter space rather than another forecast paper.

**Assessment:** **incremental but solid experimental result.**

#### ★★☆ 8. **Probing Lorentz Invariance Violation in Cosmogenic Neutrino Propagation with KM3-230213A**

**Rodrigo Sasse, Rodrigo Guedes Lang, Rita de Cássia dos Anjos**  
[arXiv:2609.12120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12120)

This one sits almost exactly at the intersection of UHECR propagation and UHE neutrino phenomenology.

They generate standard cosmogenic neutrino spectra using **CRPropa 3.2**, then introduce superluminal neutrino LIV producing neutrino splitting. The basic spectral effect is

\[
{\rm LIV}\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
\text{suppression at the highest energies},\\
\text{pile-up/enhancement at PeV--EeV energies}.
\end{cases}
\]

Using KM3-230213A as a benchmark, they find sensitivity to dimensionful LIV coefficients roughly in the range

\[
10^{-24}\lesssim
\delta_{\nu,1}/{\rm eV^{-1}}
\lesssim10^{-22}.
\]

Larger values can generate too many neutrinos in energy intervals already constrained by IceCube and Auger.

The important caveat is explicitly acknowledged by the authors: **one KM3NeT event does not permit a formal LIV constraint**, and the inferred sensitivity depends strongly on the assumed UHECR source population.

**Significance:** the study connects UHECR propagation to cosmogenic-neutrino phenomenology, but its physical conclusion remains exploratory rather than a formal bound.

**Assessment:** **interesting/speculative phenomenology.**

### Verified science news

#### Important correction: **GRB 260913A was not a GRB**

This update corrects the alert covered in the previous report.

SVOM initially triggered at **07:38:21 UTC on 13 September** on what appeared to be a faint \(8-50\) keV burst with image S/N \(=8.29\).

After the full telemetry was downloaded and reprocessed, however, the SVOM team issued GCN Circular 45561 this morning:

> the trigger was **not astrophysical**.

The apparent signal was **coding noise caused by the Crab being in the ECLAIRs field of view**. SVOM has therefore withdrawn the GRB interpretation.

[Official SVOM retraction — GCN 45561](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45561)

So yesterday's **GRB 260913A should be removed from the astrophysical transient watch list entirely**. Rapid-alert claims therefore remain provisional until refined telemetry becomes available.

#### ★★☆ GRB 260818A now has a fading radio counterpart consistent with an afterglow

A more genuine transient development comes from the older **GRB 260818A**.

New ATCA observations at \(T_0+25.5\) days detect the radio source at both 6.53 and 10.37 GHz. Comparing with the earlier \(T_0+12.4\)-day epoch, the emission declined by approximately

\[
14\% \quad (6.53~{\rm GHz})
\]

and

\[
36\% \quad (10.37~{\rm GHz}).
\]

The two-point spectral index evolved from roughly

\[
\alpha=-0.78\pm0.41
\]

to

\[
\alpha=-1.40\pm0.54,
\qquad S_\nu\propto\nu^\alpha ,
\]

consistent with optically thin synchrotron emission and spectral steepening of a fading GRB afterglow. The authors appropriately label the fluxes preliminary because absolute calibration systematics have not yet been included.

[ATCA follow-up — GCN 45560](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45560)

Interesting follow-up, but **not an exceptional multimessenger event** at present.

#### ★★☆ FRBs are becoming a surprisingly competitive probe of baryonic feedback

One recent result from outside the immediate high-energy stream is worth flagging.

A *Nature Astronomy* paper published **8 September** uses **114 localized fast radio bursts** to infer fluctuations in the cosmic baryon distribution and hence the suppression of the matter power spectrum caused by galaxy feedback over roughly

\[
k\simeq0.1-3~h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}.
\]

Despite the modest sample size, the resulting baryon-feedback constraints are already competitive with legacy **ACT and eROSITA** measurements.

This matters beyond FRB astrophysics because baryonic feedback is a serious nuisance for weak-lensing cosmology. Better independent measurements of that suppression reduce degeneracies with quantities such as

\[
\sum m_\nu,\qquad
w_0,\qquad
w_a,
\]

and dark-matter clustering. Their forecasts show substantial gains once future FRB samples are combined with galaxy clustering and weak lensing.

[Nature Astronomy paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02957-9)  
[Caltech summary](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/fast-radio-bursts-poised-to-help-with-biggest-cosmic-mysteries)

**This is not a discovery of new physics**, but it is a genuinely interesting new use of FRBs as precision-cosmology instruments.
