### ArXiv highlights

#### ★★★ Measurement of Cosmic-Ray Density in the Spiral Arms toward Galactic Anticenter

**Jia-hao Liu, Bing Liu, Rui-zhi Yang**
[arXiv:2609.14955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14955)

Using almost **17 years of Fermi-LAT data**, they isolate diffuse gamma-ray emission associated with the Local, Perseus and Outer spiral arms over \(105^\circ<l<145^\circ\). All three components show a clear **pion bump**, so the inferred emissivity is directly tracing the underlying CR-proton population rather than relying on a purely phenomenological gamma-ray spectrum.

They obtain proton spectral indices approximately
\[
\Gamma_p\simeq
2.75\quad{\rm Local},\qquad
2.55\quad{\rm Perseus},\qquad
2.80\quad{\rm Outer},
\]
with CR energy densities above \(10\) GeV of order
\[
u_{\rm CR}\sim0.2~{\rm eV\,cm^{-3}}
\]
in all three arms. The important tension is that the outer-Galaxy CR population comes out **denser and harder than standard propagation models usually predict** as Galactocentric radius increases. Possible explanations include a larger CR halo, slower radial decline of the magnetic field, or local acceleration—especially in the Perseus arm.

**Significance:** it is a clean observational handle on the radial dependence of Galactic CR transport, with obvious implications for diffuse gamma rays and Galactic-neutrino modelling.

**Classification:** **genuinely useful observational CR result.**

#### ★★★ The Loud Tail of the Supermassive Black Hole Binary Population: Multimessenger Candidates and Prospects for SKAO

**Juhan Raidal, Ville Vaskonen, Juan Urrutia, Hardi Veermäe**
[arXiv:2609.13363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13363)

They start from SMBH-binary population models fitted to the **NANOGrav 15-year stochastic-background result** and ask which already-known electromagnetic SMBH-binary candidates should populate the rare high-strain tail strongly enough to become individually resolvable PTA sources.

Two objects stand out: **3C 66B and Mrk 501**. In their population realization these systems have a good chance of rising above the unresolved stochastic background. The result is especially interesting because individual detections would discriminate between binary-evolution scenarios: strong environmental hardening changes the abundance of resolvable systems relative to a purely GW-driven inspiral population.

For the SKA era, after including confusion noise from the unresolved SMBH population, they forecast **tens of individually detectable binaries**, with 3C 66B particularly robust.

**Significance:** this is genuine **multimessenger source-population physics**—connecting radio/AGN binary candidates to PTA gravitational waves rather than treating the GW background only statistically.

**Classification:** **new multimessenger population forecast; conceptually strong.**

#### ★★★ Probe of Solar Neutrino Magnetic Moments through Spin-Flavor Precession: Resonance Structure and Antineutrino Appearance

**Pouya Bakhti, Sudip Jana, Chui-Fan Kong, Seodong Shin, Seokhoon Yun**
[arXiv:2609.14446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14446)

They perform the spin-flavor-precession problem with the full
\[
6\times6
\]
neutrino/antineutrino Hamiltonian and realistic GS98 and AGSS09 solar profiles.

For **Majorana neutrinos**, a transition magnetic moment can generate
\[
\nu_\alpha\rightarrow\bar\nu_\beta ,
\]
whereas for Dirac neutrinos the flipped state is sterile. A useful technical result is that in normal ordering they find **no finite-density Majorana level crossing** for \(1\!-\!20\) MeV solar neutrinos. A Dirac resonance appears above about \(12\) MeV, but it involves only a small \(\nu_e\) component, so the resonant disappearance signal is limited.

The more interesting signature is therefore **nonresonant Majorana conversion producing solar \(\bar\nu_e\)**. For a future 3-kton Jinping detector operating for \(5\!-\!10\) years they estimate sensitivity to
\[
P(\nu_e\rightarrow\bar\nu_e)
\sim(0.85-1.3)\times10^{-5}.
\]
Assuming optimistic transverse core fields \(B_\perp\sim7\!-\!10\) MG, this corresponds in their \(\mu_{12}\)-only example to
\[
|\mu_{12}|\sim(2.3-4.1)\times10^{-13}\mu_B .
\]

**Significance:** it gives an explicit three-flavor treatment of the distinction between **Dirac disappearance and Majorana antineutrino appearance**, which is much more informative than a simple magnetic-moment bound.

**Classification:** **good neutrino-phenomenology paper; projected rather than observed result.**

#### ★★★ Consistent treatment of flavour-specific neutrino self-interactions in cosmology

**Gabriela Barenboim, Marco Drewes, Isabel M. Oldengott**
[arXiv:2609.13821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13821)

This paper addresses a subtlety that is often glossed over in cosmological constraints on secret neutrino interactions: if the new interaction acts only on a particular **flavor**, neutrino oscillations/mixing mean that one cannot simply evolve three independent flavor fluids.

Starting from the neutrino **quantum kinetic equations**, they derive the Boltzmann hierarchy in the neutrino-mass basis and determine when a simpler combined-fluid approximation is valid.

Their practical result is neat. Existing CMB limits on an effective neutrino self-coupling should be weakened by approximately
\[
\sqrt{2}
\]
when two flavors interact and by approximately
\[
\sqrt{6}
\]
when only one flavor interacts. The \(\nu_e\)-only case is the one where the fluid approximation becomes somewhat less reliable.

**Significance:** this is one of those papers that can quietly change how constraints are recast across a large class of neutrino-BSM models; the correction is not orders of magnitude, but it is conceptually important.

**Classification:** **methodological improvement to cosmological neutrino constraints.**

#### ★★★ Less isn't more: Cosmological bounds on the neutrino masses are robust to changes in the neutrino abundance

**Sebastian J. Figueroa, Alexander C. Sobotka, Adrienne L. Erickcek**
[arXiv:2609.15970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15970)

This asks a timely question: can the increasingly aggressive cosmological limit on
\[
\sum m_\nu
\]
be evaded by changing the relic neutrino abundance through a nonstandard post-BBN thermal history?

They explicitly model late particle decays injecting photons and/or dark radiation, and then perform MCMC analyses using **Planck primary CMB, Planck+ACT lensing and DESI BAO**. Surprisingly, reducing the neutrino abundance does **not** substantially weaken the mass bound.

For degenerate masses they quote
\[
\sum m_\nu <0.0691~{\rm eV}
\]
for the standard thermal history,
\[
<0.0652~{\rm eV}
\]
for photon-only injection, and
\[
<0.0710~{\rm eV}
\]
when dark radiation is also produced. Even allowing the neutrino/photon temperature ratio to vary phenomenologically gives only
\[
\sum m_\nu<0.0724~{\rm eV}
\]
at 95% credibility.

The underlying reason is interesting: \(T_\nu\) and \(\sum m_\nu\) come out **positively correlated** once the full cosmological dataset is used.

**Significance:** the emerging tension between cosmological neutrino-mass bounds and laboratory oscillation mass splittings cannot apparently be dismissed simply by saying “perhaps the relic neutrino density is smaller.”

**Classification:** **important robustness check.**

#### ★★☆ First Light of Neutron Star Mergers: Off-axis Cocoon Cooling X-ray Emission from Short Gamma-Ray Burst Jets

**Jian-He Zheng, Wenbin Lu**
[arXiv:2609.14508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14508)

The problem is straightforward: short-GRB jets are strongly beamed, so most neutron-star mergers will not show prompt gamma rays to an off-axis observer. The authors investigate whether the **jet cocoon** provides a less-beamed early counterpart.

Hydrodynamic simulations plus radiative post-processing give a mildly relativistic cocoon producing soft quasi-thermal X-rays with
\[
L_X\sim10^{46}-10^{48}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}},
\qquad
kT\sim0.1-1~{\rm keV},
\]
lasting a few to roughly ten seconds.

For viewing angles around \(10^\circ\), Einstein Probe could see the signal to \(z\sim0.3\). For a GW170817-like nearby event, detectability may extend to roughly \(45^\circ\). Their fiducial Einstein Probe rate is about
\[
0.5~{\rm yr}^{-1}.
\]

**Significance:** a genuinely useful possible **early X-ray trigger for off-axis GW mergers**, preceding the kilonova and conventional afterglow.

**Classification:** **multimessenger prediction, not yet observational confirmation.**

#### ★★☆ First results on the search for the Galactic Center Excess in the sub-GeV band with the emulsion telescope in GRAINE 2023

**Yuya Nakamura et al.**
[arXiv:2609.14551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14551)

GRAINE is interesting because the balloon-borne emulsion gamma-ray telescope reaches angular resolutions of about
\[
1^\circ\ {\rm at}\ 100~{\rm MeV},
\qquad
0.1^\circ\ {\rm at}\ 1~{\rm GeV},
\]
making the difficult sub-GeV Galactic-center region less confusion-limited than Fermi in some respects.

Rather than looking at the familiar \(\sim2\) GeV GCE peak, they deliberately examine
\[
75-300~{\rm MeV},
\]
where predicted spectra from millisecond-pulsar and DM-annihilation interpretations diverge more strongly.

They see **no significant excess** and obtain a \(2\sigma\) upper limit
\[
E^2\Phi_{\gamma}<1.70\times10^{-7}
\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}
\]
for a \(1^\circ\)-radius Galactic-center ROI. Current statistics still allow both the DM and MSP interpretations.

**Significance:** the important part is not today's limit itself, which is still weak, but the ability to obtain **high-angular-resolution sub-GeV imaging** precisely where the GCE models become spectrally distinguishable.

**Classification:** **first instrumental result; interesting but not yet constraining enough to settle the GCE.**

<!-- paper:paper-2609-15985 -->

#### ★★☆ Inferring dark matter masses and interactions from high recoil energy events in LUX-ZEPLIN

**Stefano Palmisano, Michele Tammaro, Andrea Tesi**
[arXiv:2609.15985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15985)

There is now a flood of papers trying to model-build around the LZ \(248\) keV recoil candidate. This one is more useful than most because it is relatively **model-independent**.

They infer the posterior directly in the space of DM masses, mass splittings and effective nuclear interactions, retaining exact two-body kinematics. The single high-recoil event plus the lack of lower-energy events drives the solution into disconnected kinematic regimes.

Generic interactions favor a finely tuned **endothermic heavy-WIMP region**, while exothermic scattering permits masses ranging from sub-GeV to heavy DM with large splittings. Purely elastic interpretations survive only with momentum suppression and/or spin-dependent operators.

**Significance:** the inference characterizes what the LZ candidate implies without committing to a specific \(U(1)'\), SUSY or freeze-in construction.

**Classification:** **useful inference paper, but the underlying LZ anomaly is still weak; not evidence for DM.**

An additional methodological paper is **“A Unified Numerical Study of Axion Stars: From the Nonrelativistic Regime to General Relativity”** by Arabameri, Arias, Colipí-Marchant and Schiappacasse. They benchmark Schrödinger–Poisson, relativistic EFT and full Einstein–Klein–Gordon calculations against one another and show quantitatively where the usual nonrelativistic axion-star description breaks down. This is a reference and methodological study.

### Verified science news

#### ★★★ ATLAS finds **3.4σ evidence for \(H\rightarrow\gamma^\ast\gamma\rightarrow\ell\ell\gamma\)**

This was officially announced by ATLAS on **14 September** and is the strongest general particle-physics news item since yesterday.

The decay is exceptionally rare—roughly **one in \(10^4\) Higgs decays**—and proceeds through a loop-induced virtual photon,
\[
H\rightarrow\gamma^\ast\gamma,\qquad
\gamma^\ast\rightarrow e^+e^-~{\rm or}~\mu^+\mu^-.
\]

ATLAS combines its full Run-2 sample with the first three years of Run 3. The measured signal strength is
\[
\mu=1.03\pm0.34
\]
times the Standard-Model prediction, replacing an earlier Run-2 fluctuation that had sat somewhat above the SM expectation. The background-only significance is now **3.4σ**, which qualifies as evidence rather than observation.

The channel is interesting beyond simply finding another Higgs decay because the virtual photon gives access to low dilepton masses and potentially new information on **CP structure and loop-level new physics**.

[ATLAS official briefing](https://atlas.cern/Updates/Briefing/Rare-Light-Higgs-Decay)

#### ★★☆ CMS revives hard diffraction with **tagged intact protons**

CMS reported yesterday the **first LHC observations of W and Z bosons accompanied by a proton verified to have survived the same collision intact**, and the first proton-tagged multijet sample extending to TeV invariant masses.

Using the Precision Proton Spectrometer, CMS finds that approximately **1% of selected W/Z events** and about **0.5% of jet events** contain a correlated tagged proton. The direction of that forward proton is correlated with the central hard system, which separates the signal statistically from random pile-up protons.

This opens a rather nice testing ground for hard diffraction—Pomeron/Reggeon exchange, photon exchange and proton-dissociation descriptions—under LHC high-pileup conditions where this physics had been experimentally very difficult.

[CMS official result](https://cms.cern/news/needle-pileup-cms-revives-diffractive-physics-tagged-protons)

#### Transient watch: two real GRBs, but nothing exceptional yet

A genuinely fresh event is **GRB 260915A**, triggered by SVOM/ECLAIRs at **01:12:03 UTC today**. The ECLAIRs image significance is 21.38 in \(8-120\) keV, SVOM slewed promptly, and MXT detected a previously uncatalogued X-ray source 159 s after trigger. Fermi-GBM did not trigger onboard but later recovered a spatially consistent **sub-threshold signal**, strengthening the identification as a real GRB.

Meanwhile **GRB 260914A** now has a likely host redshift from VLT/X-shooter:
\[
z=0.778.
\]
The spectrum shows H\(\alpha\), H\(\beta\), [O III] and [O II] at that common redshift. The chance-alignment probability is only about 0.3%, though the team appropriately notes a nearby \(z=0.112\) galaxy, so the association is strong but not mathematically conclusive.

Neither event currently has an unusual high-energy property or reported **IceCube/LVK counterpart**, so both remain ordinary transient-watch events. The current GCN feed likewise shows **no new IceCube neutrino or LVK gravitational-wave alert** among the latest circulars.

### Scientific perspective

The combination of a resolved pion bump, arm-by-arm proton spectra and unexpectedly hard/dense outer-Galaxy CR populations directly probes assumptions in GALPROP-like transport models. The two cosmological-neutrino papers test whether commonly used neutrino constraints are being interpreted consistently.
