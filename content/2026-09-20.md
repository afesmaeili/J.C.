### Astrophysical Alerts

#### ★★★ IceCube-260919A: new Gold high-energy neutrino alert

IceCube detected a track-like event at **06:52:15.58 UTC on 19 September**. It passed the **Gold Astrotrack** selection, indicating a higher estimated astrophysical purity than Bronze alerts.

The refined direction is

\[
\mathrm{RA}=111.05^{+0.31}_{-0.23}\!^\circ,\qquad
\mathrm{Dec}=+6.43^{+0.27}_{-0.28}\!^\circ ,
\]

where the uncertainties describe the 90% directional containment. The estimated atmospheric-background false-alarm rate is

\[
\mathrm{FAR}=0.5044~\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.
\]

No source in the Fermi-LAT 16-year catalogue lies inside the 90% localization region. [Official IceCube circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45645)

MASTER observed the field approximately 19 hours later and reported no optical transient, reaching an unfiltered limiting magnitude of about 18.1. This follow-up is relatively shallow and late, so it constrains only bright, slowly fading counterparts. [MASTER follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45651)

**Status:** the Gold alert remains a single neutrino candidate without an identified electromagnetic counterpart or established source association.

#### Transient watch: EP260920a

Einstein Probe detected a previously uncatalogued soft-X-ray transient beginning at **00:00:54 UTC on 20 September**. Its average WXT spectrum is described by a power law with photon index

\[
\Gamma=2.61^{+0.71}_{-0.64},
\]

and an unabsorbed \(0.5\!-\!4\) keV flux of

\[
F_X=1.94^{+0.68}_{-0.50}\times10^{-10}
\ \mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
\]

The automatically triggered FXT observation localized the source to a 10-arcsecond radius and found a declining \(0.5\!-\!10\) keV flux averaging \(5.40\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). [Einstein Probe refined analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45655)

An initially reported optical candidate is doubtful: it lies about 15 arcseconds from the refined X-ray position, outside the 90% FXT region, and may have been an image-subtraction artifact caused by mismatched filters. Deeper COLIBRÍ and SVOM observations found no credible counterpart down to approximately \(r>23.5\!-\!24.0\). [COLIBRÍ follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45654), [SVOM/VT limits](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45657)

**Status:** genuine fading X-ray transient, but its astrophysical class remains unknown and no secure optical counterpart has been established.

Fermi-GBM also triggered on the likely long **GRB 260920A** at 10:32:24 UTC on 20 September. Its reported localization is degree-scale, with no exceptional high-energy or multimessenger property yet reported. [Fermi-GBM circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45658)

### Science News

#### ★★★ First strong evidence for entanglement between two \(Z\) bosons

The results were officially announced on **17 September 2026**.

ATLAS and CMS have independently studied the polarization correlations of the two \(Z\) bosons in

\[
H\rightarrow ZZ^{*}\rightarrow4\ell ,
\]

using the angular distributions of the final-state electrons and muons to reconstruct the spin-density matrix. Because a \(Z\) boson has three spin projections, the pair constitutes a system of two spin qutrits rather than the two-state spin system previously studied with top quarks.

ATLAS reports that a hypothesis in which the two \(Z\)-boson spin states are separable is disfavored by **4.7 standard deviations** relative to the entangled Standard-Model hypothesis. The measured angular coefficients are consistent with the Standard Model. [ATLAS PRL paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/y1nh-1b82)

CMS uses \(138~\mathrm{fb^{-1}}\) of Run-2 data and \(62~\mathrm{fb^{-1}}\) from 2022–2023. Its polarization-density-matrix analysis establishes an entangled qutrit state under the assumptions of quantum mechanics and CP conservation; it also finds conditions under which a Bell-type inequality could be tested with larger datasets. [CMS preliminary analysis](https://cms-results.web.cern.ch/cms-results/public-results/preliminary-results/HIG-25-011/index.html)

**Literature context:** ATLAS and CMS had previously observed spin entanglement in \(t\bar t\) pairs at greater than \(5\sigma\). The new result changes the particle system from two spin-\(\tfrac12\) fermions to two massive spin-1 gauge bosons and uses the Higgs as the common quantum source. It is consequently the first experimental evidence for entanglement between two \(Z\) bosons. [Previous top-quark measurements](https://home.cern/lhc-experiments-cern-observe-quantum-entanglement-highest-energy-yet/)

The main caveat is conceptual: these collider analyses infer entanglement statistically from decay-product distributions and depend on assumptions about the production and decay dynamics. They are not loophole-free Bell experiments testing locality in the same sense as separated low-energy quantum systems. [CERN announcement](https://home.cern/strong-evidence-for-quantum-entanglement-between-z-bosons-found-by-atlas-and-cms/)
