### Verified science news

#### ★★☆ GRB 260924A: IceCube finds no coincident high-energy neutrinos

IceCube searched for track-like muon neutrinos around the exceptionally energetic, high-redshift GRB 260924A. No events were found during the prompt window extending from one hour before to two hours after the Fermi-GBM trigger at **01:13:23.56 UTC on 24 September**.

For an assumed \(E^{-2}\) spectrum, the resulting 90%-confidence limit is

\[
E^2\frac{dN_{\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu}}{dE}
<3.5\times10^{-2}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}},
\]

with the central 90% detector-response interval spanning approximately \(0.94\) TeV–\(1.6\) PeV. A separate two-day search also found no candidate; its post-trial probability is \(p=1.0\), with a limit of \(4.1\times10^{-2}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}}\). ([IceCube circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45768))

Konus-Wind independently confirms that this was among the most energetic known long GRBs. Its two prompt episodes yielded a \(20\ {\rm keV}-10\ {\rm MeV}\) fluence of

\[
(3.83^{+0.43}_{-0.40})\times10^{-4}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}},
\]

with emission detected to roughly 8 MeV. At the reported redshift \(z=3.510\), this corresponds to

\[
E_{\rm iso}=(1.00\pm0.11)\times10^{55}\ {\rm erg},
\qquad
L_{\rm iso}=(1.30\pm0.19)\times10^{54}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}}.
\]

Despite its enormous energy, the burst lies within the 68% regions of the empirical Amati and Yonetoku relations rather than being a clear spectral–energetic outlier. These values remain preliminary. ([Konus-Wind analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45751))

##### History

Earlier IceCube stacking analyses already placed stronger population-level restrictions on efficient prompt-neutrino production in classical GRB fireballs ([IceCube 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4219)). The present result is instead a source-specific constraint on an unusually luminous burst. Its scientific value is the combination of extreme electromagnetic energetics with a clean neutrino null result; it does not materially tighten global GRB-neutrino bounds. The large luminosity distance at \(z=3.51\) strongly suppresses the expected neutrino fluence, while the numerical limit depends on the assumed \(E^{-2}\) spectrum and is principally sensitive to TeV–PeV emission within the chosen time windows. Delayed, lower-energy, or strongly flavor-dependent components remain essentially unconstrained.
