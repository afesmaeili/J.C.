#### ★★★ 1. Global Spline Fit: A unified data-driven view of the cosmic-ray spectrum and mass composition from GeV to the highest energies

**Anatoli Fedynitch, Kozo Fujisue, Hans Dembinski, Ralph Engel**  
[arXiv:2609.32649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32649)

The authors combine elemental AMS-02, CALET and DAMPE spectra, LHAASO knee-region measurements, and Auger fluorescence composition into a single nonparametric fit from GeV energies to above \(10^{20}\) eV. Four leading mass groups are represented by cubic splines; experimental energy scales, solar modulation and correlated uncertainties are fitted simultaneously.

Approximately one thousand measurements are described with \(\chi^2/{\rm ndf}\simeq1.3\), falling to 0.83 after localized dataset tensions are down-weighted. The derived all-nucleon flux differs from widely used parametrizations by \(20\!-\!50\%\) over four decades in energy—larger than the remaining fit uncertainty and therefore potentially important for atmospheric-neutrino and muon calculations.

##### History

The authors’ [2017 Global Spline Fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11432) pioneered the same data-driven combination and explicit energy-scale alignment. The present work is a major modernization: it incorporates the precision spaceborne spectra that exposed multiple spectral breaks, LHAASO’s element-separated knee measurements, Auger’s updated composition, solar modulation, and a more useful covariance/reduced representation.

The practical advance is stronger than merely updating a plot: the large change in the inferred nucleon flux means atmospheric-background calculations based on older H3a/GST-like parameterizations should be revisited. It is still a phenomenological world average, not a source or transport model. Air-shower composition retains hadronic-interaction dependence, four broad mass groups lose elemental information, and the improvement obtained by de-weighting discrepant data shows that full experimental consistency has not literally been achieved.

#### ★★★ 2. Comment on “Fermi-LAT Galactic Center Excess Morphology of Dark Matter in Simulations of the Milky Way Galaxy”

**Kevork N. Abazajian, Jason Kumar, Oscar Macias**  
[arXiv:2609.34155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34155)

This Comment identifies a consequential error in the dark-matter templates of Muru et al.: their “quadratic dark matter” map used

\[
\left[\int \rho\,s^2\,ds\right]^2
\]

rather than the annihilation \(J\)-factor,

\[
J=\int \rho^2\,ds.
\]

Because squaring an already projected positive map preserves its isophote geometry, the published similarity between dark-matter and old-stellar-population contours was largely built into the erroneous construction. Recalculation with the proper line-of-sight integral does not support the claimed morphological degeneracy.

##### History

The original [Muru et al. PRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06314) argued that realistic, merger-disturbed Milky-Way dark-matter halos could look essentially indistinguishable from the boxy stellar bulge, weakening morphology as a discriminator for the GeV excess. The Comment attacks the central observable rather than a secondary numerical detail; unless the original authors supply a different corrected analysis, their main conclusion should not be used.

The correction does not establish that the excess is dark matter, nor does it resolve diffuse-emission and point-source systematics. It restores only the narrower conclusion that a correctly calculated annihilation morphology remains meaningfully distinguishable from a projected stellar template in these simulations.

#### ★★★ 3. JWST unveils a dust-obscured supernova associated with a gamma-ray burst

**B. Schneider et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.35697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35697)

GRB 240825A, at \(z=0.659\), had conflicting prompt-emission classifications and no supernova in deep ground-based observations. JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy obtained 40.1 rest-frame days after the burst is decomposed pixel by pixel into host and transient components.

A point source at the burst position shows broad features resembling SN 1998bw. The sightline has \(A_V=1.37\pm0.08\) mag; the supernova supplies \(10\!-\!15\%\) of the NIRSpec flux but would have contributed only about 1% of a contemporaneous ground-based optical image. After extinction correction its luminosity is comparable to SN 1998bw, securely identifying a massive-star progenitor.

##### History

A previous multiwavelength study found hybrid merger/collapsar prompt properties and only an optical limit, \(m_r>26.1\), while already suggesting that dust could conceal an ordinary broad-lined Type Ic supernova. JWST converts that inference into a spectroscopic detection. Relative to the foundational GRB 980425/SN 1998bw association, the novelty is not another bright nearby GRB-SN but recovery of one beneath a bright, dusty and unusually metal-rich host.

This strengthens the warning that some apparently “supernova-less” or duration-ambiguous bursts are obscured collapsars. The inferred luminosity remains sensitive to host subtraction, extinction law and the adopted SN template; a single event cannot establish how common this failure mode is.

#### ★★★ 4. Constraints on Supermassive Black Hole Binaries from the Lack of Resolvable Sources in the NANOGrav 15-yr Dataset

**NANOGrav Collaboration; Emiko C. Gardiner et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.32306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32306)

Semi-analytic supermassive-black-hole-binary populations are confronted jointly with NANOGrav’s stochastic-background spectrum and its nondetection of a convincing continuous wave. The absence of resolved systems disfavors backgrounds dominated by a few ultramassive nearby binaries and instead favors many lower-mass contributors.

Compared with the background-only inference, the joint posterior moves toward higher galaxy-merger rates, smaller scatter in the black-hole–bulge relation, shorter binary lifetimes and steeper environmental hardening. The highest-S/N expected sources cluster around chirp mass \(10^{9.6}M_\odot\), luminosity distance \(1.3\) Gpc and frequency \(6\) nHz.

##### History

NANOGrav’s 15-year analyses established evidence for a nanohertz background and separately found only weak individual-source candidates near 4 and 170 nHz. The 4-nHz feature lost much of its significance when the correlated background was modeled, while the higher-frequency candidate lacked a plausible host. ([Continuous-wave search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16222))

This is the first population analysis to use that *absence* quantitatively alongside the detected background, breaking part of the number-versus-mass degeneracy. The result is conditional on semi-analytic prescriptions for galaxy pairing, black-hole scaling relations and sub-parsec hardening. It is not yet a measurement of any one environmental parameter, and the low-frequency candidate should not be interpreted as a resolved binary.

#### ★★★ 5. First measurement of the antihydrogen production cross section through charge exchange of low-energy antiprotons with orthopositronium

**GBAR Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2609.33990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33990)

GBAR measures \(\bar p+\mathrm{Ps}\rightarrow\bar H+e^-\) at two antiproton energies:

\[
\sigma(6.2\,{\rm keV})
 =\left(14.1\pm1.3_{\rm stat}^{+2.2}_{-1.4}{}_{\rm sys}\right)
 \times10^{-16}\ {\rm cm^2},
\]

\[
\sigma(4.15\,{\rm keV})
 =\left(8.7\pm2.4_{\rm stat}^{+1.5}_{-0.09}{}_{\rm sys}\right)
 \times10^{-16}\ {\rm cm^2}.
\]

Both agree with recent three-body calculations. This is the first direct charge-exchange cross-section measurement made with an antiproton beam.

##### History

GBAR previously reported the first antihydrogen beam produced through this channel, at only \(3\!-\!4\sigma\), without extracting a cross section. ([2023 production result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15801)) The new result converts proof of production into a calibrated atomic-collision measurement and validates the reaction-rate calculations used to design GBAR’s two-step \(\bar H^+\) source.

It is enabling metrology rather than a gravity or CPT test: only two kinetic energies are measured, the second positron-capture step remains rate-limiting, and no conclusion about antihydrogen free fall follows directly.

#### ★★☆ 6. Neutrino Direct Simulation Monte Carlo: Accurate modeling of out-of-equilibrium decaying cosmological relics

**Kensuke Akita, Miguel Escudero, Oleksii Ihnatenko, Maksym Ovchynnikov**  
[arXiv:2609.31833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31833)

The \(\nu\)DSMC framework is extended to evolve a decaying relic, nonthermal neutrino spectra, the electromagnetic plasma, cosmic expansion and Big Bang nucleosynthesis together. It includes inverse decays, flavor conversion, quantum statistics and pion production by energetic neutrinos.

Benchmarking against independent quantum-kinetic and Boltzmann solvers shows excellent agreement, with a computational advantage when spectra become strongly nonthermal. Momentum-averaged treatments can predict even the wrong sign of \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\) and substantially incorrect light-element yields.

##### History

The original \(\nu\)DSMC work introduced Monte Carlo neutrino transport for new-physics cosmologies, while the subsequent precision implementation reproduced standard decoupling at \(N_{\rm eff}=3.0439\pm0.0006\). ([Method paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07378); [precision validation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08379))

The present paper supplies the missing end-to-end relic-decay and BBN machinery, making it a useful computational advance. It does not yet provide a universal revised exclusion plot: constraints remain model-dependent, and nuclear-rate, hadronic-decay and flavor-coherence uncertainties must still be propagated separately.

#### ★★☆ 7. Evidence for an extended Galactic proton component beyond the cosmic-ray knee

**Jieshuang Wang**  
[arXiv:2609.33364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33364)

Fits to proton and helium spectra from \(0.1\) to \(1000\) PeV disfavor interpreting the few-PeV knee as a single terminal proton cutoff. When the fit is anchored down to tens of GeV, a proton contribution extending beyond \(100\) PeV is required, either as a continuous component or as a distinct component with a break near the knee.

A transport break can produce the latter behavior without requiring ordinary supernova remnants to accelerate protons continuously to \(100\) PeV; extreme Galactic accelerators such as super-Eddington X-ray binaries are discussed as possible contributors.

##### History

LHAASO directly established a proton spectrum that hardens toward 3 PeV and then softens sharply, while its helium spectrum hardens near 1.1 PeV and softens around 7 PeV. Those measurements already suggested multiple Galactic components rather than a featureless rigidity cutoff. ([Protons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14447); [helium](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05013))

The new paper extends this into a full-spectrum consistency argument and makes the \(>100\)-PeV proton tail explicit. It is not a new composition measurement, however. Its significance depends on combining experiments with different energy scales and hadronic models, and the data do not distinguish a new source population from energy-dependent transport.

#### ★★☆ 8. Discovery of minute-scale polarization-angle rotation in a supermassive-black-hole jet

**Aristeidis Polychronakis, Ioannis Liodakis, Dmitry Blinov, Anastasia Glykopoulou, Sebastian Kiehlmann, Karan Pal, Georgios F. Paraschos, Lena Debbrecht, Swati Ravi, Herman L. Marshall**  
[arXiv:2609.31836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31836)

Optical monitoring of PKS 1510−089 finds a coherent \(136^\circ\) polarization-angle rotation in about 80 minutes, corresponding to roughly \(2450^\circ\) per day. Contemporaneous 43-GHz VLBA imaging shows a new component leaving the radio core with apparent speed \(17.8c\) and inferred Doppler factor near 42.

The authors favor interaction between a moving shock and a standing-shock complex. The stable, low polarization degree and absence of detected periodicity or a strong broadband flare are argued against simple turbulent-walk, kink and reconnection interpretations.

##### History

Previously catalogued coherent rotations normally lasted days; the earlier speed record was approximately \(360^\circ\) per day. A 2014 rotation in 3C 454.3 had similar structure and a component ejection but lasted about twelve hours. Correcting for the different Doppler factors brings its intrinsic timescale within roughly \(2\sigma\) of the present event.

The new observation is the first resolved intra-night rotation, not an entirely new physical mechanism. The shock interpretation is plausible but circumstantial: the VLBA cadence does not time the interaction to 80-minute precision, and Fermi-LAT’s unfavorable viewing geometry prevents a meaningful test for a simultaneous short gamma-ray flare.

### Also notable

#### Gravitational Waves from Core-Collapse Supernovae: Dependence on the Progenitor Star, Rotation Rate and Nuclear Equation of State

**Jade Powell, Bailey Sykes and Bernhard Müller**  
[arXiv:2609.34405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34405)

A suite of 137 axisymmetric simulations maps progenitor, rotation and equation-of-state dependence and finds up to 62% variation in detection S/N from random perturbations near the explosion threshold.

##### History

Relative to the authors’ earlier small 3D samples, the advance is systematic parameter coverage and an explicit stochastic-variance estimate. The price is two-dimensional axisymmetry, which can exaggerate SASI coherence and cannot represent non-axisymmetric rotational dynamics.

#### Milky Way White Dwarfs as Inelastic Dark Matter Detectors

**Javier F. Acevedo**  
[arXiv:2609.31850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31850)

Inelastic scattering can strand captured dark matter on orbits extending outside a white dwarf, allowing some annihilation products to escape and produce a collective Galactic-center gamma-ray signal.

##### History

This contrasts with earlier white-dwarf searches that required long-lived or boosted mediators to escape the star. ([Earlier study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10843)) The external-annihilation mechanism is new, but the resulting H.E.S.S. limits are explicitly tentative and depend strongly on the poorly known central white-dwarf population and dark-matter profile.

### Verified science news

#### ★★★ CHIME achieves the first standalone autocorrelation detection of cosmological 21-cm emission

The CHIME Collaboration’s result was published on **28 September 2026**. Using 94 nights of 2019 observations, CHIME measures the neutral-hydrogen auto-power spectrum over \(608.2\!-\!707.8\) MHz, corresponding to \(z\simeq1.01\!-\!1.34\), with a reported significance of \(12.5\sigma\). The signal therefore no longer depends on cross-correlation with an external galaxy or Lyman-\(\alpha\) survey. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19620); [official UBC/CHIME announcement](https://science.ubc.ca/news/2026-09/canadian-telescope-directly-maps-earliest-glow-hydrogen-opening-new-window-universe))

CHIME had previously detected cosmological 21-cm emission through cross-correlation with external large-scale-structure tracers. The standalone measurement is the instrument’s decisive proof of principle for intensity-mapping cosmology. It does not yet constitute a competitive dark-energy constraint: foreground removal restricts the measured \(k\)-range, and BAO-scale analyses require substantially more data and control of calibration systematics.

#### ★★☆ Einstein Probe discovers the unclassified fast X-ray transient EP260927a

Einstein Probe’s wide-field telescope detected EP260927a on **27 September at 14:02:35 UTC**. The event lasted about 800 seconds with a \(0.5\!-\!4\) keV flux near

\[
2.91\times10^{-11}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
\]

Follow-up roughly fourteen hours later found an uncatalogued source inside the localization with a much lower \(0.5\!-\!10\) keV flux of \(5.6^{+4.9}_{-2.0}\times10^{-14}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). The team considers it the likely counterpart, while a second source is consistent with a previously catalogued probable QSO. ([Official GCN Circular 45779](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45779))

**Status:** a verified rapidly fading X-ray transient, but presently unclassified. No redshift, secure optical counterpart, gamma-ray burst association or multimessenger counterpart has been reported; the suggested association with the faint uncatalogued X-ray source remains preliminary.
