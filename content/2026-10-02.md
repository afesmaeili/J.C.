#### ★★★ 1. Recycling serendipitous observations of XRISM for dark matter searches

**Lidiia Zadorozhna, Denys Malyshev, Oleg Ruchayskiy**  
[arXiv:2610.01877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01877)

The authors combine 60 low-background public XRISM/Resolve observations, totaling 2.917 Ms, to search for narrow dark-matter decay lines from the Milky Way halo. Each spectrum is corrected for the observer’s velocity relative to the Galactic halo before stacking.

The resulting limits are, on average, approximately 70% stronger than XRISM’s recent cluster-stack constraints. More intriguingly, a residual appears at

\[
E=3.5311~{\rm keV}.
\]

Its significance rises from \(2.5\sigma\) in an ordinary stack to \(3.0\sigma\) locally after halo-frame correction, but falls to only \(2.0\!-\!2.5\sigma\) after the look-elsewhere effect. The excess corresponds to approximately \(26\pm10\) photons over roughly 52 background counts in a 5-eV bin. Interpreted as sterile-neutrino decay, its best-fit mixing is

\[
\sin^2(2\theta)=(2.7\pm1.0)\times10^{-10}.
\]

##### History

Bulbul et al. originally reported a cluster-stack line near 3.55–3.57 keV, while Boyarsky et al. found a compatible feature in Perseus and M31. ([Bulbul et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2301); [Boyarsky et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.4119)) XRISM’s subsequent 3.75-Ms stack of ten clusters found no line and improved on Hitomi’s decay-rate bound by a factor of 3–4, although it was still insufficient to test the original Bulbul flux fully. ([XRISM cluster-stack search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24560))

The present work materially improves the experimental strategy: low-continuum blank-field observations and halo-frame alignment can outperform a longer cluster exposure. The 3.53-keV residual is nevertheless not evidence for sterile-neutrino dark matter. Its global significance is below \(3\sigma\), it contains only a few dozen excess counts, and the inferred mixing is already in roughly \(2\sigma\) tension with existing exclusions. An independent, pre-specified analysis of additional fields—and explicit tests of detector-line and field-selection systematics—is essential.

#### ★★★ 2. Search for charged-lepton-flavour-violating \(e\mu tq\) interactions with ATLAS

**ATLAS Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.01308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01308)

ATLAS searches for contact interactions containing an electron, muon, top quark and up- or charm-type quark, using the full \(140~{\rm fb^{-1}}\) Run-2 dataset. The three-lepton analysis covers both anomalous single-top production and flavour-violating top decay inside \(t\bar t\) events.

No excess is observed. One-operator-at-a-time bounds on six EFT coefficients range from

\[
0.018\ {\rm TeV^{-2}}\quad{\rm to}\quad0.32\ {\rm TeV^{-2}},
\]

with corresponding branching-fraction limits spanning approximately

\[
{\cal B}(t\rightarrow e\mu u)<6\times10^{-9}
\]

to

\[
{\cal B}(t\rightarrow e\mu c)<2.7\times10^{-7},
\]

depending on Lorentz and flavour structure.

##### History

ATLAS’s earlier 79.8-\({\rm fb^{-1}}\) decay-only search obtained a combined limit near \({\cal B}(t\rightarrow\ell\ell' q)<1.86\times10^{-5}\). ([Earlier ATLAS search](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09048)) The quoted branching-fraction limits are numerically smaller by factors ranging from roughly 70 to several thousand, although the comparison depends on the flavour and operator assumptions. That gain is not attributable merely to luminosity: the analysis adds the often more sensitive anomalous-production channel and resolves individual EFT structures.

The limits assume one nonzero coefficient at a time. Correlations, cancellations and mediator-induced relations between operators can weaken their direct ultraviolet interpretation, while the contact-interaction description must be checked if the underlying mediator is light enough to be produced at the LHC.

#### ★★★ 3. Near Threshold Kaluza–Klein Graviton Decays and the Dark Dimension

**Kevin Langhoff**  
[arXiv:2610.01825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01825)

The paper revisits decays within a nearly degenerate Kaluza–Klein graviton tower. Angular-momentum constraints make the near-threshold decay purely \(d\)-wave, yielding

\[
\Gamma\propto p^5
\]

rather than the \(s\)-wave-like \(\Gamma\propto p\) scaling used in some dark-dimension cosmologies.

Applied to a flat dark dimension, the suppression prevents the proposed graviton cascade from efficiently creating a sufficiently cold, long-lived dark-matter population. Irreducible freeze-in also produces MeV gamma rays, while BBN and CMB requirements force the compactification radius to be about three orders of magnitude smaller than the nominal \(R\sim\Lambda^{-1/4}\) dark-dimension scale.

##### History

The dark-dimension dark-matter proposal relied on cascades ending in approximately \(1\!-\!100\)-keV KK gravitons. ([Dark Dimension Gravitons as Dark Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09249)) The new threshold suppression directly removes the decay efficiency needed for that construction. However, a near-contemporaneous independent analysis, [*A Universal KK Graviton Cascade Rate*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36234), derives the same \(p^5\) behavior and associated amplitude suppression. Thus the physical correction is important, but it should not be attributed to this paper alone.

The exclusion concerns the minimal flat-dimensional realization and its assumed production history. Warped geometries, additional light states or nonstandard cosmological histories are not generically ruled out.

#### ★★☆ 4. High-energy neutrinos from shocked circumnuclear material around optically bright and infrared-only tidal disruption events

**Mainak Mukhopadhyay, Shigeo S. Kimura, Tatsuya Matsumoto**  
[arXiv:2610.00965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00965)

A semi-analytic calculation follows cosmic-ray acceleration and transport in shocks driven into circumnuclear gas by tidal-disruption-event outflows. Radiative cooling and post-shock compression are included in calculating the \(pp\) efficiency and neutrino output.

Ordinary optically selected TDEs are generally inefficient neutrino emitters. Dust-obscured, infrared-only events can approach hadronic calorimetry and, given the still-uncertain event rate, may supply as much as roughly 10% of the diffuse neutrino intensity around 100 TeV. Detecting the predicted stacked signal would probably require a future detector with an effective volume near \(30~{\rm km^3}\) and several years of observations of nearby events.

##### History

Earlier source-specific outflow–cloud calculations for AT2019dsg predicted only about \(4\times10^{-3}\) PeV events under already favorable assumptions. ([AT2019dsg outflow–cloud model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01748)) IceCube’s recent search over 89 catalogued TDEs found neither a significant individual source nor a stacked excess. ([IceCube TDE search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00657))

The present work does not contradict that null result: most IceCube targets were optically or X-ray selected, whereas the predicted efficient population is heavily obscured and potentially missing from those catalogues. The real advance is the self-consistent population treatment of infrared-only events, including radiative compression. The 10% contribution is an upper-end estimate controlled by poorly measured event rates, gas covering factors and cosmic-ray loading; individual-event detections remain unlikely.

#### ★★☆ 5. Search for dark matter produced with a low-multiplicity jet using CMS

**CMS Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.00995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00995)

CMS searches for a dark-matter pair produced through a heavy vector or axial-vector mediator, with one dark particle radiating a light leptophobic \(Z'\). A \(0.3\!-\!3\)-GeV \(Z'\) decaying into two pions or kaons produces an unusually narrow, low-track-multiplicity “pencil jet,” rather than a conventional QCD jet.

The full \(138~{\rm fb^{-1}}\) Run-2 sample is analyzed using hadronic-\(\tau\) reconstruction followed by machine-learning classification. No excess is found. For \(m_\chi=150\) GeV, \(m_{Z'}=1\) GeV, \(g_q=0.25\) and \(g_\chi=1\), heavy mediators below approximately 4.25 TeV are excluded; at \(m_\chi=550\) GeV, the reach is about 3.5 TeV.

##### History

Bai, Bourbeau and Lin proposed the mono-\(Z'\)-jet signature in 2015, but it had not previously received a dedicated collider search. ([Mono-Z′-jet proposal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01395)) This is therefore an experimental opening of a qualitatively new channel, not simply a stronger monojet bound.

The impressive mediator reach is conditional on a very light leptophobic boson, prompt hadronic decay and the specified benchmark couplings. It cannot be compared directly with conventional monojet exclusions, which test different radiation and acceptance assumptions. Modeling extremely narrow hadronic jets and calibrating a classifier inherited partly from \(\tau\) reconstruction are important experimental systematics.

#### ★★☆ 6. Scalar MSW resonance and approximate diagonalization with scalar nonstandard interactions

**Sandhya Choubey, Andreas Lund**  
[arXiv:2610.02107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02107)

The authors extend Jacobi-style approximate diagonalization of the three-flavour neutrino Hamiltonian to scalar nonstandard interactions, which modify the mass matrix rather than adding an ordinary vector matter potential.

They identify an energy-independent scalar analogue of the MSW resonance for each diagonal scalar coupling and derive compact mappings between vacuum and in-matter mixing parameters. The electron-flavour case admits particularly accurate formulae; the \(\mu\mu\) and \(\tau\tau\) cases require an expansion in the charged-current matter potential plus controlled corrections.

##### History

Scalar matter effects in neutrino oscillations were introduced explicitly by Ge and Parke and have since been constrained with solar and long-baseline data. ([Ge and Parke](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08376)) The new paper does not introduce the interaction or improve an experimental constraint. Its contribution is to expose the resonance structure analytically and provide fast parameter mappings that can replace repeated numerical diagonalization in fits.

The “energy-independent resonance” is a property of the effective scalar mass contribution; observability still depends on density profiles, flavour structure and degeneracies with vacuum masses. Off-diagonal couplings and simultaneous multi-parameter fits remain more complicated than the presented single-coupling cases.

#### ★★☆ 7. Three New Likely Spider Millisecond Pulsar Binaries and Optical Kinematic Tracers of Intrabinary Shocks

**Jay Strader, Rebecca Kyer, Laura Chomiuk, Elias Aydi, Peter Craig, Thomas M. Do, Isabella Molina, Teresa Panurach, Subhroja Roy, Kirill V. Sokolovsky, Samuel J. Swihart, Ryan Urquhart**  
[arXiv:2610.00455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00455)

Multiwavelength follow-up of unassociated Fermi sources identifies two strong redback candidates with orbital periods of \(0.15\!-\!0.30\) days and companion mass functions around \(0.6\!-\!0.9\,M_\odot\), plus a less secure 30.5-hour candidate.

One system, J1020, shows persistent broad H\(\alpha\) emission with FWHM near \(2100~{\rm km\,s^{-1}}\). Its phase-dependent velocity is offset from the companion but consistent with material near the neutron star, providing the first kinematic evidence for an intrabinary shock wrapping around the pulsar rather than the secondary.

##### History

Phase-resolved H\(\alpha\) tomography of PSR J1048+2339 previously located emission near the companion and inner Lagrange point. ([PSR J1048+2339 shock tomography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06317)) J1020 therefore supplies a qualitatively different geometry and observationally supports the theoretically expected reversal of shock orientation when the companion wind dominates.

None of the three systems yet has detected radio or gamma-ray pulsations, so their neutron-star identifications remain probabilistic. The third association is particularly tentative, and interpreting H\(\alpha\) velocities as direct spatial tracers requires assumptions about acceleration and radiative transfer in the shock.

#### ★☆☆ 8. X-ray through radio observations and modeling of the soft X-ray flash GRB 250419A

**Genevieve Schroeder et al.**  
[arXiv:2610.00508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00508)

SVOM measured a prompt spectral peak below 4 keV. Broadband follow-up found an achromatic X-ray/optical break and evidence for two radio components. The authors model the event as a low-energy relativistic jet with

\[
E_{\rm jet}\sim10^{49}-10^{50}\ {\rm erg},
\]

supplemented by radial energy stratification, modest off-axis viewing, or both. The result favors at least some X-ray flashes being the low-energy tail of the classical GRB population.

##### History

Nearby soft events such as GRB 060218 and GRB 100316D have long admitted competing relativistic-jet and shock-breakout interpretations. The new event adds an unusually broad radio-to-X-ray dataset supporting a genuine jet, but does not settle the population question. Radial energy injection and off-axis geometry remain partly interchangeable in the fit, and a single burst cannot establish whether all X-ray flashes share the same origin.

There is **no new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy measurement** and no new precision laboratory oscillation dataset in the 2 October listings.

#### GRB 260919A and Einstein Probe transient EP260920a are probably related, but the timing is unusual

A targeted Fermi-GBM search recovered bright gamma-ray emission lasting approximately 250 seconds, detected most significantly at **23:52:04 UTC on 19 September 2026**. GBM did not issue an onboard trigger because the event occurred during a post-trigger dead interval.

Einstein Probe detected EP260920a at **00:06:04 UTC on 20 September**, roughly 600 seconds after the end of the principal GBM episode. Combining GBM and Interplanetary Network localization information gives a reported spatial-association probability of 0.9887. ([Fermi-GBM GCN Circular 45794, 1 October 2026](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45794)) The IPN region remains very broad—about \(6556~{\rm deg^2}\) at \(3\sigma\)—but contains the Einstein Probe position. ([IPN GCN Circular 45792, 1 October 2026](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45792))

**Status:** a common long-duration high-energy transient is plausible and favored positionally, but the long temporal offset means the association is not yet secure. No exceptional neutrino or gravitational-wave counterpart has been reported.

### Science Radar

#### A possible “rupture gate” linking Southern California fault systems

A peer-reviewed *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* study, published on **3 June 2026**, constructs a roughly 1,000-year earthquake-cycle model for the Cajon Pass region, combining paleoseismic, historical and radiocarbon constraints. At its final modeled time step in 2025, the model places simultaneously high stress on the San Jacinto–San Bernardino segment, approximately 3.6 MPa, and the southern Mojave section of the San Andreas, approximately 2.8 MPa.

The authors argue that Cajon Pass can sometimes behave as a gate through which rupture propagates between the two systems, potentially producing a larger compound earthquake than models treating the faults independently. ([JGR: Solid Earth research paper](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JB033213))

This is a fault-interaction and hazard-modeling result, **not an earthquake prediction**: it does not supply a date, short-term probability or precursor signal. Its implications depend on uncertain prehistoric event correlations and on how accurately the model represents stress transfer across a structurally complex junction.

#### APOE4 linked to two distinct neurodegenerative pathways in human-derived brain models

Two peer-reviewed studies report complementary mechanisms by which the APOE4 allele may promote neurodegeneration:

- A *Cell* study, published online on **24 September 2026**, combining single-cell atlases, human “miBrain” cultures, postmortem tissue and aged APOE4 mice finds that APOE4 can drive pericytes toward a fibrotic myofibroblast-like state. Blocking TGF-\(\beta\) signaling reversed pericyte loss, vascular fibrosis and perivascular amyloid accumulation in experimental models. ([Cell research paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S009286742601069X))

- A *Cell Stem Cell* study, published online on **24 August 2026**, finds that APOE4-dependent cholesterol accumulation disrupts astrocyte lysosomes, promoting accumulation and transfer of \(\alpha\)-synuclein to neurons in human-derived multicellular brain models. ([Cell Stem Cell research paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13528747/))

Together, the papers broaden APOE4 biology beyond neuron-intrinsic toxicity and point to vascular and astrocytic mechanisms connecting Alzheimer’s- and Parkinson’s-related pathology. Both results remain preclinical: organoid-like systems and engineered mice capture only parts of human aging, and neither study establishes that TGF-\(\beta\) inhibition or cholesterol manipulation will be safe or effective in patients.
