### Verified science news

#### ★★★ EP261010a: a candidate supernova shock breakout

**Event:** 10 October 2026; **refined Einstein Probe analysis announced:** 11 October 2026.

[Einstein Probe’s refined analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45893) changes the interpretation of EP261010a substantially. The Wide-field X-ray Telescope light curve began rising at 00:17:40 UTC on 10 October and remained active for about 70 seconds before autonomous follow-up. Its extremely soft 0.5–4 keV spectrum was fit by an absorbed blackbody with

\[
kT_{\rm WXT}=0.26^{+0.12}_{-0.08}\ {\rm keV},
\qquad
F_{0.5-4\,\rm keV}=1.2^{+0.7}_{-0.6}\times10^{-10}
\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
\]

Einstein Probe’s Follow-up X-ray Telescope started 110 seconds after the trigger. It recovered the uncatalogued source at a flux roughly three orders of magnitude lower, with an even cooler blackbody fit:

\[
kT_{\rm FXT}=0.10^{+0.03}_{-0.04}\ {\rm keV},
\qquad
F_{0.5-10\,\rm keV}=7.6^{+3.6}_{-2.9}\times10^{-14}
\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
\]

The rapid fading and spectral cooling are qualitatively consistent with radiation released when a supernova shock reaches a stellar surface or an optically thick circumstellar wind. This makes shock breakout a physically motivated interpretation rather than a generic transient label.

##### History

Direct soft-X-ray captures of shock breakout are rare. The benchmark is XRO 080109/SN 2008D, a roughly 220-second X-ray flash later associated with a Type Ib supernova ([Soderberg et al., *Nature* 453, 469](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06997)). Whether those photons escaped directly from the stellar surface or through a dense wind required subsequent modeling ([Chevalier & Fransson](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0371); [Svirski & Nakar](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3400)).

EP261010a is not yet at that evidentiary level. [NOT](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45882) and [SVOM/VT](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45884) found no uncatalogued optical counterpart within the FXT region, reaching approximately \(r>23.9\), \(z>21.5\), VT_B \(>23.4\), and VT_R \(>23.1\) AB at 1.4–3.2 hours. There is still no host, redshift, supernova spectrum, or bolometric energy. This is therefore a **preliminary classification based on prompt X-ray phenomenology**, not a confirmed shock-breakout supernova. A later rising optical supernova or secure host association would be decisive.

#### GRB 261010A: counterpart follow-up

Swift’s [GRB 261010A](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45889) is a confirmed gamma-ray burst with X-ray follow-up, but its [reported optical source](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45891) remains offset from the XRT position and tentative; no secure neutrino or gravitational-wave association had been announced by the 11 October cutoff.

### Science Radar

#### ★★★ OpenAI withdraws three AI-generated mathematics manuscripts and revises fourteen

**Initial release:** 6 October 2026; **withdrawal/revision log:** 7 October 2026; **independent reporting:** 9 October 2026.

These withdrawals and revisions correct the mathematics release announced on 6 October. OpenAI’s public [repository history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md) records the withdrawal of three of the original 722 manuscripts after a sign error invalidated the stabilization–trace cancellation argument in *Algebraicity of Weil classes on split abelian eightfolds*. Two dependent manuscripts—on Kuga–Satake correspondences for K3 surfaces and the rational Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces—were withdrawn with it.

The detailed [withdrawal notice](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Algebraicity-of-Weil-classes-on-split-abelian-eightfolds-September-18-2026/README.md) states that the claimed cancellation changes the signed double-point count from \(-m\) to \(-2m\), rather than zero. The hypothesis required for the invoked Eliashberg–Murphy cancellation theorem is therefore unmet. Importantly, the notice says the proof fails; it does **not** establish that the underlying algebraicity statement is false.

The same log records revisions to fourteen other manuscripts—ranging from repaired arguments and clarified hypotheses to a corrected cone-equality claim—and reference-only updates to thirteen dependent papers. Six additional Lean formalizations bring the repository’s stated coverage to 300 of the remaining 719 top-line results, about 42%.

##### History

The correction protocol worked in the limited sense that failures were publicly logged, dependent papers were traced, and archived versions remain accessible. The speed and scope of the corrections nevertheless confirm the release’s central limitation: these are unrefereed manuscripts whose claims must be checked individually. Lean formalization is valuable only when the formal theorem faithfully matches the natural-language claim and all imported assumptions are appropriate.

Independent reporting based on interviews with mathematicians found uneven exposition and cases where reviewers could not readily match formal statements to headline claims ([*The Verge*, 9 October](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008726/openai-mathematics-solutions-chaos)).

The release is a large, heterogeneous research corpus containing potentially important results, explicit failures, and a substantial verification backlog. The withdrawals demonstrate that repository status—not the original announcement—must be checked before any theorem is cited as proved.
