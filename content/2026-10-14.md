#### 1. IceCube: neutrino decoherence and the Planck scale

**R. Abbasi et al., IceCube Collaboration — [arXiv:2610.14144v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14144v1).** Submitted 9 October; preprint.

IceCube fits 368,071 upward-going muon-neutrino events collected over 10.7 years, spanning reconstructed energies of 0.5–100 TeV. Three-flavor propagation, matter effects, attenuation and experimental uncertainties enter the analysis. No anomalous decoherence is detected.

The parameterization is \(\Gamma(E)=\Gamma_0(E/1\,\mathrm{TeV})^n\). State selection drives flavor populations toward equipartition; phase perturbation damps coherence without that same relaxation. The **90% confidence upper limits**, in eV, are:

| Energy exponent \(n\) | State selection | Phase perturbation |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | \(2.36\times10^{-15}\) | \(2.41\times10^{-15}\) |
| 1 | \(4.58\times10^{-16}\) | \(4.80\times10^{-16}\) |
| 2 | \(7.26\times10^{-19}\) | \(7.26\times10^{-19}\) |
| 3 | \(6.05\times10^{-21}\) | \(6.37\times10^{-21}\) |

These test specified open-system models, not quantum gravity as a whole. The strongest leverage comes from energy-growing effects in the high-energy tail. [Full analysis, Tables 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14144v1).

##### History

Decoherence is a loss of phase information, distinct from ordinary unitary oscillations or experimental averaging. In natural units, \(1/\Gamma\) is a characteristic coherence length. Comparing experiments requires the same damping matrix, exponent, pivot energy and confidence level; a bound quoted at 1 GeV cannot be compared directly with one at 1 TeV when \(n\ne0\).

The [2024 IceCube analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00105), using 305,735 events and the same two model classes, set state-selection limits at 1 TeV of \(1.17\times10^{-15}\), \(6.67\times10^{-16}\), \(9.48\times10^{-18}\) and \(1.77\times10^{-19}\) eV for \(n=0,1,2,3\). Thus the new limits improve by approximately **1.5, 13 and 29** for \(n=1,2,3\), whereas **the energy-independent limit is about twice as weak**. This comparison is calculated from the two tables; “stronger limits” should not be applied indiscriminately.

For the particular ansatz \(\Gamma\sim E^3/M_{\rm Pl}^2\), the new result reaches the order-one Planck-suppressed benchmark. That conclusion depends on the assumed coupling and flavor structure.

#### 2. Nuclear acceleration in neutrino-emitting Seyfert coronae

**Chengchao Yuan — [arXiv:2610.18671v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18671v1).** Submitted 13 October; preprint.

Stochastic-acceleration spectra are combined with nuclear transport and electromagnetic cascades for NGC 1068 and NGC 7469. The key parameter is \(f_{\rm nuc}=L_{A>1}/L_{\rm CR}\): the fraction of injected cosmic-ray **power** in nuclei, including helium. It is not gas metallicity or the escaping heavy-nucleus fraction.

For NGC 1068, the benchmark scan over coronal radii of 10–100 gravitational radii permits helium-dominated, solar-mixture injection approaching \(f_{\rm nuc}=1\) over a restricted radius interval; CNO- and iron-rich mixtures reach roughly 0.8. These are **model-dependent flux selections**, not confidence-level composition limits from a global likelihood.

The spectra inherit an acceleration cutoff: fragmentation largely preserves energy per nucleon and cannot repair an insufficient cutoff. The calculation uses power-normalized effective injection and fixed backgrounds, without secondary reacceleration or full acceleration–cascade feedback. [Methods and parameter scan](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18671v1).

##### History

The relevant earlier comparison is [Esmaeili, Esmaili and Serpico, arXiv:2606.23812](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23812v1). That study prescribed injection spectra at comparable energy per nucleon, normalized the resulting neutrinos to NGC 1068, and followed nuclear and electromagnetic cascades. In its compact benchmark, rapid fragmentation and neutron production reduce Bethe–Heitler losses: iron injection lowers the required cosmic-ray power by about 15–20% and bolometric cascade luminosity by about 35% relative to protons. At a larger source radius, surviving charged fragments instead enhance pair losses and gamma-ray constraints. Its COMPTEL reanalysis also supplied **95% confidence photon-flux limits**, a different statistical object from a selected composition range.

The studies therefore answer related but different questions: propagation at prescribed injection versus spectra restricted by an acceleration model. Apparent differences in the viability of heavy injection must be checked at matched compactness, photon field, spectrum and normalization. The new calculation advances that comparison; it does not establish a universal upper bound on source metallicity.

#### 3. RX J1713.7−3946: the upstream field and the current acceleration ceiling

**Yuya Kawabata, Takaaki Tanaka, Hiromasa Suzuki and Tsuyoshi Inoue — [arXiv:2610.16296v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16296v1).** Submitted 12 October; published in [Communications Physics](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-026-02906-y).

Chandra identifies 195 significant compact X-ray hotspots, including a population extending approximately one parsec beyond the forward shock. Interpreting those upstream hotspots as synchrotron radiation from hadronically produced secondary electrons in dense, magnetized clumps connects their spatial extent to proton diffusion.

Under the benchmark assumptions,

\[
\frac{B_{\rm up}}{\eta}\simeq14\,\mu{\rm G}
\left(\frac{\epsilon_X}{1\,\mathrm{keV}}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\frac{B_{\rm clump}}{1\,\mathrm{mG}}\right)^{-1/2}
\left(\frac{v_{\rm sh}}{3000\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}}\right)^{-1}
\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm diff}}{1\,\mathrm{pc}}\right)^{-1}.
\]

Here \(\eta\ge1\) measures diffusion relative to Bohm diffusion. The observation constrains **\(B_{\rm up}/\eta\)**, not an assumption-free magnetic field. Inserting the inferred diffusion coefficient into an age-limited acceleration estimate gives a present ceiling near **100 TeV**, below a PeV. The secondary-electron interpretation remains conditional; primary-electron emission is discussed as an alternative. [Equations 6–7 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16296v1).

##### History

[Uchiyama et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06210) interpreted rapid X-ray hotspot variability as evidence for locally amplified, milligauss-scale fields. Those compact downstream environments should not be equated with the field ahead of the shock. Efficient diffusive shock acceleration requires particles to scatter and return across the shock; a large field in isolated emitting clumps alone does not establish the upstream confinement required for PeV acceleration.

[H.E.S.S.'s deeper spatial study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08671) already found gamma-ray emission extending beyond the X-ray shell, consistent with a precursor or escaping particles. The new X-ray population offers a complementary spatial diagnostic of that region. A current acceleration ceiling also does not exclude a higher maximum energy earlier in the remnant's evolution, when its shock was faster. This is a constraint on a particular remnant and epoch, not a general exclusion of supernova remnants as Galactic cosmic-ray sources.

#### 4. LSND and MiniBooNE after MicroBooNE: an incoherent-scattering route

**Waleed Abdallah, Raj Gandhi and Samiran Roy — [arXiv:2610.17207v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17207v1).** Submitted 12 October; preprint.

The paper organizes MicroBooNE's electron, photon and electron–positron searches by which new-physics final states they test. It illustrates a light pseudoscalar mediator producing predominantly incoherent scattering, with a **17 MeV mediator and 114 MeV heavy neutral lepton** benchmark.

The fits use assumed uncorrelated fractional systematic errors of 13% for MiniBooNE and 15% for LSND. They should therefore be read as a phenomenological viability demonstration, not an experimental joint-likelihood discovery. Predicted MicroBooNE/SBND yields also assume a 12% pair-detection efficiency.

A useful test is an explicit search for low-invariant-mass pairs accompanied by weak hadronic activity. The discussion of Dirac neutrinos concerns model-building and radiative-mass consistency; it is not evidence that neutrinos are Dirac particles. [Fit prescription, Table 1 and constraints](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17207v1).

##### History

An electron-like Cherenkov excess need not uniquely identify an electron-neutrino appearance signal. [MicroBooNE's two-beam oscillation analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07159) excludes the tested single-light-sterile interpretation of the LSND/MiniBooNE anomalies at 95% confidence. Its [dark-sector pair search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10900) separately tests neutrino-induced production followed by visible pairs and missing energy. Limits on a coherent vector-mediated topology cannot automatically be transferred to incoherent pseudoscalar scattering with different efficiencies.

**Independent constraint check:** the new paper uses the ACME electron-EDM bound \(1.1\times10^{-29}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}\). [Roussy et al., Science (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg4084) already reported \(|d_e|<4.1\times10^{-30}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}\) at 90% confidence using HfF\(^+\). Applying that bound to the paper's quoted \(d_e\simeq2.3\times10^{-26}\theta_{\rm CP}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}\), at its specified mixing benchmark, gives **\(|\theta_{\rm CP}|\lesssim1.8\times10^{-4}\)** rather than approximately \(5\times10^{-4}\). This is a conditional rescaling, assuming the same dominant contribution and no cancellations or additional molecular CP-odd effects; it does not exclude the CP-conserving limit.

#### 5. TDCOSMO 2026: better kinematics sharpen the Hubble constant

**TDCOSMO Collaboration, S. Birrer et al. — [arXiv:2610.14781v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14781v1).** Submitted 10 October; preprint submitted to Physical Review D.

Eleven time-delay lenses, including nine with spatially resolved stellar kinematics, give

\[
H_0=71.1^{+2.4}_{-2.3}\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
\]

for the baseline flat-\(\Lambda\)CDM analysis with the DES-Dovekie supernova constraint on \(\Omega_m\). This is **3.3% precision**; adding external non-time-delay lens samples improves it to approximately 3.0%.

The blinded analysis retains mass-sheet freedom, uses axisymmetric stellar dynamics and updates line-of-sight modeling. Six systems now have resolved JWST/NIRSpec kinematics. The advance is a stronger internal dynamical calibration of lens masses. The resulting uncertainty still allows considerable overlap with competing cosmological inferences; this measurement alone does not settle the Hubble tension. [Full analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14781v1).

##### History

Time delays constrain a distance scale only after the lens's gravitational potential is modeled. The mass-sheet transformation can preserve image observables while rescaling inferred time delays and hence \(H_0\). Stellar kinematics supply information that helps break this degeneracy, at the price of modeling orbital anisotropy and galaxy structure.

[TDCOSMO IV (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02941) demonstrated the precision cost of allowing that freedom: the seven time-delay lenses yielded \(74.5^{+5.6}_{-6.1}\) in flat \(\Lambda\)CDM. The eight-lens [TDCOSMO 2025 analysis, v4, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03023v4) gave \(71.6^{+3.9}_{-3.3}\) when combined with Pantheon+; adding SLACS and SL2S gave \(74.3^{+3.1}_{-3.7}\).

These are not independent measurements to combine naively: lens samples overlap, and the supernova prior and kinematic treatment change. The strongest reading of the new work is improved precision with explicitly retained lens-model freedom. Comparing the paper's rounded “3%” title to a different historical dataset combination would obscure the actual gain.

#### 6. Fermi-LAT searches for nearby moving gamma-ray sources

**Dou Lai and James Unwin — [arXiv:2610.14058v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14058v1).** Submitted 6 October; preprint announced in the 14 October listing.

A ten-year, 1–100 GeV search links annual photon clusters into tracks moving at 0.25–0.8 degrees per year. For a transverse speed of 200 km/s, this corresponds to distances of roughly 3,000–9,700 AU.

There are 577 candidate tracks versus \(533\pm27\) in time-scrambled skies. The empirical \(p\)-value is approximately **0.066**, so this is not a detection. The **90% upper limit is 80.8 recovered signal tracks**. After efficiency calibration, the strongest common-flux example permits fewer than 123 steady sources, assuming an \(E^{-2}\) spectrum, flux \(6.5\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\), and speed 0.25 degrees/year.

Recovered-track limits, incident source-population limits and primordial-black-hole abundance limits are different quantities. The latter additionally require emission and population assumptions. [Search and injection calibration](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14058v1).

##### History

[Koushiappas (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0606208) proposed gamma-ray proper motion as a discriminator for nearby dark-matter microhalos. The [Fermi-LAT Collaboration's 2018 primordial-black-hole search](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00100) already implemented proper-motion detection for 318 unassociated high-latitude catalog sources, obtaining a **99% confidence** local evaporation-rate limit of \(7.2\times10^3\,\mathrm{pc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}\).

The new contribution is a photon-level all-sky search for persistent tracks, rather than the first use of gamma-ray proper motion. Its 90% count limit cannot be ranked directly against the earlier 99% evaporation-rate limit: the selections, time evolution, spectral models and measured quantities differ. An evaporating black hole can brighten and harden, whereas a steady-source calibration imposes a different signal template. These distinctions matter before translating the result into a dark-matter constraint.

#### 7. COSI: a forecast for MeV dark-matter decay

**Robin Anthony-Petersen et al. — [arXiv:2610.17217v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17217v1).** Submitted 12 October; preprint, **forecast**.

All-sky detector-response simulations estimate COSI's two-year sensitivity to \(\chi\rightarrow\gamma\gamma\) for masses of 0.4–10 MeV. With an assumed Galactic NFW dark-matter halo, the energy-spectrum analyses project **95% one-sided lower limits on the partial lifetime reaching order \(10^{30}\) seconds**, principally below a few MeV.

Three-month simulations are scaled to two years using background-limited exposure scaling. Background normalization, instrumental lines and the spectral fitting window affect the result. A separate four-dimensional Compton-data analysis fixes the background and supplies an optimistic performance ceiling.

The projected lifetime is for the specified photon channel, and is not automatically the total lifetime if other decays exist. No observed exclusion or dark-matter signal is reported. [Model, backgrounds and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17217v1).

##### History

For two-photon decay, each photon has energy \(m_\chi/2\), and the flux scales with the line-of-sight integral of density divided by \(m_\chi\tau_{\gamma\gamma}\). This differs from annihilation's density-squared dependence. If the particle supplies only a fraction of dark matter, the inferred lifetime limit rescales with that fraction.

[Calore et al.'s 16-year INTEGRAL/SPI analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.06299v5) constrains two-photon decays across approximately 60 keV–16 MeV at 95% confidence, explicitly fitting a dark-matter spatial component. It shows why foreground correlations can strengthen or weaken limits relative to using residuals alone. The corrected version is the relevant reference. [Earlier COSI forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09310) used point-source sensitivities and restricted sky regions; the new work forward-models an extended halo over the full sky.

The claimed improvement is therefore an instrumental-analysis forecast. A strictly quantitative comparison should first align halo normalization, sky region, photon yield, confidence convention and background treatment. Spectral lines remain a particularly clean target, but instrumental activation lines must be controlled.

**Event:** 12 October 2026, **16:58:11.46 UTC**. IceCube issued a GOLD track alert at approximately RA 177.71°, Dec +8.95°. The reported false-alarm rate was **0.7647 events/year**; this is a rate statistic, not the posterior probability that this individual event is astrophysical. **13 October updates:** Fermi-LAT found no new source above five-sigma significance inside the 90% localization. Its one-day pre-alert flux upper limit above 100 MeV is **\(1.6\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\)** at 95% confidence, for the stated point-source position and spectral-index assumption. This is a specific time-window limit. [GCN 45941](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45941).

IceCube's companion search found no additional tracks within ±500 seconds and no significant excess in its two-day window. The quoted fluence sensitivities in that circular should not be relabeled as observed upper limits. No confirmed counterpart follows from these reports; gamma-ray non-detection also does not exclude an internally gamma-opaque source. [GCN 45946](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45946).

#### 2. CTA 102 enters a bright, hard gamma-ray state

Fermi-LAT's **13 October** telegram reports activity observed on **12 October**, with daily flux above 100 MeV of **\((6.7\pm0.8)\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\)**, approximately ten times its catalog average. The daily photon index is **\(1.86\pm0.07\)**, harder than its catalog value of about 2.30; errors quoted here are statistical.

The three-hour peak occurred at **15:00–18:00 UTC on 12 October**, and the collaboration requests multifrequency follow-up. This is a preliminary gamma-ray activity notice for an established blazar. It does not establish a neutrino association, including with IceCube-261012A. [ATel 18104](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=18104).

#### 3. GRB 261010A: updated energetics, with host ambiguity still consequential

This is a **13 October follow-up to the 10 October burst**, not a new trigger. Konus-Wind reports an initial-pulse spectral peak near **847 keV** and fluence **\((5.91\pm1.06)\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}}\)**.

The proposed redshifts lead to very different isotropic-equivalent energies: **\((7.1\pm1.3)\times10^{49}\) erg at \(z=0.070\)** versus **\((1.02\pm0.18)\times10^{52}\) erg at \(z=0.785\)**. Neither host hypothesis should silently be treated as settled. The initial pulse's spectral–energetic correlations favor a short/hard, Type-I-like interpretation in the circular, but this does not independently confirm a compact-binary merger or a gravitational-wave counterpart. [Konus-Wind: GCN 45945](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45945), referring to the two host proposals in [GCN 45912](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45912) and [45928](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45928).

### Science Radar

#### 1. How modified RNA changes reading-frame fidelity

**New institutional announcement: 13 October. Original peer-reviewed paper: 28 September 2026.** Poulis and colleagues investigate how N1-methylpseudouridine, used in engineered mRNA, can alter ribosomal reading-frame fidelity. Human-cell reporters, reconstituted translation and single-molecule measurements connect a susceptible sequence motif to destabilization of a ribosomal tRNA state and +1 frameshifting. Synonymous sequence redesign can suppress the unwanted translation product in the tested constructs.

The interesting advance is a mechanistic route to better sequence design. These experiments are not a clinical study of vaccine safety or effectiveness, and they do not establish a patient-level outcome. Keeping the paper's publication date separate from the announcement prevents a September result from being mislabeled as newly published on 13 October.

Sources: [Nature Communications, “Recoding by N1-methylpseudouridine guides rational mRNA vaccine design”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77796-3); [Max Planck Institute announcement](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/5341543/rn_2605).

#### 2. Equation discovery by improving both numerical tools and the model using them

**Pengze Li et al. — [arXiv:2610.17740v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17740v1), submitted 13 October; preprint.** The system first develops executable numerical tools from failed equation-fitting attempts, then trains a smaller model on successful tool-use trajectories. It reports **79.1%** accuracy on LSR-Synth at its \(10^{-2}\) fit-error tolerance and **63.8%** pooled accuracy on SRSD-Feynman.

The methodological distinction is useful: failures can arise because a necessary computational operation is missing or because the controller fails to select it. Ablations attribute much of the performance to the tool library, with an additional gain from training the controller.

Evaluation needs care. LSR-Synth participates in tool development; SRSD-Feynman and the cosmology panel supply more informative cross-benchmark checks. Formula-overlap filtering reduces a particular leakage risk but does not make benchmark success equivalent to discovering a new physical law from uncontrolled experimental data. This is promising automation of symbolic regression, awaiting broader scientific validation.
