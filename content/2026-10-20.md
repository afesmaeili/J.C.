#### Measurement of the neutrino cross section across the TeV scale using IceCube data

**Rasmi Hajjar, Mauricio Bustamante, Sergio Palomares-Ruiz.** [arXiv:2610.24068v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24068v1), submitted **16 October, 18:00:01 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Independent public-data analysis, not an IceCube Collaboration publication.

Can neutrino–nucleon interactions be measured continuously across the TeV regime? A Bayesian energy/zenith fit uses **305,735 upward-going events from 2011–2019**, including Earth propagation and marginalized flux/detector uncertainties. The reported quantity is the unweighted charged-current average \((\sigma_\nu+\sigma_{\bar\nu})/2\), per free isoscalar nucleon, divided by the CSMS Standard Model prediction:

| Neutrino energy [TeV] | Normalization; 68.27% HPD interval |
|---|---|
| 0.15–0.36 | \(1.11^{+0.12}_{-0.13}\) |
| 0.36–1.17 | \(1.22^{+0.24}_{-0.17}\) |
| 1.17–3.79 | \(1.11^{+0.15}_{-0.12}\) |
| 3.79–12.32 | \(0.92^{+0.15}_{-0.12}\) |
| 12.32–40 | \(1.01^{+0.15}_{-0.13}\) |

The neutrino/antineutrino ratio and inelasticity shapes are fixed to theory; neutral-current scaling follows charged-current scaling. A **16% Gaussian prior** anchors the first bin; other normalizations have uniform priors on [0,20]. Removing that anchor raises other bins by **20–30%**. The separate-species fit gives only **1.6σ** evidence for nonzero antineutrino scattering above 360 GeV—not a robust separation. [Full text, Tables 1–2 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24068v1).

##### History

The physical baseline is weak deep-inelastic scattering on quarks; flux uncertainty is entangled with cross section. [IceCube's 2017 Earth-absorption measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08119) fitted one scaling across **6.3–980 TeV**, obtaining \(1.30^{+0.21}_{-0.19}\) statistical and \(^{+0.39}_{-0.43}\) systematic relative to theory. [FASER's collider measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03186) supplies an independent production environment.

The directly checked [Super-Kamiokande analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10221v1) measures a **flux-averaged** \(\sigma/E_\nu=(0.51\pm0.11)\times10^{-38}\,\mathrm{cm^2\,GeV^{-1}}\) over **0.5–5 TeV**, also using accelerator information below its target bin. Its flux-weighted quantity must not be equated blindly with the unweighted average above. The new contribution is energy-resolved coverage, not the first TeV cross-section measurement; different weighting, energy ranges and priors preclude a single clean improvement factor.

#### Models of spectral bumps of primary cosmic rays and multi-messenger tests

**Qiang Yuan, Chuan Yue, Yi-Zhong Fan.** [arXiv:2610.24412v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24412v1), submitted **17 October, 03:25:48 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Comparative modeling, not a source discovery.

The question is whether spectral hardening and softening reflect acceleration, transport or a local source. GALPROP calculations compare all three against primary nuclei, boron, dipole anisotropy and diffuse gamma rays. The nearby-source example uses **250 pc**, **\(3.4\times10^5\) years** and an exponential rigidity cutoff of **30 TV**; these are model choices, not confidence bounds.

Opposing local/background dipoles can reproduce an amplitude minimum and phase reversal near **100 TeV**. However, the source direction is adjusted to the anisotropy data. The tested globally modified spectra produce more **0.1–10 TeV** diffuse gamma emission than the local-bump model, but all underproduce the comparison data. Local magnetic geometry, extra gamma components and imperfect species fits remain unresolved. The text is internally inconsistent about which model fares worst against boron; Figure 5 and the detailed secondary-nuclei discussion are more informative than the concluding shorthand. [Full text, Figures 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24412v1).

##### History

Rigidity \(R=pc/(Ze)\), rather than energy per particle, controls magnetic transport and often acceleration ceilings. A common rigidity cutoff produces charge-ordered spectral features—the Peters-cycle idea—but does not by itself locate the accelerator.

[Yue et al. (2019/2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12857), whose full discussion was checked, already compared source populations and a nearby component for the roughly 10-TV softening, using anisotropy to help distinguish them. Thus the nearby-source interpretation is established literature, not a newly invented explanation. An independently checked alternative, [Malkov and Moskalenko (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8506973/), instead reaccelerates pre-existing primaries **and secondaries** at a local stellar shock. These are physically different mechanisms. Updated composition and gamma-ray comparisons are useful discriminants, but neither a good spectral fit nor the tested dipole construction uniquely identifies Geminga or excludes all alternative transport geometries.

#### First observation of weak magnetism in neutron decay

**Miriam Camarasa-Domene, Martín González-Alonso, Martín Novoa-Brunet.** [arXiv:2610.26792v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26792v1), submitted **19 October, 17:40:03 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Combined-data inference.

The fit separates the recoil-order weak-magnetism coupling from the axial/vector ratio by combining neutron asymmetries, lifetime and superallowed nuclear-decay information. In the authors' convention, **dimensionless \(g_M\)** has conserved-vector-current prediction **4.71**. The profiled fit gives

\[g_M=4.47\pm0.87,\]

with a reported **5.1σ rejection of \(g_M=0\)**. Baseline inputs include bottle lifetime **878.36±0.43 s** and the updated \(^{26m}\)Al corrected value **\(\mathcal{F}t=3070.01\pm1.90\) s**. Experimental electron-energy windows enter the asymmetry recoil correction. Tested dataset variations give **5.0–6.1σ**.

This is not a new dedicated neutron experiment or evidence against the Standard Model. The inference assumes the stated interaction framework and nuclear/radiative corrections; it does not resolve the beam–bottle lifetime discrepancy. Simulated gains from energy-binned asymmetries are forecasts, not additional measured significance. [Full text, Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26792v1).

##### History

CVC links the weak vector current to the isovector electromagnetic current; magnetic moments therefore predict the recoil correction. The leading asymmetry alone is degenerate with the axial coupling, explaining why earlier precision measurements commonly inserted the CVC value rather than measuring it freely.

Crucially, [Falkowski et al., JHEP 02 (2024) 091](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.07688v3), already fitted weak magnetism. Its neutron-plus-superallowed fit gives **\(v^2C_M^+=3.6\pm1.1\)**, exceeding 3σ; adding mirror decays gives **3.86±0.87**, exceeding 4σ, but imports CVC information for nuclear-dependent contributions. These Wilson coefficients are not numerically identical to \(g_M\): the matching involves the vector normalization. The present claim is a stronger neutron-centered extraction using updated inputs and recoil averaging—not the first empirical indication of weak magnetism in any system. Remaining uncertainty includes the consistency of input datasets and nuclear corrections.

#### Constraints on the Cosmic Expansion Rate Using the DECam Local Volume Exploration Survey and the Fifth Gravitational Wave Transient Catalog

**Sean Patrick MacBride, Isaac McMahon, Danny Laghi, David Sánchez-Cid, Felipe Andrade-Oliveira, Marcelle Soares-Santos, Maciej Bilicki, Sarah Ferraiuolo, Surhud More, Rachel Gray, Gergely Dálya.** [arXiv:2610.25909v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25909v1), submitted **19 October, 07:12:32 UTC**; **submitted to A&A**, not yet a listed journal publication.

A joint population/cosmology analysis combines GWTC-5 distances with DELVE DR3 candidate-host galaxies, treating incompleteness and photometric-redshift bias. With **\(15.5<m_r<20\)**, luminosity weighting and an evolving luminosity function:

\[H_0=67.4^{+13.0}_{-11.2}\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\]

from dark sirens, or **\(70.6^{+8.7}_{-7.0}\)** in the same units with GW170817. Intervals are **16th–84th posterior percentiles**, not detection significances. The analysis uses FULLPOP-4.0 mass populations and Madau–Dickinson rate evolution. It cannot distinguish the early-/late-Universe expansion-rate determinations. Numerical cosmological-prior limits are not restated in the inspected text; robustness to those limits was not established here. [Full text, Sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25909v1).

##### History

Standard sirens obtain luminosity distance from gravitational-wave amplitude, but require redshift information. Without a uniquely identified host, galaxy-catalog completeness and population assumptions become part of the inference—not mere bookkeeping.

The direct predecessor, [McMahon et al., DES Y6 plus GWTC-4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04766v2), published in MNRAS and revised **7 July 2026**, reported **\(71.7^{+22.7}_{-18.3}\)** from dark sirens and **\(73.1^{+11.9}_{-8.5}\)** with GW170817, in \(\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\). It restricted catalog redshifts to **0.05–0.50**. The new work changes both the GW dataset and galaxy treatment, so attributing the entire precision change to increased sky area would be unjustified. Wider coverage is not automatically more informative when photometry is poorer. These are overlapping-data analyses, not independent measurements to multiply together; the broad posterior still cannot adjudicate the Hubble tension.

#### Lorentz-Invariance Violation with neutrinos from gamma-ray bursts using IceCube High-Energy Starting Events 12-year dataset

**Mathieu Lamoureux, José Manuel Carmona, José Luis Cortés, Ardit Gkioni, Antoine Kouchner, Maykoll A. Reyes.** [arXiv:2610.25214v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25214v1), submitted **18 October, 12:15:06 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed.

A likelihood search compares **164 HESE events** with **746 redshift-measured GRBs**, including directional uncertainties, assumed energy resolutions, source-frame emission offsets uniform over **−350 to +150 s**, and superluminal-neutrino decay. GRBs receive equal weights. For linear dispersion, \(\eta=M_P/\Lambda_{\rm LIV}\), the fit favors **\(\eta=21\)**, or **\(\Lambda_{\rm LIV}=5.8\times10^{17}\) GeV**. Scrambled-data significance is only **\(p=0.031\), 1.9σ one-sided**. This is neither a detection nor a lower limit on the LIV scale. Quadratic dispersion shows no preference. The assumed energy response and emission model matter; universal subluminal propagation is discussed as a distinct, nonstandard alternative. [Full text, Sections III–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25214v1).

##### History

A propagation-delay interpretation requires both a real source association and consistent particle kinematics. Earlier GRB/neutrino correlation claims motivate the search, but reusing overlapping events is not independent confirmation.

The decisive comparison is [Bustamante et al., *Electron stability constrains neutrino time delays*, 1 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01339). Subluminal neutrinos can permit \(e^-\to e^-\nu\bar\nu\), so removing unstable superluminal neutrinos does not eliminate all consistency constraints. For the same **linear, isotropic, flavor-universal coefficient magnitude**, that work quotes **\(|\eta_1|\lesssim1.4\times10^{-3}\) at 95% CL** from electrons inferred to have **2.34±0.145 PeV** energy in a leptonic interpretation of the Crab's **1.12±0.09 PeV** gamma ray. The confidence convention propagates the electron-energy uncertainty one-sided; the conservative degradation parameter **\(\rho=10\)** is fixed, not marginalized. Direct **15.5±1.55 TeV** H.E.S.S. electron candidates yield the much weaker **\(|\eta_1|\lesssim6.0\times10^3\)** under that prescription.

Thus the strong constraint is astrophysically conditional, not a model-independent laboratory exclusion. Nevertheless, the conventional EFT interpretation faces serious tension. A universally modified, non-EFT framework changes the assumptions rather than simply improving the fit. A weak correlation alone cannot establish quantum-gravity propagation.

#### GRB261017A: hard prompt spectrum and an ambiguous optical candidate

[Konus-Wind, GCN 45990](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45990), issued **19 October at 14:57:57 UTC**, reports the **17 October, 16:05:06.065 UTC** trigger. The multipeaked burst lasts approximately **10.8 s**, with emission reaching roughly **3 MeV**. These are preliminary spectral measurements, not redshift-derived energetics.

Other-filter upper limits do not rule out a counterpart.

#### MMX has launched: a new route to testing the origin of Mars's moons

[JAXA's launch-result release](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2026/10/20261020-1_e.html), dated **20 October JST**, confirms liftoff at **04:41:03 JST on 20 October**, equivalent to **19 October 2026, 19:41:03 UTC / 21:41:03 CEST**. H3 flight 10 separated MMX about **71 minutes 13 seconds** later and placed it on the planned trajectory. Launch and separation are completed milestones; sampling and return are not.

The scientific question is whether Phobos and Deimos are captured bodies or products of a giant impact involving Mars. [NASA's mission account, 15 October, updated 16 October](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/planetary-science-division/nasa-to-help-unlock-mysteries-of-mars-moons-in-upcoming-mission/), describes **MEGANE**, which uses gamma rays and neutrons to infer surface elemental composition, and complementary pneumatic and coring samplers. The plan targets **Mars arrival in August 2027**, Phobos sampling **no earlier than December 2028**, and Earth return in **2031**.

Laboratory analysis of returned material could discriminate formation histories more directly than remote spectra alone. That prospect remains conditional on successful cruise, operations in Phobos's weak gravity, representative sampling and safe return; a successful launch is not yet a successful sample-return mission.

#### Catch-up: healthy coral clones can seed recovery after bleaching

**Research and institutional announcement: 14 October 2026**, not a new 20 October discovery. Daniel S. Collins and colleagues' peer-reviewed [*Rapid coral bleaching recovery via symbiont transfer from healthy corals*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh1580), Science Advances, is documented in [Stanford's detailed research account](https://sustainability.stanford.edu/news/stanford-scientists-discover-way-reverse-coral-bleaching-record-speed/).

In an initial laboratory experiment with **ten healthy/bleached fragment pairs from the same animal**, all **five touching pairs** recovered within **seven weeks**, while the bleached fragments in **five separated pairs** died. Tissue fusion permitted transfer of symbiotic algae within **three days**; approximately **one week of contact** could seed recovery that continued after separation.

The interesting mechanism is reseeding a functioning symbiosis, followed by proliferation inside the damaged host—not simply transferring enough algae to recolor it. But genetically matched healthy donors are a major practical constraint, and this is not a demonstrated reef-scale treatment or protection against repeated heat stress. **Coverage limitation:** the journal full text could not be retrieved after retry; this Radar assessment uses the originating institution's account, not an asserted full-paper review. The small initial comparison should not be generalized into a universal recovery probability.
