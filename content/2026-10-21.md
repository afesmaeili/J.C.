#### The Electron-Recoil Response Tail and the LZ High-Energy Event Background

**Triparno Bandyopadhyay, Rajarajeswari Ramasamy, Nishitha TP.** [arXiv:2610.27105v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27105v1), submitted **19 October, 18:05:06 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Independent reconstruction, not an LZ Collaboration analysis.

The paper asks whether LZ's published calibration summary determines the exceedingly small electron-recoil (ER) leakage probability used near its unusual high-energy event. The authors rebuild the continuum ER component from the released NEST configuration. In the event's \(500<\mathrm{S1c}<600\) phd comparison region, LZ quotes **\(0.0106\pm0.0008\) systematic counts from all backgrounds**. The reconstruction finds that the nominal ER term relies on a per-decay low-tail probability near **\(1.3\times10^{-7}\)**, evaluated \(4.1\sigma\) below the mean of the fixed-\(N_q\) charge response.

The central result is an identifiability test. Families of distributions are constructed to reproduce the executable model's mean, variance and standardized skewness exactly, and its 10%, 50% and 90% charge quantiles at about percent accuracy, yet their relevant tail probabilities span more than five orders of magnitude. Within that construction, a **0.14%** maximum quantile shift makes ER leakage 10% of LZ's quoted total background; **0.47%** makes ER alone equal that total; and **1.10%** yields 0.1 expected ER counts. These are mathematical counterexamples, **not** physically preferred liquid-xenon response models, confidence intervals or evidence that the background actually is larger.

The study also identifies an executable-versus-printed specification issue: taking a printed supplement parameter literally would give about 0.60 ER counts, inconsistent with the reported total, so the authors use a specific repository implementation while stating that they have not established it is the exact collaboration build. A direct response-tail measurement around \(N_q\simeq5\times10^3\) down to \(10^{-5}\), or shape fits with roughly \(5.4\times10^2\) to \(2.9\times10^4\) calibration events per fixed-\(N_q\) slice for the illustrated alternatives, could constrain the ambiguity. Tritium and \(^{14}\)C calibration cuts stop below S1c=500 phd; \(^{212}\)Pb reaches the event region. The paper does **not** propagate a modified ER term through LZ's two-dimensional likelihood or revise the reported 2.6σ global significance. [Full text, Tables 2, 7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27105v1).

##### History

The baseline is the [LZ extended-energy search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02823v1): one event in **2.84 tonne-years**, reconstructed as a \(248\pm23\,(\mathrm{stat})\pm23\,(\mathrm{sys})\) keV nuclear recoil, with a maximum local significance of **3.4σ** and **2.6σ global** after a look-elsewhere correction. Those numbers refer to a profile likelihood over multiple signal spectra; they are not simply a Poisson conversion of 0.0106 counts.

Earlier xenon calibration work had already shown that recombination fluctuations control ER-band width and skewness. The [LUX discrimination study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06304) measured ER acceptance below \(10^{-5}\) near S1=120 detected photons at 50% NR acceptance, but in a different detector, coordinate and energy regime; it cannot be transplanted as an LZ bound. In argon, [DEAP-3600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08042) directly constrained pulse-shape leakage below \(1.2\times10^{-7}\) at 90% confidence over 16–33 keVee, again using different physics. Its relevance is methodological: rare-event claims require either tail data or a model with propagated tail-shape uncertainty. The new paper exposes a missing public constraint, not a contradiction with internal LZ calibration and not proof that the candidate is an ER.

#### Searching for Dense Perturbers in Cold Dark Matter Simulations

**Xingyu Zhang, Hai-Bo Yu, Demao Kong, Ethan O. Nadler.** [arXiv:2610.27236v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27236v1), submitted **19 October, 20:02:50 UTC**. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Dark-matter-only simulation comparison.

Can ordinary cold-dark-matter (CDM) assembly produce objects as compact as three recently inferred perturbers? The authors analyze the MW004 zoom simulation, a \(7.2\times10^{11}h^{-1}M_\odot\) Milky-Way analogue with particle mass \(4.4\times10^3h^{-1}M_\odot\), selecting **5,080** surviving subhalos above \(10^6h^{-1}M_\odot\). Because the 0.16 kpc force-smoothing scale does not resolve the relevant \(\sim10\) pc cores, they anchor each progenitor's NFW scale parameters at infall and fit a tidally truncated NFW profile, then extrapolate inward. A 436-subhalo LMC analogue provides a host-mass check.

For the B1938+666-\(\mathcal V\) lens perturber at \(z\simeq0.881\), none of the simulated profiles reaches even the conservative no-point-mass NFW reconstruction. At matched projected mass \(M_{\rm cyl}(<90\,\mathrm{pc})=(1.1\text{–}1.2)\times10^6M_\odot\), the simulations accumulate too little mass inside \(\sim50\) pc; the inferred concentration is about **two orders of magnitude** above the simulation maximum at corresponding mass. For GD-1 and the proposed Fornax 6 perturber, simulated \(10^6\text{–}10^7M_\odot\) halos have \(M(<10\,\mathrm{pc})\) at least **five times** below the viable observational ranges. Gaussian fits to simulated concentration tails would place the adopted GD-1 and Fornax 6 lower edges at roughly 9σ and 11σ, but those sigma values describe a fitted simulation distribution, not observational discovery significances.

Limitations are decisive: this is primarily one collisionless Milky-Way zoom, with inner structure extrapolated more than an order of magnitude below force resolution; baryons and nonstandard early formation histories are absent; and each observational perturber has substantial model dependence. A time-dependent Galactic potential may mimic the GD-1 gap/spur, Fornax 6 need not sit in a dark halo, and B1938+666-\(\mathcal V\) could be a stripped composite halo plus central black hole. The result is therefore tension with the sampled CDM population, not an exclusion of CDM. [Full text, Figures 1–5 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27236v1).

##### History

The established CDM baseline is a broad concentration–mass distribution, with tidal stripping removing outer material while leaving cuspy central structure comparatively resilient. Earlier stellar-stream analyses inferred that GD-1 encountered compact perturbers, but [Banik et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02662) found the overall \(10^5\text{–}10^9M_\odot\) inner-halo substructure abundance consistent with CDM and emphasized age and baryonic-perturber degeneracies. A single gap is not a census of a dark population.

Strong-lensing work has sharpened the compactness problem. [Şengül and Dvorkin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10635) measured an effective slope \(\gamma=1.96\pm0.12\) for the earlier B1938+666 perturber, about a 2σ CDM outlier in their mocks. [Tajalli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07944v2) found analogues for one B1938+666 detection near the edge of TNG50's distribution, while also showing that inferred redshift and profile choices change the conclusion. The newer million-solar-mass \(\mathcal V\) object is more extreme; proposed explanations include core-collapsed self-interacting dark matter and an unusually stripped CDM halo with an intermediate-mass black hole. The present simulation search makes the CDM rarity quantitative within its sample, but the remaining uncertainty is shared between unresolved formation tails and the observational reconstructions themselves.

#### Hidden engines in tidal disruption events: multimessenger signatures in a lepto-hadronic framework

**D. Kantzas, D. A. Langis, I. Liodakis, A. Paggi.** [arXiv:2610.28182v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28182v1), submitted **20 October, 13:21:01 UTC**. **Accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics.** Source-tailored modeling and detectability study.

The authors ask which particle distributions and radiation environments let tidal-disruption events (TDEs) produce escaping MeV–TeV photons and high-energy neutrinos. A one-zone lepto-hadronic code includes electron and proton acceleration, synchrotron and inverse-Compton radiation, \(pp\) and \(p\gamma\) pion production, Bethe–Heitler pairs, cascades and internal absorption. Exploratory families are built separately for AT2019dsg, AT2019ehz and ASASSN-14li, then compared with COSI, Fermi-LAT, CTAO and KM3NeT/ARCA reference sensitivities.

Hard proton spectra (\(p_p\simeq1.6\text{–}1.7\)) or high minimum proton Lorentz factors concentrate power near photomeson energies. For AT2019dsg, representative hadronic cases predict **0.25–0.32 IceCube** and **0.30–0.38 ARCA** muon-neutrino events over the adopted active interval, versus \(3\text{–}4\times10^{-6}\) in a neutrino-quiet GeV model. AT2019ehz gives **0.05–0.09 IceCube** and **0.06–0.11 ARCA** events in its hadronic cases. ASASSN-14li reaches **0.36–0.45 IceCube** and **0.41–0.51 ARCA** events in three hadronic benchmarks, while its lepton-dominated case is below \(10^{-3}\). These are expectation values under selected parameters, all below one event per source; they are not detections or confidence limits.

The gamma-ray outcome depends strongly on opacity: photon-rich coronæ enhance \(p\gamma\) interactions but can absorb TeV photons and reprocess energy toward X-ray/MeV bands, whereas transparent regions favor escaping GeV–TeV emission. The authors explicitly do not perform a common parameter grid or unique best fit; some reduced-\(\chi^2\) values are poor, source durations and geometries are imposed, and steady one-zone spectra omit time-dependent transport. CTAO/KM3NeT “reach” means crossing a reference sensitivity in a benchmark, not a forecasted joint detection probability. [Full text, Tables 1–5 and Figures 10–13](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28182v1).

##### History

The observational baseline is suggestive but not secure. [Stein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05340v2) reported IceCube-191001A near radio-emitting AT2019dsg about 150 days after optical peak, quoting post-trial chance probabilities of 0.5% for a coincident radio TDE and 0.2% for one at least as bright in bolometric flux. Those probabilities were selection-specific; one event did not establish TDEs as a source class. A subsequent [ANTARES search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15526) found no significant clustering from AT2019dsg or AT2019fdr and set flux/fluence limits.

Earlier models already showed that delayed neutrinos can emerge from X-ray targets with proton confinement, optical/UV targets, IR dust echoes or choked/outflow shocks. [Winter and Lunardini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11538) modeled three candidate associations time-dependently and emphasized that several zones reproduce \(\mathcal O(100\,\mathrm{day})\) delays. The new work adds a unified radiation–hadron code and concrete multi-instrument benchmarks for three well-observed TDEs. Its remaining uncertainty is chiefly physical degeneracy: the same sparse spectral energy distribution permits very different baryon loading, compactness, opacity and particle injection.

#### Constraints on Millimeter-wave Extragalactic Transient Populations from 4 Years of SPT-3G data

**S. Guns et al.** [arXiv:2610.28633v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28633v1), submitted **20 October, 17:59:56 UTC**. Preprint; 98 authors; no journal reference listed. Observational null result with population-model limits.

SPT-3G searched four 2019–2022 seasons over **1,500 deg²** for hour-to-day millimetre transients, targeting GRB reverse shocks. Difference maps at 95 and 150 GHz are filtered and searched jointly; an initial simultaneous 3σ cut in both bands implies a transient floor near **24 mJy**, and positive events are compared with negative-noise events through a two-band light-curve likelihood. No candidate extragalactic transient survives. Bright stellar flares are foregrounds rather than evidence that the search was insensitive.

Injected population models fold GRB rate, redshift, energy, Lorentz factor, cadence, weather-dependent noise and the actual detection pipeline. Using a Poisson construction with model-dependent thresholds, the paper reports 90% confidence upper limits. For a standard \(10^{51}\) erg burst with \(\Gamma=100\), the fraction carrying the modeled reverse shock is **\(f_{\rm RS}<0.57\)** and the corresponding Earth-pointed all-sky reverse-shock rate is **<1,226 yr\(^{-1}\)**. For the most detectable \(10^{52}\) erg, \(\Gamma=50\) “dirty” model, **\(f_{\rm RS}<0.09\)** and the rate is **<120 yr\(^{-1}\)**. Detection efficiencies for those two examples are only 1.6% and 17.3%, respectively.

These bounds condition on the synthesized light-curve family, luminosity, beaming, cosmic GRB rate and SPT selection. They do not constrain all millimetre transients, long forward shocks or reverse shocks with different microphysics. A null detection is not a statement that individual GRBs lack reverse shocks. [Full text, Table 3 and Figures 4–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28633v1).

##### History

The first untargeted SPTpol millimetre search found one uncertain candidate in one year over 100 deg². Later SPT-3G and ACT searches found Galactic stellar flares but no firm extragalactic population. The pre-existing population forecast by [Eftekhari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05494v2) predicted that wide CMB surveys could find tens to hundreds of long-GRB counterparts, dominated on sub-week scales by bright reverse shocks; some SPT-3G benchmarks were near ten detections per year.

The new injection/recovery calculation improves sensitivity by nearly a factor of two relative to that simplified forecast, yet four seasons still yield zero extragalactic events. The result therefore converts a model expectation into the first direct millimetre-population constraint of this type. It is comparable in outcome to the 2025 ACT single-observation search, which found no \(>5σ\) extragalactic event in five years, but survey cadences and detection functions differ, so no simple exposure-scaled improvement factor is warranted.

#### Catch-up/cross-list — Search for Compact Binary Coalescences with Sub-solar Mass Components in O4b

**LIGO Scientific Collaboration, Virgo Collaboration and KAGRA Collaboration.** [arXiv:2610.20276v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20276v1), submitted **14 October 2026**; unchanged v1, appearing as a 21 October cross-list. Preprint; no journal reference listed at the cutoff. Collaboration search.

Three matched-filter pipelines search LIGO–Virgo data from **10 April 2024 15:00 UTC to 28 January 2025 17:00 UTC** for binaries with detector-frame secondary mass \(0.2\text{–}1M_\odot\), primary mass \(0.2\text{–}10M_\odot\) and mass ratio 0.1–1. No significant signal is found. The loudest MBTA candidate, S241229bq, has pipeline-specific FAR \(0.0043\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\), nominal single-search 2.8σ significance and false-alarm probability 0.0027 over O4b, but GstLAL and PyCBC rank the same time as noise-like; 14 historical pipeline/run trials would further reduce its significance. S240422ed is likewise consistent with nonstationary noise and has lost its striking low-latency rank.

Combining O4a and O4b sensitivities, low-spin binary-black-hole merger-rate upper limits span about **50–5,000 Gpc\(^{-3}\) yr\(^{-1}\)** at 90% confidence across masses; the best mass-bin limit is **47.8 Gpc\(^{-3}\) yr\(^{-1}\)** in PyCBC. A fixed broad sub-solar neutron-star population gives **249 Gpc\(^{-3}\) yr\(^{-1}\)**. These are frequentist loudest-event limits with assumed mass/spin distributions, not object-abundance bounds by themselves.

For monochromatic primordial black holes (PBHs), the mapping uses specified early- and late-binary merger prescriptions. In the conservative early-binary model, \(f_{\rm PBH}=\rho_{\rm PBH}/\rho_{\rm DM}\) limits range from roughly **0.10–0.18** to **0.018–0.032** over the probed masses; late-binary assumptions only become informative enough to require \(f_{\rm PBH}<1\) above about \(0.62\text{–}0.74M_\odot\). For a dissipative-dark-matter black-hole population, conditional 90% limits reach **\(f_{\rm DBH}=(1.0\text{–}1.1)\times10^{-5}\)** at \(M_{\min}\simeq0.65\text{–}0.85M_\odot\), after marginalizing over mass-function slope and dynamic range. Binary formation and clustering dominate the model uncertainty. [Full text, Tables 1 and Figures 7–12](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20276v1).

##### History

Sub-solar black holes have no established stellar-formation channel, making a secure merger an unusually clean new-physics signature; sub-solar neutron stars are less impossible but require exotic formation or equations of state. Earlier LVK O1–O3 searches found no detections and set mass-dependent rate limits. The collaboration's [O4a search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12115) extended sensitivity and introduced explicit sub-solar-neutron-star injections; O4b adds 0.84 years versus 0.76 years in O4a, Virgo participation and pipeline changes, with up to 50% efficiency gains in places.

The combined O4a+b sensitive spacetime volume is about twice O4a alone, and the dissipative-dark-object fraction limit improves from \(1.8\text{–}2.5\times10^{-5}\) to \(1.0\text{–}1.1\times10^{-5}\) under the **same conditional construction**. That factor is comparable. It must not be compared directly with the numerically stronger-looking O4a highest-posterior-density contour, whose nonlocal two-dimensional definition was different. Likewise, PBH bounds from microlensing, CMB accretion and dynamical heating probe abundance without the same binary-formation uncertainty; the gravitational-wave result is most valuable as an independent merger-channel constraint, not automatically the strongest \(f_{\rm PBH}\) bound.

#### Catch-up/cross-list — Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments

**Miaochen Jin, Carlos A. Argüelles, Pablo Fernández-Menéndez, Iván Martínez-Soler.** [arXiv:2610.12208v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12208v1), submitted **8 October, 15:58:49 UTC**; unchanged v1, cross-listed into the 21 October hep-ph listing. Preprint; no journal reference listed. Independent analysis of public releases, not a joint collaboration result.

The unknown ordering is the sign of the atmospheric mass splitting: normal ordering places \(\nu_3\) above \(\nu_{1,2}\), inverted ordering below. The analysis simultaneously fits Super-Kamiokande I–V (**6,511.3 live days; 484.2 kt yr**), IceCube-DeepCore and six-string KM3NeT/ORCA data across 1,580 total bins. Atmospheric-flux and neutrino–water cross-section nuisance parameters are fully correlated across detectors; detector-response nuisances remain independent. The Super-K reconstruction alone uses 930 bins and a newly generated high-statistics Monte Carlo.

Assuming normal ordering and fixed \(\sin^2\theta_{13}=0.022\), the profiled one-dimensional results are

\[
\Delta m^2_{31}=2.44^{+0.06}_{-0.04}\times10^{-3}\;\mathrm{eV}^2,\qquad
\sin^2\theta_{23}=0.52^{+0.03}_{-0.04},\qquad
\delta_{CP}=1.30^{+0.24}_{-0.28}\pi.
\]

The quoted precisions are 2.2% and 6.1% for the first two quantities; \(\theta_{23}\) has no meaningful octant preference (\(\Delta\chi^2=0.3\)). Correlating the shared nuisances narrows the intervals by 19% and 9% relative to a naïve likelihood sum. The ordering difference is **\(\Delta\chi^2=11.0\)**. Because the hypotheses are non-nested, the authors calibrate with 1,500 NO and 4,397 IO pseudoexperiments; 2/4,397 IO trials are at least as extreme, reported as **99.95% confidence, 3.3σ**.

Important diagnostics temper the headline. The combined goodness-of-fit is \(p=0.011\), with ORCA6 contributing an upper-tail \(p=0.011\); a parameter-goodness test gives \(p=0.014\) (2.2σ) tension, concentrated between DeepCore and ORCA6. Finite public-MC statistics soften those goodness-of-fit discrepancies. Correlation choices, reconstructed Super-K response and fixed solar/reactor inputs matter, and a formal joint result with experiment-internal detector models could shift the significance. [Full text, Table 1 and Figures 1, 5, 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12208v1).

##### History

Atmospheric matter effects have long offered ordering sensitivity, but individual water/ice Cherenkov data sets produced hints rather than a determination. The [ORCA6 collaboration result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07015v2) used 433 kt yr with six detection units; the [IceCube 9.3-year DeepCore analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02163) measured \(\Delta m^2_{32}=2.40^{+0.05}_{-0.04}\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV}^2\) and \(\sin^2\theta_{23}=0.54^{+0.04}_{-0.03}\) under normal ordering, but neither alone supplied this significance.

Global fits are a different baseline because they combine reactor and accelerator information. [NuFIT 6.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05380v2) found nearly equal ordering fits without Super-K's atmospheric \(\chi^2\) table and only \(\Delta\chi^2=6.1\) for normal ordering after including it. The new 3.3σ claim is therefore a substantive independent atmospheric-only combination, not a final resolution: it reuses public data that the collaborations have already analyzed, and its shared-systematic model both creates the gain and supplies a principal vulnerability. Full ORCA, the IceCube Upgrade, SK-Gd and JUNO provide the necessary independent tests.

#### EP261015a: magnitude correction removes the apparent large rebrightening

### Science Radar

#### A 1-gram wireless mesoscope images cortex-wide calcium activity in freely behaving mice

A peer-reviewed [Nature paper published 19 October](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11293-x) introduces **Airscope**, a 1.0 g head-mounted wireless fluorescence mesoscope with **6 mm field of view, 4 μm lateral resolution and 12.5 Hz** acquisition. The authors report calcium imaging from **more than 8,500 segmented neurons per animal**, and simultaneous multi-animal recordings in naturalistic arenas, swimming and social-competition tasks. Five mice yielded 21,397 neurons in one demonstration; dual devices also recorded cortex and spinal cord.

This is principally an enabling instrument result. One-photon calcium fluorescence is an indirect, temporally filtered proxy for spiking and is biased toward superficial accessible tissue; the behavioral and causal examples are demonstrations rather than a general map of mouse cognition. Peer review led to independent field-of-view calibration and explicit neuron-count reporting; the open hardware, data and analysis reduce—but do not eliminate—segmentation and behavioral-decoding validation concerns. The paper was accepted **12 October** and is an early citable version subject to editorial correction.

#### A proposed “circadian depression” profile is associated with severity and self-reported treatment response

A peer-reviewed [British Journal of Psychiatry article published online 20 October](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2026.10798) analyzes cross-sectional Australian Genetics of Depression Study data. Participants meeting at least three of six self-reported features—social jetlag, seasonality, delayed sleep midpoint, evening chronotype, morning tiredness and hypersomnia—were labeled circadian subtype. Of **11,380** adults with depression, **2,604 (23%)** met the rule.

Compared with the residual group, the subtype reported earlier onset (**18.68 vs 23.20 years**) and more severe clinical features. Self-rated efficacy was lower for SSRIs (**OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.82–0.94**) and SNRIs (**OR 0.89, 0.83–0.97**). Polygenic-score associations included ADHD (**OR 1.11, 1.06–1.17**), major depression (**1.11, 1.06–1.16**), bipolar disorder (**1.09, 1.04–1.14**), BMI, triglycerides, interleukin-6 and insulin resistance.

The study does not validate a diagnostic category or show that circadian treatment works. The rule was constructed in the same enriched, mostly female Australian cohort; sleep and medication response were self-reported; cross-sectional associations cannot establish causality; and polygenic-score odds ratios are small and ancestry-dependent. Independent prospective replication, objective circadian measures and randomized treatment-stratification tests are required before clinical use.
