#### 1. A magnetic tension challenges heavy r-process production in collapsars

**Ore Gottlieb, Danat Issa and Jonatan Jacquemin-Ide — [arXiv:2610.31758v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31758v1).** Submitted **22 October, 18:00:04 UTC**. Theoretical physical-bound argument, not a yield measurement.

Heavy r-process nuclei require neutron-rich ejecta, conventionally (Y_e\lesssim0.25), but collapsar gas must first neutronize in the disk and then escape. The authors evolve (Y_e) down an inflow trajectory while choosing, at every radius, the temperature that maximizes net neutronization. That deliberately favorable construction gives a thermal-history-independent lower bound. They then impose magnetic-flux and jet-power conditions needed to unbind material, and compare the analytic result with two general-relativistic MHD simulations.

For an optimistic (3M_\odot) black hole, universal unbinding radius (R_{\rm unb}=10r_g), spin 0.3 and upper representative GRB accretion rate \(\dot M_{\rm BH}\lesssim0.4M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}\), the minimum permitted electron fraction is **(Y_e\gtrsim0.33)**. At dimensionless horizon fluxes ϕ = 20, 30, 40 and 50, the corresponding bounds are about **0.33, 0.44, 0.48 and 0.49**. Weak fields permit neutronization but struggle to eject against fallback; strong fields unbind gas while thickening the disk and shortening the weak-interaction time. Rare extreme engines, including direct-horizon collapsars, remain possible exceptions. [Full methods and simulation comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31758v1).

The bound assumes midplane gas, temperature optimized for neutronization and an optimistic ejection radius. It does not calculate a population-integrated abundance and does not apply to neutron-star-merger ejecta. A future direct-horizon GRMHD calculation with composition transport is the relevant stress test.

##### History

[Siegel, Barnes and Metzger (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00098) simulated neutron-rich collapsar-disk outflows and argued that collapsars might supply **more than 80%** of cosmic r-process material. [Brauer et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15837) showed that stochastic yields around (0.07M_\odot) per event could reproduce metal-poor stellar [Eu/Fe] scatter. Those were yield and chemical-evolution claims conditional on ejecting neutron-rich disk material. The new paper attacks that physical prerequisite and therefore conflicts with the standard heavy-yield premise rather than with the abundance bookkeeping.

Neutron-star mergers already have direct multimessenger evidence through GW170817 and remain sufficient in principle, while collapsar optical/near-infrared searches have not established a heavy-r-process component. The remaining uncertainty is concentrated in unusual engine geometries and high accretion rates, not in the authors' claim of a universal mathematical no-go theorem.

#### 2. Reconnection changes the magnetic routes by which Galactic cosmic rays escape

**Antoine Baldacchino-Jordan, Michal Hanasz, Vinod Pisharody, Dominik Wóltański and Artur Gawryszczak — [arXiv:2610.32716v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32716v1).** Submitted **23 October, 14:35:33 UTC**; submitted to ApJ Letters. Numerical transport study.

Gravity-stratified MHD boxes contain thermal gas, spectrally resolved carbon and boron nuclei in CRESP, and spectrally unresolved protons. Cosmic rays diffuse anisotropically with (D_\parallel=3\times10^{28}\ {m cm^2\,s^{-1}}) at 10 GV and rigidity slope δ = 0.3. Resistive MHD supplies a subgrid description of unresolved turbulent magnetic dissipation, with magnetic diffusivity η = 0, 30, 60 or 100 pc² Myr⁻¹. The comparison averages 210–500 Myr.

Nonzero η regenerates a more horizontal halo field, inhibiting vertical escape and increasing disk cosmic-ray energy. The simulated B/C maximum is about **0.17** near 1 GeV per nucleon in ideal MHD, **0.19–0.24** at η = 30, and **0.24–0.27** at η = 60–100 pc² Myr⁻¹. Greater confinement raises cosmic-ray pressure and outflows; hadronic gamma-ray luminosity becomes nonmonotonic at the largest η because the outflow also lowers gas density. [Equations and diagnostics](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32716v1).

This is qualitative evidence that topology and local dynamics affect the effective escape time. The boxes omit a full Galactic geometry, cosmic-ray streaming, thermal/stellar feedback and spectrally resolved protons, so they do not predict a gamma-ray spectrum. The adopted δ = 0.3 is also shallower than values near 0.5 often favored by global fits. η is a subgrid control parameter, not a measurement of the Milky Way reconnection rate.

##### History

The B/C ratio became a standard grammage and diffusion diagnostic because boron is mainly secondary. [Strong and Moskalenko's GALPROP framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9807150) fitted B/C and radioactive ratios with smooth, Galaxy-scale diffusion and reacceleration, while modern AMS-02 precision data expose departures from a single featureless power law. Those approaches infer transport coefficients from local spectra under a chosen global model.

The new simulations keep the microscopic diffusion law fixed and change magnetic topology, showing that different B/C curves can emerge from the same input (D_\parallel). This does not overturn AMS constraints or supply a stronger diffusion measurement; it identifies an environmental degeneracy. Whether the effect survives in a full galaxy with resolved feedback and streaming is still open.

#### 3. A time-dependent diffuse foreground can mimic the Galactic-center excess

**Isabel S. Sands and Philip F. Hopkins — [arXiv:2610.32845v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32845v1).** Submitted **23 October, 16:19:38 UTC**. Simulation proof of concept.

Two FIRE Milky-Way analogues with MHD and time-dependent, spectrally resolved cosmic-ray transport are converted into gamma-ray maps. The authors subtract a low-flux time-averaged baseline from instantaneous snapshots, using 10° and 20° regions and several radial cuts. The simulated interval extends from roughly 460 to 20 Myr lookback; the central-kiloparsec transport time is of order **1 Myr**, comparable with changes in the interstellar medium.

Residuals from **0.1–100 GeV** sometimes reach several times the measured Galactic-center excess and, in selected snapshots, resemble its spectrum and morphology. The result demonstrates that temporally fluctuating ordinary-source emission can generate a GCE-like residual even when the baseline model is internally consistent. [Maps, spectra and transport setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32845v1).

It is not a statistical look-elsewhere calculation: only two galaxies are used, no global probability is reported, and Fermi-LAT data are not refitted with simulation-derived templates. The simulated centers form stars **one to two orders of magnitude faster** than the Milky Way's Central Molecular Zone, making their absolute gamma-ray spectra about an order of magnitude too bright. The study expands the diffuse-model uncertainty budget; it does not identify the real excess as a transient or exclude dark matter or millisecond pulsars.

##### History

The excess has long admitted dark-matter-annihilation and unresolved-source interpretations. Statistical claims for point-source structure were weakened when [Leane and Slatyer (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12370) showed that unmodeled north–south asymmetry can create a spurious point-source preference. Conversely, [Song et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05449) found a robust preference for a boxy bulge template over dark-matter templates across improved diffuse models.

Both lines already made foreground modeling central. Sands and Hopkins add a missing dimension: time variability in cosmic-ray sources and transport. The decisive next comparison is to pass simulated skies through the actual Fermi template pipeline and ask, with a Milky-Way-matched central star-formation history, how often a residual as spectrally and spatially similar as the observed GCE occurs.

#### 4. GWTC-5 favors three mass-dependent black-hole spin populations

**Asad Hussain, Maximiliano Isi and Aaron Zimmerman — [arXiv:2610.32935v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32935v1).** Submitted **23 October, 17:31:43 UTC**. Hierarchical Bayesian population inference.

The analysis uses **259 binary-black-hole events** in GWTC-5 with false-alarm rate below 1 yr⁻¹. Three flexible components have separate mass-ratio, component-spin-magnitude and tilt distributions, with mixing fractions that vary with primary mass. Injection campaigns encode selection effects. Truncated Gaussian mixtures stabilize event likelihoods when a population component becomes very narrow, and NUTS samples the hierarchical posterior.

The low-spin component has χ₁ and χ₂ near **(0.02^{+0.03}_{-0.02})**, dominates around (35M_\odot) with fraction **(0.8\pm0.1)**, and is favored over a nonzero-spin version by (log_{10}B=6.1^{+0.2}_{-0.3}). That Bayes factor tests zero spin *inside this component*; it is not evidence for three versus two populations. A medium component near χ ≈ 0.2 is aligned and matters mainly below (20M_\odot). A broad, high-spin component with χ₁ around **(0.7^{+0.3}_{-0.4})** dominates above (60M_\odot). A fourth component becomes degenerate rather than distinct. [Model, priors and posterior checks](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32935v1).

Formation labels remain hypotheses. Low aligned spins may indicate efficient angular-momentum loss in isolated binaries; broad tilts and high spins can arise dynamically or through hierarchical mergers, but the population fit does not prove either channel. Above (100M_\odot) the result is prior-sensitive and only two events carry much information.

##### History

[GWTC-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27223) enlarged the catalog and changed the statistical base for population work. On GWTC-4, the same authors found [two mass-dependent spin components](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24281), with a low-spin regime below a broad (35–70M_\odot) transition and a rapidly spinning high-mass component. The new medium-spin population is the main structural addition; the low/high trend is a confirmation with more data and a richer model.

Comparisons of Bayes factors across these papers are not simple because the catalogs, priors, mixture parameterizations and likelihood estimators differ. The astrophysical uncertainty is whether the statistical components map one-to-one onto formation channels; metallicity, common-envelope physics, dynamical assembly and hierarchical mergers can overlap in mass and spin.

#### 5. LZ's high-recoil window probes atmospheric-neutrino up-scattering

**D. Aristizabal Sierra, V. De Romeri, D. K. Papoulias and I. M. Shoemaker — [arXiv:2610.32747v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32747v1).** Submitted **23 October, 14:57:33 UTC**. Independent phenomenological reinterpretation, not an LZ Collaboration result.

The input is LZ's **2.84 tonne-year** extended nuclear-recoil sample from **5.4–270 keV**, including one event at (248\pm23_{\rm stat}\pm23_{\rm sys}) keV. The authors approximate it with two bins: 5.4–225 keV, where 1,712 background events are expected and no excess appears, and 225–270 keV, where the reported background is 0.0106 events and one event occurred. Standard atmospheric coherent scattering contributes only about **0.11** and **(10^{-6})** events, respectively.

They calculate νN → χN for a sterile fermion of mass (m_\chi), using universal scalar, vector or axial mediator couplings (g_a) with mediator mass (m_X), plus a photon transition dipole (μ_\nu). The 90% confidence contours use Δχ² = 4.61 in each two-parameter plane, with other model parameters fixed as shown. The null lower-energy bin opens parameter space above roughly **50–60 MeV** and reaches final-state masses near or above **1 GeV**, beyond low-energy solar, reactor and stopped-pion kinematics. [Operators, likelihood and exclusion figures](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32747v1).

For scalar, vector and axial models, fitting the lone high-recoil event pushes the effective scale (m_X/g_X) to about **1.41 GeV**, the authors' perturbativity boundary; the dipole gives a poor fit. Up-scattering is therefore not compelling. The 248-keV recoil lies near a xenon nuclear-form-factor minimum, and omitted incoherent nucleon scattering can matter. A two-bin public reconstruction cannot replace LZ's event-level S1c/S2c likelihood.

##### History

The [LZ Collaboration's extended-window search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02823) reported the exposure, background model and single high-recoil event while setting dark-matter limits; it did not claim a sterile-fermion signal. Earlier neutrino-up-scattering bounds come from solar-neutrino direct detection, COHERENT and reactor CEνNS at low masses, accelerator beams such as LSND/DUNE, and LEP at collider momentum transfer. These constrain different coupling combinations and mediator assumptions.

For the dipole portal, the paper's photon-only LZ curve can exceed LSND or photon-only LEP reach above (m_\chi\sim100) MeV, while a gauge-complete LEP photon-plus-Z treatment is stronger. For scalar/vector/axial mediators there is no single earlier number to divide into an improvement factor: the result is a two-dimensional (g_a)-versus-(m_X) contour that shifts with (m_\chi), quark/lepton universality and fixed mediator choices. The robust advance is access to heavier kinematics; the constraint remains model-dependent.

#### 6. Can self-interactions explain unusually dense lensing perturbers?

**Jonathan Tekverk, Duncan K. Adams, Simon Birrer, Rouven Essig and Daniel Gilman — [arXiv:2610.31881v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31881v1).** Submitted **22 October, 20:22:44 UTC**. Semi-analytic model comparison, not a dark-matter detection or exclusion.

CDM merger trees are coupled to a gravothermal SIDM evolution model and projected into enclosed masses and deflection-density slopes. The fiducial velocity-dependent interaction has normalization σ₀/(m_\chi=147.1\ {m cm^2\,g^{-1}}) and turnover speed (w=120\ {m km\,s^{-1}}). Predictions are compared with perturbers inferred in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006 and JVAS B1938+666.

Across an entire assumed (10^{13}M_\odot) host halo, the mean numbers matching the published posterior regions are approximately:

| Lens perturber | CDM | cored SIDM | core-collapsed SIDM |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PJ011646 | 0.002 | 0.6 | none matching |
| SDSS J0946+1006 | none in 900 realizations | 0.004 | 3 |
| JVAS B1938+666 detection V | 0.2 | 20 | 220 |

These are model-predicted abundances anywhere in the virial volume, not probabilities of observing perturbers near the lensed images. Among tested sets, reproducing all three requires roughly (w\gtrsim100\ {m km\,s^{-1}}) and σ₀/(m_\chi\gtrsim100\ {m cm^2\,g^{-1}}). [Semi-analytic machinery and sensitivity tests](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31881v1).

The calculation is dark-matter-only. Baryonic contraction is especially relevant to the (5.6\times10^{10}M_\odot) J0946 progenitor, which would plausibly contain stars not included in the lens model. Late core collapse, tidal survival, host mass and the lens-inference posteriors all carry large uncertainty. Thus the preferred interaction region is a benchmark, not a confidence interval.

##### History

Strong-lensing imaging first established sensitivity to otherwise dark substructure, but individual inferences can depend strongly on the macromodel and line-of-sight treatment. [Minor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10627) found J0946 unusually concentrated: less than 1% of comparable CDM mock views achieved its steep slope, and those analogues were too luminous. [Amvrosiadis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21212) inferred the PJ011646 subhalo at about 5.8σ relative to a multipole lens model, with (M_{200}=2.78^{+0.43}_{-0.66}\times10^{10}M_\odot) and concentration (30^{+5}_{-7}).

The new work asks whether one SIDM microphysics choice can populate all three posterior regions; it finds a qualitative yes more often than CDM. It neither reanalyzes the imaging nor marginalizes over baryons. A joint forward analysis of lens images, luminous structure, line-of-sight halos and cosmological hydrodynamics is needed before treating these counts as evidence for σ/(m_\chi).

#### 1. EP261023a: a fast X-ray transient with an unsettled optical candidate

Einstein Probe's WXT saw **EP261023a** begin at **23 October, 04:02:14 UTC** and remain bright for about 64 s. Its 90% localization was a 2.749-arcmin radius at RA 3.994°, Dec +51.589°. A fixed-Galactic-column power law gave photon index (1.3\pm0.5) and unabsorbed 0.5–4 keV flux (2.0^{+0.6}_{-0.4}\times10^{-9}\ {m erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}). FXT found an uncatalogued X-ray source with a 10-arcsec error circle and flux (8.5^{+2.2}_{-1.8}\times10^{-13}\ {m erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}), but the association remained uncertain; a Gaia star at about 1.5 kpc lies inside. [Discovery circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46014).

NOT detected a source at 63.7 min with (r=22.71\pm0.13) mag, while Pan-STARRS already shows a faint red object at that position, possibly a host. [Candidate report](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46015). Later limits were (R>22.2) at 0.414 d and (J>19.1) Vega at 31.87 h. Different filters, calibrations and magnitude systems prevent a secure fading claim. This remains a candidate counterpart, not a classified extragalactic transient.

#### 2. EP261025a: a second 130-second Einstein Probe transient

WXT recorded **EP261025a** on **25 October from 07:19:57.4 UTC** for about 130 s at RA 2.554°, Dec +60.763°, with a 2.22-arcmin 90% radius. The 0.5–4 keV photon index was (1.6\pm0.3) and unabsorbed flux (2.6\pm0.3\times10^{-9}\ {m erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}). FXT later found an uncatalogued source within the WXT region, localized to 20 arcsec. [EP report](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46025).

COLIBRÍ obtained (r>21.56) AB (3σ) at 5.15 h, and WINTER (J>18.9) AB (5σ) at 5.45 h. No optical/near-infrared counterpart or physical classification had been established by cutoff. A non-detection at those limits is not evidence that no counterpart exists.

#### 3. GRB 261026A: fresh SVOM trigger, follow-up pending

At **26 October, 06:09:37 UTC**, SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on **GRB 261026A**. The best low-latency image had S/N **11.10** in 5–20 keV over 20.4 s; GRM independently reached significance **11.20**. The preliminary position is RA 337.3314°, Dec +27.0485° with a **7.22-arcmin 90% radius**, including systematics. SVOM slewed, and MXT/VT analysis was still pending at cutoff. [GCN 46030](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46030). It is a mission-detected gamma-ray burst, but redshift, spectrum and afterglow association were not yet available.

#### 4. PKS 0521−36 reached its brightest Fermi-LAT day on record

Fermi-LAT reported a historical maximum from the (z=0.05655) active galaxy **PKS 0521−36**. On **22 October**, the preliminary daily (>100) MeV flux was ((3.0\pm0.3)\times10^{-6}\ {m ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}), about 30 times its 4FGL average, with photon index (2.0\pm0.1) versus catalog 2.45. A three-hour interval peaked at ((6.7\pm0.9)\times10^{-6}\ {m ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}), about 60 times average. One 75-GeV photon had association probability above 0.99. [ATel 18118](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=18118).

This is a gamma-ray flare measurement, not a neutrino association. The telegram labels the analysis preliminary, and flux ratios inherit the catalog-baseline choice.

#### 5. A deep radio limit narrows, but does not identify, EP261015a

The VLA observed **EP261015a** at 10 GHz on **22 October, 10:15 UTC**, 6.87 d after trigger, and found no source to **17 μJy (3σ)**. If the candidate host's photometric redshift (z=0.57) is correct, the luminosity limit is (1.43\times10^{29}\ {m erg\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}). [GCN 46016](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46016).

That is about two orders of magnitude below radio-bright Einstein Probe/GRB examples at similar epochs and below many radio-detected short GRBs. It still permits some relativistic supernovae or low-luminosity GRB-supernovae. The result is a conditional non-detection, not proof of a radio-quiet source class.

### Science Radar

#### 1. Early ctDNA clearance predicts outcomes in resectable lung cancer

**Peer-reviewed Nature article, published online 25 October 2026.** A secondary biomarker analysis of the randomized phase-2 NeoCOAST-2 platform examined circulating tumor DNA during perioperative treatment for resectable non-small-cell lung cancer. Across three experimental arms, clearance by cycle 3 day 1 was associated with longer event-free survival: pooled **HR 0.22 (95% CI 0.11–0.45; (P<0.0001))**. In a parsimonious model including stage, treatment arm and pathological complete response, ctDNA clearance remained associated with EFS, **adjusted HR 0.23 (95% CI 0.11–0.48)**. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11328-3); [supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-11328-3/MediaObjects/41586_2026_11328_MOESM1_ESM.pdf); [published review record](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-11328-3/MediaObjects/41586_2026_11328_MOESM5_ESM.pdf).

The important interpretation is prognostic, not treatment-selective: clearance and pathological response added complementary information, but the trial did not randomize therapy according to ctDNA. EFS and ctDNA analyses were secondary/exploratory, no multiplicity correction covered these endpoints, serial-sample missingness reduced the evaluable cohort, subgroup intervals were wide, and post-surgical MRD analyses contained very few positive cases. Prospective ctDNA-guided randomization is still needed before changing treatment on this marker.

#### 2. Trait prediction alone is a weak real-world route to genome re-identification

**Peer-reviewed Nature Communications article, published 25 October 2026.** Théo Cavinato, Robin J. Hofmeister and Zoltán Kutalik built a probabilistic attack that matches observed traits to polygenic scores, explicitly modeling prediction error and genetic/environmental correlations. With an artificial **50% prior** that a genome and trait record belong to the same person, the method exceeded **99% precision at 40% recall**. Under their estimated realistic prior, at most **(4\times10^{-4}\%)**, precision fell below **0.13% at the same recall**. APOE-ε4 carrier inference exceeded 50% precision only below 20% recall. [Open article and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78494-w).

The study usefully separates technical separability from operational risk: high benchmark accuracy collapses when true matches are extremely rare. It does not establish that genomic data are broadly safe from re-identification. Direct surname inference, genealogy databases, linked metadata, small candidate pools and data breaches are different attack surfaces; the conclusion applies specifically to phenotype-prediction matching under the tested traits, cohorts and priors.
