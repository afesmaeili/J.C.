#### GRB 261029A: a fading optical counterpart and a preliminary redshift of 3.27 within four hours

Fermi/GBM triggered at **29 October, 01:27:03 UTC** and classified GRB 261029A as a likely long burst. Its automated ground localization was broad: RA **12.2°**, Dec **+32.3°**, with a **3.6° statistical radius**. Four seconds later, SVOM/ECLAIRs independently triggered. Its 8–120 keV image reached **S/N 58.13** over 40.96 s and localized the event to RA **8.8858°**, Dec **+34.5603°**, with a **2.40-arcmin 90% confidence radius** including a 2-arcmin systematic. The temporal agreement and overlapping localizations identify the same burst; the Fermi classification remains preliminary pending refined prompt spectroscopy. [Fermi GCN 46071](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46071); [SVOM GCN 46072](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46072).

GOTO began imaging **5.4 min** after the Fermi trigger and covered 91.3 deg², or about **40.5%** of the GBM two-dimensional probability. It found a new source, GOTO26llt / AT 2026ahaw, at RA **8.817988°**, Dec **+34.500138°**. The source faded from **L = 13.98** to **14.85 AB mag** over roughly nine minutes and was absent to **L > 19.34 AB mag** 1.63 h before the burst. This rapid appearance, fading and positional consistency make it a strong optical-afterglow identification rather than merely a catalogued object inside a large error region. [GCN 46073](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46073). COLIBRÍ independently measured **r = 14.41 ± 0.01 AB** at 0.3072 h and saw fading; TRT, KAIT and LCO recovered the source later near **R/r ≈ 17.2–17.4 mag**. [GCN 46083](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46083); [46078](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46078); [46079](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46079); [46082](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46082).

The Nordic Optical Telescope obtained four 1200-s spectra beginning at **03:53:31 UTC**. A preliminary reduction found Lyman-series, Si II, O I, C II, Si IV, C IV and Al II absorption at a common **z = 3.27**, interpreted as the burst redshift. [GCN 46081](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46081). This is a rapid circular-level redshift based on feature agreement, not yet a refereed host or intervening-absorber analysis. One near-infrared circular reports **J = 16.32 ± 0.01 AB** and **Hₛ = 16.45 ± 0.02 AB** but gives an impossible start date of **27 October** while calling it 1.5 h after the 29 October trigger; that timestamp is recorded as an unresolved reporting typo and is not used in the chronology. [GCN 46080](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46080).

#### MAXI J1609-487: a bright Galactic-plane X-ray transient lasted less than about five hours

MAXI/GSC detected an uncatalogued source on **28 October** in two ISS transit scans: **382 ± 42 mCrab** at 16:32 UTC and **531 ± 49 mCrab** at 18:05 UTC in 4–10 keV, with quoted errors of 1σ. The best position is RA **242.237°**, Dec **−48.765°**, with a **0.12° statistical radius at 90% confidence** plus a 0.1° systematic containment radius. There was no significant excess in the 14:59 or 19:38 UTC scans, each with a **20 mCrab upper limit**. [GCN 46074](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46074); [ATel 18123 index entry](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/).

The rise and disappearance between sparse scans differ from a typical long GRB, and the Galactic-plane position motivated the provisional name **MAXI J1609-487**. This remains a phenomenological classification: no counterpart, distance, spectrum beyond the GSC band, or source class was available by cutoff. The non-detections bracket a short episode but do not measure its uninterrupted duration.

#### EP261025a: Einstein Probe confirms a fading X-ray counterpart, still without a secure source class

Einstein Probe/WXT discovered EP261025a on **25 October, 07:19:57.4 UTC** as a roughly 130-s flare. The 0.5–4 keV absorbed-power-law fit, with Galactic **N_H = 7.06 × 10²¹ cm⁻²** fixed, gave photon index **1.6 ± 0.3** and unabsorbed flux **(2.6 ± 0.3) × 10⁻⁹ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹**. [Discovery circular 46025](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46025).

The complete FXT analysis reported on 29 October detects the refined 0.3–10 keV counterpart 6.29 h later at RA **2.5438°**, Dec **+60.7671°**, with a **10-arcsec 90% radius**. Its unabsorbed flux was **5.93 (+2.91/−1.53) × 10⁻¹³ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹**, and the photon index was **1.68 (+0.62/−0.60)**, both at 90% confidence under the fixed-absorption model. At 36.87 h, the source was not significantly detected; the corresponding **90% upper limit** was **1.24 × 10⁻¹³ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹**, assuming the earlier spectral shape. [GCN 46076](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46076).

The follow-up establishes a flux decrease by a factor of about **4,400** from the WXT flare to the first FXT observation, under different bandpasses and the same absorption assumption. Optical and near-infrared searches gave only limits, including **r > 21.56 AB** at 5.2 h and **J > 18.9 AB** at 5.45 h. Those non-detections constrain bright counterparts; they do not establish that no astrophysical counterpart exists or distinguish a Galactic flare from an extragalactic transient.

#### A chemically designed single molecule becomes an addressable spin–photon interface

**Peer-reviewed open-access article in *Nature*, published 28 October 2026.** Roggors et al. embed a photoactivated triplet-ground-state carbene in an isosteric host crystal and address single molecules optically at **4.5 K**. The molecule combines a **38 MHz** optical linewidth, hour-scale spectral stability at 2.4–2.6 MHz, and spin-selective optical transitions separated by at least **1.555(1) GHz**. Microwave control gives single-spin Rabi oscillations at 3.7 MHz, Ramsey **T₂* = 5.3(4) μs**, Hahn-echo **T₂ = 12.2(6) μs**, XY8 dynamical-decoupling coherence **T₂(XY8) = 2.2(3) ms**, and relaxation **T₁ = 21(2) ms**. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11147-6).

The reported readout contrast approaches 95%, but the zero-phonon-line emission efficiency is only about **2.1%**, from a 10.8% photoluminescence quantum yield and 19% Debye–Waller factor. The authors' 99% storage fidelity for 40 μs is an estimate from exponential coherence decay, not an end-to-end network-fidelity measurement. The result establishes a molecular route to a spin–photon interface with unusually long single-molecule coherence; it still needs cavity integration, higher photon collection, remote-photon indistinguishability and entanglement demonstrations.

#### Tandem repeats emerge as a large, cell-specific layer of immune gene regulation

**Peer-reviewed article in *Nature*, published 28 October 2026.** Tanudisastro et al. combine whole-genome sequencing with more than **5.4 million** single-cell RNA profiles from **1,925 people** and test over **1.7 million** tandem-repeat loci across 28 immune-cell types. They identify more than **69,000** repeat-expression quantitative-trait loci; **30.7%** are specific to one cell type. Matched chromatin-accessibility data from over **3.4 million nuclei in 922 individuals** provide accessibility QTLs for nearly one-third of expression-associated repeats. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10438-2).

Fine mapping nominates **1,490 tandem repeats** as candidate causal regulators for **6.1% of tested genes**, and colocalization links repeat-mediated regulation to more than 200 genes associated with immune or haematological traits. These are statistical and multiomic causal candidates, not experimental perturbations or clinical risk predictors. Short-read repeat genotyping, blood-cell sampling and colocalization assumptions limit interpretation; functional editing and replication in other ancestries and tissues are needed.

#### Stone availability improves models of where early humans could occupy Arabian deserts

**Peer-reviewed open-access article in *Nature*, published 28 October 2026.** Dellmour et al. surveyed roughly **1,500 km** across the Rub’ al Khali. Palaeolake deposits occurred at 55 of 125 localities, yet the transect yielded neither lithic artefacts nor knappable raw material despite more than 95% ground visibility. Across Arabia, the analysis estimates that **57% of water-accessible terrain** lies on formations lacking suitable stone. Adding lithic raw-material availability to spatial models raises discrimination of known archaeological sites from area under the curve **0.695 to 0.768**. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11180-5).

The study argues that freshwater alone overstates Pleistocene habitable range for stone-tool-dependent populations; water and workable rock had to coincide or be connected by transport. The absence of artefacts along one exceptionally visible transect is informative but cannot exclude buried or erased occupations, exchange networks or changes in tool economy. The broader model is an improved habitat hypothesis rather than a dated migration route.
