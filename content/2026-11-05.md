#### Isotropy above 100 EeV disfavors a pure-proton sky—but only inside a demanding propagation model

**Paper:** *Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulations* — **Ryo Higuchi, Eiji Kido and Shigehiro Nagataki**. [arXiv:2611.05783v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05783v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05783v1). Submitted **4 November 2026 at 00:42:11 UTC** and first listed **5 November**. The arXiv record identifies a 2026 publication in *Physical Review D* **114**, 083040. **Classification: composition and source-population inference from public arrival directions plus simulated propagation; not a direct mass measurement.**

**Question and data.** If particles above 100 EeV are mainly protons, their short energy-loss horizon and small magnetic deflections should make nearby large-scale structure visible as close arrival-direction pairs. The authors take the **35 Pierre Auger Phase-I catalogue events above 100 EeV** and count pairs separated by at most (3^circ). They find **two pairs**. In (10^5) isotropic skies with the same event count and Auger directional exposure, that value lies inside the expected distribution; the data therefore show no significant small-scale anisotropy by this statistic.

**Propagation and mock skies.** The interpretation compares deliberately pure proton, iron and uranium injections. CRPropa supplies proton and iron losses; uranium photodisintegration is calculated with TALYS 2.0 on cosmic-microwave and infrared backgrounds. Sources are sampled from the Millennium Run semi-analytic galaxy catalogue, repeated to cover distances to roughly **500 Mpc**. The source density is scanned from (10^{-6}) to (10^{-3} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}). Turbulent deflection is summarized by

\[
\alpha=\left(\frac{\lambda}{1\ {\rm Mpc}}\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\frac{B}{1\ {\rm nG}}\right),
\]

where (B) is the rms extragalactic field and (lambda) its coherence length. For each grid point and composition, **1,000 mock catalogues of 35 events** are tested with the same pair count. A point is called allowed at the paper's one-sided 95% criterion when its median pair count lies inside the corresponding isotropic range. This construction is a simulation acceptance rule, not a likelihood-based confidence interval on composition.

**Result.** With no magnetic deflection, none of the tested proton or iron source densities reproduces the observed isotropy; uranium can do so for high densities around (10^{-3})–(10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}), because nuclei with smaller Lorentz factor can arrive from a smoother, more distant source distribution. With fields, iron and uranium acquire broad allowed regions. A uranium example becomes compatible near (\rho_{\rm source}sim10^{-4.5} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) for a sufficiently strong turbulent field. Pure protons remain rejected over almost all scanned parameter combinations and survive only for the paper's extreme (alpha=10) field case.

The inference is therefore conditional: the observed statistic is simply consistent with isotropy, while the preference against protons emerges after fixing pure compositions, injection spectra, a galaxy-to-source prescription, energy losses and magnetic transport. It does not measure a uranium fraction or exclude mixed compositions.

**Limitations.** Thirty-five events give only two close pairs, so the statistic has little resolving power once strong fields erase angular structure. Uranium is an intentionally extreme proxy for nuclei beyond iron; its abundance and acceleration were not fitted. The mock universe uses repeated simulation boxes, one observer realization, a simplified exposure mapping and turbulent fields not tied to filaments, clusters and voids. The regular Galactic field changes inferred source-density limits by about an order of magnitude in the authors' test, and modern Galactic-field models still disagree. Energy-scale uncertainty, hadronic shower modelling and unmodelled mixed composition add further ambiguity.

##### History

The absence of clustering has constrained source populations before. The Pierre Auger Collaboration's [2013 analysis, arXiv:1305.1576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1576) used events above 60–80 EeV and obtained **95% confidence lower bounds of roughly ((0.06\text{–}5)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}})** for equal-luminosity, uniformly distributed sources, depending strongly on magnetic smearing; local-matter-tracing cases gave a similar ((0.2\text{–}7)\times10^{-4} {\rm Mpc^{-3}}) range. Those are not directly interchangeable with the new allowed regions because the event threshold, propagation horizon, catalogue and composition hypotheses differ. The new work's useful addition is to ask how much of the isotropy can arise from the longer propagation distance of nuclei heavier than iron, rather than only from a high source density or large deflection.

Independent composition measurements point in the same qualitative direction without identifying uranium. Auger's deep-learning reconstruction of about **50,000** surface-detector showers found the mean mass becoming heavier and its fluctuations narrowing up to 100 EeV ([arXiv:2406.06319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06319)), subject to hadronic-interaction models. The Telescope Array's large-scale-structure comparison also inferred a very heavy injected composition above 100 EeV even when allowing strong extragalactic fields ([arXiv:2406.19287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19287)). The present pair-count study is consistent with those baselines, but is less composition-direct than (X_{max}): its remaining uncertainty is the degeneracy among charge, magnetic field, source density and the actual nearby source distribution.

#### Auger turns Earth-skimming air-shower data into a limit on MeV sterile neutrinos

**Paper:** *A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory* — **Pierre Auger Collaboration**. [arXiv:2611.06284v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06284v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06284v1). Submitted **4 November 2026 at 10:23:29 UTC** and first listed **5 November**. v1 collaboration preprint; no journal reference is listed. **Classification: model-dependent reinterpretation and 90% confidence flux limit; no sterile-neutrino candidate or detection.**

**Parameter and signal.** The minimal (3+1) benchmark contains a predominantly sterile mass eigenstate (N_1) with mass from the MeV scale to **100 MeV**, mixing only with the tau flavour through (sin^2\theta_N). Its weak interaction cross section is suppressed by that factor. At ultrahigh energy, an (N_1) can therefore cross more Earth than an active neutrino, interact through its active component, create a tau lepton, and yield an upward or Earth-skimming air shower when the tau exits and decays.

**Method and observations.** A Monte Carlo propagates the sterile state through Earth, samples neutrino interactions, follows tau energy loss and decay, and folds the resulting shower through the fluorescence-detector upward-going and surface-detector Earth-skimming exposures. The upward-going sample, covering **1 January 2004–31 December 2018**, contains **one candidate** against (0.27\pm0.12) expected misreconstructed cosmic-ray showers. The Earth-skimming search through **31 December 2021** contains **zero candidates**. The corresponding 90% signal-count limits are 4.09 and 2.44 events. Tau energy loss dominates the exposure systematic at **(+34%/-28%)**; the published limit incorporates the conservative exposure treatment.

For (sin^2\theta_N\lesssim0.1), an assumed isotropic (E^{-2}) spectrum gives

\[
E^2\Phi_N \le 2\times10^{-9}\,\sin^{-2}\theta_N
\quad {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}
\]

at **90% confidence** over **0.3–200 EeV**. Thus the numerical bound is (2\times10^{-7}) in these units at (sin^2\theta_N=10^{-2}), and (2\times10^{-6}) at (10^{-3}). The inverse mixing dependence reflects transparency: weaker mixing lets more particles cross Earth but makes the final interaction less likely. The Earth-skimming channel supplies most of the sensitivity; sterile fluxes generated only by standard source neutrinos oscillating into (N_1) remain several orders below it.

**Conditional dark-matter interpretation.** The paper also assumes a (10^{13})-GeV superheavy particle (chi) that decays exclusively into one or several sterile states. At (m_N=10) MeV and (sin^2\theta_N\sim10^{-2}), the (k=1) benchmark gives (\tau_\chi\gtrsim10^{16}) yr at 90% confidence. This is far weaker than typical (sim10^{23})-yr limits on visible superheavy-dark-matter channels because the assumed products are mostly sterile and have low multiplicity. At (m_N=100) MeV, sterile decays transfer energy into active tau neutrinos before arrival. These curves are conditional exclusions in ((sin^2\theta_N,\tau_\chi)), not general dark-matter lifetime bounds.

**Limitations.** The reported flux bound fixes isotropy and an (E^{-2}) spectrum; differential limits are less assumption-heavy but weaker. The model allows only tau mixing, uses vacuum mixing and ignores additional sterile interactions. The upward-going event is background-compatible, while the zero-event Earth-skimming sample drives the result. ##### History

Auger's air-shower technique was established for active neutrinos. Using 2004–2013 data, the collaboration found no candidates and set a single-flavour **90% confidence** (E^{-2}) limit of (E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu<6.4\times10^{-9} {\rm GeV,cm^{-2},s^{-1},sr^{-1}}) over (10^{17})–(2.5\times10^{19}) eV ([arXiv:1504.05397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05397)). The new sterile limit reuses the same physical signatures but recalculates transmission and conversion for a suppressed cross section. Sterile propagation through Earth was proposed as an explanation of anomalous upward ANITA showers by **Cherry and Shoemaker**, who calculated the acceptance for EeV sterile states and showed how reduced mixing could evade Earth opacity ([*Physical Review D* 99, 063016 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.99.063016)). The present collaboration analysis moves from an event-motivated scenario to detector-specific Auger exposure and observed-count limits over a defined energy interval.

Other bounds probe different assumptions. CHARM's direct beam-dump reinterpretation constrains tau-mixed heavy neutral leptons only for roughly **290 MeV–1.6 GeV**, above the main mass range here ([*Physical Review D* 104, 095019 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.104.095019)). Big-bang nucleosynthesis, CMB and supernova bounds can be much stronger at 10–100 MeV, but depend on reheating temperature, early-Universe production and stellar-core transport. The Auger limit is less restrictive yet astrophysically independent; the open question is whether nature supplies any ultrahigh-energy sterile flux near its reach.

**Limitations.** There is no magnetic-moment measurement, confidence limit or source-specific prediction. Matter effects, three flavours, inhomogeneous fields, transition moments and Majorana neutrinos are outside this setup. Potential subleading QFT corrections are not evaluated quantitatively.

##### History

[Popov and Studenikin (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08195) obtained the coupled flavour, spin and spin–flavour probabilities by expanding neutrinos in exact stationary spin states in a magnetic field. They emphasized that helicity states themselves are not stationary in a transverse field, and that magnetic precession modulates even left-handed flavour transitions. Their equal-moment limit already contains the modulation recovered here.

The new contribution is the source-to-detector field-theory derivation: the neutrino is an internal propagating line between weak vertices rather than an externally prepared oscillating wavefunction. Agreement in the shared limit is a consistency result. Extending the treatment to realistic source localization, matter and changing fields would be necessary before it could alter an astrophysical interpretation or an experimental magnetic-moment bound.

**Paper:** *Planting neutrino SE-EdS: A < 3% accurate clustering approximation for neutrinos and other hot dark matter* — **Amol Upadhye, Yvonne Y. Y. Wong**. [arXiv:2611.06399v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06399v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06399v1). **Question and method.** Can a cheap response formula reproduce the clustering of neutrinos with a broad momentum distribution? ##### History

[Chen, Upadhye and Wong's SuperEasy method (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12504) interpolated between clustering and free-streaming limits, allowing a simple modification of an N-body force solver. [Pierobon et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05816) generalized the response to arbitrary momentum distributions and mixed hot relics, again finding that accurate total matter does not require equally accurate hot-component clustering. SE-EdS improves the transition-region physics using an analytic matter-era solution and explicit growth corrections. This is most useful when the hot component itself matters. It remains a linear-response approximation; the improvement is not a substitute for resolving nonlinear low-velocity neutrino capture.

#### Hawking-produced axions connect primordial black holes to cosmological photon signals

**Paper:** *The Cosmology of Hawking Axions* — **Abdelaziz Hussein, Gordan Krnjaic, Sarunas Verner**. [arXiv:2611.05747v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05747v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05747v1). Submitted **3 November 2026 at 23:37:05 UTC**; first arXiv listing **5 November 2026 UTC**. **Question and model.** The paper follows axion-like particles emitted by primordial black holes that **dominate the energy density before evaporating ahead of BBN**. It fixes a monochromatic, nonrotating initial black-hole population and varies its mass over **1–\(5\times10^8\) g**. The interaction is \(\mathcal L=-g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF\widetilde F/4\), with \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\) in **GeV⁻¹** and rest-frame decay rate \(\Gamma=g_{a\gamma\gamma}^2m_a^3/(64\pi)\). The relic abundance is determined by this cosmological history, not fitted freely.

**Method and result.** Greybody emission, the evolving black-hole mass, redshifting, time dilation and the boosted two-photon decay spectrum are propagated into cosmological observables. Stable relativistic relics give **\(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\simeq0.05\)–0.11** in the model. For **\(M_i=10^8\) g**, boosted decay photons extend the cosmic-X-ray-background test to axion masses around **100 eV**; high-energy tails also affect photodisintegration and spectral distortions.

**Meaning of the exclusions.** These are different statistical constructions, not one combined confidence region. BBN uses measured light-element ranges and photodisintegration calculations. FIRAS supplies the separate **95%** distortion limits \(|\mu|<9\times10^{-5}\) and \(|y|<1.5\times10^{-5}\). A **95%** Planck decaying-dark-matter bound is recast only where the relic is sufficiently cold and long lived. The **0.5–200 keV** X-ray test requires the signal not to exceed the total observed background, without subtracting astrophysical sources; it has no assigned common confidence level. Black-hole mass is fixed for each curve, not marginalized.

**Limitations.** Regions where axions dominate again invalidate the assumed background and are marked separately. Extended black-hole mass functions, subdominant initial black holes, additional axion couplings and a full joint cosmological fit can change the inference. Projected background subtraction and future CMB sensitivities are forecasts.

##### History

[Hooper, Krnjaic and McDermott (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01301) established how a black-hole-dominated era can reheat the Standard Model while leaving a nonthermal dark-radiation population. [Schiavone et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03420) specifically studied Hawking axions, including magnetic conversion, radiative decay and reionization. Thus neither Hawking production nor boosted axion decays are new here; the present study brings several cosmological tests into a common evolving distribution.

[Shallue, Muñoz and Krnjaic (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08535) constrained stable warm Hawking relics by mapping their matter-power suppression to thermal-relic limits from CMB, clustering and lensing. The new paper imports that constraint only where decays negligibly alter the surviving population, rather than applying a stable-relic limit universally.

Independent abundance-free comparisons probe different physics. The [extended CAST analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16840) reports \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}<5.8\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV^{-1}}\) for \(m_a\lesssim0.02\,\mathrm{eV}\), a **95% Bayesian upper limit** with a prior flat in positive \(g^4\), using solar Primakoff production. [Dolan, Hiskens and Volkas (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03102) obtain **\(4.7\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV^{-1}}\)** in the light-axion stellar regime from the AGB/HB star-count ratio, requiring models to remain within its observed **95%** range and taking conservative stellar-mixing choices rather than marginalizing over a universal prior. These do not require a primordial axion population. Much smaller Hawking-decay couplings can be tested only after fixing the black-hole history, so no model-independent improvement factor is justified.

#### Binary-neutron-star inspirals constrain a quadratic-curvature coupling

**Paper:** *Gravitational-Wave Constraints on R² Gravity* — **Pawan Kumar Gupta, Laura Sagunski**. [arXiv:2611.06902v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06902v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06902v1). Submitted **4 November 2026 at 18:49:50 UTC**; first arXiv listing **5 November 2026 UTC**. v1 preprint; no journal reference is listed in the checked arXiv record. **Classification: constraint · modified gravity.**

**Question and method.** For \(f(R)=R+aR^2\), with \(a>0\) in **m²**, the extra scalar has range \(\lambda=\sqrt{6a}\) and mass \(m_s=\hbar c/\sqrt{6a}\). A point-particle effective theory fixes a universal scalar charge-to-mass ratio and adds conservative Yukawa and scalar-radiation phase corrections to a GR inspiral waveform. Bayesian analyses use **GW170817 and GW190425**.

**Result.** Combining the events gives **\(a<6.24\times10^6\,\mathrm{m^2}\)** at **90% posterior credibility**, equivalent to **\(m_s>3.2\times10^{-11}\) eV** or a range below approximately **6.1 km**. The prior is uniform in \(\hat a=a/R_0^2\in[0,100]\), with \(R_0=1.47664\) km. Binary and extrinsic parameters are sampled and marginalized, with low component spins \(|\chi|<0.05\); the scalar charge prescription is fixed. The data are consistent with GR.

**Limitations.** Equal charge-to-mass ratios cancel the leading binary-neutron-star dipole contribution. The treatment retains GR tidal modeling and omits a complete finite-neutron-star scalar-charge calculation, modified Love numbers and higher-order waveform corrections. The result is consequently conditional on the point-particle coupling and waveform, and changing the prior from uniform coupling to uniform scalar mass would change the posterior bound.

##### History

[Näf and Jetzer (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2014) derived the weak-field Yukawa correction of relative strength **1/3** for the same simple quadratic theory. Their astrophysical estimates were approximately \(a\lesssim5\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{m^2}\) from Gravity Probe B and \(2.3\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^2}\) from double-pulsar spin precession. These approximate bounds are not statistically identical to the new marginalized posterior; the GW analysis nevertheless probes substantially shorter scalar ranges.

**Laboratory tests remain much stronger for the unscreened constant-coupling model.** The same earlier analysis translated torsion-balance data into an order-of-magnitude \(a\lesssim10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2}\) bound. [Lee et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11761) subsequently measured gravity down to **52 μm**, finding no Yukawa deviation and publishing **95%** limits as a function of strength and range. Their often-quoted **38.6 μm** range limit is for unit strength, so it should not be inserted unchanged into the strength-1/3 quadratic model.

[Jana and Mohanty (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04060) used GW170817 to constrain screened, more general \(f(R)\) models through a different parameter, \(|f'(R_0)-1|\). That is not an interchangeable bound on constant \(a\), and screening in those models cannot simply be assumed here. The new result is a complementary strong-gravity test, not the strongest overall laboratory-plus-astrophysical exclusion.

#### A repurposed GPCR agonist corrects Lowe-syndrome kidney defects from cells to mice

**Leopoldo Staiano, Marialuisa Testa and colleagues**, led by **Maria Antonietta De Matteis**, published [*An actionable GPCR restores phosphoinositide homeostasis and kidney function in Lowe syndrome*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-026-03054-z) in *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy* on **4 November 2026**. This is a peer-reviewed, open-access preclinical study; it does not report a patient treatment result.

Lowe syndrome is an X-linked disorder caused by loss of the phosphatase OCRL. PI(4,5)P₂ then accumulates on endosomes, traps the megalin receptor and impairs proximal-tubule protein reabsorption, producing low-molecular-weight proteinuria and progressive kidney injury. A high-content screen tested **1,280 pharmacologically active compounds** at 10 μM in engineered OCRL-deficient cells. Of 113 primary trafficking hits, 18 advanced to kidney-cell assays and four corrected multiple phenotypes. The team selected **IB-MECA, or piclidenoson**, an oral adenosine A3-receptor agonist with prior human safety data.

Mechanistically, A3-receptor activation drives phospholipase C to consume plasma-membrane PI(4,5)P₂, indirectly draining the abnormal endosomal pool. The drug restored receptor trafficking, albumin uptake, actin organization and autophagic flux in engineered cells and patient-derived proximal-tubule cells. In OCRL-knockout human kidney organoids, **10 μM for seven days** improved albumin uptake without changing the broad nephron-cell composition measured by single-cell RNA sequencing.

The in-vivo test used **15 humanized Lowe-model mice per arm**, beginning at eight weeks of age. Drug-containing chow supplied about **100 μg kg⁻¹ day⁻¹ for five months**. Treated mice showed sustained reductions in urinary low-molecular-weight protein markers, improved uptake of labelled β-lactoglobulin by proximal tubules and less collagen accumulation, with no reported loss of response or detected adverse phenotype. These endpoints link the cellular mechanism to kidney function, but the model does not reproduce the full human neurological and ocular disease.

The translational step remains deliberately small. The EU register lists an authorised, recruitment-pending **phase II study with five planned participants**, using six-month renal reabsorption as its primary endpoint ([PICLILOWE, 2026-525495-26-00](https://ctis.eu/trial/2026-525495-26-00)). The paper's extensive cross-model replication is unusually strong for a rare-disease preclinical programme, but mouse proteinuria and organoid uptake cannot establish clinical efficacy, optimal dose or long-term safety in children. The trial will test renal function; whether the approach affects cataracts, neurodevelopment or established chronic kidney damage remains unknown.
