#### GigaBREAD uses Earth's rotation to search for a fixed dark-photon polarization

**Paper:** *First broadband search for fixed-polarization dark photon dark matter with GigaBREAD* — **Jialin Yu, Stefan Knirck, Gabe Hoshino, Rakshya Khatiwada, Alex Lapuente, David W. Miller and Andrew Sonnenschein**. [arXiv:2611.07426v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07426v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07426v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 04:20:25 UTC** and first listed **6 November**. v1 Fermilab preprint, report **FERMILAB-PUB-26-0792-ETD-PPD**; no journal publication is listed. **Classification: experimental reanalysis with Bayesian credible and frequentist confidence limits; no dark-photon detection.**

**Parameter and signal.** A dark photon of mass (m_{\rm DP}) kinetically mixed with the ordinary photon by the dimensionless parameter (chi) can convert into a radio photon at a conducting surface. Unlike axions, this conversion needs no applied magnetic field. If vector dark matter was produced by a misalignment-like mechanism, its polarization may remain approximately fixed in celestial coordinates. The signal in a detector sensitive along the axis \(\hat D(t)\) is then

\[
P_{\rm det}(t)=P_0\left|\hat X\!\cdot\!\hat D(t)\right|^2,
\]

so Earth's sidereal rotation supplies a predictable modulation rather than the constant factor \(\langle\cos^2\vartheta\rangle=1/3\) used for random polarization.

**Data and analysis.** GigaBREAD converts dark photons on a vertical cylindrical conductor of effective area about **0.5 m²** and focuses the emitted 10.7–12.5 GHz radiation into a horn. The reanalysis combines a **24-day**, zenith-aligned University of Chicago run with **12,970 spectra** and a **27-day**, horizontally aligned Argonne run with **12,481 spectra**. Each spectrum contains 230,392 bins at **7.8 kHz** resolution. The reference conversion assumes a local dark-matter density of **0.45 GeV cm⁻³**.

The likelihood retains each spectrum's time stamp and matches both the frequency profile and the sidereal polarization pattern. Because absolute timing was not preserved across the 2023 sub-runs, the polarization azimuth is marginalized independently for each sub-run, while the celestial latitude remains common. Bayesian evidence and a profile-likelihood statistic are calibrated with **300 full noise simulations**, covering **1,151,965 mass templates**. The effective frequentist trials factor is about \(5\times10^5\). Limits incorporate a **23% power systematic** inherited from the detector calibration.

**Result.** The observed global Bayesian statistic is \(k_0^{\rm global}=-2.5\), below the paper's 90% candidate threshold of \(-2.3\); the frequentist scan likewise contains no globally significant mass. The authors report conservative limits by taking the weaker of their Bayesian 90% credible and frequentist 90% confidence upper limits at every mass. Over **44–52 μeV**, the resulting fixed-polarization limit is at the level of

\[
\chi \sim 10^{-12}.
\]

It is approximately **20% lower in \(\chi\)** than a naive fixed-polarization rescaling of the earlier GigaBREAD result and is comparable to, or slightly stronger than, the earlier random-polarization limit. The improvement comes from using the timing information and a second detector orientation. It applies to a fixed but unknown polarization; it is not a general improvement for every dark-photon production model.

**Limitations.** The result assumes that polarization stays coherent and fixed over the observation span and that the Standard Halo Model describes the spectral line. The missing absolute phase across 2023 sub-runs removes some directional information. Instrumental baselines are removed with a Savitzky–Golay filter, and the analysis relies on Monte Carlo calibration of a non-Wilks statistic. The constraint also scales with the assumed local density and conversion efficiency. No mass exceeded the candidate threshold, so the work supplies exclusions rather than evidence for a modulated signal.

##### History

The original GigaBREAD search used the 24-day 2023 data set and treated dark-photon polarization as random. It excluded roughly \(\chi\gtrsim10^{-12}\) at **90% confidence** over the same 44–52 μeV interval and improved previous bounds by about two orders of magnitude below 49 μeV ([Knirck et al., arXiv:2310.13891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13891), *Physical Review Letters* **132**, 131004). The new paper does not gain an order of magnitude or open a new mass range. Its substantive advance is a statistically consistent reinterpretation for fixed polarization, plus the combination of vertical and horizontal runs.

Dish searches such as SHUKET, QUALIPHIDE, DOSUE and BRASS-p constrain overlapping or neighboring masses with different receivers, geometries and polarization conventions. Resonant haloscopes including HAYSTAC, ADMX and ORGAN can also constrain dark photons after a polarization assumption is chosen, but their scan strategy and resonant response are different. Improvement factors should therefore be quoted only against the previous GigaBREAD fixed-polarization rescaling. The main remaining uncertainty is physical: structure formation may partially randomize a primordial vector direction, in which case neither the perfectly fixed nor perfectly isotropic limit is the complete description.

#### Fifteen years of Fermi data put starbursts at or below the cosmic-ray calorimetric limit

**Paper:** *Systematic Study of Cosmic Rays in Star-Forming and Starburst Galaxies using Fermi-LAT and Multiwavelength Data* — **Hinako Ochi, Tsunefumi Mizuno, Yasushi Fukazawa, Noemi Barnier, Susumu Inoue, Wataru Iwakiri, Junling Chen and Tomonori Totani**. [arXiv:2611.07189v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07189v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07189v1). Submitted **4 November 2026 at 22:25:01 UTC** and first listed **6 November**. The manuscript is accepted by the *Publications of the Astronomical Society of Japan* following peer review. **Classification: observational gamma-ray analysis with conditional one-zone cosmic-ray modelling.**

**Question and data.** Star-forming galaxies accelerate cosmic rays, whose hadronic interactions with gas produce gamma rays and neutrinos. A proton calorimeter loses nearly all of the relevant cosmic-ray power through collisions before escape. The authors ask how close ordinary star-forming galaxies and compact starbursts come to this limit, and whether their radio-to-TeV spectra require unusual electron-to-proton ratios.

The analysis uses **15 years of Fermi-LAT data** and updated Galactic diffuse and point-source models for seven galaxies: M82, NGC 253, M31, Arp 220, NGC 2146, Arp 299 and M33. M31 is decomposed into central and southeast components. Spectra are fitted from 50 MeV to about 100 GeV, then combined with published radio and TeV measurements in NAIMA-based one-zone models.

**Results.** M82 and NGC 253 are detected with test statistics **1702** and **899** and show mild curvature. Their 0.1–100 GeV energy fluxes are \(8.19\times10^{-12}\) and \(6.31\times10^{-12}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). Both spectra turn down below a few hundred MeV, reducing the electron bremsstrahlung contribution relative to earlier models. The inferred electron-to-proton ratios are **1/25–1/30 at 1 GeV** and **1/120–1/150 at 10 GeV**, close to canonical Milky-Way values.

The proton energy densities are about **190 eV cm⁻³ in M82** and **470 eV cm⁻³ in NGC 253**, respectively around eight and 2.5 times below the magnetic energy densities used in the fits. This argues against cosmic-ray–magnetic equipartition inside these one-zone starburst models.

For M31, a compact central component has a soft curved spectrum and a **95% upper limit of 0.33° on its radius**. Unresolved sources can therefore contribute substantially, so its luminosity is treated as an upper limit on interstellar emission. M33 is similarly complicated by an offset toward the young region NGC 604.

Revised fluxes for NGC 2146 and Arp 220 are approximately a factor of two lower than in the 2020 comparison analysis. Across the starburst sample, the observed ratio of gamma-ray luminosity to the calorimetric prediction is **0.33–1.20**; the value slightly above unity is within the combined modelling and source uncertainties, rather than proof of super-calorimetry. Ordinary star-forming galaxies lie around the **10% level**, except for the SMC, where point sources are likely important.

**Limitations.** Calorimetric efficiency depends on infrared luminosity, gas density, source subtraction and the assumed supernova-to-cosmic-ray conversion. Several weak galaxies have \({\rm TS}\sim50\), making their spectra sensitive to the diffuse foreground. M31 and M33 contain unresolved or localized components that are not clean measurements of diffuse cosmic-ray interactions. The multiwavelength fits assume one-zone geometry, adopted magnetic fields and published gas masses; the apparent departure from equipartition is consequently model-dependent. The work characterizes detected emission but does not directly measure the neutrino output or isolate hadronic photons event by event.

##### History

Fermi-LAT first reported M82 and NGC 253 at **6.8σ and 4.8σ** with 11 months of data in 2010, establishing a connection between massive-star formation and GeV emission. A later uniform study using about ten years of LAT data detected **11 bona-fide galaxies and two candidates**, plus a stacked below-threshold population, and estimated that star-forming galaxies contribute about **5% of the extragalactic gamma-ray background** and **3% of the IceCube astrophysical-neutrino flux** ([Ajello et al., arXiv:2003.05493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05493)). Those population fractions are model estimates, not direct source associations.

The new analysis updates individual nearby systems rather than remeasuring the total background. Its most consequential changes are the sub-GeV downturn in M82 and NGC 253, the factor-of-two downward revisions for NGC 2146 and Arp 220, and explicit source-confusion cautions for M31 and M33. It supports the established picture that compact starbursts are efficient hadronic targets, while removing the need for several systems to sit clearly above the calorimetric relation. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by gas, magnetic-field and unresolved-source modelling, which also propagates into forecasts of the diffuse neutrino intensity.

#### General-relativistic simulations sharply reduce magnetar-flare r-process yields

**Paper:** *General-relativistic neutrino-radiation hydrodynamics of crust ejection in magnetar giant flares* — **Tetyana Pitik, David Radice, Jacob Fields and Yi Qiu**. [arXiv:2611.07022v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07022v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07022v1). Submitted **4 November 2026 at 19:00:06 UTC** and first listed **6 November**. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. **Classification: one-dimensional numerical simulation and conditional nucleosynthesis forecast, not an observed yield.**

**Question and method.** The radio afterglow of the 27 December 2004 giant flare from SGR 1806−20 implies at least about \(10^{-9}M_\odot\) of baryonic ejecta. Previous hydrodynamic and analytic calculations proposed that a magnetospheric fireball drives a shock into the neutron-star crust, ejecting neutron-rich matter that undergoes r-process nucleosynthesis. This paper tests that chain with general-relativistic hydrodynamics, a tabulated HS(DD2) nuclear equation of state, M1 neutrino transport and a SkyNet reaction network.

The simulations use a fixed spherical neutron-star spacetime and a one-kilometre, whole-surface pressure layer. Its peak pressure is labelled by the equivalent field \(B=10^{15}\)–\(10^{16.5}\) G through \(P_{\rm GF}=B^2/8\pi\). Every model is repeated without neutrino interactions, and the layer width, position and shape are varied. These labels describe the imposed pressure; they are not measurements of an actual flare field.

**Results.** The models unbind approximately

\[
10^{-10} - 5\times10^{-8} M_\odot
\]

of outer crust with mass-weighted asymptotic velocities **0.44–0.52 c**. Only about \(10^{-3}\) of the imposed fireball energy becomes ejecta kinetic energy. Neutrino cooling matters strongly only at the most extreme \(10^{16.5}\)-G pressure: it removes about a tenth of the layer energy while the shock is active and halves the ejecta mass. The ejecta retain \(Y_e\simeq0.44\)–0.47.

The rapid, high-entropy expansion makes very few seed nuclei. Roughly **90%** of the ejecta becomes alpha particles, while **7–10%** remains as free neutrons. Even the strongest model produces at most **\(3.5\times10^{-11}M_\odot\)** in nuclei with \(A\ge90\), three to four orders of magnitude below the earlier analytic calculation. Lanthanides and actinides remain below about \(3\times10^{-5}\) of the ejecta mass.

For the extreme model at 10 kpc, the authors forecast about **two Super-Kamiokande and 15 Hyper-Kamiokande inverse-beta events**, but this flare is far more energetic than the 2004 event. Flares with observed energies should yield no detectable neutrino count. Free-neutron decay instead powers a predicted ultraviolet/optical flash peaking after roughly **40–55 seconds**; this is a model forecast, not an observed nova brevis.

**Connection to the 2004 flare.** Models with \(B=10^{15.5}\)–\(10^{16}\) G eject enough mass at about 0.45 c to resemble the radio afterglow, but their whole-surface fireball energies are **60–600 times** the roughly \(3\times10^{46}\) erg prompt spike. Scaling to that observed energy gives only \((2–3.5)\times10^{-11}M_\odot\), **50–100 times less** than the radio afterglow requires. The authors therefore conclude that a one-dimensional crust shock cannot by itself supply the observed baryons unless the flare released far more total energy or the true magnetic geometry couples energy much more efficiently.

**Limitations.** Spherical symmetry, instantaneous whole-surface heating and omission of magnetic forces probably overestimate ejection. Real flares are localized, laterally expanding and threaded by open and closed field lines. The deepest, marginally unbound shells dominate the already small heavy-element yield, leaving it uncertain by about a factor of two for \(A\ge90\) and more for lanthanides. The fixed spacetime, one-millisecond evolution and analytic continuation of trajectories add further approximations. Multidimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamics is required before treating these values as physical rate predictions.

##### History

**Cehula, Thompson and Metzger** modelled the same shock-ejection mechanism with special-relativistic hydrodynamics and a simplified crust. **Paper:** *DIS-secting the universe with high-energy neutrinos* — **Rhorry Gauld, Lorenzo Guerra, Ulrich Haisch, Alessio Vorona**. [arXiv:2611.08221v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08221v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08221v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 16:09:18 UTC**; first announced in the **6 November 2026 UTC listing**. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. **Classification: precision interaction calculation and event-generator study.**

**Question and method.** Neutrino telescopes infer energy and interaction inelasticity from the division between an outgoing charged lepton and the hadronic cascade. Photons radiated by the lepton change this division even when the inclusive interaction rate changes little. Gauld and collaborators calculate fully differential charged- and neutral-current deep-inelastic scattering with next-to-leading-order QCD and electroweak corrections, finite charged-lepton masses and higher-order lepton radiation. Photon-initiated processes, including coherent nuclear emission and resonant W production, are included. The calculation spans incident energies from about 40 GeV to \(10^9\) GeV and is compared with POWHEG interfaced to three Pythia shower prescriptions.

**Results.** For a bare outgoing muon, \(y=1-E_\mu/E_\nu\). At \(E_\nu=10^8\) GeV, electroweak effects increase the mean inelasticity by approximately **10% without photon-initiated channels and 15% when they are included**. The inclusive cross-section correction is only a few percent: inclusiveness cancels large radiative effects that survive in the energy partition. In the paper's atmospheric-flux-weighted neutrino/antineutrino illustration, the mean-inelasticity change reaches roughly **20% at \(10^9\) GeV**, whereas it remains below about 3% below \(10^4\) GeV. These are theoretical predictions, not measured IceCube energy biases.

The shower comparison is useful operationally. Agreement is good over much of the lower-energy range, but different showers and missing high-energy channels can produce larger differences in the tails; deviations above 10% occur at high inelasticity in some comparisons. The authors provide a prescription that preserves DIS variables when matching radiation and offer differential grids on request.

**Limitations.** The baseline uses NNPDF3.1 NLO partons and a low-scale cutoff \(Q_0=1.65\) GeV. Free-nucleon structure and an oxygen coherent-photon contribution do not constitute a complete nuclear-PDF treatment. Small-x resummation, nuclear modifications and the missing neutron photon input remain separate uncertainties. No full detector simulation, reconstruction efficiency or electromagnetic-versus-hadronic light-yield response is included. The largest relative shifts occur at energies with very few observed events; the illustrative falling astrophysical spectrum is dominated by much lower energies. Neither a universal 20% correction to existing measurements nor percent-level total theoretical accuracy follows.

##### History

[Plestid and Zhou, arXiv:2403.07984v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07984v1), already showed that final-state radiation moves energy from tracks into showers and can substantially distort telescope inelasticity distributions. Their leading-logarithmic treatment used a lepton splitting function and an inclusive hadronic cascade; it also explicitly left detector efficiencies and differing light yields outside the calculation. The new work supplies the full electroweak calculation and photon-initiated channels behind that qualitative warning. It finds that a logarithm based on the full neutrino–nucleon energy can overestimate the radiation contribution relative to the appropriate partonic scale: in the stated \(10^8\)-GeV comparison the earlier approximation gives about 15% instead of approximately 10%. This is a comparison of specified theoretical observables, not a revision to an observed neutrino flux. The outstanding task is to propagate these corrections consistently through each experiment's event simulation and likelihood.

\[
B=C+M\Sigma M^{\mathsf T},
\]

##### History

It is not a measured stochastic background, and the large enhancement is not a universal rescaling of every axion-string prediction.

The relic-density calculation gives illustrative QCD-axion requirements **\(f_a<1.2\times10^{10}\)** or **\(1.3\times10^{10}\) GeV**, imposing \(\Omega_a h^2\leq0.12\) with a specified temperature-dependent axion mass and misalignment contribution. These are model/extrapolation conditions, with no likelihood-derived confidence level.

**Limitations.** Finite scale separation, core resolution, the short time window after bubble collisions, the extraction of axion spectra and the extrapolation over cosmological time are major uncertainties. Bubble-generated radiation and the later string contribution must be separated carefully. Friction and other thermal histories could alter the initial network, and the adopted dark-radiation ceiling is an input rather than a new or strongest observational bound. Prospective PTA or interferometer reach depends on the selected axion parameters and these extrapolations.

##### History

[Gorghetto, Hardy and Nicolaescu, arXiv:2101.11007v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11007v1), combined effective-string reasoning and field simulations to compute gravitational radiation from global strings. They related emission to the growing string tension, studied finite-resolution effects and obtained a nontrivial spectrum rather than assuming perfectly scale-invariant radiation. Axions usually dominate the energy loss, making the relative gravitational-wave efficiency important.

The new work changes **how the network forms**, using bubble nucleation and collisions rather than the usual second-order initial history. Its result motivates testing whether that formation memory survives into the true long-lived scaling regime. Different definitions of tension, total versus axion-only emission and accessible simulation times must be aligned before quoting an improvement factor. The comparison supports further work on the initial-condition dependence; it does not yet establish a precision, transition-independent gravitational-wave template.

#### Velocity two-point statistics do not generally fix an acoustic gravitational-wave signal

**Paper:** *Can Two-Point Correlations Determine Gravitational Waves from Sound Waves?* — **Guangshang Chen, Jianpeng Dai, Yang Xiao**. [arXiv:2611.07877v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07877v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07877v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 11:58:30 UTC**; first announced in the **6 November 2026 UTC listing**. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. **Classification: analytic and numerical method study.**

**Physical point.** Gravitational waves generated by fluid motions depend on the unequal-time correlator of the transverse-traceless stress. Since that stress is quadratic in velocity, its power contains a **four-point velocity correlator**. Replacing the four-point function by products of two-point functions is exact for a Gaussian field, but need not be justified for a finite or correlated population of sound shells.

**Results.** The paper separates two cases often conflated. For independent, spherically symmetric shells with Poisson-distributed centres, the connected contribution can vanish after the tensor projection even though the velocity field itself is non-Gaussian. Hence Gaussianity is sufficient, but not necessary, for the familiar reduction. Conversely, a deliberately constructed three-site ensemble has the same two-point statistics in two realizations but different higher correlations: their low-wavenumber gravitational-wave spectra approach a **ratio of two**. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a universal factor-two correction to a cosmological phase transition.

For exponentially nucleated bubbles in a finite acoustic window, the authors derive conditions under which connected terms become relatively small. Their asymptotic suppression behaves schematically as **\(\log^2(1+\beta E)/(\beta E)\)**, where β sets the nucleation timescale and E is an expansion-limited effective source duration. A long source duration helps, but Hubble expansion and nonlinear steepening limit how far that asymptotic argument can be taken.

**Limitations.** Linear sound propagation, prescribed nucleation statistics and idealized shell profiles do not describe the later shock/turbulent stage of a strong transition. The counterexample establishes nonuniqueness from two-point data; it does not calculate the error for all realistic transitions. No detection forecast, particle-parameter exclusion or observed gravitational-wave background is produced.

##### History

[Hindmarsh and Hijazi's sound-shell model, arXiv:1909.10040v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10040v1), expresses the wave spectrum through the stress unequal-time correlator and then a convolution of velocity correlations, providing the established semi-analytic framework. The new derivation identifies when the statistical reduction underlying that framework is valid and when information is missing.

Independent [strong-transition simulations, arXiv:2505.17824v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17824v1), already found that a Gaussian velocity-convolution treatment could reproduce spectral shapes more faithfully than amplitudes, with shocks, enthalpy weighting and time decorrelation important. That nonlinear discrepancy is not identical to the present idealized connected-correlator effect. Together, the studies argue for checking source statistics and hydrodynamic evolution separately, rather than assuming a successful velocity power spectrum automatically guarantees a precise gravitational-wave amplitude.

#### Fast-radio-burst mean dispersion can calibrate a joint clustering analysis

**Paper:** *Joint analysis of the Macquart relation and the dispersion measure power spectrum: cross-covariance and redshift self-calibration* — **Amy Wayland, Robert Reischke**. [arXiv:2611.08481v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08481v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08481v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 18:59:19 UTC**; first announced in the **6 November 2026 UTC listing**. v1 manuscript submitted to JCAP. **Classification: covariance derivation and cosmological forecast.**

**Question.** The mean dispersion-measure–redshift relation and the angular dispersion power spectrum probe the same cosmic electrons. Can they be combined without double-counting information, and can localized bursts calibrate the redshift distribution of a larger sample? The authors derive the cross-covariance, test it in **5000 lognormal mock catalogues**, and forecast a joint FRB/weak-lensing analysis.

**Statistical result.** In the isotropic full-sky limit, the covariance of a single line-of-sight dispersion measure with an angular power estimator selects a monopole contribution and is small relative to the total line-of-sight variance. In the mocks, individual cross-correlation coefficients are typically around **1%**, with 99% below about **0.04**. Redshift-bin averaging can increase them to around **0.2** because it suppresses uncorrelated variance. In the tested likelihoods, including the cross-covariance changes the width of the mock chi-square distribution by approximately **0.2% for individual bursts** or **0.8% for binned means**. This supports an approximation in those settings, not a proof that all survey correlations vanish.

**Forecast.** The baseline combines LSST-like ten-year shear with **14,439 FRBs** over 70% of the sky, of which **1444** have host redshifts. Adding the mean relation improves the marginalized **68% forecast uncertainty on \(\omega_b=\Omega_bh^2\)** from **0.00072 to 0.00049**, approximately **32%**, and that on the mean host contribution from **29.9 to 7.3 pc cm⁻³**. It barely changes the Hubble-parameter uncertainty: the mean dispersion primarily measures \(\omega_b/h\), so fixing the baryon density would manufacture a much tighter Hubble inference. These are forecast posteriors, not new measured cosmological parameters.

**Limitations.** Host identifications are assumed to represent the wider catalogue. Host and Galactic contributions, source clustering, dispersion-dependent selection and masked geometry may couple quantities beyond the idealized calculation. Lognormal fields do not reproduce every physical bispectrum. Gas-feedback parameters remain partly limited by their priors; the forecast varies only a restricted feedback model. A separate 500,000-burst stress test illustrates redshift bias from a wrong assumed mean relation, rather than describing an existing survey or proving real-data self-calibration is unbiased.

##### History

[Macquart et al., arXiv:2005.13161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13161), established the localized-FRB dispersion–redshift framework, explicitly separating Milky-Way ISM, Galactic halo, intervening gas and host contributions and accounting for selection in the host sample. Thus the mean relation was never a foreground-free direct baryon measurement.

[Wayland et al., arXiv:2602.12174v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12174v1), already developed joint FRB dispersion, weak-lensing and clustering forecasts for baryonic feedback, including source-clustering terms and survey-systematic marginalization. The November paper adds the covariance with the mean relation and demonstrates how a localized subsample can constrain the mean host contribution and redshift calibration. Its smaller cross-covariance result does not remove the earlier need to model source selection, host structure and foregrounds.

#### ZTF calibration requirements qualify a recent Hubble-constant inference

**Paper:** *Comment on the recent claims of H0 measurements with the ZTF DR2 SN Ia sample (arXiv:2610.36051)* — **Mickael Rigault, Brodie Popovic, Ariel Goobar, Suhail Dhawan, Marie Aubert, Marc Betoule, Umut Burgaz, Georgios Dimitriadis, Lluís Galbany, Tomás E. Müller Bravo, Antoine Gilles Lordet, Madeleine Ginolin, Young-Lo Kim, Marek Kowalski, Jakob Nordin, Nicolas Regnault, Mathew Smith, Jesper Sollerman, Jacco H. Terwel, Alice Townsend**. [arXiv:2611.07794v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07794v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07794v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 10:33:31 UTC**; first announced in the **6 November 2026 UTC listing**. v1 preprint; no journal publication is listed. **Classification: methodological comment on an external cosmological analysis.**

**Issue.** Rigault and collaborators examine the use of public ZTF DR2 supernova photometry together with SH0ES calibrator supernovae in the ECH0EZ Hubble-constant analysis. A consistent relative photometric zero point is required between the Hubble-flow and calibrator samples. A brightness offset is degenerate with the inferred distance scale; to first order its magnitude is
\[
|\Delta H_0/H_0|\simeq(\ln10/5)|\Delta m|.
\]
A **0.05 mag** offset therefore corresponds to approximately **2.3% in H₀**.

**Argument.** The comment distinguishes correcting the difference between forced photometry and scene-modelling photometry from establishing an absolute cross-survey calibration. An average correction of approximately **0.06 mag** is not the full zero-point solution. The public release also lacks some of the stellar calibration, passband, detector nonlinearity and time/CCD-dependent information needed for the proposed cosmological precision. The “pocket effect” is not adequately represented by a single small average correction.

**What follows.** These omissions can introduce a coherent offset that is not constrained by simply increasing the number of supernovae. The comment therefore challenges the robustness of the quoted H₀ uncertainty and central-value interpretation. Its examples of 0.05–0.1 mag offsets illustrate sensitivity; they are **not measurements of the actual missing calibration offset**, and the paper supplies no replacement H₀ estimate. Analyses of relative populations within the same ZTF sample need not be invalidated by the same cross-survey issue.

##### History

[Karchev et al., ECH0EZ, arXiv:2610.36051v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36051v1), deliberately used a volume-limited ZTF Hubble-flow sample to reduce selection bias and combined it with 42 SH0ES calibrators in a hierarchical population/dust model. Its methods acknowledge that an unaccounted global cross-calibration offset would be absorbed into H₀. It applies average photometric corrections and marginalizes over specified redshift-dependent correction uncertainties, while **fixing the remaining global offset to zero**.

The disagreement is therefore specific: whether those available corrections establish the required cross-survey zero point and its error budget. Rigault et al. argue that they do not. Treating this as a calibration dispute preserves both the motivation of the original homogeneous-sample analysis and the missing information identified by the collaboration. It is not evidence that one side has measured a different expansion rate, nor a resolution of the Hubble tension. A fully calibrated release or an explicit independent calibration would be needed to settle the quantitative effect.

#### ATLAS adds a resolved single-lepton channel to Higgs-pair and exotic-scalar searches

**Paper:** *Search for Higgs boson production in association with another Higgs or an exotic scalar boson in the bb̄W⁺W⁻ single lepton final state at √s = 13 TeV with the ATLAS experiment* — **ATLAS Collaboration**. [arXiv:2611.08241v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08241v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08241v1). Submitted **5 November 2026 at 16:20:01 UTC**; first announced in the **6 November 2026 UTC listing**. v1 manuscript submitted to JHEP. **Classification: collider search with profile-likelihood exclusions.**

**Data and method.** ATLAS searches **140 fb⁻¹** of **13 TeV** proton–proton collisions from Run 2 for a Higgs boson decaying to b quarks alongside a second Higgs or an exotic scalar decaying to W bosons. The selected topology has one electron or muon, missing transverse momentum and resolved jets, including two b-tagged jets. A neural-network classifier uses kinematics and Higgs/top compatibility variables; control regions constrain top and W+jets backgrounds, while data estimate misidentified leptons.

**Nonresonant result.** No significant excess is found. The **95% CLs profile-likelihood upper limit** on the Higgs-pair cross section times the \(b\bar bWW^*\) branching fraction is **268 fb observed**, versus **\(178^{+78}_{-54}\) fb expected**. With the stated branching fraction of approximately **0.249**, the corresponding gluon-fusion Higgs-pair limit is about **1.08 pb**, or **35 times the Standard Model prediction**, compared with **\(23^{+10}_{-7}\)** expected. This conversion excludes theoretical uncertainty on the signal cross section; it should not be confused with a fit to the Higgs self-coupling. The excess is only about **1.1σ**.

**Exotic-scalar result.** For \(pp\to X\to SH\), the study covers **\(m_X=350–3000\) GeV** and **\(m_S=170–2500\) GeV**, with kinematically allowed mass combinations and fixed narrow widths of **10 MeV**. The limits are on production cross section times branching fractions at the simulated points. They vary strongly across the two-dimensional mass plane, and do not imply a universal excluded scalar mass. Systematic nuisance parameters are profiled; background theory and fake-lepton modelling materially limit the nonresonant sensitivity.

##### History

The strongest relevant Standard Model comparison is the earlier [ATLAS–CMS Higgs-pair combination, arXiv:2602.23991v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23991v1), rather than this single final state alone. That combination used multiple channels and found a **95% observed cross-section limit of 73 fb**, with **50 fb expected**; after including signal-normalization theory uncertainties, its signal-strength limits were **2.5 and 1.7 times the Standard Model prediction**. Other Higgs couplings were fixed when extracting its one-parameter coupling intervals, and correlated signal/background theory uncertainties were treated across experiments.

The new 35-times-SM channel limit is therefore not the strongest global Higgs-pair constraint. Its value is added information from a distinct resolved single-lepton topology, improved control of its large backgrounds and the two-mass exotic-scalar search. A numerical improvement factor against the combined bound would be meaningless because the datasets, channels, production definitions and treatment of theoretical uncertainty differ. The work reports exclusions, not evidence for Higgs self-interaction beyond the Standard Model.

#### A smartwatch screens for hypertension without estimating blood pressure

**Paolo Di Achille, Lawrence Cai and colleagues** published [*Opportunistically Detecting Signs of Hypertension on a Consumer Smartwatch*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11336-3) in *Nature* on **5 November 2026**. The peer-reviewed study tests a screening classifier, not a replacement for a cuff and not a diagnosis engine.

The model combines photoplethysmography and accelerometer waveforms collected by a consumer smartwatch, without personal cuff calibration. Its prospective multicentre validation included **196 participants** without known cardiovascular disease or antihypertensive treatment. Participants wore the device in ordinary life for about seven days, and the reference was **24-hour ambulatory blood-pressure monitoring**, with hypertension defined primarily as mean systolic or diastolic pressure of at least **130/80 mmHg**.

At the prespecified operating point, the watch classifier achieved **65.8% sensitivity** (95% confidence interval **54.0–76.3%**), **90.0% specificity** (**83.2–94.7%**) and **80.6% positive predictive value** (**68.6–89.6%**). Initial office screening in the same cohort had **55.3% sensitivity**, **90.0% specificity** and **77.8% positive predictive value**. The model score correlated with 24-hour systolic pressure at \(r=0.66\) and diastolic pressure at \(r=0.63\). Sensitivity rose to **89.5%** for the much higher \(145/90\)-mmHg threshold, but the confidence interval was wide (**66.9–98.7%**).

An exploratory analysis used a separate PPG-only encoder on **76,268 UK Biobank participants** without baseline hypertension or cardiovascular disease. A positive classification was associated with incident hypertension, adjusted hazard ratio **1.83** (95% confidence interval **1.77–1.90**). That analysis used finger pulse-oximeter waveforms, a different model and observational outcomes; it does not independently validate the smartwatch classifier's screening accuracy.

The clinical distinction is essential. With 65.8% sensitivity, roughly one third of hypertensive participants were missed at the chosen threshold. The cohort was small and deliberately risk-enriched, while predictive value will change with prevalence in the deployed population. Performance across other watches, skin conditions, motion patterns, arrhythmias and treated patients remains uncertain. The system can prompt confirmatory cuff testing; it cannot report blood pressure, rule out hypertension or establish that notifications improve clinical outcomes.
