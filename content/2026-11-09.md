#### A dark-matter potential can reshape both neutrino flavor and attenuation limits

**Paper:** *Probing flavor-dependent neutrino-dark matter interactions with the flavor composition of high-energy cosmic neutrinos* — **Ki-Young Choi and Ankhbayar Tugssaikhan**. [arXiv:2611.09037v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09037v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09037v1). Submitted **6 November 2026 at 06:12:47 UTC** and first listed **9 November**; primary category **hep-ph**. No journal publication is listed. **Classification: model-dependent phenomenological constraint; no interaction or source association is detected.**

**Physical question.** High-energy neutrinos crossing a dense dark-matter spike can scatter and lose flux. If the forward-scattering amplitude is flavor dependent, the same medium also changes the neutrino mixing eigenstates. The paper asks how those two effects combine in an observable flavor triangle, using TXS 0506+056 as a benchmark rather than claiming that every IceCube neutrino originates there.

The interaction is written as a diagonal matter potential

\[
V_\alpha(r)=A_\alpha\,\rho_\chi(r),
\]

where $A_\alpha$ has units of cm³ and only one flavor is coupled at a time. The attenuation is parameterized by the cross section per dark-matter mass, \$\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi\$, in cm² GeV⁻¹. These are distinct quantities: $A_\alpha$ controls coherent flavor evolution, while \$\sigma_{\nu\chi}\$ controls incoherent removal from the beam.

**Benchmark and method.** The source is placed at **0.023 pc** from a $3\times10^8M_\odot$ black hole. The adopted spike has radius **1.28 pc**, density \$6.4\times10^{11}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-3}}\$ at the emission point and integrated column \$X_\chi=3.6\times10^{28}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}}\$. The Milky Way column, about $10^{22}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}}$ in the same calculation, is negligible for attenuation. The propagation is solved adiabatically at a fixed neutrino energy of **100 TeV**.

The standard $p\gamma$ source composition is split into neutrinos ($ (1/3,1/3,0) $) and antineutrinos ($ (0,1/3,0) $), giving total ($ (1:2:0) $); a muon-damped ($ (0:1:0) $) source is also tested. The Earthly fractions are compared with IceCube's broad flavor contours around the approximate best fit ($ (0.30,0.37,0.33) $). The authors require that the local Galactic density, taken as **0.4 GeV cm⁻³**, not appreciably alter the measured PMNS mixing at 100 TeV, giving a working upper scale $A_\alpha\lesssim10^{-30}\ {\rm cm^3}$.

**Results.** Matter resonances occur near

\[
A_\alpha\sim10^{-38}\left$\frac{100\ {\rm TeV}}{E_\nu}\right${\rm cm^3}.
\]

At smaller potentials, propagation is essentially vacuum-like and the flux/flavor constraints reproduce attenuation bounds around \$\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi\lesssim10^{-29}\$–$10^{-28}\ {\rm cm^2\,GeV^{-1}}$, depending on the coupled flavor, potential sign and source composition. These are conditional limits for the adopted spike and source normalization.

At $A_\alpha\gg10^{-36}\ {\rm cm^3}$, the interacting flavor becomes nearly isolated in one matter eigenstate. Scattering can then remove that eigenstate while two others survive, producing a **survival plateau** instead of exponential extinction of the whole flux. Under the paper's 90%-attenuation criterion, the allowed cross section per mass can consequently weaken by as much as four orders of magnitude, reaching about **$1.4\times10^{-25}$ to $5.6\times10^{-25}\ {\rm cm^2\,GeV^{-1}}$** at $A_\alpha=10^{-30}\ {\rm cm^3}$. This is not a universal loosening of the bound: the result changes with flavor, sign and production channel, and resonant trajectories can move outside the IceCube flavor region before strong attenuation occurs.

For a muon-damped source with $V_\mu$, the unattenuated flavor ratio is already outside the adopted IceCube contour once $A_\mu\gtrsim9.52\times10^{-39}\ {\rm cm^3}$. In part of that parameter space, attenuation can move the ratio back into the contour, so the plotted curve becomes a **minimum** scattering strength rather than an upper limit. By then the total flux has lost more than 90%, underscoring that flavor agreement alone is insufficient.

**Model mapping.** For a benchmark vector mediator the paper uses the approximate relation \$\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi\sim$g_\nu g_\chi/4\pi$A_\alpha\$, illustrating coupling products $10^{-6}$, $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-12}$. These curves are benchmark mappings, not confidence intervals on the couplings.

**Limitations.** The calculation uses one energy, one source, a fixed spike profile, an adiabatic approximation and one nonzero flavor coupling. It removes scattered neutrinos without treating energy redistribution or regeneration. It also does not fit a source luminosity and spectral model jointly with the IceCube data. The flavor contours are still broad. A weaker attenuation bound in the survival plateau therefore reflects the assumed coherent propagation model, not evidence that large interactions are present.

##### History

The baseline idea predates this flavor calculation. **Ferrer, Herrera and Ibarra** used the same broad strategy—high-energy neutrinos escaping the dark-matter spike around TXS 0506+056—to constrain scattering from flux survival ([arXiv:2209.06339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06339), *JCAP* 05 (2023) 057). **Cline et al.** independently derived blazar constraints and emphasized their dependence on the spike and energy scaling ([arXiv:2209.02713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02713)). Those works largely treated attenuation without solving a flavor-dependent matter Hamiltonian.

Flavor evolution in a dark background has also been used to constrain nonstandard neutrino potentials through oscillation data and astrophysical flavor ratios; for example, **Capozzi, Shoemaker and Vecchi** showed how dark backgrounds can perturb the effective Hamiltonian ([arXiv:1804.05117](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05117), *JCAP* 07 (2018) 004). The new paper's substantive addition is to evolve the flavor eigenstates through a compact spike and apply attenuation in that matter basis, revealing the two-state plateau and sign-dependent resonances.

Its bounds should not be described as uniformly stronger than cosmological limits. CMB and large-scale-structure constraints integrate interactions in the early Universe and depend on temperature or energy scaling; the blazar constraint applies at 100 TeV and assumes a large local column. The strongest comparison is therefore internal: at fixed TXS profile, flavor-dependent propagation can move a nominal $10^{-28}\ {\rm cm^2\,GeV^{-1}}$ attenuation scale by orders of magnitude. The remaining uncertainty is dominated by the dark-matter spike, the intrinsic neutrino spectrum and composition, and whether the TXS association represents the diffuse IceCube population.

#### A projected LZ Migdal reach extends below a GeV, but it is not an observed-data limit

**Paper:** *Probing Dark Matter via the Migdal Effect in LUX-ZEPLIN Data* — **Yang Yu and Lei Feng**. [arXiv:2611.09106v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09106v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09106v1). Submitted **6 November 2026 at 07:38:27 UTC** and first listed **9 November**; primary category **astro-ph.HE**. No journal publication is listed. **Classification: Bayesian Asimov sensitivity forecast based on a published LZ exposure, not an official LZ exclusion and not a fit to observed LZ counts.**

**Question and signal.** A sub-GeV dark-matter particle transfers too little recoil energy to xenon for a conventional nuclear-recoil search. In a Migdal event, the sudden nuclear acceleration ionizes or excites the atom, supplying a detectable electronic energy. The paper asks how far LZ's **4.2 tonne-year** exposure could reach in spin-independent dark-matter–nucleon cross section if analyzed with Migdal templates.

**Method.** The calculation uses a Standard Halo Model with $v_0=220\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, $v_{\rm esc}=544\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ and Earth speed $232\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. Xenon ionization probabilities for the **1s through 5p shells** are taken from the standard atomic tables. The reconstructed electronic-equivalent window is **1–4 keVee**, and templates are generated at 20 logarithmically spaced masses from **100 MeV to 10 GeV**.

Crucially, the authors do not use the unblinded LZ event histogram. They set each bin to the expected background, $n_i=\mathcal{E}b_i$, producing an Asimov data set. The likelihood includes a **20% Gaussian signal-normalization nuisance** and **30% independent background uncertainties per bin**, integrated numerically with 16th-order Gauss–Hermite quadrature. Markov-chain sampling uses a 4,000-step burn-in followed by 20,000 samples. The reported bound is the **90th percentile of the marginalized Bayesian posterior** under the paper's stated priors.

**Results.** Below about **350 MeV**, the assumed halo cannot place an event in the 1-keVee window, so the analysis gives no finite constraint. From **350 MeV to 10 GeV**, the projected 90% upper limits span

\[
\sigma_{\chi N}^{\rm SI}=6.5\times10^{-37}\;\text{to}\;9.6\times10^{-42}\ {\rm cm^2}.
\]

The curve improves rapidly as more xenon shells and halo velocities become available, then approaches conventional recoil sensitivity toward the multi-GeV range. Halo variations matter most near threshold and shift the low-mass reach by tens of percent to factors of order unity in the authors' tests.

The paper also maps the cross-section sensitivity into a light-mediator combination

\[
\alpha_{\rm med}=\frac{g_Ng_\chi}{4\pi}
\]

for scalar and vector mediators, retaining the full momentum dependence. At the benchmark $m_\chi=1\$ GeV, the projected light-mediator sensitivity reaches roughly **$10^{-12}$**. Comparisons to kaon invisible-decay, horizontal-branch cooling and SN 1987A regions are useful orientation, but they involve different mediator couplings, production assumptions and trapping regimes. The claimed one-to-two-order gain is therefore only meaningful within the matched simplified models.

**Limitations.** This study forecasts median sensitivity from a background-only pseudo-data set. It neither accounts for the actual locations of LZ events nor reproduces the collaboration's detector response, event selection and correlated systematics. The per-bin background priors are a simplified stand-in for a full detector likelihood. Atomic Migdal probabilities and liquid-xenon response remain uncertain near threshold. A future collaboration analysis could be stronger or weaker, and the projected curve must not be cited as an LZ exclusion of dark matter.

##### History

The Migdal effect has already extended direct searches below the ordinary nuclear-recoil threshold. **XENON1T** used scintillation-plus-ionization and ionization-only data to probe masses down to about **85 MeV** ([arXiv:1907.12771](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12771), *PRL* 123, 241803). **DarkSide-50**, using a **12,306 ± 184 kg-day** argon exposure, reached **40 MeV** and reported the strongest spin-independent bounds below 3.6 GeV within its adopted model ([arXiv:2207.11967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11967), *PRL* 130, 101001). **PandaX-4T** published a low-mass ionization search in *PRL* 130, 261001 ([arXiv:2212.10067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10067)). These are observed-data collaboration results and are methodologically stronger baselines than an outside Asimov forecast.

The experimental status of Migdal ionization is nuanced. A gaseous detector reported **six candidates among nearly one million events**, a **5σ** excess over background and a Migdal-to-nuclear-recoil cross-section ratio \((4.9^{+2.6}_{-1.9})\times10^{-5}\) for recoils above 35 keVee with 5–10 keV electrons ([Nature 649, 580–583 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09918-8)). In contrast, a tagged-neutron search in liquid xenon did not observe the predicted M- and L-shell signal ([arXiv:2307.12952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12952), *PRD* 109, L051101). Thus, validation in one gas regime does not eliminate the liquid-phase response uncertainty relevant to LZ.

The 4.2 tonne-year LZ paper itself is a conventional WIMP search: it used 280 live days, observed no significant excess and set a 90% confidence spin-independent limit of **$2.1\times10^{-48}\ {\rm cm^2}$** at 36 GeV ([arXiv:2410.17036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17036), *PRL* 135, 011802). The new forecast borrows its exposure, not its low-energy likelihood. Its contribution is a concrete target for an official sub-GeV analysis; the dominant remaining questions are detector response, atomic ionization modelling and background control below 4 keVee.

#### Realistic nuclear transition densities do not remove the gallium anomaly

**Paper:** *Possible Solution to the Gallium Anomaly? Nuclear Transition Densities* — **D. Castillo, D. Frycz and J. Menéndez**. [arXiv:2611.09788v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09788v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09788v1). Submitted **6 November 2026 at 17:45:30 UTC** and first listed **9 November**; primary category **nucl-th**. No journal publication is listed. **Classification: nuclear shell-model calculation testing a recently proposed Standard Model resolution.**

**Question.** Calibration runs in GALLEX, SAGE and BEST observe about 20% fewer \$^{71}{\rm Ga}$\nu_e,e^-$^{71}{\rm Ge}\$ captures than the standard cross section predicts. A September 2026 *Physical Review Letters* paper proposed that the usual factorization of lepton and nuclear wavefunctions fails: specially fitted Gamow–Teller radial transition densities could reproduce the precisely known \$^{71}\$Ge electron-capture half-life while lowering the inverse reaction's cross section. Castillo and collaborators test whether such densities arise in realistic many-body nuclear calculations.

**Method.** The authors solve continuum and bound electron wavefunctions with a Dirac–Hartree–Fock–Slater treatment and keep the lepton functions inside the nuclear radial integral. They calculate nine shell-model Gamow–Teller transition densities using five Hamiltonians, bare and effective operators, and both (pf) and extended (pf+0g_{9/2}) spaces. Every calculation is normalized to the weighted electron-capture half-life

\[
t_{1/2}$^{71}{\rm Ge}$=11.465\pm0.003\ {\rm d}.
\]

This normalization absorbs leading quenching and small weak/radiative corrections. The shell-model densities are then folded with the electron wavefunctions for \$^{51}\$Cr and \$^{37}\$Ar source neutrinos, once using factorization and once without it.

**Why factorization works here.** Across the nuclear radius, the relevant electron Fermi function changes by only about **3%**. The nine shell-model density profiles are also remarkably similar despite the different Hamiltonians, spaces and operators. The calculation therefore finds no large radial lever arm with which the bound and continuum electron integrals could differ.

**Quantitative result.** For ground-state capture, the experimental weighted values used are

\[
\sigma$^{51}{\rm Cr}$=$4.44\pm0.33$\times10^{-45}\ {\rm cm^2},\qquad
\sigma$^{37}{\rm Ar}$=$5.21\pm0.72$\times10^{-45}\ {\rm cm^2}.
\]

The shell model gives, beyond factorization,

\[
\sigma$^{51}{\rm Cr}$=$5.26\pm0.01$\times10^{-45}\ {\rm cm^2},\qquad
\sigma$^{37}{\rm Ar}$=$6.30\pm0.01$\times10^{-45}\ {\rm cm^2}.
\]

The corresponding factorized values are $5.21\times10^{-45}$ and $6.24\times10^{-45}\ {\rm cm^2}$: agreement at about **1%**. Experiment-to-theory ratios remain near **0.83**, so the roughly 20% deficit survives.

The earlier fitted modified-double- and modified-triple-Gaussian profiles behave very differently. Their factorized capture cross sections exceed their nonfactorized values by factors of about **4** and **8**, and the factorized electron-capture half-lives collapse to **3.505 d** and **1.687 d**. Their positive and negative radial lobes cancel so that the net matrix element is only **2.5%** and **0.25%** of the integral of the absolute density. When the new authors fit a combination of shell-model-like single-particle radial functions freely, a solution requires coefficients roughly **3,000 times larger** than the computed values plus percent-level cancellation.

**Interpretation and limitations.** This is strong evidence against that particular nuclear-profile explanation, not a proof that the gallium anomaly is new physics. The shell model is not an ab initio solution for (A=71); two-body currents and more complete configuration spaces could modify the density. The authors argue that known corrections are too small to generate the required thousand-fold oscillating profile, but propose valence-space in-medium similarity-renormalization-group and other many-body calculations as independent tests. Experimental source activity, excited-state strength, cross-section inputs and sterile-neutrino interpretations remain separate uncertainties.

##### History

The modern anomaly is anchored by **BEST**, which placed a \$^{51}\$Cr source at the center of two gallium volumes and found consistent deficits in both zones ([arXiv:2109.11482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11482), *PRL* 128, 232501; see also *PRC* 105, 065502). Combined with the earlier GALLEX and SAGE source runs, the discrepancy is about 20% and exceeds 5σ under the adopted capture cross section. A sterile-neutrino oscillation can suppress both zones, but much of the preferred mixing region is disfavored by reactor, solar, accelerator and beta-spectrum data; those comparisons are not identical because their energies, baselines and systematics differ.

**Cadeddu et al.** shifted attention back to nuclear theory. Their peer-reviewed proposal, published **24 September 2026**, abandoned factorization and fitted radial Gamow–Teller profiles constrained by the electron-capture half-life ([arXiv:2512.20560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20560), *PRL* 137, 131805). It showed that a sufficiently structured profile could lower neutrino capture without changing electron capture. That was a mathematical existence result using phenomenological profiles, not a many-body derivation.

The new paper provides the missing nuclear-structure test. Its nine calculations agree that realistic densities are smooth enough for detailed balance to hold at the percent level. The disagreement is therefore physical and direct: the profile required by the proposed resolution is orders of magnitude larger and finely cancelling compared with the shell model. Remaining uncertainty lies in many-body truncation and weak currents, but the size of the required deformation makes this specific conventional explanation implausible.

#### INTEGRAL finds no Jovian MeV emission, setting a benchmark for COSI

**Paper:** *Constraints on Cosmic-Rays and the MeV Emission from Jupiter's Atmosphere* — **Dimitris Tsatsis, Thomas Siegert, Volodymyr Savchenko, Saurabh Mittal, Laura Eisenberger, Mika A. Gelowicz, Rudi Reinhardt, Manja L. Zimmerer, Tristan Bouchet, Tomohiko Oka and Manuel R. H. W. Skalka**. [arXiv:2611.09488v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09488v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09488v1). Submitted **6 November 2026 at 14:11:17 UTC** and first listed **9 November**; primary category **astro-ph.HE**. Accepted for publication in *Astronomy & Astrophysics*. **Classification: moving-source gamma-ray analysis with 2σ flux limits and a model-dependent 95% dark-matter exclusion.**

**Question and data.** Jupiter can reflect the cosmic gamma-ray background, produce atmospheric gamma rays when cosmic rays strike its atmosphere, radiate auroral bremsstrahlung, or emit photons from dark-matter annihilation into long-lived mediators. The paper searches the **30 keV–8 MeV** band in 22 years of serendipitous INTEGRAL/SPI pointings.

The authors cross-match SPI pointings with Jupiter's ephemeris and correct every model for the changing Earth–Jupiter distance. After excluding difficult Galactic-center fields, the usable exposure is **4.08 Ms**, compared with **11.6 Ms** in the full archive. They build a 1,000-km Jovian atmosphere in one-kilometre shells from Galileo profiles and simulate photons, protons, alpha particles, electrons and positrons. A forward model separates instrumental background, fixed celestial sources and a moving Jupiter component. The quasar 3C 273 is used as a calibration check.

**Results.** Every Jovian spectral point is consistent with zero, so the paper reports **2σ upper limits** rather than a detection. A cutoff-power-law fit restricts the MeV bremsstrahlung luminosity to about

\[
L_{\rm brem}\lesssim10^{18}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}},
\]

near the scale inferred indirectly from Juno/JEDI energetic electrons, but still far above the expected auroral flux at Earth. The authors estimate an intrinsic auroral continuum near $10^{-10}\ {\rm ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,keV^{-1}}$ at 20 keV, about five orders below their 30-keV limit.

For reflected background photons, the fitted amplitude has a 2σ upper limit **$9.2\times10^2$** relative to the model normalization, whereas the expected value derived from the Swift/BAT cosmic background is $$10.15\pm0.80$\times10^{-2}$. Current sensitivity is therefore about **four orders of magnitude** short. For cosmic-ray-induced emission the fitted 2σ scale is **$2.4\times10^2$**, compared with unity for the adopted cosmic-ray spectrum: about **two orders of magnitude** short. These nondetections constrain instrument sensitivity; they do not show that the cosmic-ray or reflected components are absent.

**Dark matter.** The model captures dark matter in Jupiter and lets \$\chi\bar\chi\to\phi\phi\to4\gamma\$, with mediators escaping before decay. SPI is combined with published Fermi-LAT limits, producing a continuous 95% exclusion for two mass ratios, $m_\phi=0.1m_\chi$ and $m_\phi=m_\chi/1.01$. SPI extends the Jovian search to roughly $m_\chi\sim10^{-4}$–$10^{-2}$ GeV, where its best standalone proton-scattering reach is around **$10^{-35}\ {\rm cm^2}$**. The exact curve depends strongly on mediator lifetime, annihilation equilibrium, evaporation and the width of the box spectrum. It is not a generic direct-detection bound.

**Limitations and outlook.** Jupiter subtends only 30–50 arcsec and is never a dedicated SPI target. Bright Galactic fields were discarded, losing most of the available exposure. The atmospheric yield inherits the assumed heliospheric cosmic-ray spectra and an 11-year solar modulation. The dark-matter limits assume efficient capture, long-lived mediators and specific branching. The planned COSI survey should accumulate **31–62 Ms** near the ecliptic with substantially better continuum sensitivity; the authors estimate that its exposure and response could reduce statistical uncertainties by at least an order of magnitude, but that is a forecast.

##### History

The first dedicated Jovian gamma-ray search used **12 years of Fermi-LAT data** and found no robust emission, setting flux limits near $10^{-9}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ and, under long-lived-mediator assumptions, dark-matter–proton limits as low as about $10^{-40}\ {\rm cm^2}$ ([Leane and Linden, *PRL* 131, 071001](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.131.071001), [arXiv:2104.02068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02068)). That stronger number occurs at different masses and in Fermi's GeV band; it must not be compared as a uniform five-order improvement over SPI.

The MeV work adds a new energy window and a moving-source analysis, rather than a more sensitive limit everywhere. Its cosmic-ray calculation also builds on planetary gamma-ray albedo studies and modern heliospheric spectra. The result is valuable precisely because the expected conventional components remain below reach: it establishes an empirical MeV baseline and validates a pipeline that COSI can reuse. The largest remaining instrumental uncertainty is background modelling near the Galactic plane; the largest physical uncertainty in the dark-matter interpretation is evaporation and mediator decay geometry.

#### Four high-energy transients arrived on 8 November; only two had arcminute X-ray follow-up at cutoff

**GRB 261108A** triggered SVOM/ECLAIRs at **01:13:20 UTC** and Fermi/GBM one second earlier. ECLAIRs localized it to **RA 336.5157°, Dec −20.2077°**, with a **4.79-arcmin 90% radius** and image significance 17.68 in 8–50 keV. SVOM/GRM measured $T_{90}=9.33^{+1.95}_{-1.42}$ s; Fermi's broader 50–300 keV interval gave about **24 s**, $E_{\rm peak}=87\pm4$ keV and 10–1000 keV fluence \$$8.1\pm0.6$\times10^{-7}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}}\$. The different durations reflect instruments and bands, not a contradiction. [GCN 46186](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46186), [GCN 46197](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46197). Early SVOM and ground-based images supplied only optical upper limits by the cutoff; no redshift or confirmed afterglow was available.

**GRB 261108B** was Fermi trigger 815809059 at **05:37:34.86 UTC**. Flight software initially classified it as distant particles, but the ground team corrected it to a likely long GRB at **RA 264.86°, Dec +80.18°**, statistical uncertainty **1.57°**. This is a classification correction, not a separate event. [GCN 46195](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46195).

**GRB 261108C** triggered SVOM/ECLAIRs at **21:19:10 UTC** with image S/N **13.48** in 5–20 keV, a **6.05-arcmin 90% radius** and $T_{90}\approx140$ s. MXT began after 131 s and found an uncatalogued X-ray source with a **76.22-arcsec 90% radius**. VT observations from 202 s found no credible uncatalogued optical source in the MXT region to **21.5 AB mag at 6.5σ**, at a mid-time of eight minutes. An optical non-detection does not mean there was no afterglow at other wavelengths or times. [GCN 46198](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46198), [GCN 46203](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46203).

**GRB 261108D** triggered ECLAIRs at **23:52:36 UTC**, with image S/N **7.57** in 5–20 keV and a **10.37-arcmin 90% radius** at **RA 170.8495°, Dec −77.4870°**. Sun constraints prevented an immediate MXT or VT slew, so its classification and counterpart status remained less secure. [GCN 46200](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46200). None of these bursts had a claimed neutrino or gravitational-wave association at the source-check cutoff.

#### Three Einstein Probe alerts were identified as stellar flares, not extragalactic transients

Follow-up converted three 6 November WXT alerts into likely stellar events. Trigger **01709322274**, at **21:28:37 UTC**, was accompanied by a fading optical high-proper-motion M dwarf 1.5 arcmin from the X-ray position; the star faded from 13.15 to 13.27 mag over 19 minutes. Einstein Probe associated it with Gaia DR3 111731654357442816 and estimated a 0.5–4 keV flux of about **$6\times10^{-11}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$** and luminosity **$3.6\times10^{29}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$**. [GCN 46177](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46177), [GCN 46179](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46179).

The EP team likewise associated trigger **01709322271** at **13:48:02 UTC** with the flaring star V* V1096 Tau, estimating $L_X\sim2.2\times10^{32}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$, and trigger **01709322272** at **18:19:44 UTC** with a Gaia high-proper-motion star at $L_X\sim2.9\times10^{30}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$. [GCN 46180](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46180), [GCN 46181](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46181). These classifications matter for alert accounting: the three triggers should not remain in an extragalactic fast-transient sample.

#### EP261015a is securely associated with a broad-lined Type Ic supernova at z = 0.310

VLT/FORS2 spectroscopy on **6 November**, 21.5 days after the Einstein Probe trigger, detected Hβ and [O III] λ5007 at both the transient and host positions, refining the redshift of **SN 2026ahoa** to **(z=0.310)**. [GCN 46174](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46174). Earlier spectroscopy classified the optical transient as a pre-peak broad-lined Type Ic supernova, so the new lines strengthen the host association and distance rather than establish a new class. A small velocity difference between the local supernova region and galaxy nucleus was reported but not yet interpreted. This is an association between the EP fast X-ray transient and a supernova; it does not by itself establish a successful relativistic jet or an off-axis GRB.

#### EP261105a continued to fade, while its possible low-redshift host remained tentative

The previous edition reported a red SVOM/VT candidate for **EP261105a**. VLT/FORS2 detected the same source at **R ≈ 24.3 AB** starting 18 hours after the 5 November trigger, compared with **VT_R = 22.3 ± 0.2 AB** at 0.85 hours. The two points imply a shallow approximate decay index $alpha\sim-0.5$, subject to filter and extinction differences. The source is about 4 arcsec from an $r\sim18$ galaxy with photometric redshift **$0.055\pm0.014$** and reported chance-coincidence probability **$P_{cc}\sim0.01$**; if associated, the projected offset is about **4.5 kpc**. [GCN 46170](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46170). This remains a candidate host because the redshift is photometric and the transient lacks spectroscopy.

### Science Radar

#### A liver-directed base editor suppresses ANGPTL3 for three years in primates

**Taiji Mizoguchi, Jamie E. DeNizio, Amit V. Khera and colleagues** published [*In Vivo CRISPR Base Editing of ANGPTL3 for Durable Lipid Lowering*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11233-9) in *Nature* on **6 November 2026**. This is peer-reviewed preclinical work that motivated an ongoing first-in-human trial; it is not a clinical efficacy result.

The treatment packages guide RNA and mRNA encoding an adenine base editor in a GalNAc-decorated lipid nanoparticle designed to reach hepatocytes and disrupt **ANGPTL3**, whose loss lowers LDL cholesterol and triglyceride-rich lipoproteins. In human liver cells, the authors report the intended base change with no detectable editing among roughly **3,000 nominated off-target sites** and no treatment-related structural variants in the assays used. Absence in a finite candidate panel is not proof that every rare genomic change is absent.

In nonhuman primates, one infusion reduced circulating ANGPTL3 protein by **more than 90% on average**, with follow-up to **three years**. Hyperlipidemic mouse and primate models showed about **40–50% lower LDL-C** and **50–70% lower triglycerides**. The long follow-up is the main advance: it supports durable liver editing after transient exposure to the editor.

The study must be separated from **CTX310**, a different CRISPR-Cas9 nuclease program already tested in 15 people. CTX310's 2026 phase-1 follow-up reported mean one-year reductions of about 79% in ANGPTL3, 53% in LDL-C and 48% in triglycerides at its highest dose. The new base-editor paper has not reported human outcomes. Key unknowns are rare off-target or bystander edits, immune responses, long-term liver safety, reversibility and whether lipid lowering translates into fewer cardiovascular events.

#### Catalonia's largest Palaeolithic cave-art corpus was hidden in a public cave

**Josep Maria Vergès Bosch and Míriam Salas-Altès** published [*Filling gaps: the Palaeolithic rock art of the Cova de la Font Major on the north-east Iberian Peninsula*](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10447) in *Antiquity* on **5 November 2026**. The open-access, peer-reviewed study documents **280 engraved motifs**, including **55 animal figures**, about 250 m inside a cave system in Catalonia. Quantitatively it is the third-largest known Palaeolithic parietal assemblage on the Iberian Mediterranean façade.

The cave had been open to visitors since 1957 and incorporated into a museum in 1993. Irregular walls and lighting made the shallow incisions difficult to see, while recent graffiti sometimes crossed them. The corpus includes horses, deer, an aurochs and a rare bear, alongside nonfigurative signs. Stylistic features such as double-Y extremities and tri-lined heads support a **pre-Magdalenian** attribution for many images.

The popular “26,000-year-old art” formulation is too definite. The engravings themselves have not been directly dated. Human activity in the inner cave is constrained by radiocarbon evidence to about **40,810–25,850 calibrated years before present**, and a 26,000-year layer contained a flint bladelet. At least one animal lies beneath calcite that may later permit uranium-series dating, but even that would date one surface relationship rather than the entire corpus. The robust conclusion is geographic and archaeological: a region thought nearly empty of Palaeolithic wall art contains a major assemblage, and survey visibility can create apparent cultural gaps.
