### Astrophysical Alerts

#### EP261122a — corrected X-ray fluxes reduce the inferred fading to a factor of at least 15

On **27 November at 05:32:29 UTC**, the Einstein Probe team revised its 25 November analysis of the fast X-ray transient that began on **22 November at 06:09:52 UTC**. In the first Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) exposure, starting at **07:17:14 UTC** that day, the revised unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is \(6.02^{+6.20}_{-2.75}\times10^{-13}\) erg cm⁻² s⁻¹, with photon index \(1.10^{+0.66}_{-0.65}\) (90% uncertainties). The source position is RA 49.3135°, Dec +16.4448° (J2000), with a 10″ radius at 90% confidence.

For the second FXT exposure, starting **24 November at 10:21:26 UTC**, the corrected **90% upper limit is \(4.0\times10^{-14}\) erg cm⁻² s⁻¹** in the same band. Using the first-epoch central flux, the team now infers fading by **at least 15**. The original circular gave an upper limit of \(1.2\times10^{-14}\) and inferred a factor of at least 32. The revised measurements still establish substantial fading. The source class remains open. [Revised Einstein Probe analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46366) · [Original analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46366/1)

#### GRB 261126A — corrected positions strengthen the case for a faint SVOM transient

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered on **26 November at 19:03:25 UTC** (sb26112602). The team attributed the offset in the initial wake-up localization to a structured background. The team adopted the position used to command the slew, **RA 27.319°, Dec +9.705°** (J2000), with an **8.1′ radius at 90% confidence**. Its image signal-to-noise ratio was **9.84** in **5–20 keV** over **10.24 s**. The team regards a faint transient as probable. The burst classification and any association with quasars in the field remain unconfirmed.

SVOM/MXT started at **19:05:43 UTC** and found a faint uncatalogued X-ray source at **RA 27.313°, Dec +9.706°**, with a **145″ radius at 90% confidence**. The public MXT notice used the displaced wake-up position as its reference. The mission announcement was issued at **20:20:10 UTC on 26 November**. Its **27 November 05:33:55 UTC** correction changed the reported separation from the adopted ECLAIRs position from **12.41′ to 0.35′**, placing the two centroids well within their quoted uncertainties. [SVOM correction](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46373) · [Original circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46373/1) · [SVOM public notices](https://fsc.svom.org/alerts)

MASTER-SAAO began imaging the initial alert field at **19:44:03 UTC** and reached 18.7 mag. In the MXT region, Las Cumbres Observatory obtained **\(r>21.5\)** at +1.69 h and **\(z>20.3\)** at +2.56 h (5σ AB). The Nordic Optical Telescope reached **\(r>22.4\)** at +2.9 h and **\(z>21.8\)** at +3.2 h (AB, confidence level unspecified). Both teams report observed magnitudes without a Galactic-extinction correction. These limits allow a fainter optical counterpart. [MASTER](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46372) · [LCO](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46374) · [Nordic Optical Telescope](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46376)

#### EP261126a — Einstein Probe localizes an X-ray transient near a catalogued star

Einstein Probe’s Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) triggered on **26 November at 22:04:27 UTC** (01709322957). WXT placed the source at RA 35.546°, Dec −0.629° (J2000) with a **3′** error radius. An automatic FXT follow-up then detected an uncatalogued source at **RA 35.5384°, Dec −0.6368°**, with a **20″** radius. Both radii include statistical and systematic uncertainties at 90% confidence. The mission report, issued **27 November at 02:06:27 UTC**, places the star **UCAC4 447-002984** about **26″** from the FXT centroid, just outside its 90% circle. A stellar flare remains possible, with no secure association. [Einstein Probe](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46378)

MASTER-SAAO started at **22:10:33 UTC** and reached 18.3 mag. The Nordic Optical Telescope began at **23:08:31 UTC** on 26 November. Image subtraction across the WXT region yielded **\(r>22.8\)** at +1.20 h and **\(z>22.1\)** at +1.50 h (3σ AB, uncorrected for Galactic extinction), reported **27 November at 00:23:59 UTC**. These observations constrain new optical emission at those epochs. [MASTER](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46375) · [Nordic Optical Telescope](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46377)

#### GRB 261127A — Fermi’s final real-time localization broadens the search region

Fermi/GBM triggered on **27 November at 06:42:39.34 UTC** (817454564 / bn261127280). The trigger significance was **5.1σ** over **1.024 s** in **47–291 keV**. A subsequent ground analysis measured **8.6σ** over **18.432 s** in approximately 44–280 keV. These are search integration intervals. GBM classified the event as a **likely long GRB** and had yet to report a measured \(T_{90}\) or fluence at the cutoff. [Fermi/GBM notices](https://gcn.gsfc.nasa.gov/other/817454564.fermi)

The **06:49:17 UTC** final real-time notice gave **RA 348.33°, Dec −74.59°** (J2000), with a **16.47° statistical radius**, superseding an intermediate **2.75°** estimate. The **06:50:29 UTC** circular links a HEALPix map that also incorporates localization systematics. The position was **21° from the LAT boresight**. The checked records contain no reported LAT detection or confirmed counterpart. The Swift/BAT GUANO entry still listed its analysis products as pending. ### Science News

#### ESO warns of lost observing area after the FCC approves a million orbital data centres

On **26 November 2026**, the European Southern Observatory called for international assessment of large satellite constellations after the US Federal Communications Commission authorised SpaceX’s Starmind system on **25 November**. The licence covers up to **one million** satellites in low Earth orbit, with deployment milestones extending to 2040. The FCC accepted SpaceX’s commitment to coordinate with the US National Science Foundation on optical astronomy. ESO and the American Astronomical Society question whether dimming individual spacecraft can protect observations at that scale. [ESO statement](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2614/) · [FCC order, paragraphs 16–17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf) · [AAS response](https://aas.org/press/spacex-data-center-fcc-approval)

Olivier Hainaut’s earlier peer-reviewed simulation shows why pointing direction matters. For a **300-second VLT/FORS2 exposure**, with the Sun 21° below the horizon and each satellite of visual magnitude 7 at a reference range of 550 km, trails contaminated **5.9%** of the field at zenith and **16.0%** at 30° elevation toward the Sun’s azimuth. These are predictions for a fully deployed constellation. The affected fraction depends on spacecraft brightness, orbital distribution, exposure and pointing. [Study and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202660006)

#### A shorter Riemann-hypothesis formalization passes a test stated entirely in terms of primes

On **26 November 2026 at 19:51:20 UTC**, Andrew Sutherland’s end-to-end Lean check accepted an explicit bound on fluctuations in the distribution of prime powers. It combined the compact proof released on **25 November** with a separately formalized equivalence between the classical Riemann hypothesis and Schoenfeld’s bound. For the Chebyshev function $\psi(x)=\sum_{p^k\le x}\log p$, summed over prime powers with $k\ge1$, the checked statement is

$$
|\psi(x)-x| < \frac{\sqrt{x}\,(\log x)^2}{8\pi}\qquad (x\ge 73.2).
$$

The earlier checks started with statements about zeros of zeta and Dirichlet $L$-functions. This check reaches a statement expressed directly in prime powers, with a specific constant and threshold that can be inspected independently. Several proof checkers accepted the build. The arithmetic conclusion still uses the compact RH proof as an input. The release authors report machine verification only and say a full human reading is outstanding. [Proof release](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean) · [Sutherland’s verification and logs](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/) · [Formal equivalence](https://github.com/AndrewVSutherland/schoenfeld-rh-lean)

#### Satellite measurements separate the area of the Antarctic ozone hole from its depth

ESA reported on **26 November 2026** that the Antarctic ozone hole covered **27.3 million km² by 20 September**, about **31% above** the average for that point in the season over the preceding 20 years. Copernicus Sentinel-5P measurements also showed that the depth of ozone depletion remained close to average. A strong, persistent polar vortex kept a larger region of the stratosphere cold enough for ozone-destroying reactions. [ESA satellite assessment](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/earth-observation-programmes/copernicus/sentinel-5p/ozone-hole-one-of-the-largest-in-decades)

An independent NASA–NOAA assessment, released **4 November**, averaged the area from **7 September to 13 October** and obtained **25 million km²**, the seventh largest seasonal mean in 48 years. The daily extent and the seasonal mean describe different features of the same season. Both assessments find the 2026 observations consistent with continued long-term recovery as atmospheric concentrations of ozone-depleting substances fall. [NOAA assessment](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-nasa-release-measurements-of-2026-ozone-hole)

#### Sequencing depth limits what AI models can learn about individual cells

Gokul Gowri and colleagues trained **more than 2,000 models** on gene-expression measurements, protein sequences and microscopy images. They varied measurement quality and scored each learned representation by how much information it retained about a signal withheld during training. In single-cell RNA data, discarding transcript counts progressively erased recoverable information. Protein-sequence models tolerated substitution rates typical of modern sequencing. The peer-reviewed paper was received **22 January 2026**, accepted **30 September** and published **26 November** in *Nature Machine Intelligence*. [Research article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01332-7)

The study separates two properties that matter when designing a sequencing experiment. The tested transformer models tolerated more noise, while a variational autoencoder recovered more information as measurements improved. The fitted relationship can help researchers weigh sequencing more cells against measuring each cell more deeply. Its predictive accuracy under real differences between laboratories or sequencing platforms remains untested. [Methods, Figure 2 and limitations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01332-7#Sec2)
