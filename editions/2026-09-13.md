### arXiv highlights

#### ★★★ 1. The Dark Dimension and Majorana Neutrinos

**Arturo de Giorgi, Dhruv Pasari**  
[arXiv:2609.10668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10668)

The setup is the “Dark Dimension” scenario motivated by swampland arguments: an extra spatial dimension with characteristic size

\[
R \sim 0.1-10~\mu{\rm m},
\]

with Majorana fermions propagating in the bulk and masses in roughly the \(1-10\) keV regime. The authors explicitly diagonalize the resulting neutrino/Kaluza–Klein system and then confront it with three rather different observables:

\[
\text{Daya Bay oscillations},\qquad
\text{KATRIN } \beta\text{-decay},\qquad
\text{KamLAND-Zen } 0\nu\beta\beta .
\]

The technically interesting point is the neutrinoless-double-beta-decay calculation. They derive a closed expression for the effective Majorana mass including the KK tower and find that the tower can **screen the \(0\nu\beta\beta\) amplitude** once the characteristic nuclear momentum exceeds the compactification and Majorana scales. Thus adding a tower of Majorana states does *not* simply add positive contributions to \(m_{\beta\beta}\); destructive structure emerges from the complete spectrum.

Within their preferred Dark-Dimension parameter window, **KATRIN becomes the strongest terrestrial constraint**, pushing the relevant Yukawa coupling to approximately

\[
y\lesssim {\cal O}(10^{-3}),
\]

while oscillation experiments and \(0\nu\beta\beta\) probe complementary regions of the Majorana-mass parameter space.

**Significance:** it is an unusually clean example where oscillations, direct kinematic neutrino-mass measurements and \(0\nu\beta\beta\) constrain the *same explicit BSM neutrino construction*. The KK screening of \(0\nu\beta\beta\) is a distinctive feature of the calculation.

**Classification:** **new neutrino/BSM phenomenology; high-interest theory paper.**

#### ★★★ 2. Nonthermal Solar Stalling of an Inelastic Scalar Signal in Xenon

**XinXin Qi, Hao Sun**  
[arXiv:2609.10636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10636)

This provides a counterpoint to the solar-neutrino/LZ paper covered in the previous report.

The authors construct a concrete anomaly-free

\[
U(1)_{B-3L_\tau}
\]

model containing an \(840\) GeV complex scalar DM particle undergoing an endothermic transition with splitting

\[
\delta\simeq 320~{\rm keV},
\]

mediated by a \(160\) MeV vector. Their benchmark gives about \(0.96\) expected accepted events in their reconstruction of the high-energy LZ search, i.e. roughly the scale required to interpret the single \(248\) keV LZ recoil candidate.

But the interesting physics is what happens **after DM is captured by the Sun**.

Instead of assuming rapid thermalization into a compact central DM distribution, they evolve the captured particles in energy, angular momentum and internal state, including thermal nuclear motion, finite scattering rates and excited-state decay. Because the scattering is endothermic, the particles eventually reach orbital configurations for which further up-scattering is kinematically forbidden.

They find a nonthermal population that effectively **stalls around**

\[
r_{\rm eff}\simeq0.194-0.198\,R_\odot,
\]

rather than thermalizing in the solar core. The resulting annihilation rate is only

\[
\Gamma_{\rm ann}\sim1.3\times10^{15}\ {\rm s^{-1}},
\]

which in their benchmark is more than three orders of magnitude below their IceCube-based neutrino proxy.

So the takeaway is subtle:

> Solar-neutrino constraints on *electroweak* inelastic DM cannot automatically be carried over to every inelastic-DM realization.

This paper provides an explicit light-mediator scalar counterexample.

**Significance:** the lesson is broader than the LZ anomaly. Whenever one maps DM capture to a neutrino signal, the usual capture–thermalization–annihilation equilibrium assumption can fail badly if the scattering has nontrivial kinematic thresholds.

**Classification:** **new model-specific phenomenology; particularly valuable as a counterpoint to broad solar-neutrino exclusion arguments.**

#### ★★☆ 3. Strong Black Hole Natal Kicks in Magnetized Accretion-Powered Explosions

**Sean E. Li, Ore Gottlieb, Brian D. Metzger**  
[arXiv:2609.10694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10694)

Using **3D general-relativistic MHD simulations extending to \(\sim30\) s after collapse**, they show that rapidly rotating, strongly magnetized collapsars can give newly formed black holes natal kicks of

\[
v_{\rm kick}\sim10^2-10^3~{\rm km\,s^{-1}},
\]

substantially larger than the weak BH kicks often assumed.

Three effects contribute:

\[
\text{asymmetric electromagnetic outflow},
\]

\[
\text{magnetic-flux eruptions},
\]

and

\[
\text{gravitational pull from asymmetric ejecta}.
\]

Faster-spinning BHs tend to receive larger and more spin-aligned kicks. Particularly strong asymmetries occur when the system undergoes a delayed transition into a **magnetically arrested disk (MAD)** state.

The neat astrophysical connection is that the strong coherent magnetic flux required to generate these kicks is also precisely the ingredient invoked for relativistic **GRB jets and jet-driven hypernovae**. Thus strong BH kicks might be an intrinsic consequence of some collapsar engines rather than an independent phenomenon.

Consequences include BH retention in clusters, binary disruption, spin-orbit misalignment and formation channels of high-spin BHs in or near the pair-instability mass gap.

**Significance:** this connects GRB-engine MHD directly to the compact-object populations measured by gravitational waves.

**Classification:** **genuinely new numerical result; strong high-energy-astrophysics paper.**

#### ★★☆ 4. Teglon: A Pixel-Level Pipeline for Galaxy-Informed Gravitational-Wave Follow-up Planning and Efficiency Analysis

**D. A. Coulter, P. Darc, C. D. Kilpatrick, R. J. Foley, P. McGill, S. D. Wyatt, R. Hausen**  
[arXiv:2609.10736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10736)

This is not a physics discovery but potentially useful multimessenger infrastructure.

Teglon takes a three-dimensional GW localization posterior and combines it with a **3D galaxy-completeness model**, producing a revised probability map informed by the known galaxy distribution. It can then ingest an arbitrary telescope footprint and continuously interpolate between pure galaxy targeting and ordinary sky tiling.

The clever extension is integration with **Redback**, allowing pixel-by-pixel detection efficiencies to be computed for transient models. The pipeline evaluates more than

\[
P(\mathrm{source\ is\ here}),
\]

but effectively

\[
P(\mathrm{detect}\mid\mathrm{source\ here},\,
\mathrm{instrument},\,t,\,\mathrm{transient\ model}).
\]

That is much more useful for assessing the actual efficiency of a follow-up campaign.

They report particularly substantial gains for telescopes with fields of view \(\lesssim1~{\rm deg}^2\), and Teglon has already been used through O3/O4 and is being prepared for O5.

**Significance:** the tool quantitatively connects GW posterior volumes to gamma-ray, X-ray and optical counterpart searches.

**Classification:** **methodological/tool paper, not a new astrophysical result.**

#### ★★☆ 5. Hierarchical Population Inference with Normalizing Flows for Binary Black Holes

**Leonardo Iampieri, Simone Mastrogiovanni**  
[arXiv:2609.11885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11885)

Instead of assuming a low-dimensional parametric BBH mass model, they represent the source population

\[
p(z,m_1,m_2)
\]

with a **normalizing flow embedded directly inside the hierarchical GW likelihood**. Event posterior samples propagate individual-event uncertainty, while injection campaigns encode detector selection effects.

There is a nice statistical subtlety here. Restricting the inference to the region sufficiently sampled by injections can itself create an apparent correlation between mass and redshift. They therefore introduce a mutual-information diagnostic comparing the inferred distribution with a redshift-independent reference subject to the *same support restriction*.

Applied to **GWTC-5.0**, the reconstruction independently recovers population features near

\[
m_1\sim10\,M_\odot,\qquad 35\,M_\odot,
\]

but finds **no significant evidence that the intrinsic primary-mass distribution evolves with redshift**.

**Significance:** mainly methodology—the warning about spurious correlations induced by selection/support boundaries is applicable well beyond gravitational-wave inference.

**Classification:** **strong methodological work; result itself is broadly consistent with existing LVK population conclusions.**

#### ★★★ ALICE sees a saturation-like suppression of \(J/\psi\) production at subnucleonic scales

This story received fresh broad science coverage on **12 September**, although an important clarification is needed: **the measurement itself is not new this weekend**. The ALICE paper was originally posted in 2025 and published in *Physical Review Letters* in 2026; what is new is the renewed reporting around it.

The physics, however, is interesting enough to flag.

ALICE studied **incoherent photonuclear \(J/\psi\) production** in ultraperipheral Pb-Pb collisions at

\[
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.02~{\rm TeV}.
\]

By binning simultaneously in photon-nucleus energy and momentum transfer \(t\), the measurement probes progressively smaller transverse structures within the nucleus. At the highest measured momentum transfer,

\[
0.81<|t|<1.44~{\rm GeV}^2,
\]

ALICE becomes sensitive to **subnucleonic gluon structure**.

The striking observation is that the \(J/\psi\) cross section grows with energy significantly more slowly at high \(|t|\) than at low \(|t|\). The reported suppression is at roughly the **\(3\sigma\)** level. Conventional nuclear-shadowing descriptions have difficulty reproducing the pattern, while models containing **gluon saturation** naturally generate similar behaviour.

Conceptually, the relevant competition is

\[
g\rightarrow gg
\]

versus high-density nonlinear recombination such as

\[
gg\rightarrow g ,
\]

eventually preventing the small-\(x\) gluon density from increasing indefinitely.

The effective transverse resolution reaches roughly

\[
0.2~{\rm fm},
\]

about one-quarter of a proton radius.

This is **not a discovery of the Color Glass Condensate or definitive observation of saturation**. It is better described as evidence for a suppression pattern consistent with saturation and difficult for simple shadowing models to explain.

[ALICE arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18708)  
[Published PRL DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/jmwb-75m7)

**Significance:** saturation/small-\(x\) QCD ultimately feeds into the extrapolation of hadronic interactions far above accelerator energies, so improved experimental access to nonlinear nuclear gluon dynamics is relevant conceptually to UHECR shower modelling as well.

#### ★★☆ Transient watch: SN 2026aaiv in NGC 7331

A nearby **Type Ia supernova**, SN 2026aaiv, discovered by ATLAS on **1 September**, continues to attract attention as it evolves in NGC 7331. Its host has

\[
z_{\rm host}\simeq0.00272,
\]

corresponding to a distance of order \(40\)-\(45\) million light-years. Spectroscopic observations classify the event as Type Ia.

The amusing statistical coincidence is that **NGC 7331 also hosted the bright Type Ia SN 2025rbs last year**. Two Type Ia explosions in the same nearby galaxy on such a short human timescale make this a particularly attractive monitoring target even though there is currently no indication of exotic physics or a high-energy counterpart.

**Status:** watch-list event.
