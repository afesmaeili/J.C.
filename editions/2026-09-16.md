### ArXiv highlights

#### ★★★ 1. A fast radio burst from a non-targeted low-frequency survey

**Cristian Di Pietrantonio et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.17275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17275)

Using an imaging-based search of Murchison Widefield Array observations, the authors report the **first blind—or non-targeted—FRB detection below 300 MHz**. The only previously confirmed FRB at such frequencies came from targeted monitoring of the already-known repeater FRB 20180916B.

This matters physically because plasma scattering and free–free absorption should become much stronger toward low frequencies. The inferred event rate is nevertheless compatible with rates measured above 400 MHz, disfavoring explanations in which severe low-frequency suppression is generic across the FRB population. It also demonstrates that wide-field imaging searches can handle the tens-of-seconds dispersion delays that make conventional low-frequency transient searches computationally difficult.

#### ★★★ 2. Caught by its own light: Wimpzillas, the LHAASO knee and the diffuse γ-ray sky

**D. Akl, A. J. Iovino, D. Kantzas, A. V. Macciò**  
[arXiv:2609.17315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17315)

The authors test whether decays of superheavy dark matter in the Galactic halo could explain both the proton hardening at approximately

\[
E\simeq0.34~{\rm PeV}
\]

and the knee near

\[
E\simeq3.3~{\rm PeV}
\]

reported by LHAASO. A decaying relic with

\[
m_\chi\simeq5\times10^7~{\rm GeV}
\]

provides a good fit to the proton spectrum across all three hadronic-interaction-model reconstructions considered.

The same hadronic cascade inevitably produces photons, however. Those photons exceed the measured cosmic-ray photon fraction by roughly **two orders of magnitude** and the LHAASO diffuse Galactic gamma-ray emission by about **one order of magnitude**.

The result is a particularly clean example of a multimessenger consistency test:

\[
\text{fit to CR nucleons}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{viable model once }\gamma\text{-rays are included}.
\]

#### ★★★ 3. Right Energy, Wrong Profile: Why the 43 GeV Cluster Line Is Unlikely to Be Dark Matter

**Stefano Profumo**  
[arXiv:2609.16425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16425)

This reanalysis revisits the reported narrow Fermi-LAT feature around 43 GeV from the Virgo, Fornax and Ophiuchus clusters.

The excess remains present under several instrumental and data-splitting checks, but its inferred morphology is unexpectedly broad: uniform surface brightness or strongly substructure-enhanced emission is preferred over a smooth NFW annihilation template or central point source. Accounting for the restricted energy and morphology scan gives a conditional global significance of only about

\[
2.7\sigma,
\]

with the maximum near \(44.5\) GeV. Because the three-cluster sample had already been selected, this is not an unconditional discovery significance.

A dark-matter interpretation would require an extreme subhalo boost and a large \(\gamma\gamma\) branching fraction, leading to an annihilation cross section already in tension with stronger Galactic-halo line searches. The favored explanation is therefore a statistical fluctuation, possibly assisted by diffuse-background or unresolved-source mismodelling.

#### ★★★ 4. Dark Matter Inelastic Scattering with Nuclei for Direct Detection

**Shao-Feng Ge, Oleg Titov, Yakun Wang**  
[arXiv:2609.16529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16529)

The paper calculates elastic and inelastic WIMP response functions for \(^{129}\mathrm{Xe}\) and \(^{131}\mathrm{Xe}\) using relativistic configuration-interaction density-functional theory rather than the nuclear shell model normally adopted in xenon analyses.

After performing the multipole expansion and imposing angular-momentum, parity and time-reversal selection rules, the authors find that nuclear excitation cannot always be treated as a small correction. Depending on the effective DM operator:

- the inelastic rate can be comparable to the elastic rate;
- for some operators it can dominate by as much as three orders of magnitude;
- transitions to higher excited states can be as important as the first excited state.

The resulting response functions differ significantly from shell-model calculations in some channels, potentially changing the interpretation of xenon direct-detection limits and high-energy recoil events.

#### ★★☆ 5. Multiple-Mediator-Affected Ultra-High-Energy Neutrino Attenuation: Hints for 5D \(U(1)_{L_\mu-L_\tau}\) in IceCube

**Atri Bhattacharya, Ayushi Kaushik, Kenji Nishiwaki**  
[arXiv:2609.16139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16139)

The authors attempt to reproduce the softening of the IceCube spectrum above tens of TeV through scattering of astrophysical neutrinos on the cosmic neutrino background.

Instead of one \(L_\mu-L_\tau\) mediator, a compact fifth dimension generates a Kaluza–Klein tower. This produces multiple nearby \(s\)-channel resonances, supplemented by \(t\)- and \(u\)-channel scattering. Repeated interactions can consequently create broad, energy-dependent attenuation rather than the isolated absorption dip typical of a single mediator.

The mechanism can soften an injected single power law, but its interpretation is highly model-dependent: it depends on the compactification scale, coupling, mediator spectrum, neutrino masses and assumed unattenuated source population. “Hints” in the title should therefore not be read as statistical evidence for an extra dimension.

#### ★★☆ 6. Probing multi-state dark matter via optical absorption lines in DESI spectra

**Anoma Ganguly, Shadab Alam**  
[arXiv:2609.16547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16547)

For two-state dark matter with a magnetic-dipole transition, halo DM can resonantly absorb photons with

\[
E_\gamma=\Delta E,
\]

producing a narrow line in the spectra of background galaxies or quasars.

The authors stack **145,087 DESI Early Data Release spectra** whose sightlines pass through foreground GAMA galaxy groups. No absorption line is found, and they derive \(2\sigma\) limits on the transition cross section.

The strongest sensitivity occurs for splittings around \(3\!-\!5\) eV and DM masses of a few MeV, including parameter space poorly covered by direct detection and conventional BBN/CMB limits. The full DESI sample could improve the sensitivity by more than an order of magnitude.

#### ★★☆ 7. Testing a Pre-Supernova Contribution to \(^{44}\)Ti in Cassiopeia A

**Toshiki Sato et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.16826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16826)

The usual interpretation treats the \(^{44}\mathrm{Ti}\) mapped by NuSTAR in Cas A as a tracer of asymmetric explosive burning. This paper tests whether a significant fraction could instead have been synthesized before core collapse in an oxygen–carbon shell merger.

The low- and intermediate-velocity \(^{44}\mathrm{Ti}\) components correlate more strongly with oxygen-layer tracers than with shocked iron-rich material. If that association is pre-supernova in origin, it could account for as much as

\[
M_{^{44}\mathrm{Ti}}\sim7\times10^{-5}M_\odot,
\]

roughly half the observed inventory. Producing this amount requires stronger ingestion and convection than standard one-dimensional stellar-evolution models predict. XRISM measurements of stable Fe-group abundance ratios provide an independent restriction on how much \(^{44}\mathrm{Ti}\) can come from explosive burning.

#### ★★☆ 8. Constraints on the central engine of merger-driven long gamma-ray bursts

**Muskan Yadav et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.17507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17507)

The paper examines the emerging class of long-duration GRBs apparently produced by compact-object mergers rather than massive-star collapse.

ATCA observations of seven nearby candidates at \(2.1\) GHz, taken \(520\!-\!6900\) days after the bursts, yield no late-time counterparts, with \(3\sigma\) limits of \(33\!-\!72~\mu{\rm Jy}\). These limits do not yet exclude energetic magnetar-driven ejecta when the circumburst density is low.

Combining the radio nondetections with fallback-powered prompt-emission modelling sharply restricts the allowed magnetar spin, magnetic field and jet baryon loading. A magnetar engine remains possible, but only in a comparatively narrow region of parameter space.

MAXI/GSC detected a bright, previously uncatalogued X-ray transient at **08:51:44 UTC on 16 September**. The initial position is

\[
{\rm RA}=17.811^\circ,\qquad
{\rm Dec}=-26.193^\circ,
\]

with a statistical \(90\%\) error ellipse of approximately \(0.19^\circ\times0.18^\circ\), plus a \(0.1^\circ\) systematic uncertainty. Its scan-averaged \(4\!-\!10\) keV flux was

\[
165\pm26~{\rm mCrab},
\]

whereas the preceding transit gave a \(20\) mCrab upper limit. This establishes rapid X-ray variability but does not yet establish the source class. [Official MAXI circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45592)

MASTER began optical observations about 47 minutes after the trigger and found no counterpart down to approximately magnitude 20. [MASTER follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45591)

**Status:** provisional new high-energy transient, presently designated GRB 260916A, but without a reported optical afterglow, redshift, neutrino association or unusual gamma-ray property. It is a watch-list event rather than a major multimessenger discovery.
