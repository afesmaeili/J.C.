### ArXiv highlights

#### ★★★ 1. Lepto-hadronic modeling of blazars associated with well-reconstructed high-energy neutrino events

**Leonard Pfeiffer, Margot Boughelilba, Simone Garrappa, Sara Buson, Massimiliano Lincetto, Annette Bremer**  
[arXiv:2609.19477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19477)

The authors examine six blazars that are the sole gamma-ray counterparts inside the reconstructed directions of well-localized IceCAT-1 neutrino events, also accounting for the improved localizations anticipated from IceCAT-2.

Multifrequency spectral-energy distributions are fitted using compact one-zone lepto-hadronic models. The preferred emission regions lie near the broad-line region, where external photons can substantially enhance the photohadronic interaction rate. Most fitted gamma-ray spectra remain leptonic-dominated, with hadronic cascades subdominant; one object permits hadronically dominated gamma-ray emission.

The maximum predicted IceCube rate is approximately

\[
R_\nu\sim5\times10^{-2}\ {\rm yr^{-1}}.
\]

This can be statistically compatible with one detected event for several sources, but it does not establish a physical association. The modeled objects would contribute negligibly to the diffuse astrophysical-neutrino flux.

#### ★★★ 2. Testing Higgs-Coupled Minimal Dark Matter with Solar Neutrinos after the LZ High-Recoil Event

**Mattia Di Mauro**  
[arXiv:2609.19174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19174)

This paper subjects Higgs-coupled minimal dark-matter interpretations of the isolated **248-keV LZ recoil candidate** to an independent solar-neutrino test.

The electroweak interaction responsible for endothermic xenon scattering also permits capture in the Sun. The calculation includes exact inelastic kinematics, realistic isotope mixtures, nuclear form factors, thermal nuclear motion near the capture threshold, orbital cooling through loop-induced elastic scattering, and an explicit test of capture–annihilation equilibrium.

For two thermal benchmarks within IceCube’s published mass grid, the predicted solar annihilation rates exceed the corresponding \(W^+W^-\) IceCube response by factors of approximately

\[
2.2\times10^3
\quad\text{and}\quad
91.
\]

A 22-TeV benchmark remains strongly disfavored under a conservative extrapolation beyond IceCube’s published 10-TeV grid. The 48-TeV solution is closer to the sensitivity boundary, while the conclusions for 82 and 130 TeV become increasingly response-dependent.

#### ★★★ 3. On the Clustering Bias of Unresolved Gamma-Ray Sources

**Bhashin A. Thakore, Marco Regis, Michela Negro, Stefano Camera, Daniel Gruen, Nicolao Fornengo, Aaron J. Roodman**  
[arXiv:2609.19259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19259)

Cross-correlations between the unresolved gamma-ray background and weak gravitational lensing depend not only on source luminosities and redshifts, but also on how strongly the unresolved sources trace the matter distribution.

The authors connect gamma-ray luminosity to host-halo mass and calculate the effective redshift-dependent linear bias. For a reference unresolved-blazar population, the cross-correlation is dominated around \(z\simeq1\) by sources with

\[
\langle b\rangle\gtrsim2,
\qquad
\langle M_{\rm halo}\rangle\sim10^{13}M_\odot.
\]

Replacing the blazar population with a misaligned-AGN model gives similar effective bias values. The inferred clustering is therefore comparatively insensitive to the detailed gamma-ray source prescription.

This is relevant when using UGRB–large-scale-structure correlations to constrain unresolved astrophysical populations or exotic components such as annihilating dark matter: treating the source bias too simplistically can shift the inferred component amplitudes.

#### ★★★ 4. A systematic search for long GRBs without supernovae: global properties and rate of events

**Yu-Han Yang, Eleonora Troja, Nicolas Monsalves, Massine El Kabir, Narjes Shahamat Dehsorkh, Hira Waseem**  
[arXiv:2609.20787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20787)

Using two decades of Swift data, the authors systematically search for nearby,

\[
z\lesssim0.35,
\]

long-duration GRBs lacking an accompanying bright supernova. The selected population is heterogeneous: some events lie in star-forming or dusty environments, while others are associated with relatively quiescent galaxies.

Requiring meaningful distance information and sufficiently deep supernova limits more than doubles the previous sample of credible SN-less long-GRB candidates. The inferred unbeamed volumetric rate is

\[
R=0.5\pm0.2\ {\rm Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}},
\]

comparable to the apparent rates of short GRBs and luminous conventional long GRBs.

Compact-object mergers can reproduce this rate for plausible beaming corrections, but assigning the entire population to any single merger channel already restricts the allowed parameter space. The present sample cannot establish whether SN-less long GRBs form a distinct progenitor class.

#### ★★★ 5. Constraints on the Higgs boson total width from on-shell signal-background interference in \(H\rightarrow\gamma\gamma\)

**ATLAS Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2609.19948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19948)

ATLAS introduces a direct on-shell approach to the Higgs total width. The method uses interference between the resonant

\[
pp\rightarrow H\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
\]

amplitude and the continuum diphoton background. This interference slightly distorts the diphoton invariant-mass distribution in a width-dependent manner.

Using the full \(140~{\rm fb^{-1}}\) Run-2 dataset, ATLAS obtains

\[
\Gamma_H<307~{\rm MeV}
\qquad(95\%~{\rm CL}),
\]

compared with an expected limit of \(258\) MeV and the Standard-Model prediction

\[
\Gamma_H^{\rm SM}\simeq4.1~{\rm MeV}.
\]

The numerical constraint is much weaker than indirect off-shell limits, but it does not require assumptions connecting the on-shell and off-shell Higgs couplings.

#### ★★☆ 6. Probing Scalar-Induced First-Order Phase Transitions with the Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background

**Sudipta Das, Shamik Niyogi, Manibrata Sen**  
[arXiv:2609.19271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19271)

The paper considers a light scalar coupled to neutrinos and thermalized inside proto-neutron stars. A scalar-driven first-order phase transition would generate two additional neutrino components:

- a prompt contribution from thermally produced scalars;
- a delayed, spectrally narrower component from coherent scalar excitations produced during the transition.

After integrating over the cosmic core-collapse history, the authors calculate the resulting DSNB spectra for Super-Kamiokande, Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE. The constraints depend strongly on neutrino mass ordering and absolute mass scale. Existing Super-K data already probe part of the parameter space for inverted ordering with quasi-degenerate masses.

#### ★★☆ 7. Investigating the influence of full-kinematics charged-current processes in binary neutron-star mergers

**Ramon Jaeger, Henrique Gieg, Tim Dietrich**  
[arXiv:2609.19922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19922)

Binary-neutron-star simulations commonly calculate charged-current neutrino reactions using an elastic approximation. This work compares that treatment with NuLib rates and a full-kinematics calculation within M1 neutrino transport.

The elastic approximation slightly overestimates dynamical ejecta and early neutrino luminosities while underproducing high-electron-fraction ejecta. However, NuLib and full-kinematics simulations agree within current numerical uncertainties. Their post-merger gravitational-wave spectra are indistinguishable, and most nucleosynthetic yields remain consistent.

The main exception occurs around

\[
A\simeq90-125,
\]

where the elastic approximation predicts larger abundances. Differences also appear in remnant-disk properties and could become more consequential in longer secular simulations.

#### ★★☆ 8. A 32-day quasi-periodic modulation in the post-peak light curve of SN 2018bsz

**Aiswarya Sankar K. et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.19984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19984)

A multiband reanalysis of the nearby hydrogen-poor superluminous supernova SN 2018bsz finds phase-coherent post-maximum residual variability across ten optical and near-infrared bands.

The joint rest-frame period is

\[
P=31.61\pm0.03~{\rm days},
\]

although only about three cycles are observed, so the signal is appropriately classified as quasi-periodic. Its amplitude rises toward shorter wavelengths. Tests using alternative detrending choices, comparison stars and the lunar cycle disfavor straightforward analysis or observing artifacts.

Possible explanations include Lense–Thirring precession of a central engine, interaction with circumstellar material structured before the explosion, or interaction with a surviving binary companion. Current data do not discriminate among them.

#### ★★☆ CMS reports precision measurements of six rare \(b\rightarrow s\mu^+\mu^-\) channels

CMS has released a unified Run-3 analysis of six rare decay channels using data collected from **2022–2024**:

\[
B^+\rightarrow K^+\mu\mu,\quad
B^+\rightarrow K^{*+}\mu\mu,\quad
B^0\rightarrow K_S^0\mu\mu,\quad
B^0\rightarrow K^{*0}\mu\mu,
\]
\[
B_s^0\rightarrow\mu\mu,\quad
\Lambda_b^0\rightarrow\Lambda^0\mu\mu.
\]

An inclusive dimuon trigger substantially increases the event samples, reducing some statistical uncertainties to the percent level. Across all six modes, the measured branching fractions remain systematically below several Standard-Model calculations, consistent with the pattern previously seen by CMS and LHCb.

The individual discrepancies remain modest, and hadronic theory uncertainties are still central to the interpretation. Isospin asymmetries are consistent with zero, as expected in the Standard Model. This is therefore **not evidence for new physics**, but it strengthens the experimental precision of the broader \(b\rightarrow s\mu\mu\) puzzle. [CMS official briefing](https://cms.cern/news/when-rare-decays-enter-precision-era)

#### IceCube-260916A follow-up finds no significant additional neutrino emission

IceCube searched for additional track-like events around IceCube-260916A.

In the \(\pm500\)-second window, excluding the original alert, the search found **zero additional tracks**, with \(p=1.00\). A two-day search yielded \(p=0.05\), which IceCube reports as consistent with no significant excess.

For an assumed \(E^{-2.5}\) spectrum, the quoted sensitivities at 1 TeV are

\[
E^2\frac{dN}{dE}
\simeq1.4\times10^{-1}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}}
\]

for 1000 seconds and \(1.6\times10^{-1}\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-2}}\) for two days. The alert consequently remains an isolated Bronze-track candidate with no established source association. [Official IceCube follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45615)

#### Transient watch: GRB 260918A and EP260917a

**GRB 260918A** triggered SVOM/ECLAIRs at **03:47:56 UTC on 18 September**. It was detected at image significance \(7.60\) over \(40.96\) seconds in the \(8-120\) keV band. SVOM slewed to the field, but early COLIBRÍ and LCO observations found no optical counterpart, reaching approximately \(r>23.46\) in the deeper stack. [SVOM detection](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45619), [COLIBRÍ limit](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45620)

Einstein Probe also reported **EP260917a**, a separate soft-X-ray transient lasting approximately 1500 seconds. Its average unabsorbed \(0.5-4\) keV flux was

\[
5.6\times10^{-10}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}},
\]

peaking near \(2.1\times10^{-9}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). Its astrophysical classification is not yet known. [Einstein Probe circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45621)

Neither transient currently has a reported neutrino, gravitational-wave or otherwise exceptional multimessenger association.
