#### ★★★ 1. Sterile Neutrino Dark Matter Cries for GeV Heavy Neutral Leptons

**Marco Drewes, Yannis Georis, Juraj Klarić, Mikhail Shaposhnikov, Inar Timiryasov**  
[arXiv:2609.28457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28457)

The authors perform the first modern parameter scan of the \(\nu\)MSM that consistently evolves the quantum kinetics of its three right-handed neutrinos through baryogenesis, late-time lepton-asymmetry production and resonant sterile-neutrino dark-matter production.

For a benchmark \(7\)-keV dark-matter state, producing the required late lepton asymmetry confines the heavier quasi-degenerate pair to

\[
M_{2,3}\lesssim 50~{\rm GeV},
\]

with mixing angles above the minimal seesaw floor throughout the meson-decay range. A newly identified self-amplified MSW “ignition” mechanism reopens masses below the \(B\)-meson threshold. SHiP should cover the complete allowed region below approximately \(2\) GeV and a significant part up to \(5\) GeV; LHC and future lepton-collider searches cover higher masses.

##### History

The original comprehensive \(\nu\)MSM scans found regions simultaneously compatible with neutrino masses, baryogenesis and resonantly produced dark matter, but used kinetic treatments later shown to be incomplete. ([Canetti et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3902)) Subsequent calculations improved thermal rates and resonance dynamics without producing a new global scan. ([Ghiglieri and Laine 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08814), [2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10766)) This paper closes that technical gap and turns the all-in-one \(\nu\)MSM explanation into a bounded, experimentally testable target rather than a broadly open scenario. Important residual uncertainties arise below \(M\simeq500\) MeV, where hadronic HNL decays become difficult to treat, and above \(50\) GeV, where the employed Fermi-theory description breaks down. The quantitative region also depends on the assumed sterile-dark-matter mass and its astrophysical constraints.

#### ★★★ 2. Tension between MiniBooNE and MicroBooNE within a \(3+1\) sterile-neutrino framework using simulation-based inference

**Julia P. Woodward, Austin Schneider, Joshua Villarreal, John Hardin, Janet Conrad**  
[arXiv:2609.26965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26965)

A common simulation and statistical framework is constructed for MiniBooNE and the MicroBooNE BNB and NuMI samples. Unlike the published analyses, post-hoc data-driven flux-correction factors are omitted consistently from both experiments.

Trial-calibrated simulation-based inference gives a residual preference for \(3+1\) oscillations of

\[
2.7\sigma
\]

after MicroBooNE is included. The parameter-goodness-of-fit test nevertheless finds at least \(2.5\sigma\) tension between MiniBooNE and MicroBooNE within the minimal model.

##### History

MiniBooNE reported a \(4.8\sigma\) electron-like excess and a roughly \(4.6\sigma\) oscillation preference, whereas MicroBooNE’s inclusive electron-neutrino searches found no corresponding excess. ([MiniBooNE final sample](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16883), [MicroBooNE Wire-Cell search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13978)) A previous combined fit still admitted parts of \(3+1\) parameter space, but relied on asymptotic likelihood statistics and less complete MicroBooNE information. ([Previous combined fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01724)) The new result supplies the most carefully trial-calibrated direct consistency test to date and shows that neither “MicroBooNE completely eliminates MiniBooNE” nor “the combined data prefer sterile neutrinos” adequately summarizes the situation: the residual preference is modest, and the two datasets are themselves mutually uncomfortable in \(3+1\).

This is an independent reconstruction, not a collaboration likelihood. MicroBooNE has not released event-level Monte Carlo containing all true and reconstructed variables, forcing the authors to approximate detector efficiencies and smearing. The quoted tension therefore should not be treated as a final experimental combination.

#### ★★★ 3. The universe as a superconductor: How to search for a photon mass

**Andrei Gruzinov, Anson Hook, Junwu Huang**  
[arXiv:2609.26888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26888)

Conventional photon-mass limits normally assume an explicit Proca mass. Here the mass instead arises through an Abelian Higgs field, making electromagnetism analogous to a superconductor with Meissner, vortex and symmetry-restored phases.

Because ambient magnetic fields can restore the \(U(1)\) symmetry, a photon may be massive in vacuum but effectively massless on Earth or inside galaxies. For a Type-I realization, the authors find that conventional limits can formally permit a vacuum mass as large as

\[
m_\gamma\sim 0.3~{\rm MeV}.
\]

For Type-II models, a generalized magnetohydrodynamic calculation makes Galactic-dynamo operation the principal constraint. The authors identify fast-radio-burst dispersion, frequency-dependent cosmic opacity, magnetically shielded laboratory volumes and space-based Coulomb-law tests as complementary probes.

##### History

It has been known for two decades that vortices in a Higgs realization can invalidate magnetic-energy bounds that are extremely strong for Proca electromagnetism. ([Adelberger, Dvali and Gruzinov](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0306245); [photon-mass review](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1003)) The new paper’s advance is a systematic three-parameter phase diagram, including symmetry restoration, vortex formation, plasma currents and Galactic dynamo evolution. It therefore clarifies which existing bounds apply in which physical environment and proposes searches targeted specifically at the remaining regions.

The \(0.3\)-MeV statement is not a generic relaxation of the photon-mass limit: it requires an extraordinarily light millicharged Higgs, extremely small couplings and a very flat potential. The paper excludes the most severe order-by-order tunings but still occupies an exotic theoretical regime. No established Proca bound is contradicted.

#### ★★★ 4. A coherent search for new Galactic magnetars using Fermi/GBM and follow-up Swift/BAT

**Ariel Perera, Barak Zackay, Tejaswi Venumadhav**  
[arXiv:2609.26897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26897)

A coherent Poisson matched-filter search is applied to continuous Fermi/GBM data from 2013–2025. Archival Swift/BAT imaging and spatiotemporal clustering are then used to associate the broadly localized GBM events with individual sources.

The analysis identifies 1,720 magnetar-like bursts:

- 1,170 are associated with 11 known magnetars;
- 550 remain unassociated;
- seven spatially distinct clusters are proposed as candidate new magnetars;
- three candidates possess BAT localizations sufficiently narrow for targeted X-ray follow-up.

The associated sample extends below \(10^{-8}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}}\) in fluence.

##### History

The recent independent 17-year GBM catalog associated 735 bursts over the overlapping observing span. ([Godwin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02809)) The coherent search raises that number to 1,170 and combines GBM sensitivity with BAT’s arcminute localization, making the source-association methodology—not merely the longer exposure—the key improvement. The catalog and posterior samples are public.

The seven objects remain candidates. Four have localization regions too broad for efficient pointed observations, while even the BAT-localized candidates require persistent X-ray emission, pulsations or repeated localized activity for confirmation. The search completeness as a function of fluence is not established, so the low-fluence turnover cannot yet be used for an intrinsic luminosity-function measurement.

#### ★★★ 5. Disk truncation triggers relativistic jet launching of a highly accreting supermassive black hole

**Hirofumi Noda, Kazuhiro Hada, Hiroto Maruyama, Taishu Kayanoki, Kenji Toma, Taiki Kawamuro, Yoshiyuki Inoue, Tomohisa Kawashima, Soyeon Yeo, Ilje Cho, Keiichi Asada, Yasushi Fukazawa, Yoshihiro Ueda**  
[arXiv:2609.27057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27057)

Coordinated XRISM/Resolve spectroscopy and GMVA, VLBA and EAVN imaging probe the accretion flow and jet of the broad-line radio galaxy 3C 120, which accretes at roughly \(0.1\!-\!0.2\) of Eddington.

The resolved relativistic Fe-\(K\alpha\) profile gives

\[
R_{\rm in}=17.4^{+6.3}_{-6.5}\,R_g,
\]

indicating a truncated cold disk with a hot, geometrically thick inner flow. Extrapolation of the VLBI collimation profile places the jet base within approximately \(10R_g\). The resulting geometry can advect large-scale magnetic flux and support Blandford–Znajek jet production despite the high accretion rate.

##### History

Long-term monitoring previously showed that X-ray dips in 3C 120 precede the ejection of superluminal radio knots, establishing a phenomenological disk–jet connection. ([Chatterjee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.2051)) CCD spectroscopy had also suggested disk truncation, but multiple reflection, absorption and jet-continuum models remained comparably acceptable. ([Lohfink et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4937)) XRISM separates the broad disk line from narrower distant components, providing the most robust truncation-radius measurement for this source, while contemporaneous VLBI supplies an independent jet-scale constraint.

The observations establish compatible geometry, not direct causality: the VLBI size near the horizon is an extrapolation, and the data do not prove that the inner flow is magnetically arrested or that the jet is powered uniquely by Blandford–Znajek extraction.

#### ★★☆ 6. First pre-peak ultraviolet spectrum of a tidal disruption event

**Erica Hammerstein et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.28460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28460)

HST/STIS obtained a UV spectrum of TDE 2025aarm approximately twenty days before its optical maximum—the first such pre-peak UV spectrum of a tidal disruption event.

Broad far- and near-UV absorption features are blueshifted by approximately

\[
v_{\rm out}\simeq10^4~{\rm km\,s^{-1}},
\]

demonstrating that a fast outflow was already present well before peak light. The far-UV-to-infrared continuum is substantially bluer than a single blackbody. Fitting only near-UV and optical photometry would underestimate the inferred bolometric luminosity by roughly a factor of nine for this event.

##### History

Broad UV absorption and comparably fast outflows were already observed after or near maximum in iPTF15af and AT 2019qiz. ([iPTF15af](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07446), [AT 2019qiz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01593)) The new result is the pre-maximum timing, which places strong outflow formation during the early circularization or disk-assembly phase. The continuum also provides direct evidence that optical blackbody fits can miss considerable far-UV/EUV power.

The factor-of-nine correction is object-specific and depends on extrapolating across the unobserved EUV; it should not yet be applied to the optical TDE population. Only a sample of prompt UV spectra can determine whether this is typical.

#### ★★☆ 7. Compact-object mergers and micro-tidal disruption events in dense stellar environments

**Konstantinos Kritos, Francesco Iacovelli, Rosalba Perna, Emanuele Berti**  
[arXiv:2609.26878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26878)

Using the rapid cluster code Rapster, the authors simulate \(10^5\) clusters for each of ten physical variations and jointly follow compact-object mergers, stellar disruptions by neutron stars or stellar-mass black holes, compact-object spin evolution and accretion-induced collapse.

The black-hole merger rate peaks at \(z\simeq2\!-\!3\) at several tens of \({\rm Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}\). The integrated number of micro-TDEs exceeds the merger number by a factor of approximately \(2\!-\!5\). About 88–90% of disruptions in these simulations arise from single compact-object–star encounters. Accretion can spin up low-mass black holes and push neutron stars above their maximum mass, producing black holes in the \(2.2\!-\!5\,M_\odot\) lower mass gap.

##### History

The original analytical estimate predicted a local globular-cluster micro-TDE rate of a few \({\rm Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}\). ([Perets et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07698)) Later direct cluster simulations obtained a similar local black-hole–star disruption rate near \(3\ {\rm Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}\). ([Kremer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06353)) The present globular-cluster value is about \(0.04\ {\rm Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}\), roughly 25 times below the original analytical estimate, principally because mass segregation strongly suppresses neutron-star encounters. Its new contribution is a unified cosmic calculation linking disruption rates, merger observables, compact-object spins and accretion-induced collapse.

The channel fractions are not robust population predictions: primordial binaries and triples are omitted, even though other simulations find them responsible for most micro-TDEs. The authors correctly describe their disruption rates as conservative lower limits.

#### ★★☆ 8. Origin of the high-energy spectral cutoff in gamma-ray pulsar halos

**Qi-Ling Chen, Ming-Ming Kang, Kun Fang, Hong-Bo Hu, Yi-Qing Guo, Chao-Wen Yang**  
[arXiv:2609.27496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27496)

The tens-of-TeV cutoff in Geminga’s gamma-ray halo is modeled under two physically different hypotheses:

1. the PWN termination shock reaches a finite maximum electron energy;
2. the diffusion coefficient rises sharply above a characteristic energy, allowing high-energy electrons and their inverse-Compton emission to escape beyond the HAWC analysis aperture.

Both scenarios reproduce the spectrum and radial profiles. They predict different correlations between cutoff energy and pulsar properties and different hard-X-ray cutoffs for the central PWN.

##### History

HAWC’s precision Geminga and Monogem analysis established the cutoff and strongly suppressed local diffusion but did not determine whether the spectral feature originates at injection or during propagation. ([HAWC measurement](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ad738e)) The important point here is that the measured cutoff need not directly measure the PWN accelerator limit: energy-dependent aperture leakage can generate a similar spectrum. This is a useful reinterpretation rather than a new observational detection.

The broken diffusion coefficient is phenomenological and is not derived from a self-consistent turbulence calculation. Wide-field energy-resolved morphology and PWN hard-X-ray measurements are needed to discriminate the models.

#### Supernova Gamma-Ray Echo as an Astrophysical Near Detector for Galactic Neutrino Propagation

**Garv Chauhan and Yago Porto**  
[arXiv:2609.26908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26908)

The delayed 511-keV echo from neutrino interactions in a progenitor envelope is used as a near detector for the emitted \(\bar\nu_e\) fluence. Comparing it with the terrestrial burst could reveal fully averaged pseudo-Dirac oscillations down to approximately \(\delta m^2\gtrsim10^{-18}\ {\rm eV^2}\), as well as decay or dark-matter scattering.

##### History

Relative to the original echo proposal ([arXiv:2312.13197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13197)) and the related anisotropy study ([arXiv:2609.22806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22806)), the specific advance is making otherwise invisible, energy-averaged propagation losses measurable through a near–far ratio. The required next-generation 511-keV sensitivity remains the limiting assumption.

#### Flavorful Lepton-Number-Violating SMEFT: Loop Mixing, \(0\nu\beta\beta\) and Rare Meson Decays

**Lukáš Gráf, Chandan Hati, Ana Martín-Galán and Oliver Scholer**  
[arXiv:2609.28382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28382)

Renormalization-group mixing makes neutrinoless double-beta decay sensitive to some dimension-seven operators containing heavier-generation quarks, while \(K\rightarrow\pi\nu\nu\) and \(B\rightarrow K^{(*)}\nu\nu\) constrain complementary flavor structures.

##### History

The substantive advance is showing that the tree-level separation between nuclear and meson probes is not preserved by loops; quantitative limits remain EFT- and ultraviolet-completion-dependent.

The 24 September selection includes **no new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy measurement**. The MiniBooNE–MicroBooNE study is the principal new neutrino-oscillation result, but it is an independent reanalysis rather than a new experimental dataset.

#### A persistent Fermi source lies inside the IceCube-260916A region—but no transient association is established

IceCube-260916A was a **Bronze** track alert detected on 16 September, with an atmospheric-background false-alarm rate of approximately \(3.8\) events per year. No catalogued Fermi-LAT source was initially listed inside its 90% region. ([Official IceCube alert](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45598))

A subsequent Fermi-LAT analysis finds a previously uncatalogued \(5.0\sigma\) excess, designated **Fermi J1026+0359**, in the 18-year integrated dataset. Its most plausible counterpart is the high-synchrotron-peaked blazar 3HSP J102523.0+040229.

The source is **not significantly detected during either the day or month before the neutrino**, and the alert itself has only moderate astrophysical purity. The positional coincidence is therefore a follow-up target, not evidence of a neutrino–blazar association. ([Fermi-LAT follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45705))
