#### ★★★ 1. Plasma Effects Suppress Mixing-Induced Collisional Freeze-In

**Shao-Ping Li, Josef Pradler**  
[arXiv:2609.29911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29911)

The paper examines whether electroweak gauge-boson decays can produce keV–MeV sterile-neutrino dark matter through its small vacuum mixing with active neutrinos. A flavor-covariant Kadanoff–Baym calculation shows that the collision rate is controlled by the **in-medium**, rather than vacuum, mixing angle:

\[
\sin^2 2\theta_{\rm eff}=
\frac{m_N^4\sin^2 2\theta}
{(m_N^2\cos2\theta-\Pi_\nu)^2+m_N^4\sin^22\theta}.
\]

For a 10-keV sterile neutrino, the predicted abundance is approximately \(10^{20}\) times smaller than in the semiclassical calculation; across the studied range the suppression is \(10^{12}-10^{24}\). Production from the Standard Model plasma therefore cannot supply all the dark matter.

##### History

[Datta, Roshan and Sil](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02030) previously used vacuum mixing in a Boltzmann collision term and found viable gauge-boson freeze-in. Conversely, [Lello, Boyanovsky and Pisarski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.07647) found strong medium suppression from finite-temperature Dirac equations. The new paper resolves that disagreement: the plasma rotates the propagation eigenstates, a multiplicative effect that adding thermal masses only to phase space misses. This is a substantial closure of a claimed production window, not a small rate correction. The conclusion applies to minimal Standard-Model-plasma production; resonant production from a lepton asymmetry, decays of additional heavy particles and other nonthermal channels remain viable. Narrow-width and quasiparticle approximations also warrant further checking near possible resonances.

#### ★★★ 2. Composition-dependent acceleration of UHE Cosmic Rays in AGN electric fields

**E. Koutsoumpou, I. Contopoulos, A. Nathanail, A. Loules, D. Kazanas**  
[arXiv:2609.28668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28668)

The authors connect particle acceleration in near-horizon current sheets of magnetically arrested AGN disks directly to Auger composition observables. Nuclei sample a log-normal distribution of effective voltage fractions \(\eta\), acquire energy proportional to \(Z\eta\mathcal V_{\max}\), and are propagated through photodisintegration, photopion, Bethe–Heitler and adiabatic losses.

Bayesian fits to the 2025 Auger deep-learning \(X_{\max}\) moments favor a proton-poor injection at source distances of 10–30 Mpc. At 30 Mpc,

\[
\sigma_{\ln\eta}=0.59^{+0.13}_{-0.10},
\]

corresponding to a factor \(\sim1.8\) dispersion in sampled voltage. A template containing about 53% hydrogen is disfavored by \(\Delta\chi^2=18-26\); both tested templates fit poorly at 100 Mpc.

##### History

GRMHD simulations had already identified reconnection layers and large electric potentials in magnetically arrested disks ([Ripperda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15115); [Nathanail et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03689)), while Auger source fits had established that hard, relatively heavy injection can reproduce the spectrum and composition ([Auger combined fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07155)). The new contribution is the explicit mapping from stochastic voltage sampling to both the mean and width of the latest [Auger \(X_{\max}\) distributions](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.111.022003). The preferred composition is not independently derived—the successful injection template is itself Auger-inspired—and the log-normal voltage distribution is phenomenological. A joint fit of spectrum, \(X_{\max}\), anisotropy and source population would be needed before interpreting the distance preference as evidence for nearby AGN.

#### ★★★ 3. Multi-messenger and multi-band signal from first-order phase transitions in proto-neutron stars

**Christian Ecker, Mauro Giliberti, Luciano Rezzolla**  
[arXiv:2609.29761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29761)

A first-order hadron–quark transition in a proto-neutron star should produce three correlated signals: a kHz gravitational-wave burst from global stellar contraction and oscillation, MHz acoustic gravitational waves from colliding quark bubbles, and a delayed second neutrino burst.

The authors evolve accreting stars through approximately \(5\times10^4\) model-agnostic equations of state. A 5% measurement of the kHz mode would reduce the logarithmic uncertainty in the predicted MHz frequency interval by about 8.7%. Their weighted population gives a median delay of roughly 2.1 seconds between the ordinary collapse neutrino burst and the transition-driven second burst.

##### History

Phase-transition-induced kHz emission and second neutrino bursts were studied separately in collapse simulations ([Abdikamalov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1700); [Sagert et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.4225)), while bubble-collision MHz emission has recently been proposed for compact-star transitions ([Blas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03171)). This is the first systematic attempt to correlate all three signals across a large equation-of-state ensemble, and apparently the first population estimate of the neutrino delay. The forecast is not yet a realistic detectability calculation: the stellar evolution uses cold equations of state, the kHz amplitude assumes maximally efficient mode excitation, and the MHz calculation adapts a simplified sound-shell treatment developed for cosmological transitions. MHz detection itself remains highly speculative.

#### ★★☆ 4. Probing the Metallicity Dependence of Fast Radio Burst Progenitors with CHIME/FRB Outrigger Dwarf Host Galaxies

**Y. Dong et al.**  
[arXiv:2609.28629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28629)

Among 78 accurately localized CHIME/FRB Outrigger events, the authors identify eight dwarf-galaxy candidates and spectroscopically confirm five new dwarf hosts. Combining these with the literature suggests a metallicity-dependent split:

- nonrepeaters preferentially occupy relatively metal-rich dwarfs, generally \(12+\log({\rm O/H})\gtrsim8.35\);
- repeaters preferentially occupy lower-metallicity hosts;
- nearly every repeater with a persistent radio source lies below approximately 8.15.

No correlation exceeding \(3\sigma\) is found between metallicity and host dispersion, rotation measure or scattering.

##### History

Earlier demographic studies established that FRB hosts span a wide galaxy population ([Gordon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05465)), while the DSA sample suggested an overall preference for massive, metal-rich star-forming galaxies ([Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16964)). Individual nonrepeaters in dwarfs were already known ([Bhandari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16790)). The advance is a systematic, uniformly selected dwarf-host search with direct gas-phase metallicities, revealing that the global high-metallicity preference may conceal different repeater and nonrepeater channels. The evidence remains sample-limited—particularly for repeaters—and “nonrepeating” is an exposure-dependent classification. Strong-line metallicity calibrations, heterogeneous literature follow-up and optical host-selection effects can shift the proposed thresholds.

#### ★★☆ 5. Dynamically Stable Magnetic Fields in Relativistic Neutron Stars: A Diverse Landscape of GRMHD Equilibria

**Mai Li, Vasileios Paschalidis, Antonios Tsokaros, Maria Mutz, Koji Uryu**  
[arXiv:2609.28651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28651)

Fully relativistic MHD simulations evolve slowly rotating neutron stars with several self-consistent mixed poloidal–toroidal initial fields for tens of Alfvén times. The relaxed fields are neither unique nor universally dipolar: they retain memory of the initial current geometry and contain substantial higher multipoles.

A more limited regularity survives—the angle-averaged toroidal-to-poloidal field-amplitude ratio is typically 20–40% through much of the stellar interior.

##### History

Purely poloidal and toroidal configurations were long known to be unstable, and previous calculations often suggested relaxation toward a broadly “twisted-torus” geometry ([Ciolfi and Rezzolla](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3971); [Sur et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11858)). By starting from multiple self-consistent current structures and decomposing the relaxed fields into vector spherical harmonics, the new simulations demonstrate that the twisted-torus picture is not a unique dynamical attractor. This materially changes the interpretation of magnetar internal-field models. Stability is established only in ideal GRMHD over dynamical times: resistive evolution, Hall drift, crust formation, rapid rotation and realistic stratification could erase or reorganize the retained multipoles.

#### ★★☆ 6. Binary-pulsar Timing: A 3D Solar-System Accelerometer and Unknown-Source Probe

**Zheng-Long Wang, Zi-Qing Xia, Bo Zhang, Yi-Zhong Fan**  
[arXiv:2609.29162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29162)

Orbital-period derivatives from 26 detached binary pulsars are combined to reconstruct the full three-dimensional acceleration of the Solar-System barycenter. No anomalous acceleration is detected. Directional 95%-confidence limits are \(0.198\), \(0.262\) and \(0.693~\mu{\rm as\,yr^{-1}}\) over 50%, 75% and the full sampled sky.

Including both the observer- and pulsar-side response excludes a \(10\,M_\odot\) unseen object within 0.39, 0.34 and 0.21 pc over the corresponding sky fractions.

##### History

[Zakamska and Tremaine](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0506548) introduced astronomical-clock limits on peculiar Solar-System acceleration, and later timing plus VLBI of PSR J0437−4715 improved the single-direction constraint. The new network reconstruction is the first three-dimensional binary-pulsar measurement and improves historical pulsar limits by factors of 12–28; it is also tighter than a matched Gaia EDR3 comparison over most directions. The mass bounds improve on a rescaled INPOP19a benchmark beyond roughly 0.2 pc, but that comparison is not confidence-matched. Full timing covariances are unavailable for much of the heterogeneous sample, only six or seven pulsars dominate the precision, and the method tests quasi-static perturbers rather than rapid flybys.

#### ★★☆ 7. Constraining the Quadratic-mode Amplitude Coupling in GW250114

**Yuxin Yang, Changfu Shi, Yi-Ming Hu**  
[arXiv:2609.28953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28953)

The GW250114 ringdown is fitted with the linear \(220\) mode, its first overtone \(221\), and the quadratic \(220\times220\) mode. The analysis directly infers the nonlinear amplitude-coupling coefficient, which second-order perturbation theory predicts should depend on the remnant spin.

The measurement agrees with both numerical-relativity fits and second-order black-hole perturbation calculations; none differs from the posterior by more than \(1.3\sigma\). Present data cannot discriminate between the competing spin-dependent predictions.

##### History

Earlier analyses reported evidence that a quadratic mode improves the GW250114 ringdown fit ([Yang, Shi and Hu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16903)) and, using more restrictive merger-informed priors, claimed approximately \(3\sigma\) evidence for several nonlinear modes ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05734)). The new paper advances from asking whether a quadratic component is present to measuring its mode-specific coupling coefficient. Agreement with theory is encouraging but not yet a precision nonlinear-gravity test: the result remains sensitive to ringdown start time, the assumed mode set and correlations with the \(221\) overtone, and it cannot distinguish numerical-relativity from perturbative predictions.

#### Wave-Optics Imprints of Warm Dark Matter Subhalos with Prompt Cusps on Strongly Lensed Gravitational Waves

**Bhashin A. Thakore and Shin’ichiro Ando**  
[arXiv:2609.28626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28626)

LISA-band diffraction by \(10^4-10^7M_\odot\) subhalos could distinguish approximately 20–40-keV warm dark matter from more strongly suppressed models. Prompt \(r^{-3/2}\) cusps change the modulation only mildly, so the signal probes subhalo abundance rather than the central density profile.

##### History

This extends existing wave-optics forecasts to a self-consistent WDM population, but requires a rare, strongly lensed massive-black-hole merger and percent-level waveform control.

#### Plasma instability splits axion-photon conversion lines

**Elizabeth A. Tolman**  
[arXiv:2609.28704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28704)

A parametric plasma instability can turn the usual narrow axion-conversion line into a polarized, proportionally spaced line set.

##### History

This is a useful warning that single-line radio searches may use incomplete templates. The suggested qualitative resemblance to a Crab-pulsar feature is explicitly not evidence for axions and lacks a source-specific statistical analysis.

#### A Narrow Neutrino Window for the LZ Event

**Vedran Brdar and Dibya S. Chattopadhyay**  
[arXiv:2609.30255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30255)

A two-stage atmospheric-neutrino process, \(\nu\rightarrow N_1\rightarrow N_2\), is engineered so that a roughly 250-MeV \(N_2\) can produce LZ’s isolated 248-keV recoil while remaining kinematically forbidden on lighter neutrino-detector targets.

##### History

The construction is original but highly tuned, requiring a dark-matter-induced parametric resonance and a comparatively strong gauged-\(U(1)_B\) interaction. With only one unusual LZ event, this is a testable possibility rather than evidence for new physics.

The 25 September selection includes **no new UHECR spectrum, composition or anisotropy measurement**, **no new precision laboratory-neutrino oscillation result**, and **no major new gamma-ray-cascade calculation**.

#### GRB 260924A: exceptionally bright, high-redshift long burst

Fermi triggered on GRB 260924A at **01:13:23 UTC on 24 September**. GBM measures a preliminary

\[
T_{90}\simeq338~{\rm s},\qquad
E_{\rm peak}=645\pm4~{\rm keV},
\]

with a \(10-1000\) keV fluence of approximately \(2.84\times10^{-4}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}}\). Swift independently obtains \(T_{90}=344.8\pm1.8\) s in its narrower band. ([Fermi-GBM report](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45723), [Swift-BAT analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45733))

Fermi-LAT detected a \(43.3\)-GeV photon 77.5 seconds after the trigger and measured an initial \(>100\)-MeV photon flux of \((8.3\pm0.5)\times10^{-5}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). ([Fermi-LAT report](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45721))

Calar Alto spectroscopy finds Lyman-\(\alpha\) and Lyman-limit breaks consistent with

\[
z=3.51\pm0.10.
\]

The inferred rest-frame ultraviolet magnitude near 0.7 hours is approximately \(-31\), placing the afterglow among the most optically luminous known. The redshift currently rests primarily on continuum breaks rather than high-significance metal absorption lines, so additional spectroscopy would be valuable. ([Calar Alto spectroscopy](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45740))

No secure neutrino or gravitational-wave association has been reported.
