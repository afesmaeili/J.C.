#### ★★★ GRB 260924A may be among the most energetic GRBs ever observed

A new analysis combines the preliminary break-based redshift \(z=3.51\), Fermi-GBM fluence, and Band-spectrum fit to infer

\[
E_{\gamma,\mathrm{iso}}\simeq9.74\times10^{54}\ {\rm erg}
\]

in the rest-frame \(1-10^4\) keV band. This places GRB 260924A in the extreme tail of the GRB energy distribution and close to GRB 221009A—the “BOAT”—for which Konus-Wind inferred \(E_{\rm iso}\simeq1.2\times10^{55}\) erg. ([New energetics estimate](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45750), [GRB 221009A comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13383))

The estimate uses the previously reported Fermi parameters

\[
E_{\rm peak}=645\pm4~{\rm keV},\qquad
S_{10-1000\,{\rm keV}}=2.84\times10^{-4}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}},
\]

with a bolometric \(k\)-correction.

**Status and caveats:** \(E_{\rm iso}\) is not the physical jet energy: no jet opening angle or beaming correction is yet available. The value also scales with the luminosity distance, while the present \(z=3.51\pm0.10\) estimate is based mainly on Lyman breaks rather than secure high-significance metal lines. Continued optical spectroscopy and broadband afterglow monitoring are therefore essential before ranking it definitively against GRB 221009A.
