### Verified science news

#### ★★☆ Konus-Wind measures GRB 260919A, a proposed counterpart of EP260920a

Konus-Wind independently detected GRB 260919A on **19 September**, using a reference time of **23:48:45 UTC**. Its \(20\!-\!300\) keV count rate increased by more than \(20\sigma\) for 165 seconds. Preliminary spectral analysis gives

\[
F_{\gamma}=
1.32^{+0.38}_{-0.20}\times10^{-5}\ {\rm erg\,cm^{-2}}
\]

in \(10\) keV–\(1\) MeV and a cutoff-power-law peak energy

\[
E_{\rm pk}=109^{+46}_{-14}\ {\rm keV}.
\]

These measurements confirm a long-duration gamma-ray burst and add spectral information to the proposed association between **GRB 260919A** and **EP260920a**. They do not independently establish that the two transients share a physical origin. ([Konus-Wind circular, published 3 October](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45806))

The timing remains noteworthy. Konus-Wind’s gamma-ray episode ended near 23:52:14 UTC, whereas Einstein Probe triggered on the proposed soft-X-ray counterpart at **00:06:04 UTC on 20 September**, almost fourteen minutes later. If the association is correct, the delayed soft-X-ray activity could reflect extended central-engine activity or an early afterglow component. These are possible interpretations, not conclusions of the Konus-Wind measurement. No redshift is reported in the cited circulars. ([Einstein Probe trigger](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45649), [IPN association discussion](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45792))

All Konus-Wind spectral quantities are explicitly marked **preliminary**, with quoted errors at **68% confidence**.

A separate follow-up update concerns **EP261003a**, which remains unclassified in the 4 October report: Einstein Probe measured rapid fading and a power-law X-ray spectrum, but no secure optical, gamma-ray, neutrino or gravitational-wave association has been established. ([Einstein Probe refined analysis](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45803), [COLIBRÍ optical limits](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45801))

### Science Radar

#### The dispute over formalizing the Anthropocene has shifted to scientific governance

A peer-reviewed *Earth’s Future* commentary published on **1 October 2026** argues that the 2024 rejection of the Anthropocene as a formal geological epoch should undergo independent procedural review. Its authors allege that:

- 11 of the 16 voting members may have exceeded the applicable term limit, potentially eliminating the required quorum;
- the Anthropocene Working Group was not given an adequate opportunity to answer criticism or revise its proposal;
- an internal IUGS geoethics report questioning the process remains unpublished.

The paper is a documentary and governance analysis, not new stratigraphic evidence. It asks for disclosure of voting records, communications and the ethics report rather than directly demanding that the Anthropocene be adopted. ([Schäfer et al., Earth’s Future](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025EF007638))

The competing institutional position remains unchanged: the International Commission on Stratigraphy and International Union of Geological Sciences formally approved the rejection in March 2024. The proposed boundary—around 1952, identified through globally distributed plutonium fallout—therefore has no official status, and the present remains formally within the Holocene. ([IUGS–ICS statement](https://stratigraphy.org/news/152), [formal proposal and boundary](https://quaternary.stratigraphy.org/working-groups/anthropocene))

A separate commentary by Liping Zhou and Adele Bertini, published on **8 September 2026**, presents the stratigraphic rationale for rejection and distinguishes evidence of planetary transformation from the criteria for a formal geological time unit. ([Zhou and Bertini, Nature Reviews Earth & Environment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-026-00822-x))

The controversy concerns the **rank, boundary and decision process**, not whether human activity has profoundly altered Earth systems; even the stratigraphic bodies recognize that the informal term “Anthropocene” remains scientifically useful. The new commentary requests a review; its publication does not change the formal geological timescale.
