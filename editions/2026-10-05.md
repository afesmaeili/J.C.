#### ★★★ 1. First observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at Super-Kamiokande

**K. Abe et al. — Super-Kamiokande Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.03143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03143)  
**New experimental result**

Using **411.52 live days** from two gadolinium-loaded operating phases, Super-Kamiokande observes reactor antineutrinos through inverse beta decay with delayed neutron tagging. The prompt-energy spectrum and time-dependent rates favor an oscillated reactor contribution over the no-reactor hypothesis at approximately **\(6\sigma\)**.

The reactor-only fit gives

\[
\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.500\pm0.155,\qquad
\Delta m_{21}^{2}
=\left(9.08^{+0.55}_{-0.54}\right)\times10^{-5}\ {\rm eV^2}.
\]

A joint comparison with Super-K’s solar-neutrino measurement yields a parameter-goodness-of-fit discrepancy of **\(1.9\sigma\)**—not significant evidence for different neutrino and antineutrino oscillation parameters. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03143v1))

##### History

This is the first reactor-antineutrino observation **in Super-Kamiokande**, not the first in a water Cherenkov detector. [SNO+ previously reported \(3.5\sigma\) evidence using pure water](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14154), with its nearest reactors approximately 240 km away. The new result establishes the practical reactor sensitivity of SK-Gd and enables a solar–reactor comparison within the same detector. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14154))

The principal advance is therefore experimental capability and an internally comparable dataset. The reactor mixing-angle uncertainty remains substantial, and the analysis depends on accidental-background estimation, neutron-tagging efficiency, and reactor-spectrum modeling. The quoted solar–reactor mismatch does not support a CPT-violation claim. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03143v1))

#### ★★★ 2. V4641 Sgr: two complementary detections of extended X-ray emission

**XMM-Newton Resolves Parsec-scale X-ray Jets in the PeVatron Microquasar V4641 Sgr**  
**Naomi Tsuji, Caterina Tresoldi, Kaya Mori, Hiromasa Suzuki, Matthew Lundy, Dmitry Khangulyan, et al.**  
[arXiv:2610.02966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02966)

**Candidate X-ray Counterparts of Ultra-High-Energy Emission of the Galactic Microquasar V4641 Sgr Revealed by Einstein Probe**  
**Yi-Heng Chi, Shao-Qiang Xi, Ping Zhou, Hua Feng, Ruo-Yu Liu, Songzhan Chen, et al.**  
[arXiv:2610.02962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02962)  
**New observations; emission mechanism remains non-unique**

The XMM-Newton study resolves northern and southern X-ray structures extending tens of parsecs from the binary, including knots approximately **20 pc** away. Their spectra are consistent with nonthermal emission, with a representative photon index

\[
\Gamma_X=1.9\pm0.1_{\rm stat}\pm0.5_{\rm sys}.
\]

A joint synchrotron/inverse-Compton interpretation favors magnetic fields around **\(3\!-\!5\,\mu{\rm G}\)**. The compact X-ray structures suggest localized acceleration at jet termination or recollimation regions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02966v1))

Einstein Probe independently identifies diffuse knots approximately **20–40 pc** from the source. Their softer X-ray spectra, \(\Gamma_X\sim2\!-\!3\), and different extraction regions give approximately **\(1\!-\!2\,\mu{\rm G}\)** in one-zone leptonic fits. These values should not be averaged with the XMM result: they come from different regions and modeling assumptions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02962v1))

##### History

[HAWC’s 2024 discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16117) established exceptionally hard, extended gamma-ray emission reaching approximately 200 TeV, but could not determine whether electrons or nuclei dominated. A subsequent [hadronic propagation model by Neronov, Oikonomou and Semikoz](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.111.103025) explained the large-scale gamma-ray structure through cosmic rays escaping along Galactic magnetic-field lines and predicted a potential neutrino counterpart. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16117))

The new X-ray measurements provide the missing spatially resolved evidence for energetic electrons and constrain their magnetic environment. They strengthen leptonic interpretations but **do not establish that all the gamma rays are leptonic**, particularly where gamma-ray emission extends beyond the X-ray knots. Conversely, calling the system a PeVatron does not by itself prove acceleration of PeV protons. Distinguishing localized synchrotron-emitting electrons from a more extended hadronic component now requires joint, spatially resolved modeling rather than only a broadband spectral fit. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02966v1))

#### ★★★ 3. Guide field effects on particle acceleration in three-dimensional relativistic magnetic reconnection

**Despina Karavola, Lorenzo Sironi, Maria Petropoulou**  
[arXiv:2610.03427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03427)  
**New kinetic-simulation result**

Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations examine relativistic pair-plasma reconnection at magnetization \(\sigma=10\), varying the guide field from zero to a strength comparable to the reconnecting field:

\[
0\leq B_g/B_0\leq1.
\]

The rapid acceleration channel unique to three dimensions survives even with a substantial guide field: particles escaping magnetic flux ropes can sample the large-scale upstream reconnection electric field. However, increasing \(B_g\) reduces the participating population, steepens the spectrum, lowers the cutoff, and increases directional anisotropy. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03427))

##### History

[Zhang, Sironi and Giannios (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00009) identified this three-dimensional escape-and-acceleration mechanism for weak guide fields. Their escaping particles gained energy approximately linearly with time, unlike the slower growth of particles trapped in two-dimensional plasmoids. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00009))

The new paper establishes how robust that particular mechanism is when the magnetic geometry becomes less favorable. It does **not** newly establish the general fact that guide fields can soften reconnection spectra. Its more specific contribution is separating the survival of a fast acceleration channel from the declining efficiency with which particles enter it.

The simulations use a pair plasma, one principal magnetization, and no radiative cooling. Their results cannot yet be translated directly into an ion acceleration efficiency or a universal maximum energy for radiatively bright astrophysical jets. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03427v1))

#### ★★☆ 4. Neutrino flavor instabilities intermediate between slow and fast

**Nicolás Viaux, Lucas Johns**  
[arXiv:2610.02332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02332)  
**New linear-stability analysis**

The usual distinction between “slow” vacuum-assisted and “fast” neutrino-self-interaction-driven flavor instabilities becomes less clear when neither limiting approximation is adequate.

This paper maps the transition between them while retaining finite vacuum frequency,

\[
\omega=\frac{\Delta m^2}{2E},
\]

and varying the angular distributions. It tracks both the maximum growth rate and the wavevector of the fastest-growing mode. The dominant mode can move away from spatial homogeneity or switch between branches, so restricting the analysis to \(k=0\) can miss the physically fastest instability. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02332v1))

##### History

[Fiorillo and Raffelt’s unified dispersion framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20389) already connected fast, slow, and collisional instabilities by treating vacuum splittings and collision rates as perturbations and classifying the resulting branches. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20389))

The present contribution is a more explicit map of the **slow–fast crossover**, including changes in which branch dominates. It reinforces the need to inspect the full dispersion relation rather than assign a mechanism from a growth-rate estimate or a homogeneous calculation alone.

These are linear instabilities of prescribed neutrino backgrounds. Growth rates do not determine the eventual flavor mixture, conversion depth, or observable supernova spectrum. Nonlinear saturation and coupling to transport remain separate problems. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02332v1))

#### ★★☆ 5. High Energy Neutrinos, Gravitational Waves, and Dark Matter from a Cosmological First Order Phase Transition

**James M. Cline, Savas Stoica, Yong Xu**  
[arXiv:2610.02420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02420)  
**Multimessenger model and forecast**

Dark-matter particles acquire a large mass inside expanding phase-transition bubbles. Only the energetic fraction crosses the bubble wall, setting the relic abundance; subsequent dark-matter decay produces high-energy neutrinos, while the transition produces gravitational waves.

For

\[
m_{\rm DM}\simeq4.4\times10^8\ {\rm GeV},
\]

the authors obtain benchmarks compatible with the abundance and neutrino energies associated with KM3-230213A. **The corresponding gravitational-wave peak is near \(10^4\) Hz and below planned detector sensitivities.** Lower-mass benchmarks, \(m_{\rm DM}\lesssim10^6\) GeV, move the gravitational-wave signal into potentially accessible frequency ranges. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02420v1))

##### History

[Baker, Kopp and Long](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02830) introduced filtered dark matter as a production mechanism allowing masses well above the conventional thermal-relic range. The basic filtering process and its ability to suppress the surviving abundance are therefore established ideas. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02830))

The new calculation determines the bubble-wall velocity using improved hydrodynamics rather than leaving it freely adjustable, strengthening the connection between relic abundance and the gravitational-wave prediction. That is the substantive advance.

The KM3NeT-motivated benchmark should not be presented as predicting an observable two-messenger coincidence: its gravitational-wave counterpart is inaccessible in the stated setup. The neutrino interpretation also remains a model-dependent explanation of an individual event, not evidence that the phase transition occurred. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02420v1))

#### ★★☆ 6. Higgsino dark matter compatible with the LUX-ZEPLIN high-energy nuclear-recoil event and IceCube constraints

**Katherine Freese, Dionysios P. Theodosopoulos**  
[arXiv:2610.03692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03692)  
**Reassessment of an existing exclusion**

The authors revisit whether inelastic Higgsino scattering can explain LZ’s isolated high-energy recoil while avoiding solar-neutrino limits. They vary the high-speed halo population, post-capture cooling, capture–annihilation equilibrium, and treatment of LZ’s empty high-energy sideband.

The **thermal \(1.1\)-TeV Higgsino remains only marginally compatible** when an LMC-induced high-speed tail is included **and both elastic solar cooling and the sideband constraint are neglected**. Heavier, nonthermally produced Higgsinos admit larger allowed regions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03692v1))

##### History

[Pospelov and Ramani’s September analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02775) obtained \(\delta>566\) keV for the approximately 1.08-TeV Higgsino, excluding its interpretation of the recoil under their solar-capture assumptions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02775))

The new paper does not simply invalidate that result. It identifies which assumptions permit a surviving region and extends the mass range beyond the thermal benchmark. Its most important lesson is that **capture, thermalization, and capture–annihilation equilibrium are distinct conditions**.

The high-mass conclusions are less secure experimentally: above 10 TeV, the analysis holds IceCube’s reconstructed annihilation-rate limit fixed at its 10-TeV value. That is a benchmark extrapolation, not a published high-mass detector likelihood. Neither this reinterpretation nor the original recoil establishes dark matter. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03692v1))

#### ★★★ 7. Big Bang Nucleosynthesis Constraint on \(B\)-mesogenesis

**James M. Cline, Jonas Frerick, Sebastian A. Naja, Yong Xu**  
[arXiv:2610.03455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03455)  
**New quantitative cosmological constraint**

In \(B\)-mesogenesis, a late-decaying particle reheats the Universe and produces bottom quarks; meson oscillations and exotic decays then generate the baryon asymmetry. This study follows the altered expansion, neutrino heating, and injected hadrons through nucleosynthesis.

The dominant effect is **hadron-induced neutron–proton interconversion**, not destruction of already-formed nuclei. For parent masses \(M_\Phi\lesssim100\) GeV, the approximate three-sigma requirement is

\[
T_{\rm rh}\gtrsim9.5\!-\!10\ {\rm MeV},
\]

corresponding to \(\tau_\Phi\lesssim0.02\) s. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03455v1))

##### History

[Elor, Escudero and Nelson’s original proposal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00880) connected baryogenesis and dark matter through neutral-\(B\) oscillations and visible–dark-sector decays, making low reheating temperatures central to the mechanism. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00880))

The present paper supplies the missing process-specific answer to how low that temperature can be. A generic low-reheating bound is insufficient because the decay products directly modify \(n/p\), even when the expansion and neutrino temperatures alone would seem acceptable.

This closes the lowest-temperature portion of the parameter space rather than excluding \(B\)-mesogenesis. The bound depends most strongly on hadronic interconversion rates and the adopted decay history; the authors propagate estimated rate uncertainties, which weaken it modestly. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03455v1))

#### ★★☆ 8. Dark matter bound-states made easy

**Simone Biondini, Stefan Vogl**  
[arXiv:2610.03718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03718)  
**Computational-method improvement**

The paper develops compact approximations for freeze-out calculations including Sommerfeld enhancement and bound-state formation, dissociation, annihilation, and selected excited-state transitions.

In the Abelian model tested, smooth approximations reproduce the full relic-density calculation at the **few-percent level**. Including excited-state transitions can shift the abundance by approximately **15%** relative to a ground-state-only treatment; the corresponding approximate calculation stays within about **5%** of the full result.

A semi-analytic post-freeze-out integration further reduces the numerical workload. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03718v1))

##### History

[Von Harling and Petraki (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7874) already demonstrated that bound-state formation can materially change thermal relic abundances and supplied a fit for the coupling needed to obtain the observed density. The physical importance of bound states is therefore not new. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7874))

The advance is a more systematic, reusable approximation to the rates and thermal evolution, with explicit accuracy tests against the full calculation.

A particularly useful caution is that the frequently invoked efficient-transition limit does **not** consistently simplify all excited-state processes in this setup. The prescription is not a universal replacement for coupled bound-state evolution, especially for non-Abelian models, complicated level networks, or percent-level precision targets. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03718v1))

#### ★★☆ 9. Distinguishing the origin of cosmic birefringence: dark energy, dark matter, and neutrino asymmetry

**Lu Yin, Eiichiro Komatsu**  
[arXiv:2610.02667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02667)  
**New discrimination strategy; no new detection**

Different mechanisms can rotate CMB polarization by a similar mean angle while producing different angular-scale dependence in the \(EB\) spectrum.

The authors compare axionlike dark energy, axionlike dark matter, and a neutrino-asymmetry-induced interaction. In the models studied, neutrino asymmetry and axion dark matter suppress the large-angle signal at approximately

\[
\ell\lesssim20,
\]

whereas the dark-energy case does not. Differences during recombination supply additional discrimination at smaller angular scales. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02667v1))

##### History

[Nakatsuka, Namikawa and Komatsu (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08560) already proposed using reionization- and recombination-era polarization as a tomographic test distinguishing dark energy from a more rapidly evolving axion field. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08560))

The new contribution adds the neutrino-asymmetry mechanism and works out where its predicted spectrum differs from both axion scenarios. It extends an existing tomographic idea rather than introducing it.

These are model-discrimination forecasts, not evidence that any proposed source is responsible for the reported birefringence hints. Practical inference still requires foreground treatment, polarization calibration, and sufficiently precise large-angle measurements; similar mean rotation angles alone cannot identify the underlying physics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02667v1))

#### A type-I superconductor shows evidence for broken time-reversal symmetry

**Anshu Kataria et al., “Observation of Time-Reversal Symmetry Breaking in the Type-I Superconductor YbSb\(_2\)”**  
[Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/drzq-lfn5) · [arXiv:2601.07460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07460)

**Recent-publication catch-up:** the paper appeared in *Physical Review Letters* on **23 September 2026**. ([link.aps.org](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/drzq-lfn5))

Zero-field muon-spin measurements reveal spontaneous internal magnetic fields emerging below the superconducting transition. Complementary measurements support a fully gapped, **type-I** superconducting state. The combination is unusual because time-reversal-symmetry breaking is more commonly discussed in unconventional type-II materials. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07460))

The authors’ symmetry analysis favors a particular nonunitary triplet-pairing state, and their effective model predicts Majorana surface modes.

**What is measured versus inferred:** the internal-field signal is experimental evidence for broken time-reversal symmetry. The precise pairing state and the existence of Majorana surface excitations are theoretical interpretations—not separately observed phenomena in this work. This is consequently an interesting candidate platform for unconventional superconductivity, not a direct Majorana detection. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07460))

### Science Radar

#### Quantifying how primes behave in short intervals

**Joni Teräväinen — “Quantitative Gowers uniformity of the primes in intervals of length \(X^{5/8+\varepsilon}\)”**  
[arXiv:2610.03707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03707)  
**New mathematics preprint**

This paper strengthens control over the distribution of primes inside intervals

\[
(X,X+H],\qquad H\geq X^{5/8+\varepsilon}.
\]

The relevant observable is a **Gowers norm**, which measures correlations with increasingly complicated structured patterns. Teräväinen gives quantitative bounds for the von Mangoldt function—the standard weighted proxy for primes—after subtracting an appropriate arithmetic model.

The distinction is important: this is not simply a better estimate of how many primes occupy an interval. It controls higher-order structure needed to count configurations of primes. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03707))

**Literature comparison:** [Matomäki, Shao, Tao and Teräväinen’s earlier “All intervals” paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03754) established the corresponding qualitative Gowers-uniformity result at the same \(5/8+\varepsilon\) threshold. The new theorem does **not** lower that exponent. It replaces qualitative convergence with explicit, much more usable quantitative control, requiring a strengthened treatment of non-Abelian nilsequence estimates. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03754))

The author identifies an application in separate joint work on sum–product patterns in shifted primes: configurations \(\{x,x+y,xy\}\subset\mathbb P-1\). That application should not be confused with the theorem proved here. **Status remains a research preprint; no AI contribution is claimed in this paper.** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03707))

#### BootLoops: using AI to coordinate exact calculations, including evolutionary-tree inference

**Matthew D. Schwartz — “BootLoops: an LLM-driven toolkit for exact quantitative science”**  
[arXiv:2610.02286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02286)  
**AI-assisted research-software preprint**

BootLoops connects language-model orchestration to deterministic tools for symbolic reduction, integer relations, exact enumeration, and calculations with rigorous numerical error bounds.

One cross-disciplinary example is **phylogenetics**. For restricted four- and five-species substitution models and specified priors, Bayesian evidences for competing evolutionary trees become polynomial integrals. The toolkit evaluates these as exact rational numbers, using techniques closely related to those developed for Feynman integrals. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02286v1))

**What is new:** the integration and extension of these methods across disciplines—not the invention of exact arithmetic or certified numerical computation. For example, [Johansson’s Arb library](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02831) already provides arbitrary-precision interval arithmetic with propagated error bounds. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02831))

The author discloses that AI wrote the new package code, apart from incorporated public software, and takes responsibility for the result. The checks include independent numerical comparisons and agreement between reconstructions using separate sets of primes. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02286v1))

**Caveat:** exact answers are exact **within the chosen model and assumptions**. They do not make a simplified evolutionary model biologically correct, nor does exact arithmetic automatically certify an AI-generated implementation. The examples demonstrate a promising way to make AI-assisted computation auditable; they do not yet establish a general replacement for large-scale phylogenetic inference or independent software validation. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02286v1))
