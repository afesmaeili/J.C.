#### ★★★ 1. Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T

**Yingxin Zhang and the PandaX Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.05980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05980) — submitted 5 October 2026  
*New experimental limit*

PandaX-4T searches for the diffuse supernova neutrino background through coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering, an approximately flavor-blind channel. In \(1.54\ {\rm tonne\,yr}\), the surviving events are consistent with background. The 90%-confidence limit on each heavy-lepton flavor above \(19.3\) MeV is

\[
\Phi_{\nu_x}< (2.27-2.43)\times10^3\ {\rm cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
\]


##### History

Super-Kamiokande previously obtained indirect heavy-flavor limits around \(10^3\ {\rm cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\) from neutrino–electron scattering ([Lunardini and Peres 2008](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2008/08/033)). More directly comparable, LZ has already reported a CEνNS limit of \(686-826\ {\rm cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\) per heavy flavor ([arXiv:2412.15886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15886)). PandaX is therefore neither the first public CEνNS DSNB analysis nor the strongest present bound, despite the abstract’s “first search” wording. Its value is an independent full-collaboration demonstration of the neutral-current, all-flavor method. Present sensitivity remains far from realistic DSNB fluxes: the benchmark expectation is only about \(8\times10^{-4}\) event.

#### ★★★ 2. Limits on the size of electron density fluctuations in the early universe and implications for the Hubble tension

**Gabriel P. Lynch, Jens Chluba, Richard Battye, Geoffrey Vasil**  
[arXiv:2610.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05147) — submitted 4 October 2026  
*New cosmological constraints and methodological advance*

Small-scale electron-density inhomogeneities accelerate recombination, but correlated structure also reduces the effective Thomson-scattering rate and increases diffusion damping. Incorporating both effects into a modified Boltzmann code, the authors find no evidence for fluctuations in Planck, ACT DR6 or SPT-3G D1. The variance is below \(0.21\) at 95% confidence, tightening to \(0.033\) for 2-Mpc coherence.

With a distance-ladder prior, Planck alone permits

\[
H_0=71.1\pm0.7\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},
\]

but ACT and SPT break the degeneracy and give \(69.3\pm0.5\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), with no preference over ΛCDM.

##### History

Earlier analyses principally treated accelerated recombination, including primordial-magnetic-field realizations ([Jedamzik and Abel 2013](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2013/10/050); [Lee and Ali-Haïmoud 2021](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.104.103509)). The modified-transport term was derived recently ([arXiv:2505.22242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22242)); this paper supplies the first joint data constraints including both imprints. It substantially weakens the case that unresolved electron structure alone resolves the Hubble tension. The separate-universe treatment and phenomenological variance/coherence model still require model-dependent translation from a concrete magnetohydrodynamic source.

#### ★★★ 3. Search for Higgs boson compositeness effects in high-mass dilepton final states at \(\sqrt{s}=13\) and \(13.6\) TeV

**CMS Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.04782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04782) — submitted 3 October 2026  
*Run-2+Run-3 search; journal paper following a preliminary result*

CMS fits high-mass dielectron and dimuon spectra in \(207\ {\rm fb^{-1}}\), searching for broad vector resonances and nonresonant electroweak effects associated with composite-Higgs scenarios. No deviation is found. The one-parameter 95%-confidence intervals are

\[
-3.9\times10^{-4}<\mathcal Y<3.5\times10^{-4},
\qquad
-2.1\times10^{-4}<\mathcal W<1.6\times10^{-4}.
\]

Large resonance widths are treated explicitly.

##### History

CMS’s previous lepton-plus-missing-momentum analysis supplied the first LHC determination of \(\mathcal W\), but had little direct leverage on \(\mathcal Y\) ([arXiv:2202.06075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06075)). The neutral-current spectrum adds the complementary hypercharge direction; combining the two gives CMS’s strongest \(\mathcal W,\mathcal Y\) limits to date. The paper formalizes the [EXO-25-001 result released preliminarily on 6 July](https://cms-results.web.cern.ch/cms-results/public-results/preliminary-results/EXO-25-001/index.html), so this is not an entirely new announcement. The EFT interpretation requires new states above the fitted mass range; broad-resonance exclusions remain coupling- and width-dependent.

#### ★★☆ 4. Pair echo model of CTAO/LST-1 signal from GRB 221009A

**Andrii Neronov, Ievgen Vovk, Teneman Keita, Davide Miceli**  
[arXiv:2610.06633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06633) — submitted 5 October 2026  
*Interpretive model, not a pair-echo detection*

Combining LHAASO’s early VHE spectrum with an LST-1 excess measured 1.33 days later, the authors find that a cascade echo can reproduce the late signal for

\[
\log_{10}(B/{\rm G})=-16.2\pm0.7
\]

when \(\lambda_B\gtrsim20\) kpc. At shorter coherence length, the inferred field scales as \(\sqrt{20\,{\rm kpc}/\lambda_B}\); short-coherence phase-transition relic fields would lie around \(10^{-14}-10^{-12}\) G.

##### History

Fermi-LAT non-detections or delayed GeV emission from this burst had already been used to constrain intergalactic fields ([arXiv:2306.05347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05347); [arXiv:2306.07672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07672); [arXiv:2306.05970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05970)). The advance is the joint LHAASO–LST-1 interpretation at hundreds of GeV and day-scale delay. It is not a magnetic-field measurement: the LST-1 excess was \(4.1\sigma\), later observations were non-detections, and intrinsic afterglow emission cannot be separated from a cascade. The inferred field is conditional on the intrinsic spectrum, background light, jet geometry and cascade model.

#### ★★☆ 5. Jets with Streaks: the Global Dynamics and Structure of Striped Poynting Flux Dominated Jets

**Ho-Sang Chan, Anna Chashkina, Omer Bromberg, Gregory R. Werner, Hayk Hakobyan**  
[arXiv:2610.04796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04796) — submitted 3 October 2026  
*Global-simulation advance*

Axisymmetric GRMHD simulations follow alternating-polarity magnetic loops from accretion through black-hole jet launching and dissipation out to \(10^4r_g\). Coherent loops formed within roughly \(50r_g\) launch nested relativistic mini-jets. Opposite polarities form current sheets, dissipating a few tens of percent of the observer-frame Poynting power inside the simulated domain.

##### History

Analytic striped-jet models prescribed stripe distributions and reconnection laws ([Drenkhahn 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0112509); [Giannios and Uzdensky 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/sty3502)), while earlier simulations established that alternating disk flux can launch stripes ([arXiv:2212.06058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06058)). The advance is a launch-to-dissipation causal picture with a structured core/sheath and a physical loop-survival criterion. Quantitative reconnection and particle acceleration remain uncertain because the calculation is 2D ideal GRMHD: dissipation is numerically regulated and kinetic, radiative and three-dimensional effects are absent.

#### ★★☆ 6. The Virgo Cluster as a Case Study for Ancient Cosmic Ray Halos in Radio and X-rays

**Philip F. Hopkins, Emily M. Silich, Jack Sayers, Sam B. Ponnada, Isabel S. Sands**  
[arXiv:2610.03881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03881) — submitted 2 October 2026  
*Multiwavelength physical interpretation*

Virgo’s X-ray continuum implies a cooling rate above \(10\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}\), yet little cold gas or star formation appears outside M87’s nucleus. Combining the aged radio halo with X-ray spectra and Faraday limits, the authors argue that fossil cosmic-ray leptons within approximately 40 kpc provide appreciable pressure, heat the gas through Coulomb interactions and inverse-Compton-scatter CMB photons into soft X-rays. This could reduce the true cooling requirement and bias central X-ray metallicities low.

##### History

Cosmic-ray heating has long been considered for cool-core clusters, and gamma-ray data constrain M87’s highest-energy particles ([arXiv:0910.3565](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.3565)). A companion study recently applied a related fossil-lepton framework to Perseus ([arXiv:2605.08349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08349)). This paper extends it to an X-ray-weak cool core and links radio aging, Faraday fields, soft X-rays and metallicity bias. It is not a direct cosmic-ray-pressure measurement: the crucial low-energy electron distribution is extrapolated and multiphase-gas or calibration explanations remain viable.

#### ★★☆ 7. Revisiting neutrino-assisted self-interacting dark matter

**Luqi Wang, Shu-Yuan Guo, Xuewen Liu, Bin Zhu**  
[arXiv:2610.06151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06151) — submitted 5 October 2026  
*Negative phenomenological result*

Two scalar-mediator realizations are tested using mediator-resolved potentials, numerical scattering, thermal freeze-out and current constraints. In the \(t\)-channel model, loop-induced neutrino forces are negligible wherever the relic abundance and sizable scattering coexist. In the \(s\)-channel case, the largest cross section is about

\[
6.2\times10^{-13}\ {\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}},
\]

over eleven orders of magnitude below the usual \(0.1\ {\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}\) SIDM benchmark. Sommerfeld enhancement and early kinetic decoupling can still alter the relic abundance by up to 50%.

##### History

Earlier “neutrino as the dark force” models proposed astrophysically significant neutrino-mediated scattering ([arXiv:2106.08339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08339)), with subsequent work clarifying the short-distance potential and Sommerfeld behavior ([arXiv:2112.03060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03060); [arXiv:2203.05455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05455)). Resolving the mediator, imposing current bounds and enforcing thermal abundance closes the apparent SIDM window in these two attractive scalar completions. The no-go result does not encompass asymmetric or nonthermal dark matter, repulsive forces or different ultraviolet completions.

#### ★★☆ 8. Learning Astrophysical Uncertainties in Dark Matter Direct Detection with Simulation-Based Inference

**Felix Kahlhoefer, Niklas Reus**  
[arXiv:2610.06159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06159) — submitted 5 October 2026  
*Proof-of-concept methodology*

Neural ratio estimators are trained on realistic XENONnT scintillation and ionization simulations. Mixing halo models during training implicitly marginalizes velocity-distribution uncertainties. Mixed training corrects deliberately extreme Standard-Halo-Model misspecification, while realistic SHM, SHM++ and Large-Magellanic-Cloud variants produce only modest low-mass differences. The expected exclusion agrees with XENONnT sensitivity after accounting for Bayesian/frequentist differences.

##### History

Simulation-based inference had previously been demonstrated for direct-detection parameter reconstruction ([arXiv:1810.09930](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09930)); XENONnT normally uses a profile likelihood with explicit nuisance parameters ([arXiv:2303.14729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14729)). The advance is the combination of detector-level simulation, amortized likelihood-ratio inference, astrophysical-model mixing and calibration checks. This is not a new experimental limit. The proof of concept uses a simplified flat electronic-recoil background, omits position information and bins observations coarsely; collaboration-grade backgrounds and coverage tests are still required.

#### ★★☆ 9. Stars Would Remember a Recent Gravitational Transition: Testing G-step Resolutions of the Hubble Tension with Red Dwarfs

**Jeremy Sakstein, Harry Desmond**  
[arXiv:2610.04048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04048) — submitted 2 October 2026  
*Astrophysical test of a speculative cosmological proposal*

Fully convective red dwarfs have thermal timescales comparable to the proposed 73–130-Myr lookback times of “G-step” Hubble-tension models. Comparing evolved stellar models with eight detached-eclipsing-binary radii, the authors exclude a proposed 4% change in \(G\) at 73 Myr at \(3.1\sigma\), and an 11% change at 130 Myr at \(4.0\sigma\).

##### History

Earlier G-step proposals focused on supernova calibration and background cosmology rather than stellar populations with relaxation times spanning the transition. No close prior study was found in the literature checked for this specific stellar-memory test. The paper therefore supplies an independent constraint. It is not a generic limit on time-varying gravity: the sample has eight stars, J2343+29 carries substantial weight, and omitting it reduces the quoted significances to approximately \(2.45\sigma\) and \(1.90\sigma\). A 1% systematic radius floor also weakens the result.

#### 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine: light-gated ion channels and optogenetics

On 5 October 2026, the Nobel Assembly awarded the prize to **Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel** for discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and the development of optogenetics ([official announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2026/press-release/)).

Hegemann and Nagel’s characterization of microbial channelrhodopsins supplied genetically encoded, light-driven ion channels. Deisseroth and collaborators established their use for millisecond-scale control of selected neurons in intact circuits. Optogenetics subsequently transformed causal neuroscience and spread into cardiac biology, sensory-restoration research and cell-signalling studies.

This is an award announcement, not a new peer-reviewed result. The field’s history is broader than the three laureates: multiple groups contributed early optical neuronal control, viral delivery, targeting and behavioral applications. The Nobel recognizes one discovery-to-tool lineage under its three-person limit, not an exhaustive attribution ([historical review](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.3240)).
