#### ★★★ 1. TeV Gamma-Ray Emission in the DA 495 Region from HAWC Observations of J1952+2924, J1954+2837, and J1958+2848

**HAWC Collaboration**  
[arXiv:2610.13771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13771)

**New measurement**

Using 3,071 days of fifth-pass HAWC data, the collaboration resolves three extended sources with curved spectra in a confused Galactic-plane field: HAWC J1952+2924, associated with the pulsar-wind nebula DA 495, and J1954+2837 and J1958+2848, both plausible TeV-halo candidates. A fourth component is only a hint and is sensitive to the adopted Galactic diffuse-emission model.

For DA 495, the new spectrum extrapolates to

\[
\left.\frac{dN}{dE}\right|_{1\,\mathrm{TeV}}
=(3.89\pm1.27_{\rm stat})\times10^{-13}\;
\mathrm{TeV^{-1}\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}},
\]

consistent within \(1\sigma\) with VERITAS. A one-zone leptonic interpretation uses \(B\sim10\,\mu\mathrm G\) and a roughly 50-TeV electron cutoff. The two other sources require diffusion coefficients around \(3.2\times10^{26}\) and \(1.4\times10^{27}\,\mathrm{cm^2\,s^{-1}}\) at their fitted cutoff energies, far below the canonical interstellar value. 

##### History

The earlier [VERITAS/Fermi follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10423) confirmed TeV emission from DA 495 but measured a substantially smaller flux than HAWC’s 2HWC catalog; the later [HAWC–VERITAS–NuSTAR study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07327) could not resolve whether the mismatch arose from morphology or source confusion. The dedicated three-source likelihood fit now removes that discrepancy without invoking variability and is therefore a substantive observational advance. 

It also strengthens, but does not establish, the TeV-halo interpretation of J1954+2837 and J1958+2848. Their pulsar associations are positional and model dependent, the tentative fourth source changes with the diffuse template, and the DA 495 distance and age remain uncertain. At the often-used 1-kpc distance the inferred transport can fall below the Bohm limit; this indicates that the geometry or distance assumptions need revision, not exotic diffusion physics.

#### ★★★ 2. Perpendicular transport in isotropic magnetic turbulence: The role of stochastic drifts

**F. Pucci, P. Blasi, O. Pezzi, C. Evoli, F. Malara**  
[arXiv:2610.13865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13865)

**Methodological/theory advance**

The authors propagate test particles through wavelet-generated, magnetostatic isotropic turbulence spanning six to seven decades in wavenumber—about two orders of magnitude more dynamic range than conventional grid realizations. Parallel diffusion follows \(D_\parallel\propto E^{1/3}\) at low rigidity and \(D_\parallel\propto E^2\) in the quasi-ballistic regime. The ratio \(D_\perp/D_\parallel\) is nearly constant only for \(r_L\lesssim10^{-2}L_c\), then rises with rigidity.

Extending nonlinear guiding-center theory with stochastic curvature/gradient drifts and a telegraph closure reproduces this transition better than standard NLGC/UNLT formulas. 

##### History

Test-particle simulations had already found unequal parallel and perpendicular energy scalings, notably in [Dundović et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.102.103016). [Kuhlen, Mertsch and Phan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05882) instead emphasized magnetic-field-line subdiffusion as the origin of the growing ratio. The new work both extends the resolved inertial range and supplies a quantitatively successful alternative mechanism—random guiding-center drifts caused by turbulent field components parallel to the guide field.

It does not decisively exclude the field-line explanation; the paper explicitly allows both effects to contribute. The calculation uses prescribed, isotropic, magnetostatic turbulence, a fitted closure parameter, and no self-consistent cosmic-ray feedback, so its scaling should not be transplanted directly to anisotropic Galactic turbulence.

#### ★★★ 3. Seeing in the Dark: Gravitational Waves from Resonant Production of Dark Photon Dark Matter

**Yanou Cui, Pankaj Saha, Evangelos I. Sfakianakis**  
[arXiv:2610.12564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12564)

**Nonlinear calculation and forecast**

An oscillating Abelian-Higgs condensate can parametrically amplify the longitudinal mode of a dark photon. Dedicated three-dimensional lattice simulations now follow the linear resonance through backreaction, fragmentation, vacuum trapping, scalar–vector rescattering, and gravitational-wave production.

The final relic is generically a mixture of dark photons and dark-Higgs excitations; the partition is fixed by nonlinear evolution rather than by the linear Floquet exponent. If this sector supplies all dark matter, the gravitational-wave peak and amplitude correlate with the resonance scale. Frequencies from nanohertz to about 100 Hz map, in the studied benchmarks, to dark-photon masses from roughly \(10^{-18}\) eV to the sub-µeV range. 

##### History

[Dror, Harigaya and Narayan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.07195) established the analytic parametric-resonance mechanism but could not determine the post-backreaction abundance, phase-space distribution, or gravitational-wave spectrum. The new lattice calculation changes that status materially: the scalar remnant can be comparable to the vector abundance, and the observable is a correlated multicomponent-dark-matter/GW signal rather than a pure-vector relic.

This is still a forecast, not a detection claim. Finite lattice dynamic range, approximate post-trapping number-conserving scatterings, the minimal quartic potential, and the assumption that the sector makes all dark matter control the reach; astrophysical constraints on the mixed scalar–vector population are not yet propagated globally.

#### ★★☆ 4. The road to \(H_0\), \(\Omega_m\), and \(w\) with a global neutrino-telescope network

**Pavel Zhelnin, Gonzalo Herrera, Nicholas Kamp, Carlos A. Argüelles**  
[arXiv:2610.12577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12577)

**Current reanalysis plus forecast**

This study treats the empirical Seyfert neutrino–X-ray luminosity relation, \(L_\nu\propto L_X^\beta\), as a distance indicator. Applying it to 14 years of public IceCube data for 26 northern and southern Seyferts, anchored by geometric distances to NGC 1068 and NGC 4945, gives

\[
H_0=66^{+44}_{-37}\;
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},\qquad
\beta=0.72^{+0.14}_{-0.16}.
\]

A 2030 IceCube+KM3NeT-ARCA+Baikal-GVD forecast with five anchors gives \(H_0=66^{+15}_{-13}\) and \(\beta=0.72\pm0.04\), a nearly 65% reduction in the quoted \(H_0\) uncertainty and a projected \(\gtrsim5\sigma\) rejection of the calorimetric slope \(\beta=1\). A high-redshift “little-red-dot” extension to \(\Omega_m\) and \(w\) is explicitly conditional on those objects becoming neutrino sources obeying the same relation. 

##### History

The authors’ [initial neutrino \(H_0\) analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00923) used a smaller northern-sky Seyfert sample and one distance anchor. The present real-data fit adds the southern sky and a second anchor but remains a proof of principle: its roughly 60% fractional uncertainty is not cosmologically competitive.

The principal advance is the all-sky network design and demonstration that better angular reconstruction and multiple anchors can separately constrain \(H_0\) and \(\beta\). The forecast assumes that a single luminosity law—with controlled X-ray absorption, variability, source selection, and intrinsic scatter—applies to every Seyfert and future detector. Those systematics may dominate before the advertised statistical precision is reached. The high-redshift \(\Omega_m,w\) exercise is more speculative still.

#### ★★★ 5. nCTEQ26 nuclear parton distribution functions

**nCTEQ Collaboration: K. Greve, T. Ježo, A. Kusina, J. Wissmann, M. Klasen, K. Kovařík, C. Léger, J. G. Morfín, F. I. Olness, P. Risse, I. Schienbein, J. Y. Yu**  
[arXiv:2610.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13633)

**Global analysis and public resource**

nCTEQ26 fits 3,598 measurements—neutral- and charged-current DIS, neutrino dimuons, fixed-target Drell–Yan, electroweak bosons, inclusive hadrons, open heavy flavor and quarkonia from RHIC and LHC Runs 1–2—with 36 free parameters at NLO.

A flexible \(x\) dependence is combined with quadratic logarithmic nuclear-\(A\) dependence, target-mass and deuteron corrections, and the CJ15 proton baseline. The data reach \(x\sim10^{-5}\), while relaxed cuts admit high-\(x\) Jefferson Lab information. Hessian grids are released through LHAPDF and HEPForge. 

##### History

[nCTEQ15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00792) used 740 points, chiefly fixed-target DIS/Drell–Yan plus RHIC pion data. [EPPS21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12462) and [nNNPDF3.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12363) had already shown the leverage of LHC dijets, electroweak bosons and heavy flavor on small-\(x\) gluons. 

nCTEQ26’s advance is therefore not a first observation of shadowing but the consolidation of the nCTEQ program into a much larger, process-diverse fit with explicit high-\(x\) corrections and public error sets. Direct precision comparisons across groups remain sensitive to parametrization and tolerance choices: nCTEQ26 uses a Hessian tolerance \(T=50\), works at NLO, fixes the proton baseline rather than propagating its full uncertainty, and does not include a general missing-higher-order error. Data-driven quarkonium/open-heavy-flavor constraints may also absorb production-model effects into the fitted nPDFs.

#### ★★★ 6. Complete four-loop pressure of cold QED

**Aapeli Kärkkäinen, Pablo Navarrete, Mika Nurmela, Kaapo Seppänen, Samuel Abreu, Maximilian Delto, Aleksi Kurkela, Risto Paatelainen, Aleksi Vuorinen**  
[arXiv:2610.13873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13873)

**First complete calculation**

The authors complete the next-to-next-to-next-to-leading-order pressure of a zero-temperature ultrarelativistic electron gas. The calculation combines hard, soft and mixed momentum regions; the remaining finite-density four-loop integrals are evaluated with dense loop–tree duality.

It is the first thermodynamic quantity calculated completely through four loops in a physical gauge theory. The series shows good apparent convergence and weak renormalization-scale dependence even at relatively large couplings, although a Bayesian missing-order analysis gives uncertainties larger than scale variation alone. 

##### History

Freedman and McLerran’s 1977 calculation fixed the cold-QED pressure through N2LO. The 2023 result [Degenerate fermionic matter at N3LO: Quantum electrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11893) determined all logarithmic and factorized pieces but left the finite hard four-loop constant unevaluated. This paper supplies that last contribution and is therefore a genuine completion rather than an incremental coefficient update.

Its largest impact is methodological: it validates tools aimed at the still-incomplete N3LO cold-QCD pressure relevant to neutron-star equations of state. QED itself is weakly coupled, so the direct phenomenological correction to ordinary electron matter is small; furthermore, scale variation alone demonstrably understates truncation uncertainty. The analogous non-Abelian calculation remains substantially harder.

#### ★★☆ 7. ALP interactions with mesons and baryons at \(\mathcal O(1/f_a)\): a general field-redefinition-invariant approach

**Jorge Alda, Stefano Rigolin**  
[arXiv:2610.13671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13671)

**Formalism and phenomenology**

Starting from general CP-conserving quark and gluon couplings, the paper identifies field-redefinition-invariant Wilson combinations and matches them into \(U(3)\) chiral perturbation theory. LSZ reduction is used to choose a no-mixing basis, so physical ALP–meson amplitudes depend only on invariant direct couplings.

The authors derive general vector and axial couplings to the baryon octet, including a second topological-singlet ALP–baryon structure, constrain flavor-changing terms with rare hyperon decays, and show in a UV example how running generates flavor change from diagonal high-scale couplings. In a gluophilic benchmark the summed baryon–antibaryon width can exceed any individual exclusive mesonic width for \(2\lesssim m_a/\mathrm{GeV}\lesssim3\). 

##### History

Earlier weak-chiral analyses, notably [Bauer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13112), enforced consistent mesonic matching, while [Balkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15637) developed a covariant description of ALP–hadron interactions. The new work extends field-basis invariance beyond the anomalous Wess–Zumino–Witten sector and systematically includes both flavor-changing quark couplings and the baryon octet.

No close prior study was found in the literature checked for the second topological-singlet term used as an independent dynamical ALP–baryon coupling; that is the paper’s sharpest formal novelty. The 2–3 GeV width estimates sit near the edge of controlled chiral reasoning and use phenomenological form factors/duality assumptions. Rare-hyperon bounds and collider comparisons also depend on the ALP decay branching fractions and UV flavor structure.

#### ★★★ 8. Nailing down the Hubble constant value through time-delay cosmography: A 3% measurement from SN Refsdal

**S. Schuldt, C. Grillo, S. H. Suyu, A. Acebron, L. Bazzanini, P. Bergamini, G. Di Rosa, A. Halkola, A. Mercurio, P. Rosati**  
[arXiv:2610.12595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12595)

**New cosmographic measurement**

The analysis adds an extended-source reconstruction of 77,000 HST pixels from the SN Refsdal host to the MACS J1149.5+2223 cluster lens and its measured supernova time delays. In flat \(\Lambda\)CDM it obtains

\[
H_0=65.0^{+2.0}_{-1.8}\;
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},\qquad
\Omega_m=0.30^{+0.08}_{-0.09}.
\]

With \(\Omega_m\) free, the surface-brightness information halves the \(H_0\) uncertainty relative to the same point-image model, reaching 3.0%. Four source/weighting realizations give mutually consistent results. 

##### History

The blinded multi-model [Kelly et al. analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06367) found \(H_0=64.8^{+4.4}_{-4.3}\), while the position-and-delay cluster model of [Grillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10980) obtained \(65.1^{+3.5}_{-3.4}\) in a broader cosmological fit. The central value is therefore confirmation, not a shift; the new result is the factor-of-two precision gain obtained by using resolved host-galaxy structure and the first joint \(H_0,\Omega_m\) inference from such an extended lensed-SN reconstruction. 

The 3% figure is conditional on a fixed family of cluster mass parametrizations. The authors vary source reconstruction and weighting but deliberately do not explore the full mass-model space, external convergence, or every line-of-sight systematic. It should therefore not yet be read as a 3% total-error resolution of the Hubble tension.

#### GRB 261011A: a long, very soft event with a short hard spike — *preliminary alert sequence*

SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered at **13:49:04 UTC on 11 October** and reported a bright long burst with a preliminary \(T_{90}=66.77\) s; Swift/BAT had independently triggered at **13:46:41 UTC**.

Swift saw a broad, mostly sub-50-keV pulse from trigger to about \(+130\) s, a shoulder extending to roughly \(+260\) s, and a separate approximately five-second structure near \(+95\) s visible through the 100–350 keV band. The prompt emission combines an extended soft component with a separate short, harder structure. ([SVOM circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45895), [Swift circular](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45896)) 

Swift/UVOT found a \(White\simeq17.78\) candidate 321 seconds after the trigger. Multiple facilities confirmed an optical counterpart; by 18:56 UTC, 5.17 hours after the burst, LCO measured \(g=21.91\pm0.17\) and \(r=21.09\pm0.14\) AB, demonstrating rapid fading. ([LCO follow-up](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45909)) 

**Status:** confirmed gamma-ray burst with a fading optical afterglow, but still preliminary. No spectroscopic redshift, secure progenitor classification, neutrino association or gravitational-wave counterpart was reported in the circulars checked by the report cutoff. The two-timescale prompt morphology is observationally interesting but does not by itself establish a new burst class.
