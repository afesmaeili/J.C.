#### 1. Cosmic-ray self-confinement: damping depends on the waves actually present

**Rouven Lemmerz, Mohamad Shalaby, Ellen Zweibel, Christoph Pfrommer and Timon Thomas — “The impact of nonlinear Landau damping on cosmic ray streaming and the role of external turbulence.” [arXiv:2610.20695v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20695v1).** Submitted **14 October, 18:00:00 UTC**; **16 October listing**. Preprint; no journal reference listed.

Can nonlinear Landau damping (NLLD) saturate the streaming instability by itself? One-dimensional fluid–particle-in-cell simulations toggle Landau closures while keeping other inputs fixed. A monoenergetic cosmic-ray shell begins at **\(v_d=5v_A\)**, with **\(v_A=0.01c\)**, **\(\beta_i=\beta_e=1\)** and **\(n_{\rm CR}/n_{\rm bg}=5\times10^{-4}\)**.

Without external waves, damping-on/off runs agree and relax toward **\(v_d\simeq v_A\)**. Seeded, counter-propagating large-scale waves make NLLD effective and can instead drive the drift toward the background frame. Reducing particle sampling from **4,080 to 204 cosmic-ray macroparticles per ion skin depth** produces spurious backward waves and an inverse cascade.

This is a controlled numerical result, not a measured Galactic diffusion coefficient. The shell distribution, reduced mass ratio, non-cascading external modes and absence of thermal-particle trapping in the closure limit generalization. [Full text, Sections III–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20695v1).

##### History

Self-confinement couples cosmic-ray transport to the waves the particles excite; damping then controls scattering, heating and momentum transfer. [Bai et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.10219v1) established detailed MHD-PIC tests of streaming growth and pitch-angle scattering, but ideal-MHD backgrounds do not capture NLLD.

[Schroer, Caprioli and Blasi (2024 preprint; PRL 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02230v1) found NLLD, heating, an inverse cascade and a steady drift around **\(3.7v_A\)** in a continuously replenished hybrid-PIC setup. The new paper challenges the interpretation of inverse-cascade signatures, but it does not reproduce that experiment identically: boundaries, driving, plasma treatment and sampling differ. Its numerical-artifact explanation is a testable hypothesis about the disagreement, not proof that the earlier calculation is invalid. Realistic broad cosmic-ray spectra and cross-code comparisons remain necessary.

#### 2. Vertical air showers could extend UHE-neutrino follow-up

**Jaime Alvarez-Muñiz, Ruben Conceição, Pedro J. Costa, David Dias and Mário Pimenta — “Identification of ultra-high-energy neutrinos in vertical air showers with surface detectors.” [arXiv:2610.21495v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21495v1).** Submitted **15 October, 08:32:23 UTC**; **16 October listing**. Preprint; no acceptance listed.

CORSIKA/HERWIG showers and Geant4 detector simulations test whether combined scintillator and water-Cherenkov signals distinguish electromagnetic-rich neutrino showers from protons. The quantitative benchmark is **\(E_\nu=10^{18}\) eV**, **\(\theta=30^\circ\)**, with **1,500 m** station spacing.

At proton leakage **\(\varepsilon_{\rm bkg}=2\times10^{-5}\)**, motivated by a one-hour, **3°** transient search, the detector-level electron-neutrino effective area is approximately **\(1.6\times10^{-2}\) km²**; ideal electromagnetic/muon separation yields **\(4.0\times10^{-2}\) km²**. These are simulated responses, not observed flux limits.

Important simplifications include fixed shower cores, dense virtual sampling at 1,000 m instead of reconstructed lateral profiles, and a lowered **1.2 VEM** station threshold. Efficiency across energy and zenith angle remains unvalidated. Photon showers resemble electron-neutrino showers, so this selection identifies neutral-particle candidates rather than uniquely identifying neutrinos. [Full text, Sections II–VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21495v1).

##### History

Established Auger searches exploit old, muon-rich inclined cosmic-ray showers versus young, electromagnetic-rich neutrino showers, including Earth-skimming tau decays. The [2019 Auger point-source analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.07419v2) searched **1 January 2004–31 August 2018**, finding no candidates and reporting declination-dependent limits over its UHE response range.

The new method targets a complementary field of view, conventionally called vertical here for **\(\theta\leq60^\circ\)**. Its strongest case is short, directionally constrained follow-up, where backgrounds are reduced externally. An instantaneous single-energy effective area cannot establish an improvement over a published exposure-integrated, flavor-dependent bound. The paper's wider-angle sensitivity extrapolation assumes constant identification efficiency; it is not a demonstrated full detector response.

#### 3. NGC 1068: compact-source inference with an important statistical qualification

**Zhi-Peng Ma and Kai Wang — “Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of supermassive black hole in NGC 1068.” [arXiv:2610.21110v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21110v1).** Submitted **15 October, 02:13:36 UTC**; **16 October listing**. Preprint; no journal reference listed.

A steady proton model includes **\(pp\)** and **\(p\gamma\)** production, losses and electromagnetic cascades. It samples **10,000** five-parameter configurations and five magnetic-energy ratios, adjusting proton luminosity against neutrino and Fermi-LAT spectra. The radius scan spans **\(0–15R_s\)**, where **\(R_s=2GM/c^2\)**; escape is fixed initially to **\(10R/c\)**.

The authors quote exclusions of **\(R\gtrsim6R_s\)** at “3σ” and **\(R\gtrsim10R_s\)** at “5σ.” However, their statistic treats **15 points sampled from a power-law confidence band as independent**, without the fitted-spectrum covariance. These nominal significances should not be adopted as calibrated confidence limits.

Also, **\(f_{\rm cov}>0.8\)** means over 80% of sampled energy points lie inside the band—not 80% of the neutrino flux. The selected models favor **\(R\lesssim3R_s\)** and stochastic acceleration, conditional on this spectral criterion and geometry. Sample percentiles are not marginalized likelihood intervals. [Methods, Tables I–II and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21110v1).

##### History

The underlying puzzle is established: a bright neutrino source with much weaker escaping gamma rays requires opacity and energy reprocessing. The [IceCube analysis used here](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13403v2) fits an unbroken spectrum; points drawn along its band are not additional independent measurements.

[Das, Murase and Zhang (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01222v2) already favored **\(R\sim3–10R_s\)** for photohadronic coronae, while allowing larger regions in some hadronuclear settings. Those are conditional physical constraints, not directly comparable Gaussian radius exclusions. The new scan changes spectral acceptance and parameter sampling; it does not supply a model-independent improvement factor.

Composition is another unvaried assumption. [Esmaeili, Esmaili and Serpico (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23812v1) explicitly propagate nuclei and nuclear/electromagnetic cascades in NGC 1068. Their framework is not identical—their illustrated electromagnetic treatment neglects synchrotron losses—but it shows why a proton-only scan does not cover all source physics. A covariance-aware likelihood and explicit near-horizon geometry are needed before interpreting the new radius thresholds as robust exclusions.

#### 4. Solar neutrinos test explanations of the gallium anomaly

**Steven R. Elliott and R. G. Hamish Robertson — “Gallium Anomaly and Solar Neutrinos.” [arXiv:2610.22219v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22219v1).** Submitted **15 October, 17:02:46 UTC**; **16 October listing**. Preprint.

Can an incorrect gallium capture cross section or extraction efficiency explain the artificial-source deficit? The authors combine Borexino elastic-scattering measurements, SNO neutral-current data, the solar luminosity constraint, updated capture cross sections and neutrino mixing inputs.

They predict **\(67.5^{+1.4}_{-1.1}\) SNU**, versus the radiochemical solar result **\(66.1\pm3.1\) SNU**; the measured/predicted ratio is **\(0.980^{+0.050}_{-0.049}\)**. One SNU is **\(10^{-36}\) captures per target atom per second**. These are rate estimates with propagated uncertainties, not sterile-neutrino exclusion contours.

The agreement challenges a universal approximately 20% response suppression. It does not resolve the artificial-source anomaly. The inference retains solar-density dependence through MSW conversion, a luminosity-constrained pp flux, oscillation inputs and correlated nuclear uncertainties. Extending solar-run efficiency validation to radioactive-source runs is an inference from similar procedures, not a direct remeasurement. [Calculation, Tables II–III and Figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22219v1).

##### History

[BEST](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.07364v2) measured deficient capture rates in both concentric gallium zones, without establishing the distance dependence needed to identify an oscillation pattern. The anomaly concerns neutrinos from intense chromium/argon sources; it is distinct from the historical solar-neutrino problem.

[Cadeddu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20560v3), now published in PRL, proposed that treating leptonic wave functions beyond factorization could lower the capture cross section while preserving the germanium electron-capture lifetime. The new solar comparison supplies an independent empirical consistency test of such explanations. Whether an energy-dependent correction could reconcile both datasets requires a joint calculation with the same nuclear response and covariance. Neither paper alone establishes sterile neutrinos or definitively eliminates all conventional explanations.

#### 5. Reconnection injection need not pass through electric-dominated regions

**Omar French, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Qile Zhang, Yi-Hsin Liu, William Daughton and Hui Li — “Are E>B regions essential for particle injection in relativistic magnetic reconnection?” [arXiv:2610.22118v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22118v1).** Submitted **15 October, 15:58:42 UTC**; **16 October listing**. Submitted to MNRAS; not reported accepted.

Two-dimensional pair-plasma PIC simulations partition each tracer's electric work before **\(\gamma_{\rm inj}=\sigma/4\)** into work inside **\(E>B\)** regions, post-exit work, and remaining **\(B>E\)** work. Here **\(\sigma=B_0^2/(4\pi n_0m_ec^2)\)** uses the total pair density; **\(\sigma=12–800\)**.

Table 2 gives the magnetized channel the largest contribution for **93%, 70%, 65% and 63%** of injected tracers at **\(\sigma=12,50,200,800\)**, respectively. These exact table entries are preferable to rounded abstract percentages. Many particles reach injection without earlier **\(E>B\)** contact.

This is a trajectory-budget result with negligible guide field and no radiation losses. “Magnetized” here means a field-magnitude classification, not demonstrated adiabatic motion. Three-dimensional, radiative and electron–ion systems need separate tests. [Full analysis, Sections II–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22118v1).

##### History

The injection question asks how thermal particles first enter the nonthermal population, before subsequent high-energy acceleration. [Sironi (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.04342v1) argued that encounters with nonideal fields were necessary, using energetic-particle histories and altered test-particle trajectories.

[Gupta, Sridhar and Sironi (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00979v1) found large pre-injection electric-dominated work fractions for particles selected to end at especially high energies. The new paper instead emphasizes the injection shares of the whole injected population and separately tracks post-injection selection. These statistics answer different questions: a channel can inject a minority yet be overrepresented near the eventual cutoff. Boundary conditions and altered-trajectory experiments also differ. The result directly challenges universal necessity of **\(E>B\)** contact in the tested regime; it does not show that every definition of nonideal electric work is unimportant.

### Science Radar

#### 1. Designed enzymes tackle peptide-bond cleavage

**Hojae Choi and colleagues — “De novo design of cysteine proteases”; peer-reviewed Nature publication: 14 October 2026; reported here as a catch-up.** Computationally designed proteins use an activated cysteine to cleave peptide backbones in a sequence-dependent manner. The study reports **13 active designs out of 69 tested (19%)**, with catalytic rate enhancement over the uncatalysed reaction up to **\(3\times10^7\)**. Six crystal structures agree with design models within **1.2 Å Cα RMSD**.

The key advance is experimentally validated catalysis of a difficult chemical reaction, beyond predicting a stable protein fold. The rate enhancement is relative to uncatalysed chemistry, not a claim of outperforming natural proteases. Active-site organization and substrate positioning still limit performance. Cleavage in mammalian cells supports a synthetic-biology application, not demonstrated therapeutic safety or clinical efficacy. [Original publication and accessible experimental captions](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11143-w); [15 October crystallography-facility announcement](https://necat.chem.cornell.edu/2026/10/15/ai-used-by-baker-lab-to-design-proteases/).

#### 2. A Jurassic bird adds a terrestrial branch to early avian diversity

**L. Xu, M. Wang, G. Lin and colleagues — “Jurassic flightless bird reveals early ecological diversity of avialans”; peer-reviewed Nature publication: 14 October; publisher announcement: 15 October 2026.** The approximately **148–150-million-year-old** *Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis* from southeastern China combines avialan and dinosaur-like anatomy, including a hyperextensible second toe.

Its forelimbs are roughly one-third shorter than its hind limbs. Phylogenetically informed comparisons, including **377 living bird species**, support a terrestrial, flightless interpretation. This expands the ecological diversity inferred for early avialans rather than implying that every early bird followed a single trajectory toward improved flight.

The inference rests on one incomplete specimen and comparative anatomy; behavior is reconstructed, not observed. Its precise evolutionary placement and the history of flight loss require more fossils. [Original paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10972-z); [publisher's dated account](https://www.natureasia.com/en/info/press-releases/detail/9462).
