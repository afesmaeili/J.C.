#### 1. Quiet black holes as UHECR accelerators: an energy ceiling, not a source detection

**Elena Manao, Elisa Resconi, Frank M. Rieger, Paolo Padovani and Gia Dvali — “Exploring Cosmic Rays from Gravity-Limited Acceleration at Supermassive Black Holes.” [arXiv:2610.23393v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23393v1).** Submitted **16 October, 12:51:28 UTC**; **19 October listing**. Accepted for PRL, according to arXiv.

A gravity-based magnetic-energy estimate gives \(B_{\max}\propto M^{-1}\), hence a formal horizon-scale voltage ceiling

\[
E_{\rm geom}=aZeBR_S\sim10^{27}Za\ {\rm eV},
\]

where \(a\) is dimensionless spin and \(Ze\) the particle charge. This is **not an achievable-energy prediction**: realistic fields are much smaller, and curvature losses impose another ceiling. The quoted \(E_{\rm curv}\propto M^{1/4}\) assumes the gravity-limited field scaling and horizon-scale curvature.

An angular scan correlates **2,635 Auger events above 32 EeV** with **90 galaxy nuclei**, separated into active and quiescent classes. The strongest local excess, reported as **2.79σ**, falls to **1.25σ after trials**. The authors find no significant class preference. [Full text and supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23393v1).

Gap survival, magnetic-flux supply and nuclear escape remain model-dependent; the formal ceiling does not establish an accelerator population or explain its flux.

##### History

The physical issue is whether a source can supply enough **voltage while remaining transparent**, rather than merely confine a high-rigidity particle. [Boldt and Ghosh, “Cosmic rays from remnants of quasars?” (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9902342), already proposed horizon-scale acceleration in dormant nuclei, with induced voltage approximately **\(4.4\times10^{20}B_4M_9\) V**, where \(B_4=B/(10^4\,\mathrm G)\) and \(M_9=M/(10^9M_\odot)\). Their low-luminosity condition was intended to avoid photon losses and shorting of the accelerator.

The present work adds a gravitational ceiling and an explicit modern arrival-direction test to that established proposal. Its much larger formal voltage is not a measured improvement over earlier attainable energies: the magnetic-field assumptions differ. Radiatively inefficient accretion must still provide currents and magnetic flux without screening the gap. A nonsignificant correlation also cannot decide whether these nuclei contribute alongside other source classes. The source-power budget, composition-dependent propagation and self-consistent gap dynamics remain unresolved.

#### 2. JUNO-TAO reports its first reactor antineutrino sample

**JUNO Collaboration — “Reactor Antineutrino Detection with JUNO-TAO: Commissioning Performance and First Results.” [arXiv:2610.23331v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23331v1).** Submitted **16 October, 11:32:51 UTC**; **19 October listing**. Preprint; no journal acceptance listed.

TAO operates a **2.8-ton scintillator target, 43.2 m from a reactor core**, at approximately **−50°C**. Physics data taking began **10 February 2026**. Prompt positron–delayed neutron coincidences give candidate rates of **\(2900\pm10\) per day reactor-on** and **\(419\pm4\) per day reactor-off**, using **31.3 and 29.4 days**, respectively. The total selection efficiency is **\((46.6\pm5.0)\%\)**.

Calibration yields approximately **4,000 photoelectrons/MeV** after dark-noise/crosstalk corrections and **2.8% energy resolution for central \(^{68}\)Ge events**. These are source- and position-specific calibration results, not a universal positron resolution law. [Full text, Sections 4–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23331v1).

Reactor-off subtraction and a forward-folded response validate the detection chain. The spectrum is still primarily a commissioning result: statistics, fiducial-volume efficiency, nonlinear response and energy leakage limit precision. It is neither a mass-ordering measurement nor a new sterile-neutrino exclusion.

##### History

[TAO's 2020 conceptual design report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08745) proposed a high-resolution near-reactor reference spectrum to reduce JUNO's dependence on reactor calculations. Its roughly **4,500 photoelectrons/MeV** and sub-percent resolution over much of the energy range were **design projections**, including energy-dependent effects; they cannot be compared directly with one central gamma calibration point as a simple success/failure ratio. The realized baseline and selections also differ from the original design.

The need for a measured reference is well established. [Daya Bay's full-sample result (PRL, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00746), using **4.7 million IBD candidates**, measured the total IBD yield as **\((5.84\pm0.07)\times10^{-43}\) cm²/fission** and found spectral disagreement with the tested reactor models, including the familiar excess around 5 MeV. This yield includes the IBD interaction cross section; it is not simply neutrinos emitted per fission.

TAO's advance is demonstrated operation of the proposed precision technology and a reactor-correlated sample. A fine-structure reference competitive in systematic control requires more exposure and calibrated response covariance; small calibration widths alone do not provide it.

#### 3. Mapping MeV nuclear lines to test low-energy Galactic cosmic rays

**Tomohiko Oka, Thomas Siegert, Tristan Bouchet, Laura Eisenberger, Mika A. Gelowicz, Saurabh Mittal, Rudi Reinhardt, Manuel R. H. W. Skalka, Dimitris Tsatsis, Hiroki Yoneda and Manja Zimmerer — “Three-dimensional spatial modelling of nuclear de-excitation lines.” [arXiv:2610.23380v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23380v1).** Submitted **16 October, 12:36:28 UTC**; **19 October listing**. Accepted for A&A, according to arXiv.

GALPROP transport plus TALYS nuclear reactions produce sky maps of cosmic-ray-induced lines, especially **4.439 MeV carbon** and **6.129 MeV oxygen**. Additional low-energy ions can enhance the central signal: concentrated models reach approximately **\(10^{-6}\) photons cm⁻² s⁻¹** within **2°** of the Galactic centre. Instrument-response/background simulations are still needed to establish detectability. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23380v1).

The bright models demand **\(7\times10^{42}\)–\(10^{43}\) erg/s** of injected cosmic-ray power, versus approximately **\(10^{41}\) erg/s** in the conventional Galactic budget. This is a major physical difficulty. The imposed **20% allowance relative to the local baseline proton flux** is a modeling selection, **not a confidence limit**; metallicity, gas and cross sections add uncertainty.

##### History

Low-energy ions efficiently ionize gas but are difficult to reconstruct from local particle data because of transport and heliospheric effects. Their collisions excite nuclei, making gamma lines a complementary probe of the ion population.

[Benhabiles-Mezhoud et al. (2013; 2012 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1622) already connected cloud ionization to these lines. For their extended inner-Galaxy region they predicted **\((0.1–2.0)\times10^{-5}\) photons cm⁻² s⁻¹** in the 4.4-MeV line, with substantial cosmic-ray and metallicity uncertainty; they found existing SPI sensitivity inadequate. That was a prediction, not a detection or bound on the ion population.

The new contribution is a three-dimensional spatial treatment and explicit comparison with the spatially varying continuum. Its small central aperture and added cosmic-ray distribution differ from the older integrated calculation, so the fluxes are not an improvement-factor comparison. Moreover, modern ionization-rate reassessments alter the normalization problem. Spatially localized acceleration and transport may be more realistic than a smooth extra population, but must be tested against both line brightness and available power.

#### 4. The 20 GeV halo residual survives some foreground tests, but its dark-matter interpretation weakens

**Kevork N. Abazajian, Youyou Li and Oscar Macias — “Foreground dependence of the 20 GeV Galactic halo gamma-ray excess and its proposed connection to the LUX-ZEPLIN event.” [arXiv:2610.23713v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23713v1).** Submitted **16 October, 16:34:04 UTC**; **19 October listing**. Preprint; no acceptance listed.

The authors reanalyze **15 years of Fermi-LAT data** with **281 diffuse configurations**, alternative bubble/Loop-I templates and finer, response-folded bins. Outside the original Fermi-bubble mask, nominal halo-template significances of **4–16σ** become approximately **1–7σ** after enlarged boundaries are excluded. These are **conditional template significances**, not a global dark-matter significance.

Independent sky regions do not support one common NFW normalization. Simulated foreground mismatches can create substantial halo-correlated emission, although they do not reproduce every observed spectral feature. A **420 GeV \(W^+W^-\)** benchmark fits some foreground choices and fails others; the full LZ model/likelihood is not fitted. [Full text, Sections III–VI and validation appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23713v1).

This weakens attribution of the entire residual to a smooth annihilation halo without excluding a smaller dark-matter contribution.

##### History

For annihilation, the intensity scales as **\(\langle\sigma v\rangle J/m_\chi^2\)**, with **\(J=\int\rho^2ds\)**. A template-shaped residual therefore does not determine particle physics without a density model and reliable foreground separation.

[Totani's original analysis (JCAP, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07209) favored approximately **0.5–0.8 TeV**, **\(b\bar b\)** annihilation with **\(\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim(5–8)\times10^{-25}\) cm³/s**. These were conditional best fits, not exclusion limits. [Cholis, Hu and Zhong (7 October 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10695) instead modeled bubble substructure as the origin. The new paper supports a major foreground role while finding additional residuals in some fits; differing templates, fit freedoms and data selections matter.

[Galactic-centre constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10416) and [dwarf-galaxy analyses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25586) independently test annihilation. Comparing them requires the same channel, velocity dependence, density/J-factor treatment and dark-matter abundance; this reanalysis supplies no new universal exclusion curve or improvement factor.

Separately, [LZ's extended-recoil search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02823) reported **one 248 ± 23(stat) ± 23(sys) keV recoil candidate**, observed **16 June 2023**, with **2.6σ global background-only tension**. Its 2026 reporting date is not its event date. A shared mass scale or an acceptable gamma spectral fit does not establish a common origin or combine their significances.

### Science Radar

#### 1. AI agents learn from wet-lab failures—but the aptamer result is a feasibility study

**Technical report dated 12 October; publicly highlighted by Google Research on 16 October 2026.** Zachary Cutts and colleagues' *Aptamers as a sandbox for lab-in-the-loop agentic molecular discovery* tests three design–experiment cycles against two proteins, using pooled DNA candidates, bead-binding assays and sequencing feedback. Reported bead-corrected hit rates rise **0 → 26.0 → 42.1%** for α-thrombin and **10.2 → 3.7 → 33.5%** for myoglobin. The temporary decline exposes nonspecific “sticky” binding rather than useful affinity. [Primary technical report](https://www.gstatic.com/science-ai/agentic_aptamer_sandbox.pdf); [16 October announcement](https://www.research.google/blog/grounding-ai-agents-in-biological-reality-building-blocks-for-autonomous-wet-labs/).

Single-clone tests find preliminary **24.6–133 nM apparent dissociation constants** for **modified literature-derived motifs**; strictly de novo scaffolds show **\(K_D>1\) μM**. The nanomolar values lack a fully saturated titration plateau. They should not be conflated with the separate protein-binder results in the announcement.

The agent system changes between rounds, and matched-budget no-feedback/non-reasoning controls are absent. This demonstrates a workable empirical feedback loop, not isolated causal proof that agent reasoning outperforms other search methods. Human researchers executed the wet-lab work; peer review is not established by this technical report.

#### 2. Chemical experiments expose blind spots in AI protein–ligand predictions

**Peer-reviewed, open-access Nature Chemical Biology article, published 16 October 2026.** Rachel E. Hayward, Raymond F. Berkeley and colleagues vary chemical groups distant from a probe's reactive site and measure protein engagement in human cells. They identify **95 stereoselectively liganded cysteines** across a survey of **more than 24,000 cysteines**. The result shows how modest chemical changes can open a different landscape of protein targets. [Original research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-026-02348-1), DOI **10.1038/s41589-026-02348-1**.

One compound engages **GRPEL1 C124**, disrupts its association with the mitochondrial chaperone **HSPA9**, and impairs protein import and respiration. Inactive mirror-image compounds and a C124 mutant provide mechanistic controls.

Boltz-2 cofolding often places probes in familiar binding pockets instead of the experimentally engaged sites and does not reliably reproduce stereochemical preferences. Correctly preserving input stereochemistry in a later version does not fix the site-placement problem. These tests concern this chemical series and prediction setup, not every task for structure models. The compounds are research tools demonstrated in cells and protein assays; the study does not establish a therapeutic treatment.
