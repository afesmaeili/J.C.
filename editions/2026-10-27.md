#### 1. Super-Kamiokande reports a 2.6σ indication of the diffuse supernova antineutrino background

**Super-Kamiokande Collaboration — [arXiv:2610.35619v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35619v1).** Submitted **26 October, 15:35:48 UTC**; preprint, with no journal status stated. This is an indication from a model-dependent spectral fit, not a discovery.

The diffuse supernova neutrino background (DSNB) integrates electron antineutrinos from past core-collapse supernovae. Super-Kamiokande analyzes **5003.0 live days** from SK-IV through SK-VIII: 3349.3 days of pure water and 1653.7 days with gadolinium. Inverse beta decay supplies a positron plus neutron. The fit uses six Cherenkov-angle/neutron-multiplicity regions, improved neutral-current rejection, updated neutron tagging and nuisance-constrained background components. The one-neutron threshold falls from 15.5 to **11.5 MeV**, while the analysis conservatively raises the other-neutron-multiplicity threshold to **21.5 MeV** because of residual spallation; that choice removes **13.9%** of the expected signal.

For the Horiuchi et al. 6-MeV-temperature spectrum, the fit gives

\[
\Phi_{\bar\nu_e}(E_\nu>13.3\,\mathrm{MeV})=3.6^{+1.6}_{-1.5}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}},
\]

or (4.8^{+2.2}_{-2.0}) fitted events per year. The null is disfavored at **2.6σ** (one-sided 99.5%); two alternative DSNB shapes give 2.6–2.7σ. Pure-water and Gd phases separately give **1.4σ** and **2.2σ**, with mutually consistent fluxes. A model-independent event-count analysis over 11.5–29.5 MeV reaches only **1.8σ**. Above 17.3 MeV, the fitted flux is (1.8^{+0.8}_{-0.7}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}).

The inference assumes one of several fixed spectral shapes and fits its normalization. Signal-normalization systematics are about 2.9–3.1%, but background-shape errors, residual spallation, atmospheric-neutrino modeling and the core-collapse emission spectrum remain material. The result sits at the high end of the paper's representative (2.1)–(3.9\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}) model range and is below the conventional evidence threshold.

##### History

The DSNB has been a long-standing prediction, but backgrounds above reactor energies and below atmospheric-neutrino energies kept searches in an upper-limit regime. Super-K's [2021 pure-water analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11174) combined 5823 days and set a roughly **2.7 cm⁻² s⁻¹ 90% CL upper limit above 17.3 MeV**. The first [956.2-day SK-Gd search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02222) used enhanced neutron capture and found only a **~1.2σ** spectral excess. The new work combines later Gd exposure with the earlier phase, changes the background rejection and fit, and moves the best-fit preference to 2.6σ rather than merely tightening a limit.

The comparison is energy-window dependent. KamLAND's [full 9.02–9.42 kton-year analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29381) found seven candidates against (16.2\pm9.4) expected background events and set model-dependent **90% CL limits of 38–43 cm⁻² s⁻¹** over a lower-energy window; it does not conflict with Super-K and supplies complementary low-energy information. The remaining uncertainty is whether more exposure and independent detectors preserve the fitted excess with the same spectrum and cross-phase consistency.

#### 2. CMS searches for nonresonant semivisible jets with supervised and anomaly-detection taggers

**CMS Collaboration — [arXiv:2610.33140v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33140v1).** Submitted **23 October, 16:11:58 UTC**; submitted to *JHEP*. Experimental search with 95% confidence exclusions.

A confining hidden sector can produce jets containing visible dark-hadron decays and stable invisible hadrons, so missing transverse momentum aligns with a jet. CMS searches **138 fb⁻¹** of 13-TeV proton-proton data for nonresonant production through a bifundamental scalar mediator (Phi). The benchmark parameters are (m_\Phi), a quark–dark-quark Yukawa coupling (lambda), dark-hadron mass (m_\mathrm{dark}), and invisible-hadron fraction (r_\mathrm{inv}). A supervised ParticleNet graph network and an unsupervised Wasserstein-normalized autoencoder target complementary jet structures; multijet backgrounds are estimated with a data-driven ABCD construction using decorrelated discriminants.

No significant excess appears. With (m_\mathrm{dark}=20\) GeV and (lambda=1), the supervised analysis excludes (m_\Phi=0.5)–**2.4 TeV** at 95% CL for its most favorable (r_\mathrm{inv}=0.2)–0.5 region. The unsupervised analysis reaches (0.5)–**2.1 TeV**, strongest at (r_\mathrm{inv}=0.6)–0.9. For (r_\mathrm{inv}=0.3), (m_\mathrm{dark}=20\) GeV, the supervised search excludes all tested couplings for (m_\Phi<1.6) TeV; the anomaly detector excludes (lambda>1.8) through the tested (m_\Phi=3.0) TeV endpoint. Limits use asymptotic modified-frequentist (CL_s).

Sensitivity falls near (r_\mathrm{inv}=0), where missing momentum is small. The supervised tagger also weakens as (r_\mathrm{inv}\to1), where visible substructure disappears; the unsupervised tagger performs better there but its signal response depends on training and reconstruction choices. These exclusions apply to the stated shower, flavor, mass and coupling assumptions. They are not generic mass limits on strongly coupled dark matter.

##### History

CMS's [2021 resonant search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11125) targeted a leptophobic (Z') and excluded (Z') masses from **1.5 to 4.0 TeV**, reaching 5.1 TeV with a boosted-decision-tree tagger for a fixed SM-quark coupling of 0.25. That established the semivisible-jet signature, but its s-channel resonance and parameters are not directly comparable to the new bifundamental t-channel mediator.

ATLAS then performed the first [nonresonant t-channel semivisible-jet search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18037) in 139 fb⁻¹ and excluded mediator masses up to **2.7 TeV at 95% CL** for unit coupling. CMS now adds two modern jet-tagging strategies and maps how they exchange sensitivity across (r_\mathrm{inv}), but the two experiments use different hidden-flavor and cross-section conventions; quoting an improvement factor would be misleading. A common signal prescription and combined reinterpretation would be required for a clean comparison.

#### 3. Magnetically arrested LLAGN disks could add a hard PeV neutrino component

**Fan Wu and Benzhong Dai — [arXiv:2610.33548v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33548v1).** Submitted **24 October, 09:01:41 UTC**; accepted for publication in *The Astrophysical Journal*. Population-model prediction, not a source identification or flux measurement.

The authors model cosmic-ray acceleration in magnetically arrested disks (MADs) inside low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (LLAGNs). They solve single-source particle transport across black-hole mass and accretion rate, include (pp) and (p\gamma) pion/kaon production, charm, secondary cooling and three-flavor propagation, then integrate an Hα luminosity function weighted by a black-hole mass function. The estimated MAD fraction, derived from the ratio of low-excitation radio-galaxy to LLAGN number densities, is (f_\mathrm{MAD}^{\rm LF}\simeq1.6\times10^{-3}).

At that normalization, the all-flavor diffuse intensity peaks near **1.5 PeV** at

\[
E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu\simeq2.6\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}.
\]

Photomeson production on inverse-Compton optical/UV photons dominates the peak; (pp) pions dominate below about 0.1 PeV, and kaons dominate the cooled high-energy tail. The predicted Earthly flavor ratio near the peak is (0.296:0.361:0.344), with mild muon damping. Comparison with IceCube's segmented diffuse spectrum permits at most

\[
f_\mathrm{MAD}^{\max}\simeq2.3\times10^{-3}\left(\frac{0.0495}{\epsilon_{\rm NT}\epsilon_{\rm dis}}\right),
\]

and the fiducial spectrum stays below IceCube's 90% CL extremely-high-energy differential limit while approaching IceCube-Gen2 sensitivity near 30 PeV.

This is a normalization ceiling inside the model, not a frequentist confidence limit on the astrophysical MAD fraction. IceCube constrains the product (f_\mathrm{MAD}\epsilon_{\rm NT}\epsilon_{\rm dis}); the source fraction, nonthermal efficiency and dissipation efficiency are degenerate. Neglected redshift evolution, the uncertain Hα and black-hole mass functions, hadronic yields, acceleration microphysics and the assumption that low-excitation radio galaxies trace neutrino-luminous MADs dominate the interpretation.

##### History

The foundational LLAGN-RIAF calculation by [Kimura, Murase and Toma](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3588) showed that stochastic proton acceleration could reach beyond 10 PeV and, if about 1% of accretion power entered nonthermal ions, yield as much as (E_\nu^2\Phi_\nu\sim3\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{GeV\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,sr^{-1}}). A later [thermal-photon plus neutrino population model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01934) connected LLAGN hot flows to both the MeV gamma-ray and TeV–PeV neutrino backgrounds. Those studies established plausibility but allowed broad source and acceleration normalizations.

The new calculation specifically isolates the MAD state, averages sources over a black-hole mass function and uses the observed low-excitation radio-galaxy abundance to set (f_\mathrm{MAD}). Its fiducial peak is about an order of magnitude below the early maximal RIAF estimate, so it adds a high-energy component rather than claiming to explain the entire IceCube flux. Updated IceCube data already disfavor some higher-intensity earlier MAD curves. The decisive uncertainty remains population normalization and whether the assumed acceleration efficiencies occur in real LLAGN disks.

#### 4. Realistic supernova transport extends dark-gauge-boson exclusions, with large model dependence

**Zun Wang and Yongchao Zhang — [arXiv:2610.35379v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35379v1).** Submitted **26 October, 13:03:10 UTC**; preprint, with no journal status stated. Astrophysical constraint calculation.

The paper recalculates production, absorption, decay and geometric escape for a kinetically mixed dark photon, a (U(1)_{B-L}) boson and a (U(1)_{L_\mu-L_\tau}) boson. For dark photons and (B-L), it retains full momentum and mediator-mass dependence in one-pion-exchange nucleon bremsstrahlung and includes in-medium transverse/longitudinal modes. It evaluates conventional SN1987A cooling with three proto-neutron-star profiles and a separate low-energy-supernova (LESN) condition: exotic energy deposited in the mantle must not exceed the small observed explosion energy. The headline values use the SFHo-18.8 (18.8M_\odot) profile.

For transverse dark photons, SN1987A cooling excludes approximately (1.7\times10^{-10}\lesssim\epsilon\lesssim2\times10^{-7}) and masses to **230 MeV**; no longitudinal region survives as an exclusion with the realistic profiles. The LESN calculation excludes (5.8\times10^{-12}\lesssim\epsilon\lesssim6\times10^{-7}) for masses to **450 MeV**. For (B-L), combining modes gives LESN coverage around (1.9\times10^{-12}\lesssim g_{BL}\lesssim3.1\times10^{-7}) up to **440 MeV**; the smallest quoted longitudinal SN1987A coupling is (2.9\times10^{-16}). For (L_\mu-L_\tau), SN1987A gives roughly (1.4\times10^{-10}\)–(6.7\times10^{-7}) to 300 MeV, while LESNe give (6.4\times10^{-12}\)–(9\times10^{-7}) to **600 MeV**.

These are exclusion regions under specified supernova profiles, transport prescriptions and energy-budget criteria, not laboratory confidence intervals with a single probabilistic coverage. The OPE nuclear treatment carries known factor-level uncertainty, the historical SN1987A neutrino sample is sparse, and LESN explosion energies and progenitor structures are model dependent. Time-dependent stellar evolution and feedback of exotic transport on the explosion are not simulated self-consistently.

##### History

The classic SN1987A argument excludes couplings that let a new light particle free-stream enough energy to shorten the observed neutrino burst; trapping reopens the large-coupling region. [Chang, Essig and McDermott (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03864) incorporated plasma effects and realistic profiles for dark photons below roughly 100 MeV, finding profile and transport uncertainties of order factors of a few. Later work emphasized that weakly coupled particles can also alter the shock or deposit energy in unusually faint supernovae. The recent [Heavy Dark Photon Handbook](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15785) assembled cooling, LESN, fireball, gamma-ray, cosmological and other constraints across (0.1)–(10^3) MeV.

The new work is valuable because it applies a common geometry and profile treatment to three vector models and extends the mass reach of its LESN bands. It does not erase earlier limits: laboratory bounds dominate at larger couplings, cosmology can dominate elsewhere, and earlier SN analyses use different nuclear and dynamical approximations. The very low quoted couplings should therefore be read as profile-dependent astrophysical exclusions rather than precision determinations.

#### 5. Adaptive axion rescans can retain frequentist coverage without the usual loss in power

**Andrea Gallo Rosso and Jan Conrad — [arXiv:2610.34325v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34325v1).** Submitted **25 October, 14:33:45 UTC**; preprint, with no journal status stated. Statistical-method paper with a projected experimental example.

Axion haloscopes rescan frequencies when a power excess crosses a threshold. Because the decision to acquire more data depends on the data already observed, an upper limit must average over the complete stopping tree. The authors model the standardized power measurements as correlated Gaussian cumulative statistics, write the likelihood on each possible stopping branch, and calibrate a one-sided upper confidence limit across all branches. They also derive a practical “mix” rule that reduces sensitivity to detector settings for rescans that never occur.

For a representative two-scan problem at **90% CL**, the calibrated Bayesian-power-measured (BPM) limit is about **20% higher**—weaker—than the likelihood limit; without frequentist calibration it can be about **80% higher**. In an ALPHA Phase-I projection over **10–20 GHz (40–80 μeV)** with rescan thresholds (3\le\beta\le5), uncalibrated BPM and the older geometric construction give coupling limits **30% and 115% higher**, respectively, than the likelihood method. The mix rule worsens the likelihood limit by only about **0.5%** in that benchmark.

These are expected limits under Gaussian noise, specified thresholds and an idealized signal model; they are not measured exclusions on (g_{a\gamma}). Explicit numerical comparisons are shown only for at most two scans, even though the formal construction extends further. Persistent spectral lines, non-Gaussian tails, calibration drift and frequency correlations require experiment-specific validation.

##### History

Early HAYSTAC analyses used a geometric pass/fail protocol calibrated to a discovery benchmark. That approach is simple but discards measured excess information and can confuse conditional false-negative rates with unconditional coverage over an adaptive sequence. The [Bayesian power-measured framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08510) reanalyzed HAYSTAC Phase I, tightened coupling constraints and estimated a **36% scan-rate improvement** over the threshold method, while explicitly adopting Bayesian evidence rather than frequentist confidence coverage.

The new paper shows that a fully specified frequentist likelihood can recover much of that statistical efficiency while preserving nominal coverage, and it quantifies the cost of BPM calibration. Its ALPHA example uses the collaboration's [Phase-I 40–80 μeV design projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10292), so the quoted percentage gains are analysis forecasts, not added hardware sensitivity. The open question is whether the advantage persists under each experiment's real vetoes, line cleaning, rescans and changing noise environment.

#### 1. Three extraordinary AI-generated proof claims enter public verification, at different levels of openness

**OpenAI announcement, 26 October 2026; research claims, not peer-reviewed results.** OpenAI announced AI-generated work that it says proves local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation in all dimensions (d\ge2), gives a deterministic subexponential-time exact algorithm for fixed-width CNF satisfiability that would refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and constructs four-dimensional interacting gauge theories with fermions, including QCD and a proposed ultraviolet completion of the Standard Model. [Announcement](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/).

The released ETH manuscript claims running time

\[
(n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{n/(\log n)^{1/2+o(1)}}
\]

for exactly counting satisfying assignments of fixed-(k) CNF formulas, with a public natural-language manuscript and Lean artifacts. The local-smoothing output and Lean proof are also public. By contrast, the Yang–Mills/QCD artifacts were withheld for a working group led by David Gross, with Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov. None of those names constitutes independent validation of the result.

The independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence [called on 26 October for immediate release](https://agmai.org/) of the Yang–Mills natural-language and Lean outputs, arguing that a completed formalization should be open to equal scrutiny. For the two public claims, Lean checking can establish that encoded theorems follow from encoded assumptions, but experts must still verify that definitions, axioms and formal statements match the advertised problems. For the unreleased physics claim, outsiders cannot yet inspect even that correspondence. These should remain labeled claims until the relevant communities complete open technical review.

#### 2. Continuous mass photometry follows one biomolecule for hours

**Peer-reviewed open-access *Nature Methods* brief communication, published 26 October 2026.** Loewenthal, Asor and colleagues combine supported-lipid-bilayer tethering with interferometric scattering to keep individual biomolecules in view while reading changes in refractive-index contrast as mass. They report **hour-long** trajectories with **millisecond** time resolution and, in favorable conditions, **sub-kilodalton** mass precision. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-026-03252-1).

The advance changes mass photometry from repeated arrival snapshots into a time series for the same molecule, exposing association, dissociation, dwell times and heterogeneous pathways. Demonstrations include extended observation of SARS-CoV-2 spike glycoprotein on a patterned membrane. The measurement still requires surface attachment and optical contrast; tethering, the supported membrane and local environment may alter some kinetics, and sub-kilodalton precision is not uniform for every molecule or time bin. The next test is agreement with solution-phase kinetics across diverse complexes rather than only longer observation time.
