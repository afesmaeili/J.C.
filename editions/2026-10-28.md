#### 1. CONUS+ turns reactor coherent scattering into a 10.2σ precision measurement

**N. Ackermann et al. (CONUS Collaboration) — [arXiv:2610.37318v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37318v1).** Submitted **27 October, 14:32:54 UTC**; preprint, with a [simultaneous detailed analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37345v1), and no journal status stated.

Coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering (CEνNS) tests the weak neutral current at low momentum transfer. CONUS+ uses three low-threshold high-purity germanium detectors 20.7 m from the 3.6-GW thermal core of the Leibstadt reactor. The blind run-2 likelihood fits reactor-on and reactor-off spectra together with muon-, neutron-, radon-, cosmogenic and material-background components. The useful nuclear-recoil thresholds are **140–170 eV electron-equivalent**, and the reactor-on exposure is **1296 kg·days**.

The fit gives

\[
N_{\rm CE\nu NS}=1555\pm116\;({\rm stat})\pm98\;({\rm syst})=1555\pm152,
\]

with a quoted **10.2σ** signal significance. The Standard Model prediction is \(1701\pm198\) events, so the measured-to-predicted ratio is \(0.91\pm0.14\), only **0.6 standard deviations** from expectation. The three detectors independently give 7.2σ, 7.8σ and 2.4σ results. Removing the reactor-off data still gives 3.1σ; adopting the alternative background likelihood reduces the combined significance to 9.3σ.

Interpreting the rate through the weak nuclear charge, \(Q_W=N-(1-4\sin^2\theta_W)Z\), gives

\[
\sin^2\theta_W(Q\simeq10\,\mathrm{MeV})=0.225\pm0.022,
\]

consistent with the low-energy Standard Model value 0.2387. This is a measurement, not a new-physics signal. Its precision is limited by the predicted rate: energy-scale and sub-keV germanium-quenching uncertainties contribute 8.0% and 7.5% to the prediction, while background modeling is the largest systematic on the fitted signal. The weak-angle extraction fixes the Standard Model interaction form apart from \(\sin^2\theta_W\); it is not a model-independent constraint on all possible neutrino interactions.

##### History

CEνNS was predicted by Freedman in [1974](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.9.1389), but the tiny recoil energy delayed observation for four decades. COHERENT first detected it at **6.7σ** in a 14.6-kg CsI[Na] detector at the pulsed Spallation Neutron Source in [2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01294). That established the process at tens-of-MeV neutrino energies and with timing information. Reactor antineutrinos probe lower energy and momentum transfer, where coherence is more complete, but demand much lower recoil thresholds and continuous-background control.

CONUS+ run 1 supplied the first direct reactor observation: [395 ± 106 events in 327 kg·days at 3.7σ](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09322-2), against \(347\pm59\) expected. Run 2 has about four times the exposure, lowers the relative signal-count uncertainty by almost a factor of three, and changes the status from first observation to a rate measurement. The 2026 [COHERENT germanium result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17951) provides a complementary high-momentum-transfer measurement with a different source and background environment. Dresden-II's earlier reactor excess depends on a sub-keV quenching response larger than the Lindhard model and corresponds to a higher rate than CONUS+ and other germanium reactor results, so it is not a directly comparable precision baseline.

The main remaining uncertainty is detector response to sub-keV nuclear recoils and the reactor-flux prediction. Better neutron calibrations and background rejection can turn the large event sample into stronger tests of neutrino magnetic moments, non-standard interactions and light mediators, but those bounds require their own parameter definitions and nuisance treatment.

#### 2. GRANDProto300 reconstructs 51 inclined cosmic-ray candidates from raw radio voltages

**Marion Guelfand, Valentin Decoene, Olivier Martineau, Simon Prunet, Sebastian Castro-Isern and Nathan Lebas, for the GRAND Collaboration — [arXiv:2610.36282v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36282v1).** Submitted **26 October, 21:07:25 UTC**; proceedings contribution to Moriond VHEPU 2026, not a refereed journal result.

GRAND's eventual neutrino program depends on autonomously recognizing very inclined air showers in a sparse radio array. This paper combines a spherical radio-wavefront fit with an angular distribution function (ADF) for the 50–200 MHz amplitude pattern. Simulations of cosmic-ray showers above 60° zenith give better than **0.07° directional resolution** and about **15% primary-energy resolution** when the calibrated electric field is used.

The commissioning application is less mature. Sixty-five of the planned 289 GP300 antennas were deployed in the described configuration. From roughly **18,000 preliminary candidates** collected between July and October 2025, the authors apply a zenith cut below 80°, require all triggered antennas to lie within 3° of the shower axis, and retain events with reduced ADF \(\chi^2<20\). That leaves **51 high-quality cosmic-ray candidates**. One example is reconstructed at \(\theta=75.1^\circ\pm0.03^\circ\), \(\phi=358.7^\circ\pm0.02^\circ\), with a first-pass energy of \(2.08\times10^{18}\) eV and an emission point about 49 km from the shower core.

The 51 events validate a useful reconstruction chain, but they are not yet a measured cosmic-ray spectrum or a neutrino sample. The quoted 0.07° and 15% resolutions are simulation results. The data analysis acts directly on voltage traces before full electric-field reconstruction, assumes a 5-ns timing error and 7.5% amplitude error, and still has unfinished timing, amplitude and energy calibration. The retained rate is far below the roughly 20 events per day expected over about 1500 hours because the run prioritized hardware commissioning and used deliberately restrictive selections. Primary mass is not reconstructed, the horizon region is cut, and no data-derived selection efficiency or residual-background rate is reported.

##### History

Radio calorimetry is already established for ordinary cosmic-ray showers. The Pierre Auger Engineering Radio Array measured the coherent radio energy and obtained [22% energy resolution for its full sample and 17% for a high-quality subset](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.93.122005). GRAND targets a different geometry: huge footprints from nearly horizontal cosmic rays and upward-going showers initiated by Earth-skimming neutrinos. The collaboration's staged design therefore emphasizes autonomous triggering, sub-degree pointing and rejection of anthropogenic radio transients.

GP300's [ICRC 2025 analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06695) used 46 antennas and a multi-stage pipeline to report an initial set of **26** candidates from December 2024 to May 2025. The present work adds 51 candidates from a later data interval and applies the ADF reconstruction directly to raw voltages, giving per-event directions and a provisional energy scale. The underlying inclined-wavefront and ADF performance was developed in earlier simulation work, including [arXiv:2507.04324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04324).

What changes is the demonstration that the reconstruction behaves sensibly on commissioning data: the sky distribution has the expected geomagnetic north–south asymmetry and the energy distribution agrees with an independent preliminary reconstruction. What remains uncertain is the absolute efficiency, purity and energy scale. Until those are measured with calibrated fields and cross-checked against a mature particle detector or a well-validated radio selection, the result supports the detection concept but does not establish GRAND's projected UHE-neutrino sensitivity.

#### 3. Three-flavor transport changes where merger-remnant neutrinos become unstable

**Harry Ho-Yin Ng, Jiabao Liu, Arthur Offermans, Shoichi Yamada, Elias R. Most and Luciano Rezzolla — [arXiv:2610.36486v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36486v1).** Submitted **27 October, 01:43:36 UTC**; preprint, with no journal status stated.

Neutrino absorption sets the electron fraction of neutron-star-merger ejecta and therefore its r-process nucleosynthesis and kilonova opacity. Most merger calculations combine muon and tau neutrinos into one heavy-lepton species. Here a three-dimensional grey-M1 merger simulation is handed off at about **38 ms after merger** to axisymmetric general-relativistic Boltzmann transport of six distinct neutrino and antineutrino species. The calculation includes muons, charged-current muonic reactions, pair processes and multienergy transport, then diagnoses fast flavor instability (FFI) and collisional flavor instability (CFI) through linear stability analysis. Local three-flavor quantum-kinetic-equation calculations test selected points beyond the linear regime.

The calculation finds widespread CFI in all three flavor sectors. Ordinary CFI reaches roughly \(10^8\)–\(10^{10}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), while e–τ fast modes reach about \(10^9\)–\(10^{10}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). Muonic reactions move the e–τ FFI from an extended low-density atmosphere in the electron-only model into denser outer-disk material below about \(10^{11}\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}\). Continued disk demuonization creates a large \(\nu_\mu\) excess and mostly one-signed muon-lepton-number angular distributions, suppressing e–μ and μ–τ fast modes.

At three nearby test points, initial CFI growth rates span \(3.68\times10^6\) to \(6.27\times10^8\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\). Nonlinear coupling activates coherence in a sector that was linearly stable. Including diagonal collision terms changes the asymptotic flavor populations; omitting them drives the calculation too close to flavor equipartition. Although coherence can grow rapidly, relaxation takes **100–1000 μs**, comparable to local dynamical times.

This is a transport and stability calculation, not a kilonova prediction. It remains axisymmetric after the handoff, evolves only an 8-ms remnant interval, uses closure approximations in parts of the stability analysis and evaluates quantum kinetics locally without feedback on global hydrodynamics. Narrow resonance-like layers are not fully resolved. The work therefore demonstrates failure modes of two-flavor subgrid recipes but does not yet quantify the change in ejecta abundance or observable light curves.

##### History

Fast flavor conversion arises when the angular distribution of one flavor's lepton number crosses another; neutrino self-interactions can then amplify coherence on scales far shorter than vacuum oscillations. Earlier merger studies found such crossings with moment transport, including a systematic two-flavor stability framework in [Froustey et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.043046). A 2026 multienergy moment analysis found fast modes over much of a remnant but also regions where only collisional or slow instabilities survive ([Froustey et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/htq2-d1t7)). Separate numerical-relativity work showed that imposed flavor conversion can alter ejecta composition and nucleosynthetic yields ([Qiu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11758)), but that did not derive the conversion self-consistently from six-species Boltzmann transport.

Muon physics is independently known to matter in merger remnants. Earlier equilibrium calculations found net muon fractions **30–70%** of the electron fraction and pressure changes up to **7%** ([Loffredo et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244927)). Recent grey-M1 simulations with muonic rates found more modest bulk-structure changes but altered thermal and compositional evolution ([Gieg et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/qmb8-q3cx)). The new paper connects these classical muon populations to three-flavor quantum stability and shows that μ–τ degeneracy is not harmless: it changes both the location of FFI and the nonlinear endpoint of CFI.

The remaining uncertainty is the feedback loop. Only a self-consistent 3D quantum-transport merger calculation can determine whether the identified instabilities persist, how they saturate in an evolving angular field, and whether they materially change ejecta \(Y_e\), r-process yields, neutrino signals or post-merger dynamics.

#### 4. SPT-3G small-scale lensing sharpens cosmological limits on interacting dark matter

**Cynthia Trendafilova, Fei Ge, Ali Rida Khalife and Christian L. Reichardt — [arXiv:2610.37604v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37604v1).** Submitted **27 October, 17:35:34 UTC**; preprint, with no journal status stated.

Dark matter scattering can shift CMB acoustic peaks and suppress small-scale matter growth. The authors fit three phenomenological extensions of ΛCDM using Planck PR3, ACT DR6 and SPT-3G D1 temperature/polarization spectra; Planck, ACT and SPT-3G lensing; and, where stated, DESI DR2 baryon-acoustic oscillations. Final chains use CLASS-family Boltzmann solvers and Cobaya. All quoted upper bounds are marginalized **95% confidence limits** under flat interaction priors and the paper's cosmological priors.

For elastic dark-matter–baryon scattering,

\[
\sigma(v)=\sigma_{\rm DM-b}v^{n_b},\qquad n_b\in\{-4,-2,0,2\},
\]

the dark-matter mass is fixed at **1 GeV**, all dark matter is assumed to interact with all baryonic components, and the relative bulk velocity is fixed to 30 km s⁻¹ for the negative-power cases. Planck+ACT previously gave \(\sigma_{\rm DM-b}<7.9\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\) for \(n_b=0\). Adding SPT-3G D1 primary spectra and small-scale MUSE lensing yields

\[
\sigma_{\rm DM-b}<4.6\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\quad(n_b=0),
\]

and \(1.4\times10^{-41}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\) for \(n_b=-4\). Adding DESI DR2 tightens the \(n_b=0\) number to \(3.5\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\), partly because DESI prefers a lower matter density than the CMB combination. These are not mass-independent direct-detection limits; smaller interacting fractions or another mass rescale the allowed cross-section.

For dark-matter–dark-radiation scattering, with \(m_{\rm DM}=1\) GeV and a fully interacting component, the CMB combination gives

\[
\Gamma_{0,\rm nadm}<5.2\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{Mpc^{-1}},\qquad \Delta N_{\rm eff}<0.11.
\]

With DESI these become \(2.5\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}\) and 0.17. For annihilation, the deposited-power parameter is constrained to \(p_{\rm ann}<3.7\times10^{-28}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}\,GeV^{-1}}\); it combines the annihilation cross-section, mass and deposition efficiency rather than directly bounding \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\). Its limit changes with the optical-depth prior: omitting low-ℓ polarization gives \(2.7\times10^{-28}\) in one tested combination. The authors flag a greater-than-3σ inconsistency between SPT+ACT and DESI in the dark-radiation model and do not quote that joint fit.

##### History

The CMB has long constrained interactions through drag before recombination and the resulting loss of small-scale power. ACT DR4 set dedicated dark-matter–baryon limits in [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08985); ACT DR6 then broadened the extended-model analysis ([Calabrese et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14454)). The new ingredient is SPT-3G D1's low-noise lensing reconstruction out to \(L=3000\), based on [2019–2020 SPT-3G data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20707). For the same fixed 1-GeV, fully interacting, velocity-independent model, the CMB-only comparison moves from \(7.9\times10^{-26}\) to \(4.6\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\); this is a comparable improvement because the parameter definition and confidence level are held fixed.

For dark-radiation drag, the Planck+ACT combination gives \(\Gamma_{0,\rm nadm}<4.3\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}\), while including SPT-3G yields \(5.2\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}\). The gain comes mainly from small-scale lensing rather than the primary D1 field, which covers only about 4% of the sky. Cosmological large-scale-structure probes can already give numerically stronger bounds for some \(n_b=0,2\) models—such as Lyman-α forest and Milky Way satellite analyses ([Rogers et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10386))—but those sample other redshifts and rely on nonlinear, thermal and astrophysical modeling. They should not be converted into a single improvement factor against the CMB limit.

Remaining uncertainties include fixed dark-matter mass and interacting fraction, nonlinear power modeling, optical-depth priors and dataset tension. The DESI-assisted tightening is especially model- and consistency-dependent: it can arise because an extended model is forced to accommodate discrepant preferred matter densities, not because DESI directly measures dark scattering.

#### 5. A deep network builds an all-sky Fermi-LAT source list, with source confusion as the main caveat

**Saptashwa Bhattacharyya, Giacomo Principe, Dmitry Malyshev, Roberto Ruiz de Austri, Gabrijela Zaharijas, Sascha Caron, Fiorenzo Stoppa and Guðlaugur Johannesson — [arXiv:2610.37287v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37287v1).** Submitted **27 October, 14:11:29 UTC**; preprint, styled as an *Astronomy & Astrophysics* manuscript but with no journal status stated on arXiv.

The standard Fermi-LAT catalogs iteratively fit sources and diffuse emission with likelihood methods. This paper trains a modified U-Net to segment source-like photon patterns, then separate networks refine positions, estimate flux above 1 GeV and assign a probability that each candidate is real. Training uses Monte Carlo skies matched to ten years of LAT exposure, instrument response, source populations and two interstellar-emission models. The pipeline is then run on the same ten-year photon interval used by 4FGL-DR2.

After merging detections separated by less than 0.1°, the pipeline finds more than 5100 candidates; its final classifier retains **3821** in the 1FDL list. Of these, **816** have no match within 0.25° in 4FGL-DR2. At high Galactic latitude, both effective precision and recall exceed **90%** above \(2\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). The network recovers more than **98%** of high-latitude 4FGL-DR2 sources with catalog significance above 20σ, and more than 75% of sources above 10σ over the full sky.

The unmatched count is not a discovery count. Only ten unmatched entries are both outside the Galactic plane and brighter than \(5\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{ph\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). Seven lie on dense gas where diffuse-emission residuals are plausible, and one lies about 0.5° from the bright blazar PKS 1502+106. Two others later appear independently in the 16-year Fermi list. The current network was trained for point sources, performs poorly for close pairs below about 0.1°, misses some bright sources in crowded Galactic regions and depends on how simulations represent diffuse emission. The manuscript also says the source-list repository and Zenodo record are in preparation, so the paper's claim of immediate public availability was not independently reproducible at cutoff.

##### History

The collaboration's likelihood-based [4FGL catalog](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10045) used eight years of 50-MeV–1-TeV data and extensive diffuse modeling; the ten-year DR2 reference list contains **5787 sources** and applies approximately \(TS>25\) for new entries, with quality flags for confusion and diffuse residuals ([Fermi Science Support Center](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/lat/10yr_catalog/)). Machine learning has previously classified known unassociated sources or inferred unresolved populations, but those tasks start from catalog entries or statistical source counts rather than producing a full-sky point-source list directly from photon maps.

A closer precursor is the 2025 neural-ratio analysis of 14 years of high-latitude LAT data ([Eckner et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/p4gf-d2zt)). Above \(3\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\), it recovered more than 98% of 4FGL sources but found no additional source in that restricted bright range. Under an equivalent bright, unflagged, high-latitude selection, 1FDL also recovers about 98%; its change is end-to-end all-sky segmentation plus per-source position, flux and reliability estimates.

The [FL16Y list](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22148), an early 16-year precursor to 5FGL, contains **7220 sources**. Its independent inclusion of two bright 1FDL-only candidates is a useful validation, but does not validate the other 814 unmatched entries. Conventional likelihood refits with alternative diffuse templates, localization checks and multiwavelength associations remain necessary before any 1FDL-only object is treated as an astrophysical gamma-ray source.

#### GRB 261026A develops a tentative blue optical bump while its X-ray afterglow fades

Einstein Probe began an FXT follow-up at **26 October, 06:59:08 UTC**, 49.52 minutes after the SVOM trigger. It found a fading, uncatalogued 0.5–10 keV source at RA **337.3535°**, Dec **+27.0268°**, with a 10-arcsec error radius and flux \((2.80\pm0.18)\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). Its position agrees with the already established optical afterglow. [GCN 46058](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46058).

SVOM/VT's combined light curve then showed a broad feature in its 400–650 nm channel between roughly 10 and 30 ks: amplitude **0.3 ± 0.1 mag**, peaking around **5.3 h** after trigger. The simultaneous 650–1000 nm channel showed no comparable feature, with amplitude below 0.1 mag. The Circular was edited at **28 October, 00:11:52 UTC**; the team explicitly says the physical origin is unclear. [GCN 46063 v2](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46063).

At 19.9 h, MDM/MIRAGE reported an Hs-band source at only about **2.5σ**, \(H_s=20.48\pm0.49\) AB, and a J-band limit \(J>19.83\) AB. [GCN 46064](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46064). The X-ray decay and positional agreement strengthen the afterglow identification; the blue bump and late infrared point remain low-significance photometric features rather than evidence for a particular emission component. No redshift or host association had been reported by the cutoff.

#### 1. A mammogram model forecasts three-year breast-cancer risk, but the clinical operating point is projected

**Peer-reviewed open-access article in *npj Breast Cancer*, published 27 October 2026.** Wei et al. trained BREST-AI on negative screening mammograms from **58,591 women**, within a 65,419-woman development dataset, to predict cancer within three years. In an external UK case–control cohort of 1899 cases and 5697 matched controls, the area under the ROC curve was **0.751 (95% CI 0.738–0.764)**, compared with **0.700 (0.687–0.714)** for Mirai (\(p<0.001\)). [Article and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41523-026-01069-x).

The cross-vendor UK cohort gave AUC 0.719 versus 0.685 (\(p=0.019\)). A much smaller US cohort gave 0.670 versus 0.633, but the difference was not significant (\(p=0.199\)). Projecting the case–control results onto the English screening prevalence of eight cancers per 1000 examinations gives an estimated **8.0% positive predictive value** and relative risk 10.1 at 99% specificity.

Those last figures are modeled prevalence adjustments, not outcomes from a prospective screening program. A case–control design cannot measure calibration, downstream biopsies, interval-cancer reduction or benefit–harm balance under real deployment. The weaker and underpowered US result and the vendor shift also show that transportability remains unresolved. The study supports prospective risk-stratified evaluation; it does not establish that screening intervals should yet be changed.

#### 2. Single-neuron recordings link category-specific amygdala firing to later memory

**Peer-reviewed open-access article in *Nature Neuroscience*, published 27 October 2026.** Xiao et al. recorded intracranial single neurons from patients undergoing monitoring for drug-resistant epilepsy while they encoded and later recognized images. Task 1 included **42 patients** and 1871 well-isolated neurons, including 575 in the amygdala. Task 2 independently included **95 patients**, 5744 neurons and 1651 amygdala cells. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02493-9).

For the preferred visual category of each amygdala cell, firing was higher for images later remembered than forgotten (196 neurons, Wilcoxon \(p=4.3\times10^{-8}\)); no effect appeared for nonpreferred images (208 neurons, \(p=0.44\)). The signal emerged about 350 ms after image onset and persisted to roughly 1850 ms. Successful encoding also increased cross-region spike–field coherence: amygdala spikes locked more strongly to 8–18 Hz hippocampal activity (\(p<0.001\)), with a reciprocal 6–9 Hz effect for hippocampal spikes (\(p=0.002\)). The second task replicated the category-specific subsequent-memory effect, and statistical adjustment found it was not explained by independent valence or arousal ratings.

This is unusually direct human cellular evidence that the amygdala participates in neutral episodic encoding, beyond its familiar role in emotional salience. It remains observational: electrodes were placed for clinical need, participants had epilepsy, and stronger firing could mark a successful network state rather than cause memory formation. The suggested right-amygdala intervention target is therefore a hypothesis for future causal stimulation studies, not a demonstrated therapy.
