#### 1. S106: gamma rays do not uniquely identify a proton accelerator

**“HAWC, Fermi-LAT and Chandra Detections of High Energy Particles in the S106 Star Forming Region” — Rishi Babu, Stephen DiKerby, Tulun Ergin, Shuo Zhang, Mehr Un Nisa and the HAWC Collaboration** (arXiv author listing; the PDF gives the full collaboration roster). [arXiv:2610.41163v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41163v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41163v1). Submitted **30 October 2026, 02:38:48 UTC**; listed **2 November**; primary category **astro-ph.HE**. **Observational analysis plus competing emission models; preprint.**

Can a star-forming region's very-high-energy gamma rays be produced by electrons, or do they require accelerated protons and accompanying neutrinos? The authors combine approximately **2,900 days of HAWC data**, Fermi-LAT observations from **4 August 2008 to 20 July 2024**, and a **45 ks archival Chandra exposure** of Sharpless 106, at an adopted distance of **1.4 kpc**. They fit neighboring sources and Galactic diffuse emission, then model the broadband spectrum with synchrotron radiation, bremsstrahlung, inverse-Compton scattering and, alternatively, pion decay.

The HAWC fit using a Planck dust spatial template gives **TS = 31** and photon index **2.61 ± 0.11 statistical, with systematic uncertainty +0.21/−0.19**. The fitted differential flux at **7 TeV** is **4.8 (+1.1/−0.9 statistical; +2.3/−1.4 systematic) × 10⁻¹⁵ TeV⁻¹ cm⁻² s⁻¹**. These are the reported fit errors, not a separately calibrated confidence interval for source association. The Fermi analysis favors an extended source, with Gaussian radius **0.39° ± 0.07°** and a **200 MeV–1 TeV** photon index **2.18 ± 0.06**.

The important modeling ingredient is S106's own infrared radiation:

\[
u_{\rm IR}(r)=\frac{L_{\rm dust}}{4\pi r^2c}
\simeq60\left(\frac{r}{\mathrm{pc}}\right)^{-2}\mathrm{eV\,cm^{-3}}.
\]

With gas density fixed at **250 cm⁻³**, one electron population can reproduce the broadband spectrum. Its representative fit has **B = 2.38 μG**, electron cutoff **1,753 TeV**, electron energy above 1 GeV **4.11 × 10⁴⁵ erg**, and characteristic inverse-Compton distance **3.55 pc**. These correlated model values are **not independent measurements or confidence limits**. Figure 7 demonstrates spectral compatibility, not a unique identification of the particles.

A hybrid model also works: its magnetic field is about **18.2 μG**, with a proton component carrying about **3.1 × 10⁴⁶ erg above 1 GeV**. The proton cutoff reaches the imposed **1,000 TeV upper boundary**. A boundary-hitting fit is not a measured cutoff, and does not by itself establish a proton PeVatron.

The diffuse-background treatment matters: using the unmasked diffuse template gives a reported **4.8σ post-trial** result, whereas the adopted analysis masks the dense S106 cloud out of that background and fits its contribution separately. The electron-only fit also uses a field below the **10–500 μG** range cited for the core; different emission zones may explain this, but that spatial interpretation is not independently established. Unresolved stellar X-rays can contaminate the nominal diffuse component, and the Chandra snapshot is not contemporaneous with the long gamma-ray integrations. No neutrino detection from S106 is reported.

##### History

**Giardino, Favata and Micela (2004)**, [astro-ph/0406344](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0406344), already analyzed the **3 November 2001 Chandra observation**, identifying **87 X-ray sources** within 4.4 arcmin and examining the central young massive object. The new paper reuses this exposure but masks resolved sources to investigate diffuse emission. Its contribution is the broadband connection to GeV–TeV emission, rather than discovery of S106's X-ray activity. The earlier source census makes unresolved-source contamination a concrete concern.

**Aharonian, Yang and de Oña Wilhelmi (2019)**, [arXiv:1804.02331v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02331v2), argued for hadronic emission around massive clusters using spatially resolved gamma rays and gas maps. Their approximately **1/r cosmic-ray density profiles** supported sustained injection; cooling and the absence of a central inverse-Compton brightening disfavored electrons for those extended systems. Their argument was morphological and source-specific, not a theorem that every star-forming region must be hadronic.

S106 therefore does not overturn the cluster evidence. It illustrates why a compact region with a strong local infrared field needs its own transport and radiation model. The decisive unresolved issue is whether a physically consistent spatial model can reproduce the X-ray, gas and gamma-ray morphologies together. The current spectral degeneracy leaves both electron-dominated and hybrid interpretations open.

#### 2. “Missing mergers”: a long-delay claim driven by one old host

**“Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star Mergers” — Ryan J. Foley.** [arXiv:2610.40782v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40782v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40782v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:10 UTC**; listed **2 November**; primary category **astro-ph.HE**. **Population inference under restrictive assumptions; preprint.**

The delay-time distribution, or DTD, describes the interval between star formation and a binary-neutron-star merger. For gravitational-wave inspiral, the strong dependence of merger time on orbital separation motivates a broad distribution close to **t⁻¹**. GW170817's host, NGC 4993, instead presents a particularly old stellar population.

Foley fits ultraviolet-to-infrared photometry and an integrated VLT/MUSE spectrum with a 20-bin, nonparametric star-formation history. The nominal model uses a Chabrier initial mass function, MIST stellar tracks, MILES spectra and a continuity prior between adjacent star-formation bins, while fitting metallicity, dust and nuisance parameters. It yields a **mass-weighted stellar age of 12.4 (+0.5/−0.7) Gyr, at 90% posterior credibility**. This is a host-population inference, not a direct clock reading for the merging binary.

The population model is

\[
\mathrm{DTD}(t)=A t^\alpha,\qquad
 t_{\min}\leq t\leq13.68\;\mathrm{Gyr}.
\]

It combines the host history with a cosmic star-formation history and two GW-detected BNS events, GW170817 and GW190425. Only the first supplies host-age information. The nominal priors are uniform in **−10 < α < 0** and **0.01 < t_min < 13.45 Gyr**; the maximum age is fixed. Metallicity-dependent binary formation is ignored.

Under the paper's **formation-efficiency normalization treatment**, marginalizing the normalization and slope gives **t_min > 7.5 Gyr at 90% credibility** and **>5.6 Gyr at 95%**, with approximately **0.2 Gyr run-to-run scatter** in each threshold. That scatter is not an additional observational confidence interval. A fixed model with **(t_min, α) = (12.6 Gyr, −1)** has a normalization-marginalized likelihood **40.6 ± 9.4** times that of **(10 Myr, −1)** in the efficiency treatment, or **138 ± 32** in the rate treatment. These are comparisons of specified DTD shapes, not Bayes factors integrated over their shape parameters or probabilities that a formation channel is correct.

A crucial limitation appears in the likelihood itself. In the rate-normalized treatment, the Poisson count, surveyed volume-time and rate prior factor out of the DTD-shape inference. Its remaining dependence is proportional to

\[
\frac{\int_{t_{\min}}^{t_{\rm univ}}
\mathrm{SFH}_{4993}(t)t^\alpha\,dt}
{\int_{t_{\min}}^{t_{\rm univ}}
\psi_{\rm cosmic}(t)t^\alpha\,dt}.
\]

The posterior then accumulates at its upper prior boundary. Changing to the efficiency treatment changes the implied shape prior and produces the quoted finite bounds. Thus the result is driven chiefly by **one host relative to cosmic star formation**, rather than by several independently dated mergers. The stellar fit also leaves abundance-ratio systematics unmodeled, and the paper acknowledges that a mixture of formation channels is more plausible than its single truncated power law. “Missing mergers” is a model-dependent tension, not evidence that LVK overlooked a measured population.

##### History

**Zevin et al. (2022)**, [arXiv:2206.02814v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02814v2), inferred a DTD from **68 short-GRB hosts**, obtaining **α = −1.83 (+0.35/−0.39)** and **t_min = 184 (+67/−79) Myr**, both 90% credible intervals. They marginalized host-history and DTD uncertainties, fixed the binary formation efficiency, and assumed detection selection did not depend on DTD parameters. Their minimum-delay prior covered **5–500 Myr**. Faint hosts, large offsets and afterglow selection were explicit limitations. Those results concern a selected GRB population and cannot be translated directly into a universal BNS bound.

An independent, more recent comparison is **Pracchia and Salafia**, [arXiv:2601.03861v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03861v2), revised **5 August 2026**. Their hierarchical population analysis uses **210 flux-selected Fermi short bursts** and a joint Fermi/Swift sample of **18 bursts, 16 with redshifts**, explicitly modeling detection and redshift-selection cuts. Both empirical luminosity-function and structured-jet treatments favor short delays. Their power-law models give mean-delay medians of **77 Myr** and **105 Myr**, respectively, with broad 90% intervals extending to hundreds of Myr. These are population means, not minimum-delay bounds, and their slope convention has the opposite sign to Foley's.

The new paper identifies a potentially important conflict between an old nearby GW host and selected cosmological GRBs. It does not yet resolve that conflict: host-age modeling, selection, neutron-star–black-hole contamination of the GRB sample, and multiple BNS channels remain alternatives. The new conditional lower bound is numerically much larger than earlier estimates, but calling that an “improvement” would be misleading because the populations, priors and likelihoods differ.

#### 3. Quasar lenses favor collapsed SIDM halos—but not a unique particle model

**“Hints of self-interacting dark matter from strong gravitational lensing” — Gilman, Daniel, Petraki, Kalliopi, Pignard, Thomas, Gurian, James, Kusenko, Alex, Nierenberg, Anna.** [arXiv:2610.40766v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40766v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40766v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40766v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:06 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **astro-ph.CO**. **Lensing inference and particle-model interpretation.** Preprint submitted to Physical Review Letters. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

The paper interprets **29 quadruply imaged quasars** using gravothermal collapse in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). Both long- and short-mean-free-path heat conduction are included: very strong scattering can slow collapse rather than accelerate it indefinitely. The example particle model contains symmetric Dirac dark matter and a light scalar mediator.

The relevant statistic is the viscosity cross section, \(\sigma_V=(3/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,(d\sigma/d\Omega)d\Omega\), averaged over the relevant particle populations, and \(\sigma_{10}=\sigma_V(10\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})/m_\chi\). Two halo prescriptions peak near **1,000 and 200 cm² g⁻¹**; they differ in subhalo abundance, inner slope and tidal enhancement. With **αχ fixed to 10⁻³**, likelihood features favor **mχ ≈ 20–80 GeV** and **mφ below 20 MeV**. These are conditional preferred regions, not standalone confidence bounds.

The **8:1–24:1 Bayes factors** compare specified collapsed-halo scenarios with their CDM reference. They do not independently exclude CDM, and the underlying lensing data are reused from a companion analysis. Cluster-scale scattering penalties and thermal-relic/kinetic-decoupling requirements are imposed inputs. Halo evolution and abundance assumptions remain decisive; the figures demonstrate this dependence rather than a unique particle solution.

##### History

Gravothermal SIDM can first form cores and later dense collapsed centers. The direct observational predecessor is the [companion lensing analysis, 2609.35974v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35974v2), not an independent second data set.

Independent constraints deserve comparison. [Andrade et al., 2012.06611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06611), inferred **σ/m < 0.13 cm² g⁻¹ at 95% confidence** from eight cluster lenses at characteristic relative speed about **1,458 km s⁻¹**, conditional on their cluster-evolution model. [Ando et al., 2503.13650v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13650v1), used eight classical and 23 ultrafaint dwarfs, Jeans modeling and SIDM subhalo evolution. With a log-uniform cross-section normalization prior, their 95th-percentile limits on **σ₀/m** are **0.8 cm² g⁻¹ at fixed turnover speed w = 10 km s⁻¹**, or **0.04 cm² g⁻¹ for velocity-independent scattering**; a high-cross-section mode can reappear for smaller w. Those normalization definitions, velocity dependences and galaxy-formation assumptions differ from σ₁₀ here. Potential dwarf-scale tension remains important, but a universal improvement factor or model-independent exclusion would be unjustified.

#### 4. When a good 21-cm fit is a bad astrophysical model

**“When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation” — Breitman, Daniela.** [arXiv:2610.40770v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40770v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40770v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40770v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:08 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **astro-ph.CO**. **Statistical diagnostic tested on forecasts and galaxy data.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

Breitman tests whether disagreement between subsets of data can reveal missing astrophysics despite an acceptable overall fit. The mock signal includes atomic- and molecular-cooling galaxies; an intentionally incomplete 21cmEMU inference model includes only the former. Bayesian suspiciousness removes an information-volume term from the evidence ratio: \(\log S=\log R-(D_A+D_B-D_{AB})\), with D the posterior-to-prior Kullback–Leibler divergence.

For a **1,080-hour SKA mock**, the incomplete model fits the power spectrum while shifting the logarithmic X-ray luminosity per star-formation rate from injected **40.5 to approximately 41.5**, and the escape-fraction slope from **0 to −0.5**. A representative **log S = −7.45** is more extreme than all **150 calibration mocks**, giving a calibrated lower significance of **2.72σ**; stronger extrapolations depend on distributional assumptions. In another test, suspiciousness flags **48/50** misspecified fits at a 5% threshold, compared with **15/50** for the chosen χ² diagnostic.

Splitting JWST luminosity functions below and above **z = 10** yields roughly **5σ** tension within the restricted galaxy model and Gaussian calibration, falling to **2.1σ** after doubling high-redshift errors. This is not a ΛCDM falsification. Cosmic variance, interlopers, Eddington bias, emulator error and partition selection matter. The relevant setup fixes **σ₈ = 0.8118** and samples astrophysical parameters with stated uniform or informative priors. HERA upper limits offer much weaker consistency discrimination.

##### History

[Handley and Lemos, 1902.04029](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04029), introduced suspiciousness to reduce arbitrary prior-volume dependence in Bayesian consistency tests. It is not a posterior probability that a model is true. Its invariance to prior-width changes assumes that those changes leave the posteriors essentially unchanged. The advance here is controlled calibration for 21-cm model misspecification and a galaxy-data application, not a new statistic. The false-alarm interpretation remains conditional on the simulated model and tested partition; searching many partitions creates an additional selection issue.

#### 5. Hidden-sector sound waves can transfer their energy to visible matter

**“Leaking Sound Waves: Gravitational Waves From Dark Sectors Weakly Coupled to the Standard Model” — Finetti, Marco, Kolešová, Helena, Krichevskiy, Daniil.** [arXiv:2610.40781v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40781v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40781v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40781v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:10 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **gr-qc**. **Phenomenological gravitational-wave prediction.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

The usual acoustic gravitational-wave calculation treats the fluid excited by a first-order transition as effectively isolated. This work couples two ultrarelativistic fluids through an energy-momentum exchange current. For common temperature and sound speed squared **1/3**, the relative velocity decays while the total-fluid mode survives. The assumed interaction time is longer than bubble growth but can be shorter than the acoustic-source lifetime.

The unequal-time gravitational-wave source acquires factors \(F(\tau)=[1+(w_v/w_d)e^{-2\Gamma\Delta\tau}]/[1+w_v/w_d]\), with w the enthalpy density and Γ the interaction rate. For benchmark dark transition strength **0.1**, wall speed **0.9c**, visible/dark enthalpy ratio **10**, and **Γ/H = 100**, the peak power can fall to about **4%** of the isolated-fluid result for acoustic lifetime **0.1 H⁻¹**; a lifetime of **0.01 H⁻¹** gives a ratio around **0.3**. These are model examples, not measured suppression or confidence limits.

The infrared **k³** behavior survives, but the spectral modification is not generally one constant rescaling. Γ remains phenomenological. No full nonlinear two-fluid transition simulation or model-independent portal-coupling bound is supplied; a particle model must also explain why its interactions did not already change bubble growth.

##### History

The [sound-shell model of Hindmarsh and Hijazi, 1909.10040](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10040), derives acoustic spectra from velocity correlations and bubble-scale flow profiles, controlled by transition strength, wall speed and bubble separation. Its limitations from nonlinear evolution and particular flow regimes were already recognized. The new ingredient is post-transition energy exchange between fluids. This modifies the isolated-source assumption, rather than invalidating acoustic production or establishing the same suppression for every hidden-sector transition.

#### 6. DESI BAO tests acceleration without an absolute ruler calibration

**“Model-independent and reconstruction-free evidence for cosmic acceleration from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations” — Herold, Laura, Addison, Graeme E., Riess, Adam G..** [arXiv:2610.40785v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40785v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40785v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40785v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:13 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **astro-ph.CO**. **Kinematic observational tests.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

The authors construct three inequalities that a never-accelerating universe must obey, using **DESI DR2 BAO** and FLRW geometry without calibrating the absolute sound horizon or imposing a dark-energy equation of state. One involves \(S_F=(1+z)\ln(1+z)-F_{\rm AP}\), where positive values violate the corresponding flat-universe non-acceleration inequality. Others compare distances across redshifts, with the BGS measurement as a low-redshift reference.

For anisotropic bins around **z = 0.510, 0.706 and 0.934**, the three constructions give maximum-bin significances **2.39σ, 3.04σ and 4.06σ** after the stated three-bin correction; average-based versions give **2.19σ, 2.85σ and 3.22σ**. These share data and must not be combined as independent discoveries. Scanning **|Ωk| ≤ 1** leaves two tests near **2.4–3.5σ** over the explored cases; that scan is not Bayesian curvature marginalization.

This is kinematic evidence for acceleration under geometric assumptions, not a measurement of its physical cause. FLRW distance relations and a common BAO ruler still enter. Removing an absolute r_d or H₀ calibration does not remove all early-universe, geometry or observational-systematics assumptions.

##### History

[Sutherland's cosmic speed-trap, 1105.3838v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3838v2), already used BAO distance ratios and one-sided inequalities to test acceleration with limited dependence on gravity and the absolute ruler scale. The present advance is the DESI DR2 implementation, complementary statistics and explicit redshift-bin and curvature checks. Its correlated tests strengthen the assessment of one physical question; they should not be advertised as three independent confirmations or the first model-light BAO acceleration test.

#### 7. MADGRID: a plasma haloscope aimed at meV dark matter

**“Layered Wire-Grid Haloscope for Millielectronvolt Axions and Dark Photons” — Caputo, Andrea.** [arXiv:2610.40793v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40793v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40793v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40793v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:00:25 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **hep-ph**. **Instrument concept and sensitivity forecast.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

MADGRID proposes stacked copper-wire grids that convert axions or dark photons into electromagnetic power, combining a dispersive wire-medium response with discrete Maxwell solutions and extraction through the stack's end face. Five cartridges with approximately **8–15 grids** span **0.25–8 meV**. The projections assume **0.1 m²** aperture, **10 T** for axions, **0.1 K**, local dark-matter density **0.4 GeV cm⁻³**, and the candidate comprising all local dark matter.

For **one live year per octave** and local **S/N = 5**, an illustrative **1 meV** threshold is **g_aγ ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻¹³ GeV⁻¹**, or dark-photon mixing **χ ≈ 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁶** with random-polarization factor 1/3. These are forecasts, not 95% confidence exclusions. The kinetic-inductance option assumes **NEP = 6 × 10⁻¹⁹ W Hz⁻¹/²**. A more ambitious photon-counter option assumes **50% efficiency**, **1 mHz dark counts per channel**, and 100 or 400 channels; it forecasts KSVZ coverage over roughly **0.8–3.9 meV**, and DFSZ over **0.8–1.2 meV**.

The combined magnet, detector, terahertz material response and cryogenic performance have not been demonstrated here. Coupling losses and adopted aperture efficiency around **0.65** directly affect reach. This is a technically specified proposal, not an operating dark-matter search.

##### History

[Lawson et al., 1904.11872v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11872v2), established tunable wire-plasma haloscopes, with illustrative coverage at **35–400 μeV**. MADGRID develops a layered finite geometry and a higher-mass reach. Independently, [CAST, 1705.02290v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02290v2), measured **g_aγ < 6.6 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻¹ at 95% confidence for m_a ≲ 0.02 eV** using solar axions. CAST does not require axions to make up Galactic dark matter, whereas this forecast does. Their numerical difference is therefore neither an achieved improvement nor an assumption-matched comparison. QCD-axion lines are theoretical benchmarks, not observed confidence regions.

#### 8. Automating coherent pion production for neutrino generators

**“Phenomenological Chiral Effective Field Theory for Neutrino Event Generators” — Graham-Jones, Zara, Hoppe, Mareen, Isaacson, Joshua, Li, Shirley Weishi, Toman, Miša.** [arXiv:2610.40866v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40866v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40866v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40866v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 18:41:31 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **hep-ph**. **Interaction-model and software development.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

This work exports chiral-theory amplitudes through **FeynRules/UFO to Sherpa/Comix** and connects them to **Achilles**, combining coherent Δ and nonresonant Born contributions with spectral-function initial states and nuclear final-state interactions. It addresses interaction modeling needed by oscillation experiments, but is a tree-level phenomenological implementation rather than a complete EFT truncation-error calculation.

Resonance conventions matter strongly. With a constant Δ pole width, free-nucleon cross sections for **W < 1.4 GeV** overshoot reference data by about **80%, 50% and 15%** in the three displayed charge channels. A running width proportional to \((k/k_0)^3m_\Delta/W\), together with a revised Breit–Wigner parametrization, improves agreement. The illustrated nuclear excesses change from about **50% to 5–10% for T2K**, and **60–70% to 10–20% for MicroBooNE**.

Those improved running-width results use reweighting: they are not yet a complete implemented generator feature. The axial normalization changes from **C₅ᴬ(0) = 1.27** to **1.18** in the revised treatment. Unitarization, higher resonances, two-body and medium effects remain unresolved. Better agreement in these samples does not establish full phase-space validity or a measured reduction in oscillation-parameter bias.

##### History

[Hernández, Nieves and Valverde, hep-ph/0701149v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0701149v2), already included coherent Δ-pole and chiral-background amplitudes and showed their impact on extracting the axial transition form factor from ANL data. Interference is established physics, not the novelty. The advance is the automated amplitude-to-nuclear-generator workflow and demonstrations of sensitivity to width conventions. C₅ᴬ values must be compared within their fitted amplitude and resonance prescriptions, rather than as independently measured constants.

#### 9. How orbiting stars erode superradiant boson clouds

**“Stars Can Quench Superradiance Around Supermassive Black Holes” — Cheng, Yu, Caputo, Andrea, Tomaselli, Giovanni Maria, Witte, Samuel J..** [arXiv:2610.40895v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40895v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40895v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40895v1). Submitted **29 October 2026, 19:17:01 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **hep-ph**. **Theoretical environment calculation.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

The authors calculate stellar perturbations that transfer bosons from gravitationally bound cloud states into continuum states. They combine hydrogenic wavefunctions, relativistic growth rates and orbital averages, with evolution \(\dot M_c/M_c=\tau_{\rm sr}^{-1}-\tau_{\rm loss}^{-1}\). A Sgr A*-like benchmark fixes **M = 4 × 10⁶ M⊙** and spin **0.9**; the latter is not an observed spin determination.

Gravitational encounters generally do not quench cloud growth in the studied stellar populations. For **α = GMμ = 0.45** in natural units, an illustrative 433 state has loss/growth-time ratio about **2**, halving net growth; a 322 case has a ratio near **8**, slowing growth by roughly **13%**. ##### History

MAGIKS combines simulated gravitational-wave maps and source information with irregular Rubin-like optical light curves. ##### History

The adapted Fink benchmark does not by itself establish superiority to the live broker under its own observing conditions or selection objectives.

#### 23. IndIGO dark-force precision is a conditional forecast, not a new exclusion

**“Probing dark sector with decihertz gravitational wave observations of compact binary inspirals in IndIGO-D” — Tahelyani, Divya, Sharma, Abhishek, Sengupta, Anand S., Bhattacharyya, Arpan.** [arXiv:2610.41473v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41473v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41473v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41473v1). Submitted **30 October 2026, 09:24:16 UTC**; UTC listing **2 November 2026**; primary category **gr-qc**. **Proposed-detector Bayesian forecast.** Preprint; no journal reference was shown on the checked version record. Added retrospectively **11 November 2026**.

The model gives neutron stars effective dark charges, parametrized by \(\alpha=(\hbar c/G)(q_1/m_1)(q_2/m_2)\), \(\gamma=(\hbar c/G)(q_1/m_1-q_2/m_2)^2\), and \(\mu=G(m_1+m_2)m_A/(\hbar c)\). A perturbative, long-range inspiral waveform includes dark-force and radiation effects, matched across mediator-emission thresholds. The proposed heliocentric decihertz IndIGO detector is assumed to observe a GW170817-like binary for **three months over 0.364–10 Hz**, at **40.4 Mpc** and **S/N ≈ 77.9**.

For the nonzero injection **α = 0.035, γ = 4.1 × 10⁻⁴, μ = 5 × 10⁻⁵**, the reported mediator energy is **m_Ac² = [2.42 (+0.0000208/−0.0000209)] × 10⁻¹⁵ eV**, at **90% credibility**. That strikingly small interval is local precision around an injected signal, not an observed mass or an upper limit. The uniform priors are narrowly centered on the injection: approximately **α ± 1.5 × 10⁻⁴**, **γ ± 5 × 10⁻⁷**, and **μ ± 1.5 × 10⁻⁸**. Distance, sky position and spins are sampled, but this is not a broad global discovery search.

For a GR injection the mediator mass is unconstrained, and weak-coupling examples also lose identifiability. Threshold modeling, the perturbative waveform domain and astrophysical formation of sufficiently charged stars require validation. Abrupt emission-threshold approximations could contain more apparent information than a refined physical transition.

##### History

[Owen et al., 2503.04916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04916), developed the related waveform treatment and confronted ground-based neutron-star-containing events. Its restrictions on mediator parameters include waveform-validity conditions, which must not be reclassified as observational exclusions. The present paper extrapolates to a proposed lower-frequency detector and a nonzero injected signal; its local precision cannot be divided by a ground-based upper bound to claim an experimental improvement.

[Kopp et al., 1807.02527](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02527), independently examined how neutron stars could acquire dark charges and showed that ordinary halo capture can make effects extremely small, while more exotic formation scenarios allow larger ones. The benchmark charge is therefore a physical assumption to justify, not a measured or automatically viable population property.

#### 24. Galaxy dipoles probe dark-sector forces—but coupling degeneracies dominate

**“Tests of equivalence principle violation in doubly coupled dark energy” — Kegel, Yannick, Zheng, Ziyang, Amendola, Luca.** [arXiv:2610.42056v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42056v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.42056v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42056v1). The model combines a universal scalar–gravity coupling with a distinct dark-matter coupling, \(f_1=e^{-2Q\phi}\) and \(f_2=e^{2Q_{\rm dm}\phi}\), defining \(\beta=-\sqrt6 Q/2\) and \(\beta_{\rm dm}=-\sqrt6 Q_{\rm dm}/2\). Dark-sector nonuniversality can violate the equivalence principle; the universal coupling alone does not. The proposed observable uses the relativistic imaginary cross-spectrum of bright and faint galaxies to compare their motion and gravitational redshift.

An **SKA2 forecast over z = 0.2–0.8** uses six redshift bins, a freely sampled matter spectrum in **12 bins over k = 0.01–0.12 h Mpc⁻¹**, and fitted bias/damping terms. It assumes a **1% Gaussian distance prior**, fixed evolution bias and accurately known magnification effects. At fiducial **βdm = 0.2, β = 0.1**, allowing both couplings gives marginalized **1σ uncertainties σ(βdm) ≈ 0.26 and σ(β) ≈ 0.43**. In the simpler dark-coupling-only model with the universal coupling fixed to zero, **σ(βdm) ≈ 0.044**, or **22%** relative precision.

The large degradation is the scientific point: a precise projected observable need not yield a precise unique coupling. These are Fisher forecasts around specified models, not new experimental bounds. The chosen coupling benchmark can conflict with constraints on a coupling assumed constant throughout cosmic history; the paper's late-time setting and growth/quasistatic approximations must be kept explicit.

##### History

[Castello et al., 2412.08627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08627), already introduced a multitracer, model-light equivalence-principle statistic and forecast redshift-dependent SKA2 precision around **7–25%**. The new work projects such information onto a doubly coupled dark-energy model, exposing parameter degeneracy. It does not invent the relativistic-dipole test or supersede laboratory and cosmological equivalence-principle limits that constrain different species, scales and time dependences.

**“Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks” — Vaccaro, Maria Paola, Trinca, Alessandro, Mapelli, Michela, Boco, Lumen, Schneider, Raffaella, Valiante, Rosa, Wylezalek, Dominika.** [arXiv:2610.40787v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40787v1) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40787v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40787v1). ARISE connects FastClusterAGN calculations of capture, migration, gas hardening, encounters and hierarchical mergers to evolving AGN abundances and supermassive-black-hole populations. Disk examples span central masses **10⁵–10⁹ M⊙**, Eddington ratios **10⁻³–10**, and viscosity parameters **α = 0.01 or 0.1**, with alternative disk/cluster and high-redshift population prescriptions.

#### Chemical imaging of living cells reaches 19.2 three-dimensional volumes per second

**Masato Fukushima, Keiichiro Toda, Yusei Sugawara, Shotaro Kawano and Takuro Ideguchi**, “Video-rate volumetric chemical imaging via mid-infrared photothermal optical diffraction tomography,” published **30 October 2026** in *Nature Communications*, **17, 11075**. [Research article and full text](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78699-z); [dated university announcement](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/press/z0508_00481.html).

Mid-infrared absorption heats molecular bonds slightly; visible-light measurements detect the resulting refractive-index change. Rapid scanning over **11 illumination angles** and differential tomographic reconstruction produce label-free chemical volumes at **19.2 volumes s⁻¹**, about **52 ms per volume**, without temporal averaging. A separate multi-wavenumber mode covers **300 cm⁻¹ in 1 s**—the fastest volumetric rate should not be confused with a complete spectrum at every time point.

The biological demonstration tracks **50 lipid-droplet trajectories** and distinguishes motion that two-dimensional projection obscures. This is a peer-reviewed imaging-method result, not a disease-treatment result. The live-cell experiment used **three cells from one dish**; larger replication applies to other imaging tests. Demonstrated specimens are only several to about ten micrometers thick. Water absorption, scattering and the weak-scattering reconstruction assumption limit extension to deep tissue. The advance is fast three-dimensional chemical dynamics at the single-cell scale; broad biological generalization remains to be established.
