#### Can baryon-carrying dark-sector particles make cosmic antinuclei observable?

**Paper:** *Cosmic-Ray Signals from Dark Matter Annihilation into Scalars with Baryon Number* — **Mattia Di Mauro, Caleb Gemmell, Dan Hooper and Fabrizio Vassallo**. [arXiv:2611.03785v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03785v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03785v1). Submitted **2 November 2026 at 19:00:01 UTC**, one second after the relevant cutoff; first listed **4 November**. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. **Classification: phenomenological dark-matter model, antiproton constraint and conditional antinucleus forecast; no antinucleus detection.**

**Question and method.** Standard hadronic dark-matter annihilation has difficulty making heavy antinuclei without overproducing antiprotons. The authors study self-conjugate dark matter \(\chi\) annihilating into color-singlet scalars \(\phi\) with baryon and lepton numbers \(B=L=3\). Each scalar then decays schematically to three copies of \(udd\nu_e\), distributing energy among many quarks and placing more antinucleons close enough in phase space to coalesce.

Pythia 8.309 generates the shower and hadronization. Antideuterons and antihelium-3 form when all constituent pairs satisfy calibrated momentum and spatial cuts: \(p_{\rm coal}=0.219\) GeV and \(d_{\rm coal}=3\) fm for antideuterons, and \(0.206\) GeV and 3 fm for antihelium-3. The spectra are propagated through a stationary two-zone Galactic model with a 4.2-kpc halo, NFW density profile normalized to \(\rho_\odot=0.4\ {
m GeV\,cm^{-3}}\), convection at **13 km s⁻¹**, and force-field solar potential **0.736 GV**. Continuous energy losses, reacceleration and charge-sign-dependent heliospheric transport are omitted.

At each dark-matter mass \(m_\chi\) and mediator mass \(m_\phi\), the authors derive a **one-sided 95% confidence-level upper limit** on the velocity-averaged annihilation cross section \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\) from the seven-year AMS-02 antiproton spectrum. They profile the secondary-antiproton normalization, a spectral-index shift limited to ±0.1, and the Fisk potential. The bound spans approximately **1.5 × 10⁻²⁹ to 6.5 × 10⁻²⁵ cm³ s⁻¹** over the scanned plane; the familiar **3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³ s⁻¹** thermal reference is excluded for roughly \(m_\phi\lesssim20\) GeV in this setup. This is a model- and propagation-specific cross-section bound, not a generic limit on dark matter.

**Quantitative forecast.** Saturating that antiproton upper limit, broad parts of the plane yield at least one expected antideuteron event at AMS-02, and a region around \(m_\chi,m_\phi\sim50\)–100 GeV also reaches GAPS. A benchmark with \((m_\chi,m_\phi)=(66,53)\) GeV and \(\langle\sigma v\rangle=6.3\times10^{-26}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\) predicts **9.9 AMS-02 antideuterons** and **1.2 GAPS antideuterons**. By contrast, antihelium-3 remains difficult: the favorable \((43,5)\)-GeV benchmark, using \(9.8\times10^{-28}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\), gives **3.2 antideuterons and 0.5 antihelium-3 events** at AMS-02, a ratio **0.16**. Those event numbers are forecasts under assumed future acceptances, not measured excesses or confidence limits.

The same cascade fits the *shape* of the Galactic-centre gamma-ray excess near \((m_\chi,m_\phi)=(35,27)\) GeV, but requires \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\simeq1.14\times10^{-25}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\), above the paper's antiproton limit. Even a larger allowed inner-Galaxy J-factor only reduces it to about \(5.6\times10^{-26}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\). The authors therefore disfavor a simultaneous explanation under their reference halo and transport assumptions.

**Limitations.** The mediator decay is sampled with isotropic massless phase space rather than a specified ultraviolet completion. Coalescence universality, antiproton production, Galactic transport and solar modulation dominate the systematics; the plotted ±20% coalescence band does not encompass all of them. Most importantly, AMS-02 has not published a conclusive antideuteron or antihelium flux, and the analysis uses a privately communicated antihelium acceptance estimate. The result identifies testable parameter regions; it does not explain established antinucleus events.

##### History

The production mechanism was introduced by **Gemmell, Hooper, Koren and Vassallo** in [*Dark Matter, Baryon Number, and Cosmic-Ray Antinuclei*, PRL 137, 171802 (2026), arXiv:2603.12314v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12314v2). That paper used event-level coalescence to show that a \(B=L=3\) mediator could enhance antideuteron and antihelium multiplicities by factors of roughly 10–100 relative to direct \(b\bar b\) annihilation. The new work's substantive step is to propagate those particles, constrain the accompanying antiprotons with AMS-02, fold detector acceptances and test gamma rays. It consequently narrows the earlier qualitative claim: antideuterons can be observable in viable regions, while antihelium is only marginal under the more conservative sensitivity prescription.

The experimental baseline is still a null result. BESS-Polar II's published search gives \(\Phi_{\bar d}<6.7\times10^{-5}\ ({\rm m^2\,s\,sr\,GeV/n})^{-1}\) over **0.163–1.100 GeV/n**; AMS-02 candidate reports have not become a published flux measurement. Conventional WIMP studies, such as [Cholis, Linden and Hooper, *PRD* 102, 103019 (2020), arXiv:2001.08749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08749), already found that antideuterons may approach experimental reach while antihelium generally does not. The baryon-carrying cascade changes that hierarchy, especially the antihelium-to-antideuteron ratio, but uncertainties in formation and acceptance remain large enough that confirmation requires published events, backgrounds and efficiencies.

#### The years before core collapse may beat the supernova as a MeV-particle laboratory

**Paper:** *Presupernovae Strongly Constrain Electrophilic MeV Bosons* — **Yu Cheng, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo, Chui-Fan Kong and Edoardo Vitagliano**. [arXiv:2611.03813v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03813v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03813v1). Submitted **2 November 2026 at 19:00:16 UTC** and first listed **4 November**. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. **Classification: model-dependent astrophysical exclusion from an existing gamma-ray dataset.**

**Parameter and physical question.** A dark photon \(A'\) kinetically mixed with the photon has dimensionless coupling \(\epsilon\) and, above \(2m_e\), can decay to \(e^+e^-\). Supernova explosions are standard factories for MeV particles, but their dense plasma has a frequency of order **10 MeV**, suppressing in-medium mixing for lower-mass dark photons. Massive stars during carbon, oxygen and silicon burning are cooler but remain hot for years, with plasma frequencies only **30–70 keV**. The paper asks whether their much longer exposure produces more escaping particles than the explosion.

**Method.** MESA r26.4.1 evolves solar-metallicity stars of **9–60 solar masses** to the onset of core infall. For a representative 20-solar-mass star, the core advances from approximately **80 keV** during carbon burning, through **190 keV** in oxygen burning, to **320 keV** in the final silicon-burning days. The authors calculate in-medium dark-photon production, decay and escape throughout these profiles, then average the positron yield over a Salpeter initial-mass function. A Galactic core-collapse rate of **2.3 per century** and a spiral-arm source distribution are convolved with an intentionally broad **1-kpc** positron-smearing scale.

The predicted disk-like 511-keV intensity is compared with INTEGRAL/SPI longitude bins at \(|l|<20^\circ\). The one-sided statistic counts only model flux above the data and imposes \(\chi^2\le4\), a **2σ** construction without fitting or subtracting the bulge. It limits the number of locally annihilating positrons to **2.0 × 10⁵² per progenitor** for the 1-kpc benchmark.

**Result.** For dark-photon masses approximately **1–3 MeV**, the excluded lower-coupling boundary reaches \(\epsilon\sim10^{-14}\), up to **one to two orders of magnitude stronger** than the paper's supernova comparisons. This improvement is genuinely comparable because it constrains the same kinetic-mixing parameter and mass interval, using presupernova rather than explosion emission. The authors also calculate an analogous electron-coupled axion-like-particle limit near \(g_{ae}\sim10^{-14}\). At larger couplings, decay inside the star, trapping and energy loss comparable to the neutrino luminosity delimit the calculation; above the \(L_{A'}=L_\nu\) curve the unperturbed stellar tracks cannot support a reliable exclusion.

**Limitations.** The bound assumes that massive-star evolution, the Galactic core-collapse distribution, positron escape and propagation, and the SPI disk profile are described well enough by the adopted models. Varying the source distribution has little effect in the authors' tests, while changing the smearing length from 1 kpc to 100 pc produces a visible but modest shift. Dense circumstellar material weakens the 3-MeV bound by about **3%** for the stated benchmark and less below 2.5 MeV. The upper boundary is deliberately conservative because sufficiently strong dark-photon cooling would modify the progenitor itself. The calculation is therefore an exclusion within a specified stellar and Galactic model, not a laboratory bound.

##### History

The observable builds on [Calore et al., *PRD* 105, 063026 (2022), arXiv:2112.08382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08382), which used the same Galactic 511-keV line to constrain positrons from **core-collapse supernova** production of sterile neutrinos and dark photons. That work demonstrated that the line could exceed the reach of the SN 1987A energy-loss argument for particles that escape and decay. The new paper moves the production epoch before collapse and gains exposure time plus weaker plasma screening; this is why its improvement applies specifically around 1–3 MeV rather than uniformly across mass and coupling.

Presupernova dark-photon emission was also calculated by [Sieverding, Rrapaj, Guo and Qian, *ApJ* 912, 13 (2021), arXiv:2101.08672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08672). They evolved massive stars with extra cooling and proposed changes to the presupernova neutrino signal; for a 15-solar-mass star at 500 pc, the illustrative sensitivity was around \(\epsilon\lesssim10^{-12}\) and depended on a nearby, identified progenitor. The present diffuse method integrates the entire Galactic population and counts decay positrons, reaching smaller coupling without waiting for a nearby star. Remaining uncertainty lies in the nonperturbative region: both studies indicate that new cooling can alter advanced burning, so standard stellar profiles cannot be extrapolated through the upper-coupling edge without self-consistent evolution.

#### Extended GeV emission appears around the evolved remnant G156.2+05.7

**Paper:** *Fermi-LAT Detection of Extended GeV Emission toward the Middle-aged Supernova Remnant G156.2+05.7* — **Wei-Liang Xu, Qing-Wen Tang and Long Li**. [arXiv:2611.04227v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04227v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04227v1). Submitted **3 November 2026 at 03:06:07 UTC** and first listed **4 November**. Accepted by **Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society** on 2 November; arXiv v1. **Classification: observational gamma-ray detection plus conditional one-zone emission modelling.**

**Data and detection.** The analysis uses **17 years** of Fermi-LAT Pass 8 Source-class data from 4 August 2008 to 3 August 2025 over **100 MeV–1 TeV**, with standard Galactic and isotropic diffuse models and 4FGL-DR4 sources. Replacing two catalogue point sources with an extended template gives extension test statistics \({\rm TS}_{\rm ext}=74.9\) for a uniform disk and **75.7** for a two-dimensional Gaussian. The adopted Gaussian has \(R_{68}=0.84^\circ\pm0.09^\circ\); at the reference distance **2.7 kpc**, this is a physical radius near **40 pc**. The Gaussian and disk are effectively tied under the Akaike criterion, while a foreground CO template fits worse.

The integrated **100 MeV–1 TeV** photon flux is **1.41 × 10⁻⁸ ph cm⁻² s⁻¹**. A log parabola is preferred to a power law by \({\rm TS}=11.429\), nominally **3.4σ for one extra parameter**, with spectral parameters \(\Gamma=2.101\pm0.089\) and \(\beta=0.187\pm0.081\). This is evidence for curvature, not a separate component detection. A 17-bin annual light curve has \({\rm TS}_{\rm var}=16.1\), below the paper's 99%-confidence variability threshold **34.0**.

**Emission modelling.** Radio points and the LAT spectrum are fitted jointly with NAIMA and MCMC. The baseline fixes \(B=14\ \mu{\rm G}\), \(d=2.7\) kpc and proton cutoff \(E_{p,{\rm cut}}=1\) TeV; that cutoff is not constrained by the LAT data. The mixed leptonic–hadronic model has **BIC 38.62**, versus **68.84** for a single-electron leptonic model and **45.40** for a proton-dominated gamma-ray model with separate synchrotron electrons. Thus a hadronic contribution is strongly preferred to the deliberately minimal one-population leptonic fit, but the **ΔBIC = 6.8** separation from the proton-dominated alternative is not decisive. The baseline finds electron cutoff about **1.18 TeV** and steep proton index about **3.23**.

The inferred proton energy above 1.22 GeV is
\[
W_p\simeq1.4\times10^{50}\left(\frac{n}{1\ {\rm cm}^{-3}}\right)^{-1}
\left(\frac{d}{2.7\ {\rm kpc}}\right)^2\ {\rm erg}.
\]
At the Suzaku-scaled preshock density \(n=0.036\ {\rm cm}^{-3}\), the one-zone requirement is roughly **3.7–4.0 times** a canonical \(10^{51}\)-erg supernova energy across the tested magnetic fields. Denser clumps, a multiphase medium or re-acceleration of existing cosmic rays are therefore needed if the hadronic interpretation is correct. This is an energy-budget argument, not a measured gas density.

**Limitations.** The result depends on the diffuse foreground, spatial template and assumed distance. The northeast and southwest quadrants are brighter, but their spectral difference is only about **1.6σ**; association with Hα filaments is suggestive. The model fixes the proton cutoff, magnetic field and one-zone geometry and uses broad priors, while target density and proton normalization are degenerate. The LAT detection of extended GeV emission is robust within the performed template tests; its division between inverse Compton and pion decay is not.

##### History

G156.2+05.7 was discovered as a large X-ray shell with unusually faint radio emission. [Uchida et al., *PASJ* 64, 61 (2012), arXiv:1205.4188](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4188) used six Suzaku pointings to separate shocked interstellar gas and metal-rich ejecta. Once a background galaxy cluster was removed, the hard emission was consistent with the cosmic X-ray background: there was **no robust nonthermal X-ray component**. The present electron cutoff is therefore merely compatible with that null result, not measured by the X-rays.

The distance and age baseline changed with [Katsuda et al., *ApJ* 826, 108 (2016), arXiv:1605.07271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07271). Comparing Hα filaments over about 12 years gave a **1σ proper-motion upper limit of 0.06 arcsec yr⁻¹**. Combined with an approximately 500-km-s⁻¹ shock, that implied **distance >1.7 kpc** and an age of at least a few \(10^4\) years, disfavoring an association with nearby 0.2–0.3-kpc clouds. The new paper adopts 2.7 kpc rather than measuring distance anew. Its actual advance is the first detailed extended GeV characterization and conditional particle budget. Whether it reveals freshly accelerated hadrons, re-accelerated Galactic cosmic rays or mixed electron/proton emission remains open.

#### EP261103a: an X-ray transient with a faint optical candidate

Einstein Probe's WXT triggered on **EP261103a** at **3 November 2026, 20:18:26 UTC**. The initial position was **RA 81.849°, Dec −25.330°**, with a **3-arcmin radius at 90% confidence**, including statistical and systematic uncertainty. Automatic FXT follow-up found an uncatalogued X-ray source at **RA 81.8516°, Dec −25.3269°**, narrowing the 90%-confidence radius to **20 arcsec**. The circular supplies no spectrum, flux, duration or false-alarm rate, so the physical class is still unknown. [GCN 46142](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46142).

Nine 120-s NOT/ALFOSC r-band exposures subsequently found an uncatalogued source inside that FXT circle at **RA 05:27:24.31, Dec −25:19:30.74**, positional uncertainty about **0.5 arcsec**, with **r = 23.86 ± 0.25 AB mag** before Galactic-extinction correction. It is absent from a deeper Legacy Survey r-band image. This makes it a plausible transient counterpart, but no fading, spectrum or chance-coincidence probability has yet been reported; one-epoch detection does not confirm the association. [GCN 46143](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46143).

#### A stem-cell model connects early trophectoderm to a three-dimensional human chorion

**Miaoci Zhang and colleagues**, led by **Mijo Simunovic**, published [*Reconstructing the emergence of the human chorion via Hippo-mediated trophoblast induction*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02082-9) in **Nature Cell Biology on 3 November 2026**. The peer-reviewed work is classified by the journal as a **Technical Report**.

The human chorion is an early fetal membrane and structural precursor of the placenta. Starting with transiently reset human embryonic stem cells, the researchers inhibited LATS kinases to activate the Hippo–YAP route toward trophectoderm without adding external morphogens. Time-resolved bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing mapped an ordered trajectory against integrated embryo and first-trimester chorion references. Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing found an initially hypomethylated state followed by remethylation, while the tested placenta-associated imprinting regions largely retained methylation and allelic bias.

Agitating cell aggregates in suspension produced three-dimensional structures containing trophoblast and stromal-like compartments, outward-facing trophoblast polarity and an inside-out topology relative to the in-vivo chorion. Endogenous WNT signalling was unnecessary for the initial trophectoderm induction but changed the later balance of chorionic lineages. Sorting **CLDN6-positive, ITGA6-negative** cells isolated a proposed intermediate with villous and extravillous trophoblast potential.

This is a model of developmental transitions, not a synthetic placenta or an embryo capable of implantation. Its identities are inferred through transcriptomic projection, markers, epigenetic measurements and in-vitro lineage potential; the topology differs from native tissue, and maternal interactions, vasculature and full placental function are absent. Its value is experimental access to a poorly observable human developmental interval, with the usual limits of stem-cell models. [Article, methods, extended data and deposited datasets](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-026-02082-9).
