#### Can baryon-carrying dark-sector particles make cosmic antinuclei observable?

**Paper:** *Cosmic-Ray Signals from Dark Matter Annihilation into Scalars with Baryon Number* — **Mattia Di Mauro, Caleb Gemmell, Dan Hooper and Fabrizio Vassallo**. [arXiv:2611.03785v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03785v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03785v1). Submitted **2 November 2026 at 19:00:01 UTC**, one second after the relevant cutoff; first listed **4 November**. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. **Classification: phenomenological dark-matter model, antiproton constraint and conditional antinucleus forecast; no antinucleus detection.**

**Question and method.** Standard hadronic dark-matter annihilation has difficulty making heavy antinuclei without overproducing antiprotons. The authors study self-conjugate dark matter \(\chi\) annihilating into color-singlet scalars \(\phi\) with baryon and lepton numbers \(B=L=3\). Each scalar then decays schematically to three copies of \(udd\nu_e\), distributing energy among many quarks and placing more antinucleons close enough in phase space to coalesce.

Pythia 8.309 generates the shower and hadronization. Antideuterons and antihelium-3 form when all constituent pairs satisfy calibrated momentum and spatial cuts: \(p_{\rm coal}=0.219\) GeV and \(d_{\rm coal}=3\) fm for antideuterons, and \(0.206\) GeV and 3 fm for antihelium-3. The spectra are propagated through a stationary two-zone Galactic model with a 4.2-kpc halo, NFW density profile normalized to \(\rho_\odot=0.4\ {\rm GeV\,cm^{-3}}\), convection at **13 km s⁻¹**, and force-field solar potential **0.736 GV**. Continuous energy losses, reacceleration and charge-sign-dependent heliospheric transport are omitted.

At each dark-matter mass \(m_\chi\) and mediator mass \(m_\phi\), the authors derive a **one-sided 95% confidence-level upper limit** on the velocity-averaged annihilation cross section \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\) from the seven-year AMS-02 antiproton spectrum. They profile the secondary-antiproton normalization, a spectral-index shift limited to ±0.1, and the Fisk potential. The bound spans approximately **1.5 × 10⁻²⁹ to 6.5 × 10⁻²⁵ cm³ s⁻¹** over the scanned plane; the familiar **3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³ s⁻¹** thermal reference is excluded for roughly \(m_\phi\lesssim20\) GeV in this setup. This is a model- and propagation-specific cross-section bound, not a generic limit on dark matter.

**Quantitative forecast.** Saturating that antiproton upper limit, broad parts of the plane yield at least one expected antideuteron event at AMS-02, and a region around \(m_\chi,m_\phi\sim50\)–100 GeV also reaches GAPS. A benchmark with \((m_\chi,m_\phi)=(66,53)\) GeV and \(\langle\sigma v\rangle=6.3\times10^{-26}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\) predicts **9.9 AMS-02 antideuterons** and **1.2 GAPS antideuterons**. By contrast, antihelium-3 remains difficult: the favorable \((43,5)\)-GeV benchmark, using \(9.8\times10^{-28}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\), gives **3.2 antideuterons and 0.5 antihelium-3 events** at AMS-02, a ratio **0.16**. Those event numbers are forecasts under assumed future acceptances, not measured excesses or confidence limits.

The same cascade fits the *shape* of the Galactic-centre gamma-ray excess near \((m_\chi,m_\phi)=(35,27)\) GeV, but requires \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\simeq1.14\times10^{-25}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\), above the paper's antiproton limit. Even a larger allowed inner-Galaxy J-factor only reduces it to about \(5.6\times10^{-26}\ {
m cm^3\,s^{-1}}\). The authors therefore disfavor a simultaneous explanation under their reference halo and transport assumptions.

**Limitations.** The mediator decay is sampled with isotropic massless phase space rather than a specified ultraviolet completion. Coalescence universality, antiproton production, Galactic transport and solar modulation dominate the systematics; the plotted ±20% coalescence band does not encompass all of them. Most importantly, AMS-02 has not published a conclusive antideuteron or antihelium flux, and the analysis uses a privately communicated antihelium acceptance estimate. The result identifies testable parameter regions; it does not explain established antinucleus events.

##### History

The production mechanism was introduced by **Gemmell, Hooper, Koren and Vassallo** in [*Dark Matter, Baryon Number, and Cosmic-Ray Antinuclei*, PRL 137, 171802 (2026), arXiv:2603.12314v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12314v2). That paper used event-level coalescence to show that a \(B=L=3\) mediator could enhance antideuteron and antihelium multiplicities by factors of roughly 10–100 relative to direct \(b\bar b\) annihilation. The new work's substantive step is to propagate those particles, constrain the accompanying antiprotons with AMS-02, fold detector acceptances and test gamma rays. It consequently narrows the earlier qualitative claim: antideuterons can be observable in viable regions, while antihelium is only marginal under the more conservative sensitivity prescription.

The experimental baseline is still a null result. BESS-Polar II's published search gives \(\Phi_{\bar d}<6.7\times10^{-5}\ ({\rm m^2\,s\,sr\,GeV/n})^{-1}\) over **0.163–1.100 GeV/n**; AMS-02 candidate reports have not become a published flux measurement. Conventional WIMP studies, such as [Cholis, Linden and Hooper, *PRD* 102, 103019 (2020), arXiv:2001.08749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08749), already found that antideuterons may approach experimental reach while antihelium generally does not. The baryon-carrying cascade changes that hierarchy, especially the antihelium-to-antideuteron ratio, but uncertainties in formation and acceptance remain large enough that confirmation requires published events, backgrounds and efficiencies.

#### The years before core collapse may beat the supernova as a MeV-particle laboratory

**Paper:** *Presupernovae Strongly Constrain Electrophilic MeV Bosons* — **Yu Cheng, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo, Chui-Fan Kong and Edoardo Vitagliano**. [arXiv:2611.03813v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03813v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03813v1). Submitted **2 November 2026 at 19:00:16 UTC** and first listed **4 November**. v1 preprint, with no journal publication listed. **Classification: model-dependent astrophysical exclusion from an existing gamma-ray dataset.**

**Parameter and physical question.** A dark photon \(A'\) kinetically mixed with the photon has dimensionless coupling \(\epsilon\) and, above \(2m_e\), can decay to \(e^+e^-\). Supernova explosions are standard factories for MeV particles, but their dense plasma has a frequency of order **10 MeV**, suppressing in-medium mixing for lower-mass dark photons. Massive stars during carbon, oxygen and silicon burning are cooler but remain hot for years, with plasma frequencies only **30–70 keV**. The paper asks whether their much longer exposure produces more escaping particles than the explosion.

**Method.** MESA r26.4.1 evolves solar-metallicity stars of **9–60 solar masses** to the onset of core infall. For a representative 20-solar-mass star, the core advances from approximately **80 keV** during carbon burning, through **190 keV** in oxygen burning, to **320 keV** in the final silicon-burning days. The authors calculate in-medium dark-photon production, decay and escape throughout these profiles, then average the positron yield over a Salpeter initial-mass function. A Galactic core-collapse rate of **2.3 per century** and a spiral-arm source distribution are convolved with an intentionally broad **1-kpc** positron-smearing scale.

The predicted disk-like 511-keV intensity is compared with INTEGRAL/SPI longitude bins at \(|l|<20^\circ\). The one-sided statistic counts only model flux above the data and imposes \(\chi^2\le4\), a **2σ** construction without fitting or subtracting the bulge. ##### History

Earlier [combined germanium work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27121) profiled the Lindhard quenching parameter and obtained **\(0.237^{+0.027}_{-0.022}\)** at 1σ using a Klein–Nystrand form factor and radiative corrections. [CONUS+ run 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37318) reported **\(0.225\pm0.022\)** at roughly **10 MeV**. These results use different data and nuclear/quenching assumptions; their error-bar ratios are not a clean measure of the gain due solely to the new nuclear priors. Electron-sector precision tests remain more precise, while CEνNS supplies a complementary neutrino-sector test and better response modelling for dark-matter experiments.

#### Constraints on a Long-Lived X17 Vector Boson with FASER

**Authors:** FASER Collaboration. **Paper:** [arXiv:2611.03904v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03904v1), submitted 2 November 2026; first announced **4 November**. **Status:** preprint; reinterpretation of existing displaced-decay searches.

**Question and data.** Does a long-lived vector interpretation of the nuclear X17 anomaly survive FASER's forward LHC searches? The analysis combines orthogonal selections from its **27 fb⁻¹ dark-photon** and **57.7 fb⁻¹ axion-like-particle** searches at **13.6 TeV**. The former contains zero candidates against **\(0.0023\pm0.0023\)** expected background; the latter has one against **\(0.44\pm0.39\)**. The integrated luminosities overlap in time and must not simply be added.

**Model and result.** Fermion couplings are defined by \(e\varepsilon_f X_\mu\bar f\gamma^\mu f\). In the principal benchmark, the proton coupling is zero, the neutron coupling is fixed to **\(\varepsilon_n=0.01\)**, neutrino couplings vanish and **\(\mathrm{Br}(X\to e^+e^-)=1\)**. At **\(m_X=17\ \mathrm{MeV}\)**, a profiled **90% CLs** analysis excludes approximately

\[
2\times10^{-8}<\varepsilon_e<3\times10^{-4}.
\]

This covers the remaining low-electron-coupling window in that benchmark. The larger-coupling region decays too promptly for the same reach. Production includes meson decays and nucleon bremsstrahlung, with a substantial uncertainty from the off-shell treatment. A zero static proton coupling does not eliminate finite-momentum-transfer bremsstrahlung. Changing the fixed neutron coupling, decay branching ratio or vector structure changes the exclusion; this is not a model-independent rejection of every X17 interpretation.

##### History

The nuclear anomaly concerns unexpected electron–positron angular distributions, which motivated a roughly 17 MeV mediator. The key recent development was [Celentano, Marini and Marsicano's E141 reanalysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22102), which treated production and experimental acceptance more precisely. In its revised analysis, a remaining interval around **\(6.5\times10^{-5}\lesssim\varepsilon_e\lesssim1.1\times10^{-4}\)** survived electron-coupling searches. FASER's proton-collision production supplies a complementary test, conditional on its specified quark couplings.

[MEG II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07994) found no significant X17 evidence in excited-beryllium decays and placed 90% limits on the ratios of X17 to photon emission, **\(R_{18.1}<1.2\times10^{-5}\)** and **\(R_{17.6}<1.8\times10^{-6}\)** across **16.5–17.1 MeV**. Those nuclear-transition limits are not directly comparable to a FASER electron-coupling interval without a production model. Together these studies constrain interpretations of an unresolved anomaly; none of the FASER candidates constitutes an X17 detection.

#### Search for heavy neutral leptons in top quark decays with same-sign, same-flavor dilepton final states in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV

**Authors:** CMS Collaboration. **Paper:** [arXiv:2611.04904v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04904v1), submitted 3 November 2026; first announced **4 November**. **Status:** submitted to JHEP; collider search and exclusion limits.

**Question and method.** Can top-pair events reveal a Majorana heavy neutral lepton through \(t\to bW\to b\ell N\), followed by a same-sign lepton from the decay of \(N\)? The search uses **138 fb⁻¹** of CMS data at **13 TeV**, selecting same-sign electron or muon pairs with jets and a b tag. Boosted decision trees separate signal from top-related backgrounds; nonprompt leptons are constrained from data. Rate and shape systematics enter a simultaneous profile-likelihood fit across the data-taking years.

**Results and assumptions.** No significant excess is found. For a **prompt Majorana** state with **only one flavour mixing nonzero**, the observed **95% CLs** branching-fraction limits span **\(1.1\times10^{-5}\)–\(4.8\times10^{-5}\)** for \(t\to beN\) and **\(3.7\times10^{-6}\)–\(2.4\times10^{-5}\)** for \(t\to b\mu N\), over the mass hypotheses between **20 and 100 GeV** that are tested. The corresponding mixing limits range from **\(1.6\times10^{-4}\) to 0.11** for \(|V_{eN}|^2\), and **\(4.2\times10^{-5}\) to 0.039** for \(|V_{\mu N}|^2\).

These are ranges along mass-dependent curves, not uniform bounds across the entire interval. The analysis omits **75–85 GeV**, where kinematics near the W mass strongly reduce acceptance; interpolation across that gap would be misleading. Prompt-decay and single-flavour assumptions also prevent direct application to displaced particles, Dirac states or arbitrary multi-flavour models. The sensitivity is limited by nonprompt-lepton backgrounds and by the small fraction of signal events in which all decay jets can be reconstructed.

##### History

[ATLAS's earlier top-decay search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05000), using **140 fb⁻¹**, established this channel over **15–75 GeV**. Its best observed electron and muon mixing limits were **\(2.0\times10^{-4}\)** and **\(6.8\times10^{-5}\)** at 95% confidence. Those are minima of mass-dependent curves; comparing only the minima does not define a universal improvement factor. CMS provides an independent implementation and extends the tested mass range above the W threshold.

The stronger direct-production baseline is [CMS's trilepton search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00100), which examined **10 GeV–1.5 TeV** and considered separate Majorana and Dirac hypotheses. Below the W mass it is generally more sensitive to the same single-flavour mixing. The top channel therefore broadens the experimental test and supplies a complementary production environment, rather than setting the strongest overall heavy-neutral-lepton limit.

#### DAMA/LIBRA dark matter signal: a global analysis

**Authors:** Matti Heikinheimo, Sebastian Sassi, Alex Stendahl, Kimmo Tuominen and Niklas Zimmermann. **Paper:** [arXiv:2611.04890v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04890v1), submitted 3 November 2026; first announced **4 November**. **Status:** preprint; multi-experiment phenomenological reanalysis.

**Question and method.** Can the DAMA/LIBRA annual modulation be reconciled with xenon, fluorine, iodine and germanium null results by broadening the interaction and halo model? The study scans nonrelativistic effective operators in separate leading-, next-to-leading- and next-to-next-to-leading-order groups, allows inelastic scattering and variable Na/I quenching, and compares with LZ, PICO and CDMSlite. Halo freedom consists of a colder rescaling of a Maxwellian distribution: the velocity dispersion is \(\alpha\,220/\sqrt2\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\), with **\(0.07\leq\alpha\leq1\)** and escape speed fixed to **544 km s⁻¹**. This is not an arbitrary halo reconstruction.

**Results.** Within the tested families, fitting DAMA tends to overproduce events in other detectors. For example, one next-to-leading-order fit using the reconstructed-count likelihood predicts approximately **316 LZ events** where the background expectation used in that analysis is **18**, and approximately **877 PICO-CF₃I events** in its zero-background treatment. These are illustrative incompatible model predictions, not measured excesses.

The statistical treatment is a central limitation. A Skellam likelihood built from reconstructed DAMA counts gives errors smaller than DAMA's published errors and overweights the modulation data. A Gaussian likelihood using the published errors instead drives the allowed dark-matter signal towards zero. Modulation amplitudes and time residuals are treated as independent despite unavailable covariance information. The paper therefore reinforces tension within its specified model space, but its nominal discrepancy significance should not be interpreted as a universal exclusion of every dark-matter explanation. Operator groups are fitted separately and the full halo/model space is not exhausted.

##### History

[Catena, Ibarra and Wild (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04074) already tested general Galilean-invariant **elastic** interactions, including operator interference and several target materials, and found strong tension under the standard halo model. The November study revisits the problem with newer null data and additional inelastic, quenching and cold-halo freedom; the general observation that changing a single operator rarely reconciles all targets is not new.

An especially useful independent comparison comes from the same detector material. The [ANAIS-112/COSINE-100 combined study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19559) reported, for a simple combination of their six-year results, a **1–6 keV** modulation amplitude of **\(0.0005\pm0.0019\ \mathrm{counts\,day^{-1}\,kg^{-1}\,keV^{-1}}\)** with one-year period and phase fixed to 2 June. It differs from the DAMA amplitude by **4.7σ** in that comparison. This tests the modulation more directly than translating between xenon and NaI interaction models, although background treatment, calibration and quenching remain relevant to physical interpretation. It is not the same statistic as the global EFT fit.

#### SBi3PCF: cosmology with Dark Energy Survey Year 3 cosmic shear

**Authors:** David Gebauer, Anik Halder, Zhengyangguang Gong and Cora Uhlemann. **Paper:** [arXiv:2611.05367v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05367v1), submitted 3 November 2026; first announced **4 November**. **Status:** preprint; cosmological inference from observed shear data.

**Question and method.** How much information beyond two-point cosmic shear can be extracted from the correlation between aperture mass and locally measured shear correlations? The study applies an integrated three-point statistic to the **DES Year 3** catalogue, containing approximately **100 million galaxies over 4,143 deg²**. It compresses a **1,440-component** joint data vector into 17 components and learns its likelihood from CosmoGrid simulations. The analysis fits flat **wCDM**, with baryonic-feedback and intrinsic-alignment parameters and marginalized shear/photo-z uncertainties; the summed neutrino mass is fixed to **0.06 eV**.

**Results.** The joint posterior gives

\[
\Omega_m=0.272^{+0.038}_{-0.033},\qquad
S_8=0.784^{+0.024}_{-0.021},\qquad
w=-1.04^{+0.25}_{-0.19},
\]

where \(S_8=\sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}\) and the intervals are marginalized **16th–84th percentiles**. Relative to its own two-point-only analysis, the joint \((\Omega_m,S_8,w)\) figure of merit improves by **1.59**. This is a result from survey data, rather than the forecast of that improvement.

The posterior depends on the simulation domain and priors, including **\(-2<w<-1/3\)**, and on a restricted intrinsic-alignment model. Coverage tests on held-out simulations pass, but validation against an independent N-body implementation is absent. Full-data posterior-predictive checks give a marginal **p = 0.021**, and a test omitting the third redshift bin gives **p = 0.001**. The authors trace these difficulties to nuisance-related compressed directions and base their consistency criterion on cosmological components, which pass. Removing that bin shifts \(S_8\) by about 2.2 times the original posterior half-width; this is not itself a 2.2σ inconsistency test. These qualifications matter when interpreting the modest differences from other cosmological analyses.

##### History

[Secco et al.'s DES Y3 two-point analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13544) established the cosmic-shear baseline and its sensitivity to intrinsic alignments, baryons and scale cuts. [Gomes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14018) subsequently combined two-point shear with aperture-mass skewness, obtaining **\(S_8=0.749^{+0.027}_{-0.026}\)** and **\(w=-1.39\pm0.31\)** in their wCDM analysis. These use the same underlying DES catalogue, different third-order summaries and different modelling choices; their posteriors are correlated and cannot be treated as independent measurements in a simple tension calculation.

[The first SBi3PCF paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13805) developed and tested the likelihood-learning pipeline on simulations, showing why the integrated three-point likelihood need not be Gaussian. The November paper advances from that validation to real-data cosmology. It demonstrates additional non-Gaussian information without establishing evolving dark energy or a resolution of the clustering-amplitude discrepancy. Its quoted comparison with Planck+BAO is a multidimensional wCDM comparison, not a standalone significance for an \(S_8\) discrepancy.

#### A New Precise Measurement of the e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻(γ) Cross Section with the Initial-State Radiation Method at BaBar

**Authors:** J. P. Lees et al. (BaBar Collaboration). **Paper:** [arXiv:2611.04596v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04596v1), submitted 3 November 2026; first announced **4 November**. **Status:** preprint; experimental cross-section measurement. The [detailed companion, 2611.04621v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04621v1), reports the same measurement and is linked here rather than counted as a second independent result.

**Question and method.** Can an alternative separation of pion and muon pairs clarify the inconsistent cross-section inputs to the Standard Model prediction for the muon magnetic anomaly? BaBar uses **460 fb⁻¹** collected near the Υ(4S), selecting initial-state-radiation events to measure the lower-energy two-pion cross section. Unlike its earlier particle-identification analysis, it statistically separates pions and muons through their different angular distributions. Their ratio cancels common luminosity, photon-efficiency and vacuum-polarization effects. Data-derived corrections, unfolding and bootstrap covariance propagation are applied before integrating the bare cross section, including final-state radiation.

**Results.** Over **0.3–1.4 GeV**, the inferred leading-order two-pion hadronic-vacuum-polarization contribution is

\[
a_\mu^{\pi\pi}=\bigl(525.9\pm3.5_{\rm stat}\pm3.8_{\rm syst}\bigr)\times10^{-10}.
\]

Combining the new and 2009 BaBar measurements, accounting for correlations and enlarging uncertainties where local disagreement warrants it, gives **\((517.8\pm2.4_{\rm stat}\pm3.3_{\rm syst})\times10^{-10}\)** over the same interval. These are contributions to \(a_\mu=(g_\mu-2)/2\), not measurements of the complete anomaly. Near the ρ resonance the new cross section is about **2% higher** than BaBar's earlier result. In the shared **0.6–0.8832 GeV** comparison interval, the new integral is compatible with CMD-3 at **0.9σ**, but differs from KLOE by **4.2σ** and BESIII by **3.0σ**, using the paper's stated comparison uncertainties.

The alternative method provides a valuable systematic cross-check, but it loses statistical discrimination where muons overwhelm pions near threshold and at higher masses. The overall integral is not simply more precise because the luminosity doubled. The muon-to-QED validation agrees at **0.69% total precision**; it does not eliminate every relative pion/muon systematic. This one channel cannot by itself establish the significance of any full Standard Model–experiment discrepancy.

##### History

[BaBar's 2009 measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3589) used **232 fb⁻¹**, an ISR pion/muon ratio and particle identification. It already reduced several common systematics, so the new ingredient is primarily angular separation, fuller data usage and treatment of additional radiation—not the ratio method itself. [KLOE's ratio measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4524) supplied another independent implementation, while [CMD-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08834) used an energy scan and obtained a higher two-pion cross section than several earlier experiments.

The new BaBar result moves toward the higher CMD-3 normalization while leaving disagreement with lower measurements unresolved. Earlier published integrals often use different energy limits and must not be subtracted directly. The collaboration's matched-range comparisons and explicit correlation study are therefore more informative than a comparison of headline \(a_\mu^{\pi\pi}\) numbers.

#### A stem-cell model connects early trophectoderm to a three-dimensional human chorion

**Miaoci Zhang and colleagues**, led by **Mijo Simunovic**, published [*Reconstructing the emergence of the human chorion via Hippo-mediated trophoblast induction*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02082-9) in **Nature Cell Biology on 3 November 2026**. The peer-reviewed work is classified by the journal as a **Technical Report**.

The human chorion is an early fetal membrane and structural precursor of the placenta. Starting with transiently reset human embryonic stem cells, the researchers inhibited LATS kinases to activate the Hippo–YAP route toward trophectoderm without adding external morphogens. Time-resolved bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing mapped an ordered trajectory against integrated embryo and first-trimester chorion references. Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing found an initially hypomethylated state followed by remethylation, while the tested placenta-associated imprinting regions largely retained methylation and allelic bias.

Agitating cell aggregates in suspension produced three-dimensional structures containing trophoblast and stromal-like compartments, outward-facing trophoblast polarity and an inside-out topology relative to the in-vivo chorion. Endogenous WNT signalling was unnecessary for the initial trophectoderm induction but changed the later balance of chorionic lineages. Sorting **CLDN6-positive, ITGA6-negative** cells isolated a proposed intermediate with villous and extravillous trophoblast potential.

This is a model of developmental transitions, not a synthetic placenta or an embryo capable of implantation. Its identities are inferred through transcriptomic projection, markers, epigenetic measurements and in-vitro lineage potential; the topology differs from native tissue, and maternal interactions, vasculature and full placental function are absent. Its value is experimental access to a poorly observable human developmental interval, with the usual limits of stem-cell models. [Article, methods, extended data and deposited datasets](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-026-02082-9).
