#### Revisiting the Neutrino Emission and Physical Parameter Constraints from Time-Resolved Spectra of Gamma-Ray Bursts

**Yang-Dong-Jun Ou, Hou-Jun Lü, Hao-Yu Yuan et al.** [arXiv:2611.14902v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14902v1); submitted 2026-11-11; first announced 13 November 2026; primary **astro-ph.HE**. Accepted for publication in ApJ.

This recalculation asks how prompt variability changes photohadronic neutrino expectations for four bright GRBs, especially 221009A. It sums independently modelled time bins, including proton synchrotron and pion/muon cooling, then folds the muon-neutrino fluence through IceCube effective area: \(N_\nu=\int\Phi_{\nu_\mu}A_{\rm eff}\,dE\). The adopted zero-event criterion is \(N_\nu<2.3\) at 90% confidence; this is a source-model reinterpretation, not a new event-level IceCube search.

For the BOAT, fixing \(R=1.07\times10^{15}\) cm and baryon loading \(\epsilon_p/\epsilon_e=1,3,10\) gives \(\Gamma>254,371,537\), compared with 188,273,395 in the matched time-integrated calculation. In internal shocks with variability time 0.01 s, \(\Gamma=400\) gives loading below 0.66 versus 1.30; at \(\Gamma=700\), below 6.98 versus 19.20. These are conditional limits, not joint marginalized bounds. Time resolution can either increase or decrease the yield: the ratio spans roughly 0.6–3 because increased target density competes with secondary cooling. Proton spectral assumptions change absolute yields by orders of magnitude. The useful result is therefore a correction to the photon-field treatment, rather than a model-independent baryon-loading measurement.

##### History

[IceCube's dedicated BOAT search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05459) established the underlying nondetection. [Veres et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16748) had already used time-resolved Fermi spectra and obtained strong, model-dependent constraints. This paper extends that line using unsaturated GECAM-C spectra and a different cooling prescription; its quoted improvement is against its own time-integrated baseline. It does not establish a universal improvement over Veres, whose magnetic partition, radii and statistical threshold differ.

#### New Physics in Ultra-High-Energy Neutrino Flavor

**Samiur R. Mir, Carlos A. Argüelles and Vedran Brdar.** [arXiv:2611.14624v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14624v1), submitted 10 November 2026, first announced in the 13 November UTC batch; hep-ph; preprint. **Classification: forecast.**

The study asks how flavor measurements between **100 PeV and 10 EeV** could test isotropic Lorentz-invariance violation (LIV). It adds one real flavor coefficient at a time to the propagation Hamiltonian, \(H_{\rm LIV}\propto E^{d-3}\mathring\kappa^{(d)}_{\alpha\beta}\), where the coefficient has units \(\mathrm{GeV}^{4-d}\). SimProp supplies the production-redshift distribution of cosmogenic neutrinos; propagation is folded into projected flavor likelihoods combining radio-in-ice and Earth-skimming experiments. For the **moderate-flux, GRAND50k** configuration, Table S1 gives a projected **95% confidence sensitivity** of \(1.6\times10^{-73}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-4}\) for the dimension-eight \(e\mu\) coefficient, compared with \(4.0\times10^{-73}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-4}\) using Earth-skimming channels alone. The corresponding \(e\tau\) values are \(3.0\times10^{-71}\) and \(2.6\times10^{-70}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-4}\).

These are conditional sensitivities, not observed bounds. They assume a cosmogenic production model, detector deployments and sufficient event statistics, fixed best-fit PMNS parameters, and one nonzero real LIV coefficient; \(ee\) and \(\mu\tau\) structures remain weakly constrained. The forecast inherits detector-response simplifications from the underlying flavor study, including its omission of systematic errors where statistics were assumed dominant.

##### History

[IceCube's astrophysical-flavor analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04654v2) already constrained isotropic LIV with **7.5 years of observed HESE data**, spanning reconstructed energies of 60 TeV–2 PeV. Its source-composition-independent dimension-six \(\tau\tau\) bound is \(3\times10^{-42}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}\) using **Bayes factor greater than 10**, a criterion different from the new forecast's 95% contours. [Brdar and Mir's earlier UHE tau study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19880v2) established the Earth-skimming opportunity; [Burgos-Mondéjar, Thompson and Argüelles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22556v1) supplied the complementary flavor forecast. The advance is the explicit three-flavor LIV assessment with production redshifts. The paper's large cross-sector improvement claims compare different photon/neutrino operators and statistical conventions; they are not a universally comparable improvement in one measured parameter. The clearest like-for-like comparison is the channel combination within this forecast.

#### Earth-sized tests of long-range neutrino interactions

**Dyer F. Cardenas and Mauricio Bustamante.** [arXiv:2611.14642v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14642v1), submitted 10 November 2026, first announced in the 13 November UTC batch; hep-ph; preprint. **Classification: observed constraint and improved propagation treatment.**

A flavor-dependent ultralight vector force changes atmospheric-neutrino oscillations through a Yukawa potential sourced by terrestrial electrons and nucleons. The authors integrate that potential throughout a **12-layer Earth**, instead of assigning its detector value to the whole trajectory, and fit **21,914 DeepCore events from 2011–2019**, with 2,724 live days and reconstructed energies **6.31–158.49 GeV**. Their Hamiltonian includes vacuum, standard matter and new-force terms; all six oscillation parameters and 18 nuisance parameters are profiled, with NuFIT 6.1 priors on four oscillation parameters and normal mass ordering assumed.

For \(L_e-L_\mu\), the dimensionless gauge coupling has a nominal one-sided **90% confidence upper limit** \(g'<3.54\times10^{-26}\) at mediator mass \(m'=10^{-15}\) eV, weakening to \(6.59\times10^{-25}\) at \(10^{-12}\) eV and \(6.56\times10^{-22}\) at \(10^{-9}\) eV ([machine-readable results](https://github.com/mbustama/nu-lri-earth/blob/main/data/limits.json)). The scan constrains eight of 14 symmetries; forces acting equally on muon and tau flavors have no comparable upper limit. Sensitivity comes chiefly from the disappearance minimum near **24 GeV**. The stated 90% intervals cover **\(87.0\pm1.4\%\)** of the tested pseudoexperiments; thresholds restoring nominal coverage would weaken coupling limits by 1.4–2.3%. This statistical caveat is material.

##### History

The same public sample was used in [Garg et al.'s earlier DeepCore search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01220v1), with a constant long-range potential, some oscillation parameters fixed, and a different confidence threshold. Consequently, a blanket ratio between the two published curves is not a controlled improvement factor. Holding all other choices fixed in the new analysis, following the spatial potential strengthens the coupling limit by **1.2 at \(10^{-15}\) eV**, rising to **2.7 at \(10^{-9}\) eV**. The result implements a physical effect already developed in [Smirnov and Xu's potential calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07505) and [Wise and Zhang's depth-dependent study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00591), rather than discovering the possibility of depth dependence.

The independent terrestrial baseline includes [MICROSCOPE's final equivalence-principle test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15487), which found \(\eta_{\rm Ti,Pt}=(-1.5\pm2.3_{\rm stat}\pm1.5_{\rm syst})\times10^{-15}\), and [Eöt-Wash torsion-balance measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2442). Converting those accelerations into \(g'\) depends on each symmetry's matter charges. Under those specified conversions, the new neutrino constraints dominate below mediator masses of roughly \(1.7\times10^{-11}\)–\(2.1\times10^{-10}\) eV, depending on symmetry. Longer-range solar/Galactic constraints outside this mass interval are not displaced by this Earth-only calculation.

#### Solar Neutrinos at the ESSνSB experiment

**Authors and status:** ESSnuSB Collaboration (J. Aguilar et al.; 100 authors), v1 submitted Thu 12 Nov 2026 13:44:31 UTC; first announced Fri 13 Nov; new primary hep-ph, cross hep-ex. Preprint, 18 pages/12 figures. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16807v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16807v1).

**Study and results:** The ESSνSB far detector model has two 270-kt fiducial water-Cherenkov tanks at Zinkgruvan, about 1 km underground (roughly 2700 m water equivalent). It folds an adiabatic three-flavor MSW survival probability and neutrino–electron elastic scattering with 90% efficiency and Gaussian recoil-energy response. The particularly important quasi-single-field diagram joins three inflaton fluctuations to a cubic spectator interaction through three massive propagators. Its nested time integrals have made general analytic treatment difficult. The authors reorganize the diagram with mixed inflaton–spectator propagators, apply a Mellin representation, and derive explicitly summable double series. They first treat weak quadratic mixing and then resum that mixing to all orders, while retaining the leading cubic spectator interaction.

**Result.** For constant mass \(m\) and mixing coefficient \(\rho\), the oscillatory index becomes
\[
\nu_\rho=\sqrt{(m^2+\rho^2)/H^2-9/4},
\]
where \(H\) is the inflationary Hubble rate and \(\rho\) has mass units. Thus the oscillation measures an effective combination of mass and mixing, rather than the bare spectator mass alone. The weak-mixing squeezed limit reproduces the characteristic logarithmic oscillation with a shape envelope proportional to the square root of the soft-to-hard momentum ratio and the heavy-particle Boltzmann suppression. In the large-mass limit, the equilateral triple-exchange integral scales as \(m^{-6}\), as expected from integrating out three massive propagators.

The practical advance is an analytic representation of the **complete momentum shape for this diagram**, including nonperturbative quadratic mixing. In the ordered ratios \(r_1=k_1/k_3\le r_2=k_2/k_3\le1\), the principal nested series converges for \(|r_1|<1\) and then \(|r_2|<1+r_1\); the equilateral endpoint and slowly converging regions receive alternative expansions. Summing the falloff branches before the outer series is essential to cancel a spurious folded singularity. Direct numerical integrations provide checks, but the paper does not supply a universal numerical error or speedup benchmark.

**Limitations.** This is an analytic result in the de Sitter, constant-parameter, Bunch–Davies setup, exact in quadratic mixing but perturbative in the cubic interaction. It is neither a detection of an inflationary particle nor an observational bound on its mass. Other interactions, loops, time-dependent turning and departures from the assumed background can change the observable bispectrum. Series convergence and numerical efficiency depend on momentum configuration; the analytic representation is not guaranteed to be the fastest numerical method everywhere.

##### History

[Chen and Wang (2009/2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3380) established the quasi-single-field baseline: an isocurvature fluctuation transfers its self-interaction to curvature perturbations through three mixing vertices, giving intermediate squeezed shapes whose scaling encodes the spectator mass. Their explicit in-in calculation and approximate templates already contained this physical mechanism. The new paper addresses the analytic evaluation of its full shape and the treatment of mixing, rather than introducing a new source of non-Gaussianity.

Recent work also changed the analytic baseline substantially. [Huenupi, Muñoz, Palma and Sypsas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18248v3) derived exact linear solutions and a closed power spectrum for arbitrary mass and coupling in the constant-background two-field system. Independently, [Belrhali, Poisson and Renaux-Petel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23243) used Laplace-space kernels to construct strongly mixed bispectra and convergent series, including a detailed discussion of the collider signal. These are substantive predecessors, so the present result should not be described as the first analytic access to strong mixing. Its contribution is a complementary Mellin-space construction for the triple-massive-exchange shape, explicit recursion and convergence prescriptions, and connections between weak-mixing and arbitrary-mixing limits. Whether one representation is preferable for data analysis depends on stability, speed and the interaction basis; that comparison is not settled by this paper.

A continuous-spin graviton remains massless but has a nonzero Pauli–Lubanski invariant \(W^2=-\rho_g^2\); \(\rho_g\) is an energy-valued **spin scale**, not a graviton mass. The calculation fixes the photon's spin scale to zero and treats the stochastic background as isotropic, stationary, Gaussian and unpolarized, with its production unaffected by \(\rho_g\). Production into additional helicities and a full nonlinear gravity description remain outside the calculation; the posterior plateau also makes the lower logarithmic prior boundary relevant to interpreting the credibility statement.

##### History

[NANOGrav's 2023 background analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16213) found Hellings–Downs correlations among **67 pulsars**, supplying the observational baseline that a modified response must preserve. Massive-graviton dispersion limits and photon continuous-spin constraints concern different parameters and should not be ranked numerically against \(\rho_g\).

#### From Matched Black-Hole Scalar Charges to Gravitational-Wave Inspirals in a Massive Scalar–Gauss–Bonnet Effective Theory


**Jie Wu, Mengfei Sun and Jin Li.** [arXiv:2611.14934v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14934v1), submitted 11 November 2026; first announced 13 November; gr-qc. Preprint. **Effective-theory derivation.**

These are not interchangeable: in the action \(16\pi\mathcal L=R-\tfrac12(\nabla\phi)^2-\tfrac12\mu^2\phi^2+\beta_0\phi\mathcal G\), with \(\beta_0\) a length squared, the large-radius solution contains a forced \(48\beta_0 M^2/(\mu^2r^6)\) tail as well as a homogeneous Yukawa term. The coefficient of the exponential therefore cannot simply be declared the worldline charge.

For a weakly coupled nonspinning black hole and \(\epsilon=2\mu M\ll1\), matched asymptotics give the local coefficient **\(C_{\rm loc}=4-\tfrac43\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3\log^p\epsilon)\)**, with no linear mass correction. The renormalized worldline charge also contains scale-dependent logarithms that cancel corresponding terms in the field. Numerical reconstruction finds the quadratic coefficient \(-1.3333334\) in the stated fitting window; independent shooting and boundary-value solutions agree in the decaying-mode coefficient to better than \(1.51\times10^{-8}\) in the reported small-mass tests. These are numerical consistency checks, not observational errors.

The paper then derives leading-PN circular-inspiral dynamics and scalar radiation while retaining the finite-range Yukawa factor. Dipole radiation opens only at orbital frequency \(\Omega>\mu\), with phase-space suppression \((1-\mu^2/\Omega^2)^{3/2}\); scalar quadrupole radiation opens earlier, at \(2\Omega>\mu\), with the corresponding fifth-power suppression. Equal scalar charge-to-mass ratios remove the dipole. Scalar breathing and longitudinal strains satisfy \(h_l/h_b=\mu^2/(n\Omega)^2\) for a propagating harmonic. Source phasing is kept distinct from propagation dispersion.

The illustrative binary is \(30+20\,M_\odot\), with \(\beta_0=0.02(20\,M_\odot)^2\) and a small local scalar-mass parameter. Importantly, those binary examples still use the **leading massless charge** \(Q_A=2\beta_0/m_A\): the full matched correction has not yet been combined with all conservative and radiative terms at a consistent higher order. Spin, merger, ringdown, cosmological propagation and a detector likelihood are absent; the stationary-phase approximation is nonuniform at radiation thresholds. No new observational bound follows.

##### History

Their Section 4.4.1 explicitly left the massive black-hole charge matching unresolved and used massless charges for illustrative systems. The new local-versus-far-field distinction addresses that open ingredient, while its leading-PN radiation model complements rather than replaces the earlier higher-order conservative calculation. [Existing massive-sGB inspiral constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14801) already reach \(\sqrt{\alpha_{\rm GB}}\lesssim1\) km at 90% credibility for \(\mu\lesssim10^{-13}\) eV under their action normalization, dipole model and priors. The present paper offers ingredients for improving such waveform tests, not a more stringent coupling limit. A consistently matched, higher-PN waveform with data inference remains to be demonstrated.

**Hong Su, Tao Yang and He Wang.** [arXiv:2611.17038v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17038v1), submitted 12 November 2026; first announced 13 November; gr-qc. Preprint. **Inference method and reanalysis.**

SVOM/C-GFT reported on **13 November at 06:59:31 UTC** that G 6-43 faded in g, r and i over approximately 1.98 h, with the largest amplitude in g, consistent with stellar-flare decay. The circular's **body identifies trigger 01709322455 and G 6-43, but its subject line names the older trigger 01709322272**; its stated start time and relative delay are also inconsistent. The optical evidence is therefore linked here by the explicit source identification, without adopting the ambiguous timing. This is a likely stellar classification, not a confirmed extragalactic transient. [GCN 46243](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46243).

#### Basal-ganglia atlas provides a shared cellular reference across species

#### Gene importance does not predict lower mutation rates in two microbial models

A peer-reviewed [*PLOS Biology* study published 12 November](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3004089), by Sofia Radrizzani and Laurence Hurst, revisits whether evolution preferentially protects important genes from mutation. It analyses accumulated spontaneous mutations in wild-type *Escherichia coli* and budding yeast, controlling for genomic covariates and comparing correlations with simulations that preserve trinucleotide-dependent mutation probabilities. Essentiality and protein abundance did **not** predict lower mutation rates in either organism; replication-strand orientation also did not predict mutation density in *E. coli*.

The authors identify statistical artefacts associated with sparse mutation counts and gene length, and show why mismatch-repair-deficient strains need not reproduce wild-type patterns. Weak transcription-associated trends differed between species. The result challenges a general protective relationship between gene importance and mutation rate; it does not imply uniform mutation rates or rule out every organism-specific mechanism.

The disputed background includes [Monroe and colleagues’ 2022 *Arabidopsis* study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04269-6). Independent [population-genetic scrutiny by Charlesworth and Jensen](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9907473/) showed that its population-genomic pattern could arise from purifying selection without reduced mutation rates. That critique concerns inference from population data; the new microbial analysis is not a direct replication in *Arabidopsis*.

#### Large pain GWAS expands the association map while exposing cohort imbalances

A peer-reviewed, open-access [*Nature Neuroscience* study published 12 November](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02455-1) combines **1,235,695 European-ancestry participants** from the Million Veteran Program, UK Biobank and FinnGen. Their pain measures differ: longitudinal pain intensity, number of chronically painful body sites, and diagnosis-based records. The meta-analysis identified **343 independent loci**, including **92 new pain associations**, and integrated molecular datasets to prioritise 490 candidate genes.

The shared signals support investigating biological processes across different pain presentations, but candidate-gene rankings measure convergent annotation evidence, not demonstrated causality. Mendelian-randomisation findings involving psychiatric traits, inflammation and brain structure remain assumption-dependent causal hypotheses. The ancestry restriction limits generalisation; differing phenotypes complicate interpretation. The authors specifically caution that most apparent sex-specific signals reflect unequal cohort composition and statistical power rather than established sex differences in genetic architecture. These are research associations, not a clinically validated diagnostic test or evidence that manipulating the implicated pathways treats pain. The published PDF’s methods, results and discussion were reviewed.

#### Iron-mineral chemistry offers a qualified explanation for early molybdenum-based nitrogen fixation

A peer-reviewed [*Nature Communications* article published 12 November](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79171-8), by Weiduo Hao and colleagues, investigates why molybdenum-dependent nitrogenase apparently evolved early when oxygen-poor oceans are often assumed to have contained little available molybdenum. The study combines a compilation of iron-rich sediments with laboratory iron-oxidation and ferrihydrite-precipitation experiments and synchrotron spectroscopy.

At approximately **pH 8** and **0.56 M NaCl**, adding **2.2 mM dissolved silica** reduced molybdenum removal into ferrihydrite to **less than 4%**, while vanadium remained strongly scavenged. Applied to banded iron formations, the measured partitioning suggests that some Archean waters could retain more molybdenum relative to vanadium than previously assumed. This provides a plausible environmental explanation for early molybdenum-dependent nitrogen fixation; it does not directly date the enzyme’s origin.

The [published peer-review record](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-79171-8/MediaObjects/41467_2026_79171_MOESM5_ESM.pdf) challenged whether the reconstruction represents the global ocean. The final article explicitly allows elevated molybdenum to have been confined to particular hydrothermally influenced water masses, reconciling it with contrasting shale and pyrite records. Extrapolation also depends on mineral precursors, pH, competing ions, redox chemistry and alteration after deposition. The published article-in-press PDF, including methods and discussion, was reviewed; the earlier September 2025 preprint was not treated as the final paper.

#### Kelp retains a conditional asexual pathway when mates are absent

A peer-reviewed [*PLOS Biology* study published 12 November](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3004065) combines developmental experiments, gene-expression analysis and population genetics in *Laminaria pallida*. Both male and female laboratory cultures produced sporophytes without fertilisation. Male development involved enlarged, egg-like cells and extensive changes in expression away from the usual male programme; asexual offspring showed genome duplication and poorer growth and developmental performance than sexually produced offspring.

Separating potential partners with a **5 µm mesh** reduced asexual development when the opposite sex was present, consistent with a diffusible mate-recognition signal. The signal remains unidentified, and the authors could not completely exclude sperm passage through the mesh. Screening **523 wild sporophytes** from Namibia and South Africa found no unambiguous population-genetic evidence that asexual reproduction contributes substantially to natural recruitment. The study supports a latent, context-dependent reproductive option, while leaving its importance in sparse or warming wild populations unresolved. The published PDF’s results, experimental descriptions and discussion were reviewed.
