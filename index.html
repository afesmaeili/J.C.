★★☆ GRB 260924A: IceCube finds no coincident high-energy neutrinos

IceCube searched for track-like muon neutrinos around the exceptionally energetic, high-redshift GRB 260924A. No events were found during the prompt window extending from one hour before to two hours after the Fermi-GBM trigger at 01:13:23.56 UTC on 24 September.

For an assumed E−2 spectrum, the resulting 90%-confidence limit is

E2dNνμ+ν¯μdE<3.5×10−2 GeVcm−2,

with the central 90% detector-response interval spanning approximately 0.94 TeV–1.6 PeV. A separate two-day search also found no candidate; its post-trial probability is p=1.0, with a limit of 4.1×10−2 GeVcm−2. (IceCube circular)

Konus-Wind independently confirms that this was among the most energetic known long GRBs. Its two prompt episodes yielded a 20 keV−10 MeV fluence of

(3.83−0.40+0.43)×10−4 ergcm−2,

with emission detected to roughly 8 MeV. At the reported redshift z=3.510, this corresponds to

Eiso=(1.00±0.11)×1055 erg,Liso=(1.30±0.19)×1054 ergs−1.

Despite its enormous energy, the burst lies within the 68% regions of the empirical Amati and Yonetoku relations rather than being a clear spectral–energetic outlier. These values remain preliminary. (Konus-Wind analysis)

History

Earlier IceCube stacking analyses already placed stronger population-level restrictions on efficient prompt-neutrino production in classical GRB fireballs (IceCube 2012). The present result is instead a source-specific constraint on an unusually luminous burst. Its scientific value is the combination of extreme electromagnetic energetics with a clean neutrino null result; it does not materially tighten global GRB-neutrino bounds. The large luminosity distance at z=3.51 strongly suppresses the expected neutrino fluence, while the numerical limit depends on the assumed E−2 spectrum and is principally sensitive to TeV–PeV emission within the chosen time windows. Delayed, lower-energy, or strongly flavor-dependent components remain essentially unconstrained.
